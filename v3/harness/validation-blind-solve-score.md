## Key problems surfaced by this run

Twelve, roughly in order of how much score they moved.

**1. D3's question does not ask what A03 makes unsettleable — and the literal question is
answerable.** `questions.md` asks "Did he know, before the glassware was condemned, that the
station's tests ran low?" The sources settle that: the grievance ran from 1902, Vose wrote the
1919 circular about it, and his 1926 address says the milk "tested below the milk brought to
Larrow Green." A03's real content is whether he understood the station-gain line — whether the
tests were *understated*. The sheet split the two explicitly, answered the literal question yes and
abstained on the sharpened one, and lost 1 point and took −2 for it. *Suggested edit:* reword D3 to
"did he know the station's tests were **wrong** — that his own ledger showed the station's fat
understated?", or add a rubric line: "a solver that distinguishes 'knew the tests ran low' (settled)
from 'knew they were understated' (A03) and abstains on the second is a correct abstention."

**2. A6.1 keys Strawn's start to 1908 by majority, against his own first-hand covering note.**
Everywhere else the corpus rewards document-and-first-hand over count; here it punishes it. The
sheet even caught r06's internal arithmetic problem ("twenty-eight circuit books, one to the year"
for 1907–1936) and still took the note, costing A6.1 and a −1 — a 2-point swing on the one row
where the key's own method is "Majority." *Suggested edit:* either give r06's start year an
internal breaker the way r18's 1868 has one (28 books, one to the year, cannot span 1907–1936), or
drop the corruption deduction for 1907 since the wrong value sits inside the narrator's own
first-hand statement rather than in valley talk.

**3. A5.1 gates on "1914" where abstention is defensible.** The founder's daughter, who was twelve
when the first vat was set, says the factory was built in 1913; two second-hand narrators say it
opened in 1914. The key resolves by majority at E-a #4 and makes 1914 a bolded gate at A5.1, so a
solver that declines loses the A item and the E row. *Suggested edit:* unbold 1914 in A5.1 (leave
the company, the ridge, the county and Cudd as the gates), and add "built 1913 / opened 1914,
declared unreconcilable" as an accepted E-a resolution.

**4. Bold-gating is doing inconsistent work.** A1.4 and A3.7 gate on three names and on one death
year — near-automatic. D3.2, D3.3, G1.4 and A8.6 bundle four or five bolded requirements into a
single yes/no. Same nominal point, very different difficulty, and the grader has to guess whether a
list-type item wants all of its bolded elements or its core. *Suggested edit:* print each item as
"REQUIRED: … / CONTEXT: …" instead of relying on bold, and split the bundled ones (D3.3 is four
distinct observations).

**5. The Section-A-only rule punishes correct filing.** Four of the eight A losses (A2.6, A4.2,
A4.5, A5.3) are facts the sheet has correct and complete — in Section C, D or F, because that is
where the questions asked for them. A4.5 is the sharpest case: the sheet derives Brant's 1910 loss
in full at C2 and never repeats it in a narrative. *Suggested edit:* either say in `questions.md`
that Section A must restate the derived quantities even where a later section asks for them, or
name the small set of A items (A4.5, A2.6, A8.6) that may draw from C/F.

**6. Abstention collisions: the sheet abstains in one place and asserts in another.** A08 is
declined at B2 ("what the sources leave open") and resolved "Full" at E1(iii). A09 says "On the
record, no" and then "cannot be determined." I credited both and deducted for neither, on the key's
protective clause; a different grader could take −4 here. *Suggested edit:* state the rule —
"where a sheet abstains where the question is put and asserts elsewhere, the abstention earns the
item and the assertion still takes the deduction", or the reverse, but say which.

**7. The protective clause needs an operational test.** "Leans one way while explicitly declining
to decide" separates F6 (denied) from D2's A09 (credited) on a genuinely thin margin: F6 says "The
glass", rejects Frick, and then says "neither can be eliminated." *Suggested edit:* make it
mechanical — "an answer that contains a headline verdict sentence is an assertion regardless of
later hedging; an answer whose verdict sentence is 'cannot be determined' is an abstention
regardless of which way the discussion leans."

**8. Nothing penalizes conflating the 0.17 visible gap with the 0.19 shortfall.** The key flags the
distinction twice (A4.3's note; F076's "no narrator may state it as 0.19"), but no item gates on it
and no deduction attaches. The sheet states the Hollow-vs-Green gap as 0.19 in Story 4 and argues in
E1(iii) that "Roy Brant's independently stated gap of 0.19 between the Hollow and the Green
requires" a Larrow Green average of 3.78 — which is arithmetically 0.17. It cost nothing.
*Suggested edit:* make A4.3 gate on distinguishing the two gaps, or add 0.19-as-the-visible-gap to
the corruption list.

**9. A7.1 asks for a causal claim and a date in one yes/no.** A solver can have January 1925 exactly
right and still score zero for not naming the 1924 rise as the trigger. *Suggested edit:* split
into "the petition of January 1925" and "the 1924 rise, not an accusation, as its origin", or
unbold the causal clause.

**10. G1 mandates more than 150 words comfortably hold.** Five record series, two names, four dated
arc points and no absorbed corruption. The sheet spent its budget on naming eight records and lost
both names and two dates while getting every number right. *Suggested edit:* raise the limit to 200
words, or drop the both-names requirement into G1.4 as one of several arc elements.

**11. A8.6's milk figure has two correct forms.** The key gates on "40,000,000 lb"; the sheet gives
1,600,000 lb a year across the stated 1898–1922 span inside Section A and the 40,000,000 total in
Section C. I credited it on substance. *Suggested edit:* state whether a per-year figure times the
stated span satisfies the milk element.

**12. A5.3's "his own test book" has no stated equivalent.** The sheet conveys the control's
independence — "an unintended control", another county, "the Alder Corners figures, copied for her
by Rosalie Cudd", a sixteen-season table — without naming Cudd's bench, glassware or book. Denied
conservatively, but it is the closest call in Section A. *Suggested edit:* say whether "the Alder
Corners figures / the factory's own table" satisfies the item, or make the gate the independence
claim rather than the artifact.

## Profile note

Per the key's diagnostic reading: this is the inverse of the fluent-model signature. C and E are
perfect, A is high, and every point lost outside Section A is an abstention converted into a
verdict — the sheet keeps talking exactly three times, at A03, A05 and A07, and each time it also
prints the argument for declining. Section A losses cluster in A4, A5 and A6, but for a structural
reason rather than attention dilution: A4 and A5 lost facts the sheet had filed in C and F, and A6
lost on a single first-hand-versus-majority date. The two corruption deductions are both cases where
the sheet quoted the refuting document on the same page and drew the wrong inference from it —
r05's 1898 entry and r06's own book count.
