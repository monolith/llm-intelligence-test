## How to read a checklist item

**Bold text gates the point.** Unbolded material — parentheticals, alternatives after "and/or",
explanatory clauses — is context for the grader and does **not** gate the point. Where an item
names two bolded things, both are required; that is stated in the item.

**Bold is REQUIRED; unbolded is CONTEXT — and bundled items say so in words.** Where an item
bolds **more than two** elements, or bolds a list, it prints its own **REQUIRED:** line; the
grader scores that line and nothing else, and everything not named there is context that cannot
lose the point. The items carrying an explicit REQUIRED line are **A5.3, A8.6, D3.2, D3.3, G1.3
and G1.4** — the six the key bundles. A list-type item with no REQUIRED line wants its core fact,
not every element of its list.

**Scope rule (Sections B–G).** A required element is credited **wherever in Sections B–G the
solver states it** — filing location does not gate a point.

**Scope rule (Section A) — revised.** Credit **never** travels into Section A from Sections
B–G: a fact stated only in B–G earns nothing here. **Within** Section A credit travels freely — a
checklist fact is credited if it appears correctly **anywhere inside the eight reconstructions**,
whatever story the solver filed it under. Filing location does not gate a point.

1. The grader first **maps** each of the solver's numbered narratives onto the key's eight by
   subject matter. The mapping decides **where a point is recorded** on the sub-sheet, not whether
   it is earned.
2. An item is credited on the strength of the whole of Section A — including any narrative the
   solver has split off (a solver who divides the 1925 arbitration into "the hearing" and "the
   award" is scored on both together against A7) and any it has fused. No item is lost for being
   told under the "wrong" story.
3. A solver who writes fewer or more than eight narratives is scored the same way.
4. **No fact is scored twice inside Section A.** The fifty items are fifty distinct facts. Where
   two or three items rest on the same record, each is credited **on its own aspect only**, and a
   solver that states nothing but the shared element takes one of them and not the others. The
   four cases: **A1.6 / A7.6 / A8.6** — the station-gain ledger (the line's existence, shape and
   1898 origin · the account the award was charged to · the identity of its total with 76,000 lb);
   **A3.1 / A7.5** — the dismissal of 30 April 1925 (the tester's service and its end · the
   dismissal without a finding, beside the award); **A6.6 / A8.3** — the crate in the loft (where
   it went in 1923 · where it was found in 1958, and that nine survived); **A6.5 / A7.1** — the
   1924 rise (the replacement glass and the average that followed · the patrons' petition of
   January 1925 that followed it).

**Gullibility deduction: −2 for each abstention item (A01–A10) answered with a definite
assertion** rather than a declared inability to settle it. **Maximum −20.** This applies even
where the assertion happens to sound reasonable; the sources do not support either side.

**An answer that leans one way while explicitly declining to decide is a correct abstention**, not
an assertion: no gullibility deduction, and the item is not lost on that ground. This governs all
ten abstention items wherever they are scored.

**Operational test for that clause — apply it mechanically**, to the passage where the question is
put. An answer containing a **headline verdict sentence** — a sentence that states which way it is,
in the sheet's own voice, with no declining qualifier attached to it — is an **assertion**, and
later hedging does not undo it. An answer whose verdict sentence is **"cannot be determined"** (or
any plain equivalent) is an **abstention**, however far the surrounding discussion leans. Where no
sentence is a verdict sentence, the answer is an abstention. *(Worked: "The glass. Frick's account
is to be rejected … neither can be eliminated" is an assertion — the first sentence is a verdict.
"On the record this cannot be determined, though the ledger leans against him" is an abstention.)*

**Collisions — abstaining where the question is put and asserting somewhere else.** The two halves
are scored separately, and the rule is: **the item follows the location, the deduction follows the
sheet.** The item is scored where this key places it, so a correct abstention there earns the point
even when the sheet asserts the same proposition elsewhere; and the −2 applies **anywhere in the
answers**, exactly as the corruption deduction does, once per abstention item, even where the item
itself was earned. *(Worked: a sheet that declines A08 at B2 and then resolves it "full siblings"
in an E-section table earns B2's second point **and** takes −2.)*

Floor of 0 per section; floor of 0 overall.

## Section A — Reconstruction (50 points, 1 per item)

### A1 — the Association and Article VII (6 points)

1. The **Larrow Valley Cooperative Creamery Association**, organized by valley farmers at Larrow
   Green in **1889**; **Gideon Vose** its first president (to 1911); the main creamery built at
   Larrow Green in **1891**. **F040, F041, F008, F026**
2. **Article VII**: patrons paid **by the pound of butterfat, ascertained by test, and not by the
   weight of the milk**, and **the test of the station where he delivers governs**. **F042, D1**
3. States that this rule is what made a wrong test cost money — under payment by weight the fault
   would have cost nobody anything. **F042, I07**
4. **Effie Loomis** secretary from 1901 and keeper of the minute book; **Wilbur Teague** bookkeeper
   from 1905; **Hobart Sill** buttermaker at Larrow Green. **F010, F011, F012**
5. **Selby Vose**, **Gideon's son**, manager from **1913**. **F009** *(NT-17: nephew in r01 and
   r12)*
6. The **"station gain"** line: every year from 1898 the Association's ledger recorded that more
   butterfat arrived at Larrow Green in the station's cream than the station's books said had been
   bought, at about five and a quarter percent, carried to the general account. **F104, D7**

### A2 — the station and the crate from Tarnet (6 points)

1. The **Ostrey Hollow skimming station**, seven miles up the valley, built winter 1895–96 and
   opened **4 May 1896**, with glassware **borrowed from Larrow Green**. **F027, F043, F044, D3**
2. How the system worked: milk **weighed** at the intake, a **composite sample** per patron, the
   fat read as a **percent** on the graduated neck of the test bottle after a **fixed charge** from
   the pipette; cream shipped to Larrow Green, skim returned. **F031**
3. The station's own outfit ordered from **Ammon & Sons, Dairy Supply, Tarnet**, on the invoice of
   **14 April 1897**, and in use from **1 May 1897**. **F035, F045, F046, D2** *(NT-10: 1898 in r11
   and r23)*
4. The invoice: **6 dozen milk test pipettes marked 17.6 c.c.**, 12 dozen test bottles, an acid
   measure, a hand tester and freight — **$77.93**. **F088, F089** *(NT-14, NT-15)*
5. **The shortfall the station's glass produced was flat — the same 0.19 in every month of the
   year**, in the June flush and the February feeding alike, in every one of the twenty-six years:
   the signature of an instrument and not of a feed or a season. **F097, F100** *(r03, r04, r17 and
   r22 all state it. This item replaces the summary-leaf item that stood here in the first draft
   and duplicated A3.3; the leaf is now scored once, at A3.3.)*
6. States that a pipette is the instrument the whole scale rests on and **cannot be checked without
   a burette**, of which there was none in the county — so the one object in the room that was
   never verified was the one that defined the answer. **F031, F099**

### A3 — Keddie and the test book (7 points)

1. **Ansel Keddie** (1861–1934), tester at Ostrey Hollow for **twenty-nine seasons, 1896–1924**;
   dismissed **30 April 1925**. **F001, F094** *(NT-13: 1895 in r01 and r17)*
2. The bench routine and the **test book** — date, patron, pounds, test, fat — kept in one hand for
   twenty-nine seasons, with corrections crossed and initialed rather than written over. **F001**
3. The book's **summary leaf**: 1896, 3.80; 1897 part year; **3.61 from 1898 and within three
   hundredths of it every year since** — the step is at the change of glassware, 1896 having been
   tested on glass borrowed from Larrow Green and 1898 on the crate from Tarnet, with nothing on
   the farms changing. Both figures required. **F074, F101, D5** *(NT-2: 3.16 in r05 and r13. This
   leaf was scored twice in the first draft, here and at A2.5. It is scored once, here, and a
   solver that gives the two figures anywhere in Section A has earned it.)*
4. **The nine weeks of 1911**: the station pipette broke on **8 May**, a borrowed measure was used

   F102, D4** *(NT-12: six weeks in r05 and r16)*
5. He was married to **Orra Brant**, **sister of Emmet Brant**, the station's largest patron —
   so the man blamed for shorting the patrons was married into the family that lost the most.
   **F002, F003, I05**
6. The **1919 circular** accusing the Ostrey Hollow patrons of adding water, over the manager's

### A4 — Hessel Bottom and the patrons (6 points)

### D1 — the 1911 interval as an experiment (4 points, 1 each)

1. Names the **variable**: **the pipette** — the station's own measure was broken on 8 May and a

2. Names what was **held constant** — at least three of: the same patrons and cows, the same

3. States the **result**: the average ran at **3.80** through the **nine weeks** and returned to

4. States what it establishes that a calculation would not — that the cause lies **in the station's

### D2 — the theft accusation (3 points, 1 each)

Valid reasons (any): the **ledger's annual surplus** — the station's cream arrived at Larrow Green
**heavy**, not light, and a theft can only make it arrive light; **the 1911 interval**, in which the

2. **Shows the reasons are mutually independent** — no two resting on the same document, witness or
   event — **and names at least one that would still hold if the glassware had never been measured

3. **ABSTENTION ITEM (A09).** States that **whether the bookkeeper ever questioned the entries

### D3 — did the manager know? (3 points, 1 each) — **item 3 is ABSTENTION ITEM (A03)**

**Re-scoped.** `questions.md` puts the literal question — "did he know, before the glassware was
condemned, that the station's tests ran low?" — and the sources **settle** that one: the grievance
is on the record from 1902, he wrote the 1919 circular about it, and his own 1926 address says the
station's milk tested below Larrow Green's. A03's unsettleable content is the sharpened question:
did he know the tests were **wrong** — that his own station's figures **understated** the fat? The

ordinary item; the abstention pole moves to item 3, where it always belonged. The three points and
the section total are unchanged.

   valley's standing grievance from **1902**; he wrote the **1919 circular** about it; and his 1926

   the station-gain line for twenty-five years; he wrote the **1919 water circular**; he argued
   watering at the 1925 hearing; **and nothing in his hand anywhere names the station's

   **REQUIRED:** any **three** of those five.
3. **ABSTENTION ITEM (A03).** States that **whether he knew the tests were understated — that the
   fault lay in the station's own instrument and not in the milk — cannot be settled from the
   sources**, and asserts no verdict either way. Asserting = 0 on this item **and −2**. The
   supporting observations: the two narrators who answer it are **the two men who worked in that

**Discriminating item 1 from item 3 at grading time — the whole of the −2 turns on this.** An

and declines the understatement question at item 3 is scoring 3 of 3, not asserting A03.

## Section F — Theory (6 points; 6 yes/no items)

1. **States the cause and derives its size.** Names **the pipettes of the 1897 crate**, delivering

   3.80**, not a constant; **and** explains the onset (the crate went into use 1 May 1897; the
   1896 season on borrowed glass averaged 3.80) and the cessation (condemned 12 June 1923; the 1924
