# Answers & Scoring Key, v3 (SECRET — never show to the system under test)

Total: **100 points.** A 50 / B 8 / C 14 / D 10 / E 8 / F 6 / G 4.

Section C is objective (exact match, tolerances stated). A, D, F and G are scored as **yes/no
checklists** — one point per item, present or not, no partial credit and no point bands. B is
scored against stated criteria. E is scored in four capped buckets. Score with a human or an
independent judge given this key and `canon.md`; never the system under test, never a model of
the same family.

Every item cites a fact id from `canon.md`. Every C item was re-derived from canon before this
key was finalized; the derivations are printed with the answers.

---

## How to read a checklist item

**Bold text gates the point.** Unbolded material — parentheticals, alternatives after "and/or",
explanatory clauses — is context for the grader and does **not** gate the point. Where an item
names two bolded things, both are required; that is stated in the item.

**Scope rule (Sections B–G).** A required element is credited **wherever in Sections B–G the
solver states it** — filing location does not gate a point.

**Scope rule (Section A) — stricter in v3.** Credit **never** travels into Section A from
Sections B–G. Within Section A, a checklist item is credited only where the fact appears in the
solver's reconstruction **of that story**. Because the solver draws its own boundaries cue-lessly:

1. The grader first **maps** each of the solver's numbered narratives onto the key's eight by
   subject matter, before scoring anything.
2. A checklist is then scored against the mapped narrative, **plus** any narrative the solver has
   clearly split off from it (for example a solver who divides the 1925 arbitration into "the
   hearing" and "the award" is scored on both together against A7).
3. A fact filed under a **different** story — the 1911 interval reported only in the 1958
   narrative, the invoice total reported only in the arbitration narrative — is **not** credited
   on the checklist it belongs to. This is the bite of the rule, and it is what distinguishes a
   solver that has reconstructed eight histories from one that has produced one long history in
   eight pieces.
4. A solver who writes fewer or more than eight narratives is scored the same way: each checklist
   against whatever narrative(s) cover that subject.
5. No fact is scored twice inside Section A.

## Section A — Reconstruction (50 points, 1 per item)

### A1 — the Association and Article VII (6 points)

1. The **Larrow Valley Cooperative Creamery Association**, organized by valley farmers at Larrow
   Green in **1889**; **Gideon Vose** its first president (to 1911); the main creamery built at
   Larrow Green in **1891**. **F040, F041, F008, F026**
2. **Article VII**: patrons paid **by the pound of butterfat, ascertained by test, and not by the
   weight of the milk**, and **the test of the station where he delivers governs**. **F042, D1**
3. States that this rule is what made a wrong test cost money — under payment by weight the fault
   would have cost nobody anything. **F042, I07**
4. **Effie Loomis** secretary from 1901 and keeper of the minute book; **Wilbur Teague** bookkeeper
   from 1905; **Hobart Sill** buttermaker at Larrow Green. **F010, F011, F012**
5. **Selby Vose**, **Gideon's son**, manager from **1913**. **F009** *(NT-17: nephew in r01 and
   r12)*
6. The **"station gain"** line: every year from 1898 the Association's ledger recorded that more
   butterfat arrived at Larrow Green in the station's cream than the station's books said had been
   bought, at about five and a quarter percent, carried to the general account. **F104, D7**

### A2 — the station and the crate from Tarnet (6 points)

1. The **Ostrey Hollow skimming station**, seven miles up the valley, built winter 1895–96 and
   opened **4 May 1896**, with glassware **borrowed from Larrow Green**. **F027, F043, F044, D3**
2. How the system worked: milk **weighed** at the intake, a **composite sample** per patron, the
   fat read as a **percent** on the graduated neck of the test bottle after a **fixed charge** from
   the pipette; cream shipped to Larrow Green, skim returned. **F031**
3. The station's own outfit ordered from **Ammon & Sons, Dairy Supply, Tarnet**, on the invoice of
   **14 April 1897**, and in use from **1 May 1897**. **F035, F045, F046, D2** *(NT-10: 1898 in r11
   and r23)*
4. The invoice: **6 dozen milk test pipettes marked 17.6 c.c.**, 12 dozen test bottles, an acid
   measure, a hand tester and freight — **$77.93**. **F088, F089** *(NT-14, NT-15)*
5. **The 1896 season averaged 3.80 on the borrowed glass; the first full year on the station's own
   glass, 1898, averaged 3.61, and it stayed at 3.61 for twenty-five years.** Both figures required.
   **F101, D5** *(NT-2)*
6. States that a pipette is the instrument the whole scale rests on and **cannot be checked without
   a burette**, of which there was none in the county — so the one object in the room that was
   never verified was the one that defined the answer. **F031, F099**

### A3 — Keddie and the test book (7 points)

1. **Ansel Keddie** (1861–1934), tester at Ostrey Hollow for **twenty-nine seasons, 1896–1924**;
   dismissed **30 April 1925**. **F001, F094** *(NT-13: 1895 in r01 and r17)*
2. The bench routine and the **test book** — date, patron, pounds, test, fat — kept in one hand for
   twenty-nine seasons, with corrections crossed and initialed rather than written over. **F001**
3. The book's **summary leaf**: 1896, 3.80; 1897 part year; **3.61 from 1898 and within three
   hundredths of it every year since**. **F074, F101, D5** *(NT-2: 3.16 in r05 and r13)*
4. **The nine weeks of 1911**: the station pipette broke on **8 May**, a borrowed measure was used
   until **10 July**, the tests ran at **3.80** through the interval and returned to 3.61 the day
   the station's own measure came back — and Keddie set a **question mark** against it. **F052,
   F102, D4** *(NT-12: six weeks in r05 and r16)*
5. He was married to **Orra Brant**, **sister of Emmet Brant**, the station's largest patron —
   so the man blamed for shorting the patrons was married into the family that lost the most.
   **F002, F003, I05**
6. The **1919 circular** accusing the Ostrey Hollow patrons of adding water, over the manager's
   signature; and Keddie's own statement of 17 March 1925 that he **tested as he was taught and
   cannot say why the figures are what they are**, and took no cream. **F058, D9**
7. He died in **1934**, still under the imputation, and the test book was impounded in 1925 and
   went to the county historical rooms in 1959. **F066, F020**

### A4 — Hessel Bottom and the patrons (6 points)

1. **Hessel Bottom**, the wet meadow at Ostrey Hollow; **six farms** had rights on it; the largest
   was **Emmet Brant's**. **F028, F003**
2. Brant kept a **private weigh book from 1 January 1897** — his own scale against the station's,
   daily, for decades — **and the two scales agreed**, which disposes of any question of short
   weight. **F003, D10, F105**
3. **Complaints from 1902 that Ostrey Hollow milk tested below Larrow Green milk** — the visible
   gap that started everything. (Larrow Green's own average of about 3.78 against the station's
   3.61 is context and does not gate the point: the figure is single-source.) **F048, F098a**
4. The **1908 committee under Nyle Grigg** sampled **January, February and March 1908** and
   reported that the wet hay of the bottom thinned the cream — stated as the finding, not as fact.
   **F050, F096, D15** *(A solver who asserts the hay explanation as true loses this item and takes
   a corruption deduction.)*
5. **Brant's 1910 year: 200,000 lb delivered on a recorded test of 3.42**; and the loss was
   **proportional**, so the richer herds lost more — the Vose Jerseys at a recorded 4.56 were short
   0.24 of a point where Brant's herd was short 0.18. **F084, F087, I08**
6. The book passed to **Roy Brant** in 1929 and was **lent by him to Ivy Keddie in 1958**; Hazel
   Brant after 1962. **F021, F006**

### A5 — Alder Corners, over Redlow Ridge (6 points)

1. The **Alder Corners Cheese Company**, over **Redlow Ridge** in **Vessey County**, opened by
   **Jerome Cudd** in **1914**. **F017, F029, F055**
2. From **1915**, **four** of the Hessel Bottom patrons hauled there in the **summer flush** and to
   Ostrey Hollow the rest of the year — for a cheese price, a flush market and a shorter haul.
   **F056**
3. Cudd tested with **his own glassware** and kept **his own test book**, in another county, and
   never compared it with anybody's. **F023, D11**
4. **In every overlapping season the same four men's milk tested about 0.19 higher at Alder Corners
   than at Ostrey Hollow** — sixteen seasons, 1915–1930. **F098, F075**
5. States what the comparison disposes of: **the same milk, off the same meadow, in the same
   months, tested differently in two buildings** — which kills the hay explanation and the watering
   explanation together. **F105, F107, I13**
6. **Rosalie Cudd**, Jerome's **daughter**, ran the factory 1941–1962 and **copied sixteen seasons
   of figures out for Ivy Keddie in 1958**. **F017, F023** *(NT-21: niece in r04 and r19)*

### A6 — Strawn's circuit and the condemnation (6 points)

1. **Merle Strawn**, state dairy inspector for the Ordell circuit **1908–1936**, kept a pocket
   notebook a year and was **related to and employed by nobody in the valley**. **F013**
2. On every visit he checked the scales and **ran the lactometer for added water — and never once
   found any at Ostrey Hollow**, across fifteen years including 1919 and after. **F058, F105**
3. On **12 June 1923** he ran the station's pipettes against a **burette** and **condemned the whole
   lot**. **F059, D6** *(NT-11: 12 July in r08 and r12)*
4. He **wrote no figure for the deficiency and made no report beyond the condemnation** — "I did not
   weigh what it has cost them" — and drew no inference about the years behind it. **F108, D6**
5. **Replacement glass ordered 28 June, acknowledged 2 July 1923, in use that July; the station's
   average was 3.80 in 1924 and stayed there — and nobody connected the rise with the condemnation.**
   **F060, F061, F103** *(r08's 3.90 loses the item.)*
6. The crate went to the **station loft**; the notebooks went to the state office and, in **1947**,
   to the college library where Ivy Keddie found the 1923 volume. **F024, F022**

### A7 — the board of arbitration, 1925 (6 points)

1. **The trouble began with the 1924 rise, not with an accusation**: patrons credited 3.61 for
   twenty-five years were credited 3.80, nothing on their farms having changed; they petitioned in
   **January 1925**. **F061, F062**
2. A **board of arbitration under Alonzo Frick** sat **17 March – 4 May 1925** in three sessions,
   **two of them in the station's own weigh room**. **F016, F063, F036**
3. **Three explanations were argued and none was accepted**: **watering** (Selby Vose), **theft of
   cream by the tester** (Wilbur Teague), and **the bog hay** (the 1908 report revived). **F063,
   F105, F106, F107**
4. The exhibits were **four books** — the station test book, the receiving book, Brant's weigh book,
   and **Strawn's 1923 notebook, which contains the answer and was read for its other lines**.
   **F063, D6**
5. **Ansel Keddie dismissed 30 April 1925 without a finding against him**; the award of **4 May
   1925: $3,800, "without admission," charged to the station account**. **F064, F065, D8** *(NT-9:
   $3,500 in r01 and r21)*
6. **The award was one fifth of the $19,000 actually lost, and it was charged to the very account
   into which the loss had been accumulating as "gain" since 1898.** **F082, F104, I10**

### A8 — Ivy Keddie and the nine pipettes, 1958 (7 points)

1. **Ivy Keddie** (b. 1931), **Ansel's granddaughter**, graduate student in dairy husbandry at the
   **State Agricultural College at Farland** under **Leland Vaught**. **F005, F018** *(NT-18:
   great-niece in r19 and r22)*
2. She assembled **records that had never been in the same room**: the station test book, Brant's
   weigh book, the Alder Corners figures, Strawn's notebooks, and the Association's ledgers.
   **F020–F023, I14**
3. **February 1958**: she found the condemned crate in the **station loft**; **nine** pipettes
   survived. **F070, F024, F089** *(NT-16: eleven in r12 and r19)*
4. **9 March 1958**: measured against a calibrated burette, mean delivery **16.72 c.c.** against a
   marked **17.6** — **short 0.88 c.c., which is 0.95 of the mark**. **F033, F034, D13** *(NT-1:
   16.27 in r11 and r19)*
5. Therefore **every test from 1 May 1897 to 12 June 1923 read 95 percent of true: 3.61 recorded
   where 3.80 stood, 0.19 short** — and 0.19 is not a constant but five percent of 3.80. **F073–F076,
   F100**
6. **40,000,000 lb of milk in the 25 full years 1898–1922 gives 76,000 lb of butterfat, about
   $19,000 at 25 cents** — **and the same 76,000 lb is the sum of the "station gain" entries in the
   Association's own ledgers**. **F077, F080, F081, F104, I02** *(All three of the milk, the fat and
   the money must be right; NT-5, NT-6, NT-8 each lose the item.)*
7. **Bulletin No. 471, October 1958**, states the cause and clears him — **35 years after the glass
   was condemned, 33 after the dismissal, 24 after his death**. **F072, F093, D14**

## Section D — Logic (10 points)

### D1 — the 1911 interval as an experiment (4 points, 1 each)

1. Names the **variable**: **the pipette** — the station's own measure was broken on 8 May and a
   borrowed one used until 10 July; **one object in the room changed**. **F052, D4**
2. Names what was **held constant** — at least three of: the same patrons and cows, the same
   pasture and season, the same tester, the same scale, the same acid, the same bottles, the same
   hand tester. **F102**
3. States the **result**: the average ran at **3.80** through the **nine weeks** and returned to
   **3.61** on 10 July, the day the station's own measure came back. **F052** *(NT-12 loses the
   item.)*
4. States what it establishes that a calculation would not — that the cause lies **in the station's
   glassware and not in the milk, the cows, the feed, the men or anyone's honesty**, all three rival
   explanations failing at once — **and** states a limit: it does not give the size of the error in
   cubic centimeters, does not identify **which** piece of glass, and does not tell us whose measure
   was borrowed. Both halves required. **F102, A06**

### D2 — the theft accusation (3 points, 1 each)

Valid reasons (any): the **ledger's annual surplus** — the station's cream arrived at Larrow Green
**heavy**, not light, and a theft can only make it arrive light; **the 1911 interval**, in which the
same man tested and the figures went up; **Strawn's fifteen years of clean visits**, having watched
the man make a test through; **the Alder Corners book**, testing the same milk higher in a building
Keddie never entered; **the 1923 condemnation and the 1924 rise**, which followed a change of glass
and not a change of tester (Keddie stayed until 1925); **Brant's weigh book**, showing no short
weight. **F106**

1. **Three valid, distinct reasons**, each attributed to a narrator or a named document, **drawn
   from at least three different narrators**.
2. **Shows the reasons are mutually independent** — no two resting on the same document, witness or
   event — **and names at least one that would still hold if the glassware had never been measured
   in 1958** (the ledger surplus, the 1911 interval, the Alder Corners book, and the 1924 rise under
   the same tester all qualify).
3. **ABSTENTION ITEM (A09).** States that **whether the bookkeeper ever questioned the entries
   cannot be determined**: he wrote the line every year from 1905 and inherited it from 1898 and
   testified that the cream "always ran a little to the good," but one narrator (himself) says he
   put it to the manager in 1916 and another (the office clerk) says he never asked anybody
   anything, no minute records a question, and there is no paper either way. Asserting = 0 **and
   −2**. **A09**
