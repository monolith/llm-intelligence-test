## Notes for the key-holder

- **r13, X48.** The brief phrases the error as "the shortfall was **later** shown to be 67,000 lb."
  Frick's article is dated 1946 and Bulletin No. 471 is 1958, so a "later shown" framing would have
  required him to know a document that did not yet exist. The wrong value is carried instead as the
  patrons' own reckoning put before the board and never disturbed — same figure, same lack of
  hedging, no anachronism. If the scoring key expects the words "later shown," this is the one
  place the brief could not be followed literally.
- **r14.** X53 needs a change in the station's figures for "nine families all got honest in one
  summer" to have something to attach to, but Strawn's condemnation is on his forbidden list. The
  rise is therefore stated with no cause and no date beyond "one summer, along in the twenties,"
  and Sill supplies the watering explanation himself.
- **Near-tie strings** are carried in the exact forms the briefs give: "3.16" (r13), "sixty-seven
  thousand pounds" (r13), "3.85" (r14), "twenty-seven cents" (r14 and r16), "two tenths" (r15 and
  r16), "0.21"/"twenty-one hundredths" (r16), "six weeks" (r16), "about nine hundred thousand"
  (r14), "1893" (r15).
