# Narrator Briefs, v3 (SECRET — never show to the system under test)

One self-contained brief per retelling. Each is written for an author who will draft that
retelling **without reading any other brief**. Everything the author needs — facts with values,
errors with their exact wrong wording, documents to quote verbatim, and the prohibitions — is
inside the brief.

**Rules for every retelling.**
- Target length **1,000–1,500 words**. Prose, in the narrator's own voice and form, not a fact
  list.
- Quote the documents assigned to you **word for word**. Do not paraphrase them, do not correct
  their spelling or punctuation, do not add or drop a figure.
- State the facts assigned to you with the exact values given. Do not add facts that are not in
  your brief, and never invent a number, a date, a name, or a relationship.
- Reproduce your assigned errors **exactly as worded here**, in the narrator's own idiom, with no
  hedging and no wink to the reader. The narrator believes them.
- Where a brief names a **near-tie partner**, the wrong value must be reproduced *exactly*, not
  paraphrased into a different wrong number. A near-tie only works if both carriers say the same
  thing.
- Never say or imply that any narrator is unreliable, that a fact is corrupted, that another
  retelling exists, or that the reader is being tested.
- Do not mention anything in your "must NOT mention" list, even in passing.
- American spelling throughout. No real people or places.

**Shared world vocabulary** (safe for any narrator to use): Ordell County · Ashlin (the county
seat) · Larrow Green (the village and the main creamery) · Larrow Creek · Ostrey Hollow (the
hamlet and the skimming station, seven miles up the valley) · Hessel Bottom (the wet meadow) ·
Redlow Ridge · Alder Corners, in Vessey County, over the ridge · Farland (the State Agricultural
College, ninety miles east) · Tarnet (the city; Ammon & Sons, Dairy Supply) · the Larrow Valley
Cooperative Creamery Association.

## r01 — Vira Toland, a Hessel Bottom patron's daughter

**Voice.** Sixty-seven, born 1912 at Ostrey Hollow, recorded on tape in 1979 by somebody from the
county. Long spoken paragraphs, doubles back, corrects herself about small things and never about
large ones. Warm, blunt, entirely uninterested in glassware.

**Form.** Transcribed oral history, lightly punctuated.

**Vantage.** She grew up in one of the nine families. She remembers the circular arriving, her
father reading it at the table, and the coldness between the Brants and the Keddies afterward.
She never read a document in her life.

**What she knows (state these as given).**
- The station stood at the top of the Hollow and Ansel Keddie ran it; he kept a beautiful ruled
  book and everybody knew it. (F001)
- In 1919 a letter came from the Association over the manager's signature saying there was water
  in the cans, and nine families got it, and some of them kept it till they died. (F058)
- The tests "went up all at once one summer and nobody ever said why," and after that there was a
  hearing at Ashlin and Keddie was put out. (F061, F063, F064)
- Emmet Brant was the biggest man on the Bottom and Ansel Keddie had married his sister. After
  1919 they did not speak. (F003, F002, I05)
- Keddie died in the thirties and was still under it. (F066)

**Errors (reproduce with these wordings).**
1. **X01 — the lore, flat, never withdrawn.** "He was taking the cream. They caught him at it and
   they put him out, and that is why the numbers went right the summer after." Say it as fact.
   Never hedge it, never attribute it to "people said," never mention any glassware.
2. **X02 — "thirty-five hundred dollars."** The award to the patrons was **$3,500**. (**Near-tie 9**
   — partner r21. Do not soften to "about thirty-five hundred.")
3. **X03 — "he went up there in ninety-five."** Keddie went to the station in **1895**, "the year
   before they opened it, to get it ready, and he tested that first summer too." (**Near-tie 13** —
   partner r17.)
4. **X04 — "his nephew."** Selby Vose was old Gideon Vose's **nephew**. (**Near-tie 17** — partner
   r12. Use the word *nephew*.)
5. **X05 — "first cousin once removed."** The young woman from the college who came round in 1958
   was Hazel Brant's **first cousin once removed**. (**Near-tie 19** — partner r21.)
6. **⌀X06 — abstention pole (A01).** "Orra Keddie and old Mrs. Vose were first cousins. Everybody
   knew that." State it as common knowledge, cite nothing.

**Allowed rhetoric (not scored, not an error).** "He tested a thousand cans a week." She may
exaggerate frequency freely as long as she never states a counted total.

**Must NOT mention.** Any cubic-centimeter figure, any pipette, any burette, the invoice, the
1911 interval, the ledger, Alder Corners, Bulletin No. 214 or No. 471, the state inspector by
name, Article VII, or any test figure (3.61, 3.80, 0.19).

**Documents.** None. She quotes nothing.

## r02 — Effie Loomis, "Notes toward a history of the Association"

**Voice.** Sixty-six, writing in 1941 after forty years as secretary. Orderly, modest, fond of the
exact minute reference, quietly proud of the institution. She writes the way a good secretary
writes: dates, offices, motions carried.

**Form.** A memoir in numbered sections, written for the Association's files.

**Vantage.** She kept the minute book from 1901 to 1940 and recorded the 1925 award herself. Her
authority is the record; she says so, and she says twice what the record does not contain.

**What she knows (state these as given).**
- The Association was organized by valley farmers at Larrow Green in **1889**; **Gideon Vose**
  elected first president and served to **1911**. The main creamery was built in **1891**.
  (F040, F041, F008, F026)
- **Article VII** and what it did. Quote **D1** verbatim (below). Explain in her own words that a
  patron's pay turned on the station's test and not on his scale. (F042)
- Officers: herself secretary 1901–1940; **Wilbur Teague** bookkeeper from 1905; **Hobart Sill**
  buttermaker at Larrow Green; **Selby Vose**, **son of the late president**, manager from **1913**.
  (F010, F011, F012, F009)
- The Ostrey Hollow patrons petitioned in **January 1925**; a board of arbitration under **Alonzo
  Frick** sat from **17 March to 4 May 1925**; she recorded the award. Quote **D8** verbatim.
  (F062, F063, F065)
- Milk received at the station over the affected years: **forty million pounds**, an average of
  **one million six hundred thousand a year**. She has this from the ledgers. (F077)
- The price paid for butterfat over that period averaged **twenty-five cents**. (F081)
- **The 1889–1892 minute book was lost when the office moved in 1938.** She says this plainly and
  more than once. (F068)

**Errors.**
1. **X07 — "seven farms."** **Seven** farms had rights on Hessel Bottom. (Unique; canon is six.)
2. **⌀X08 — abstention pole (A10).** "Article VII was adopted at the annual meeting of **1892**, the
   year after the creamery opened." She gives no citation and cannot, the early minutes being lost —
   but she states the year with confidence.
3. **⌀X09 — abstention pole (A07).** "The crate was never mentioned in the hearing at all. I took
   every word down and it is not there." Say it as settled.

**Must NOT mention.** Any pipette measurement, the 1911 interval, Bulletin No. 214 or No. 471, the
Alder Corners factory, the 1958 study, or any test figure.

**Documents.** **D1** and **D8**, both verbatim.

## r03 — Ansel Keddie, his statement to the board and an unsent letter

**Voice.** Sixty-four in 1925. Plain, exact, unrhetorical, wounded and controlled. He describes
process the way a craftsman does, at length and without ornament, and he says almost nothing about
what he feels except by omission.

**Form.** His nine-sentence statement of 17 March 1925, followed by a longer letter he wrote that
spring and never sent, found among his papers.

**Vantage.** He handled everything he reports. He is the corpus's most valuable single witness and
its most easily discounted one.

**What he knows (state these as given).**
- The bench routine in full: the intake at half past five, the scale, the composite jar made up
  over ten days, the warming and mixing, the charge drawn to the mark and blown into the numbered
  bottle, the acid, the four bottles in the hand tester, the crank at the taught speed, the reading
  off the neck with dividers. (F031)
- The book: date, patron, pounds, test, fat; corrections crossed and initialed, never written over.
  Twenty-nine seasons, 1896–1924. (F001, F094)
- Quote **D3** (the first leaf), **D5** (the summary leaf), **D4** (the two 1911 entries) and **D9**
  (his statement) verbatim.
- **The 1896 season averaged 3.80 on the borrowed glass; 1898 and every year after averaged 3.61 or
  within three hundredths.** (F074, F101)
- **The nine weeks of 1911**: pipette broke **8 May**, borrowed measure until **10 July**, tests at
  **3.80** through the interval and back to 3.61 the same day his own measure came back. He sets a
  question mark against it and explains nothing. (F052, F092)
- The gap was there in July as in February — **flat in every month of the year**. (F097)
- The 1924 average was **3.80**. (F061, F103)
- His wife **Orra** is **Emmet Brant's sister**; he says it without elaboration. (F002, F003)
- The complaints from **1902**; the committee that came up in **1908**; the **1919 circular** and
  what it did to nine families. He never accuses anyone of anything. (F048, F050, F058)
- He was dismissed **30 April 1925**. (F064)

**Errors.**
1. **X10 — internal contradiction.** He writes: "The glass I began on in ninety-six was borrowed off
   the **Ashlin** creamery." Two paragraphs later he transcribes his own first leaf, which reads
   *"Glass borrowed of Larrow Green until our own comes."* Do not flag the discrepancy; let both
   stand.
2. **X11 — the declared null (A05).** He must **never state a belief about the cause.** The only
   things in his hand on the point are the question mark and *"I cannot tell you why the figures are
   what they are."* Do not let him hint.

**Must NOT mention.** Any measurement in cubic centimeters, any burette, the invoice or its total,
the ledger's station-gain line, Alder Corners, the 1958 study, or the number of pipettes.

**Documents.** **D3, D4, D5, D9** — all verbatim.

## r04 — Ivy Keddie, "What the Crate Held," a talk to the Ordell County Historical Society

**Voice.** Twenty-eight in 1959, a year past her degree. Clear, disciplined, warm at the edges and
severe in the middle. She explains arithmetic to a lay audience without condescension and she does
not dramatize her own family.

**Form.** A lecture, with the documents read aloud.

**Vantage.** The corpus's principal explainer. She assembled five record series that had never been
in the same room and she measured the glass herself.

**What she knows (state these as given).**
- She is **Ansel Keddie's granddaughter** — her father **Duncan** was Ansel's son. (F005, F004)
- The records she used and where each came from: the station test book (Association office); the
  Brant weigh book (**lent by Roy Brant**); the Alder Corners figures (copied out by Rosalie Cudd);
  Strawn's circuit notebooks (**in her own college's library since 1947**); the Association's
  ledgers. (F020–F023, I14)
- **February 1958**: the crate in the **station loft** under a tarpaulin, stenciled AMMON & SONS
  DAIRY SUPPLY TARNET; **nine** pipettes unbroken. (F070, F024, F089)
- **9 March 1958**: measured against a calibrated burette. Quote **D13** verbatim. Mean **16.72
  c.c.** against a marked **17.6**; **short 0.88**; **0.95 of the mark**. (F033, F034)
- The chain, worked aloud: 16.72 ÷ 17.6 = **0.95**; 3.61 ÷ 0.95 = **3.80**; the gap **0.19**, which
  is **five percent of 3.80** and therefore a proportion and not a constant; **40,000,000 lb ×
  0.0019 = 76,000 lb**; at **25 cents**, **$19,000**; the 1925 award of **$3,800** is **a fifth** of
  it. (F073–F082)
- The ledger's mirror: fat credited **1,444,000 lb**, fat received **1,520,000 lb**, difference
  **76,000 lb** — **five percent of the fat delivered and five and a quarter percent of the fat
  credited**, and she is careful to say which is which. (F078, F079, F083)
- Because the error is proportional, **the richest milk lost most**: a recorded 4.56 was short 0.24
  where a recorded 3.42 was short 0.18. (F087)
- The **1911 interval** as an experiment: one object changed, everything else held. (F102)
- **Bulletin No. 214** — quote **D12** verbatim — five hundredths in winter and nothing in summer,
  against a gap of nineteen hundredths flat in every month. (F095, F107)
- The invoice total **$77.93** and **six dozen** pipettes bought. (F088, F089)
- Quote **D5** and **D14** verbatim. **35 / 33 / 24 years.** (F072, F093)

**Errors.**
1. **X12 — "fourteen seasons."** The Alder Corners overlap ran **fourteen seasons**. (Unique; canon
   is sixteen, 1915–1930.)
2. **X13 — "his niece."** Rosalie Cudd was Jerome Cudd's **niece**. (**Near-tie 21** — partner r19.)
3. **X101 — "from 1908."** Strawn's notebooks are "kept by the state's dairy inspector for this
   county **from 1908**." (**Near-tie 23** — partner r22; canon is **1907**, from Strawn's own
   covering note.) Catalogued 2026-08-28 by validation ruling 2; the retelling already reads this
   way and needs no edit.
4. **⌀X14 — abstention pole (A06).** "The measure they borrowed in 1911 came up from Larrow Green
   with the cream wagon." State it flatly, cite nothing.

**Must NOT mention.** Anything about what her grandfather privately believed; whether the 1925
board saw the crate; whether the manager knew; the year Article VII was adopted; whether Emmet and
Orra were full or half siblings; the 1896 intake figure.

**Documents.** **D5, D12, D13, D14** — all verbatim.

## r05 — Wilbur Teague, bookkeeper (ret.), memoir

**Voice.** Sixty-eight in 1948, writing after forty years at the books. Precise in manner,
self-satisfied, fond of the phrase "in my experience." He believes he is the most reliable witness
in the valley and writes accordingly.

**Form.** A chapter of a longer unpublished memoir, "The Books of the Association."

**Vantage.** He wrote the station-gain entries and he gave evidence at the 1925 hearing. He is the
only narrator who transcribes a ledger entry.

**What he knows (state these as given).**
- The station account and how it worked: fat credited to patrons on the station's test against fat
  received at Larrow Green in the station's cream, the difference carried to the general account.
  Quote **D7** verbatim — **and the transcription must carry its date, 1898.** (F104)
- The Association's officers and his own term, 1905–1945. (F011)
- He gave evidence to the board of arbitration in 1925. (F063)
- The award was **$3,800**. (F065)

**Errors.**
1. **X15 — "three and sixteen."** The station's long-run average test was **3.16**. (**Near-tie 2** —
   partner r13.)
2. **X16 — "forty-four million."** The station took **44,000,000 lb** over the period. (**Near-tie
   5** — partner r12.)
3. **X17 — "six weeks."** The 1911 borrowed measure was in use **six weeks**. (**Near-tie 12** —
   partner r16.)
4. **X18 — the wrong denominator.** "The gain ran at five and a quarter percent **of the fat that
   was actually in the cream**." (**Near-tie 20** — partner r11.)
5. **X19 — internal contradiction.** "The station-gain line was my own device; I began it when I came
   in **1905**." He then transcribes **D7**, whose entry is dated **1898**. Let both stand.
6. **X20 — DECOY THEORY 2, the theft.** "Cream is portable, and that station was in one pair of
   hands for twenty-nine seasons. I said so to the board and I say it here." Offered as sober
   professional judgment, never as gossip, never hedged.
7. **⌀X21 — abstention pole (A03).** "Mr. Vose knew what that account was doing. He signed the sheet
   every year."
8. **⌀X22 — abstention pole (A09).** "I put it to Mr. Vose in 1916 and was told to let it alone."

**Must NOT mention.** Any pipette, any cubic-centimeter figure, the 1958 study, Bulletin No. 471,
Alder Corners, the lactometer, or Strawn's condemnation.

**Documents.** **D7** verbatim, dated 1898 in the transcription.

## r06 — Merle Strawn, "Extracts from twenty-eight circuit books," with a covering note

**Voice.** Sixty-seven in 1936, just retired. Terse, technical, faintly dry, wholly without
theories. He writes about plants the way a good mechanic writes about engines.

**Form.** A covering note of a page, then dated extracts from the notebooks, arranged by year.

**Vantage.** The corpus's disinterested witness: kin to nobody, patron of nothing, employed by the
state. He went to Ostrey Hollow between one and three times a year across the whole circuit,
1907 to 1936.

**What he knows (state these as given).**
- The circuit: fifty-odd plants in four counties; scales against sealed weights, the lactometer for
  added water, the boiler and cold room, and watching the tester make a test through. (F013)
- **He never once found added water at Ostrey Hollow** — extracts for 1912, 1916, 1919, 1920 and
  1922 should each carry the line, so the run is visible. (F105, F058)
- The state's **burette program: burettes issued to field men in 1921, discontinued in 1926.** He
  had carried one **two seasons** before the check. (F098b)
- **12 June 1923**: quote **D6** verbatim.
- Extracts for 1924 and 1925 reading "scales true, no added water, tests improved." The 1924
  extract adds one flat line copied off the tester's book — **the year at 3.80** — and nothing
  else. He draws no inference from either. (F103, F061)
- His notebooks went to the state office on his retirement. (F022)

**Errors. None** — r06 is the corpus's one error-free narrator.
1. **~~X23~~ — WITHDRAWN 2026-08-28 (validation ruling 2).** His covering note's "I took the Ordell
   circuit in nineteen hundred and seven" is **canon**: F013 and F051 now read **1907–1936**, and
   the **1908** of r04 and r22 is the planted error (X101, X102, **NT-23**). The retelling's text is
   unchanged; only its keying is. His closing note's "twenty-eight of them, one to the year" fits
   neither 1907–1936 (thirty) nor 1908–1936 (twenty-nine), so it breaks nothing — it is a genuine
   internal inconsistency on the page and is creditable at **E-d**.

*Everything else from r06 is canon-correct.* He must not speculate about causes anywhere.

**Must NOT mention.** The station's long-run average (3.61), the ledger, the arbitration, the
invoice, the Keddie or Brant families by relationship, Alder Corners, or the 1958 study. **One
exception**, and only in the 1924 extract: the **3.80** he copies off the tester's book, which
makes him the corpus's second correct carrier of F061. He gives it as a figure he read, with no
comment.

**Documents.** **D6** verbatim.

## r08 — Selby Vose, manager's annual address to the Association, 1926

grave, and defensive under a layer of confidence. He speaks of "this Association" and "our

  (F009, F054)

  generous settlement of an unproved claim. (F062, F063, F065)
- **The Vose home farm's Jersey herd tested 4.56 in 1910** — he offers this in passing, as evidence
  that the valley's milk is good, and has no idea what it means. (F087)

**Errors.**

2. **X28 — "the twelfth of July."** The state man condemned the glassware on the **twelfth of July,
   1923**. (**Near-tie 11** — partner r12.)
3. **X29 — "three ninety."** The 1924 average was **3.90**. (Unique.)
4. **X30 — DECOY THEORY 1, watering.** "Water was going into cans at the head of that valley and it

**Must NOT mention.** The words glass, pipette, measure or burette — **anywhere, in any

## r09 — Roy Brant, "My father's book"

**Voice.** Sixty-seven in 1955, still farming. Dry, factual, slow-moving, proud of the book and of
his father's habit of writing everything down. He is not bitter; he is precise, which is worse.

**Form.** A written account prepared for the county farm bureau's local-history collection.

**Vantage.** Story 4 entire, and the family lines. He handled the weigh book from 1929 and read it
all his life.

**What he knows (state these as given).**
- **Hessel Bottom**: a hundred and sixty acres of wet meadow; **six farms** had rights on it; his
  father's was the largest, sixty-odd acres and thirty-eight cows in a good year. (F028)
- His father **Emmet Brant** (1854–1929) kept the weigh book: every can, every day, by his own scale
  in his own barn, with the station's figure beside it — **and the two scales agreed**, for
  decades. Quote **D10** verbatim. (F003, F105)
- The 1910 leaf: **200,000 lb even, paid on a test of 3.42**. (F084)
- The price for butterfat **in 1910 was 24 cents**; over the whole period it averaged **25 cents**.
  (F086, F081)
- The gap between the Hollow and Larrow Green was **seventeen hundredths of a point** — 3.78
  against 3.61, the gap a man could see on a pay slip — and it was there in July as in February.
  (F098a) **Not nineteen hundredths.** 0.19 is the shortfall against what the milk actually held
  (F076), which nobody could know before 1958; it is carried by r04 and r22.
- Complaints from **1902**; the **Grigg committee of 1908** and what it reported; the **1919
  circular** and how nine families took it. (F048, F050, F058)
- From **1915**, **four** of the Bottom men hauled over Redlow Ridge to the cheese factory in the
  summer flush and to Ostrey Hollow the rest of the year, for **sixteen seasons**. The factory
  opened in **1914**. (F055, F056, F098)
- The family: **Orra Keddie was his father's sister**; his own father and Ansel Keddie were
  brothers-in-law and did not speak after 1919; **he and Duncan Keddie were first cousins**, and
  **Hazel and Ivy are second cousins**. (F002, F003, F007)
- **He lent the book to Ivy Keddie in 1958**; Hazel had it after him. (F021)

**Errors.**
1. **X31 — "the first of January, 1898."** His father began the weigh book on **1 January 1898**.
   (Unique; canon is 1897.)
2. **⌀X32 — abstention pole (A06).** "The state man left his own measure that June and that is what
   they used." State it flatly.
3. **⌀X33 — abstention pole (A08).** "They were full brother and sister, same mother and same
   father." State it flatly.

**Must NOT mention.** Any cubic-centimeter figure, the invoice, the 1958 measurement, the ledger's
station-gain line, Bulletin No. 471, the total pounds of milk at the station, or the $19,000.

**Documents.** **D10** verbatim.

## r10 — Rosalie Cudd, letter to Ivy Keddie

**Voice.** Fifty-seven in 1958, a working cheesemaker writing a long friendly letter to a young
woman who asked a sensible question. Practical, unliterary, affectionate about her father, a little
amused by Ordell County.

**Form.** A letter, with a table of figures copied out at the end.

**Vantage.** Story 5 entire. She has run the factory since 1941 and grew up in it.

**What she knows (state these as given).**
- The **Alder Corners Cheese Company**, over **Redlow Ridge** in **Vessey County**, built by her
  father **Jerome Cudd** — *"my father"* throughout, never a looser word. (F017, F029)
- The road over the ridge: four miles of switchback, shut November to April. (F029)
- From **1915**, **four** Hessel Bottom men hauled over in the **summer flush** — June to
  mid-September — for a cheese price, a flush market and a shorter haul in good weather; the rest of
  the year they hauled to Ostrey Hollow. **Sixteen seasons, 1915 through 1930.** (F056, F098)
- Her father tested with **his own glassware** on **his own bench** and kept his own book, and
  compared it with nobody's, having no reason to. (F023)
- Quote **D11** verbatim. Note that her father thought the Ordell talk about the Bottom was
  nonsense, and said so in the book.
- Across every overlapping season the four men tested **about 3.80** with her — she gives season
  figures in the high 3.7s and low 3.8s. (F075, F098)
