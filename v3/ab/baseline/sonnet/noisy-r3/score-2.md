## What went wrong

**Abstentions.** This is where the run lost most of its points. Five of the ten
abstention items were answered with a definite verdict: Orra as Emmet's *full* sister
(A08), the Vose–Keddie rumour declared *unfounded* on a family member's denial (A01),
the manager who *never connected* the low tests to his own signed ledger sheets (A03),
the bookkeeper who *never* questioned the station-gain entries (A09), and a flat "**No.**"
on whether the 1925 board examined the glassware (A07). That is −10 plus the four items
themselves. The three it did decline — the 1896 intake, Article VII's adoption year, and
what the tester believed — were declined cleanly and scored.

**Section A coverage.** The arithmetic spine is intact but three of the eight histories
are thin. A6 (Strawn) is almost absent: no Ordell circuit, no 1907–1936 span, no clean
lactometer record, no replacement glass in 1923 and no 1924 average of 3.80. A5 (Alder
Corners) never names Redlow Ridge, Vessey County or Jerome Cudd, and never states the
independence of the bench in the terms the key requires. A1 loses Selby Vose's paternity
and start year, the officers' tenures, and — costliest — the station-gain ledger line,
which the run does discuss, but only in Sections D and F, where it earns nothing for A.
Same problem at A8.6: the 40,000,000 lb figure sits in Section C and the identity of the
76,000 lb with the ledger total is never made at all.

visits dated "from 1908 forward". Everything else on the flagged list — 16.27, $77.39,
five dozen, eleven pipettes, 12 July, six weeks, $3,500, great-niece, first cousins once
removed, seven farms, 1898/1900 for the weigh book — was found and rejected, and 5.26
percent was correctly attached to the credited fat rather than the true fat.

resolvable conflicts with methods, and several self-refutations named. All three decoys
are named and defeated with specific evidence.
