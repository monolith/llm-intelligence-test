# Exhaustive notes — retellings r01–r08 (Ostrey Hollow / Larrow Valley milk-testing saga)

All eight retellings concern the same core historical episode: the Ostrey Hollow skimming station (part of the Larrow Valley Cooperative Creamery Association, Larrow Green, Ashlin, Ordell County) and the decades-long dispute over its low butterfat test readings, tester Ansel Keddie's dismissal, and the eventual 1958 forensic discovery that the station's test pipettes were mechanically short, exonerating Keddie. Sources conflict on dates, dollar amounts, percentages, and even genealogy — all conflicts are flagged below.

## Cast of people (with relationships)

- **Ansel Keddie** — tester at Ostrey Hollow skimming station from its opening (1896) until dismissed 30 April 1925. Kept meticulous test book in five columns (date, patron, pounds, test, fat) from 4 May 1896. Married to **Orra Keddie** (née Brant). Never remarried info given. Died 1934 (per r04) / "died in the thirties" (per r01, consistent). Grandfather of **Ivy Keddie** (r04's narrator) through his son **Duncan Keddie**, who taught school at Ashlin.
- **Orra Keddie** (née Brant) — Ansel Keddie's wife; sister of Emmet Brant. Per r01, also first cousin of "old Mrs. Vose" (Gideon Vose's wife), which is why the 1919 accusatory letter felt personal/political to the Hollow families. After the 1919 circular, she and her brother Emmet never spoke again; she'd walk to Larrow Green rather than pass his lane. Outlived Ansel by some years, per r01.
- **Emmet Brant** — biggest patron on Hessel Bottom (most cows, most hay ground, most milk hauled); Orra's brother; station's largest patron every year on record (r02). Stopped speaking to Ansel Keddie after the 1919 circular (r01, r03).
- **Roy Brant** — Emmet Brant's son; inherited/kept the Brant weigh book from 1929; lent it to Ivy Keddie in 1958 for her research (r04, r07).
- **Hazel Brant** — mentioned in r01 as a relative of a 1958 researcher (the young woman from the college was "Hazel Brant's first cousin once removed"); also received the farm weigh book (Item 4) in 1962 before it went to the Historical Rooms in 1963 (r07). Possibly the same "young woman" is Ivy Keddie herself — r01's timeline (young woman visited 1958, of "the family both sides") lines up with Ivy Keddie's 1958 research trip described in r04, but r01 doesn't name her as Keddie; this may be Ivy Keddie or a different, unnamed researcher — ambiguous/possible identity overlap not confirmed by any source.
- **Gideon Vose** — organized/founded the Larrow Valley Cooperative Creamery Association in 1889 at Larrow Green; first president, served 1889–1911 (22 years, longest-serving); personally drafted the by-laws including Article VII, adopted 1892; died 1917 (r02). Kept a herd of Jerseys at the "home place" that later passed to his son/nephew Selby (r08).
- **Selby Vose** — manager of the Association from 1913–1938 (r02); signed the accusatory 1919 circular to the nine Ostrey Hollow families (r01, r08); gave the 1926 annual address (r08) defending the Association and asserting his belief that patrons had been watering milk. **CONFLICT:** r01 states Selby Vose was "old Gideon Vose's nephew"; r02 states Selby was "son of the late president" Gideon Vose; r08 (Selby's own words) says "My father, who was your first president... kept a herd of Jerseys." So r02 and r08 agree Selby was Gideon's son, while r01 says nephew — a direct contradiction.
- **"Old Mrs. Vose"** — Gideon Vose's wife; first cousin of Orra Keddie (per r01), which locals took as significant given the accusatory letter.
- **Effie Loomis** — secretary of the Association 1901–1940; wrote "Notes toward a history of the Association" in 1941 (r02); kept the minute book in her own hand; recorded the 1925 arbitration award personally.
- **Wilbur Teague** — bookkeeper of the Association 1905–1945; came in as bookkeeper in 1905 (r02) confirmed; wrote memoir "The Books of the Association" (chapter as r05) in winter 1948, three years after retiring (1945). Invented the "station-gain" ledger line in 1905. Raised suspicion to Selby Vose in 1916 about the account; was told to let it alone and did. Testified before the 1925 board that the station's one-man arrangement invited embezzlement, without accusing anyone specifically.
- **Hobart Sill** — buttermaker at Larrow Green; kept the churn book and cream receiving book (r02).
- **Alonzo Frick** — attorney of Ashlin; chaired the board of arbitration that sat 17 March – 4 May 1925 (r02, r03, r05, r08). Called "as able and impartial man" (Selby Vose, r08) and "as fair a chairman as ever a hearing had" (Teague, r05).
- **Vira Toland** — narrator of r01; born 1912 at the top of Ostrey Hollow; a Hessel Bottom patron's daughter; interviewed/taped in 1979 at Ostrey Hollow (Hazel/family connection noted above). Lived her whole life in the Hollow except two years at Ashlin after first marriage.
- **Ivy Keddie** — Ansel Keddie's granddaughter (through son Duncan Keddie); gave a 1959 talk "What the Crate Held" to the Ordell County Historical Society at Ashlin (r04); affiliated with the State Agricultural College at Farland; discovered the crate of condemned pipettes in the station loft in February 1958; measured them against a calibrated burette on 9 March 1958; her findings became State Agricultural College Bulletin No. 471 (October 1958), authored with "Professor Vaught."
- **Rosalie Cudd** — runs the Alder Corners Cheese Company (Vessey County, over Redlow Ridge); niece of **Jerome Cudd** (the factory's founder); copied out Alder Corners' historical test figures for Ivy Keddie in 1958; the original book never left Vessey County.
- **Merle Strawn** — state dairy inspector for the Ordell circuit, 1907–1936 (retired that year); wrote "Extracts from twenty-eight circuit books" in 1936 (r06); kept one oilcloth pocket notebook per year (28 total), later held at the state dairy office, then to the library of Farland college (Ivy Keddie's college) in 1947.
- **Professor Vaught** — co-author (with Ivy Keddie) of the concluding paragraph of Bulletin No. 471 (October 1958), which exonerated Keddie.
- **Halbert Nym** — curator, wrote 1972 accession notes for the Ordell County dairy collection at the Ordell County Historical Rooms (r07); compiled Items 1–16 with provenance.
- **Duncan Keddie** — Ansel Keddie's son; taught school at Ashlin; father of Ivy Keddie.

## Timeline of key dates (as variously given; conflicts noted)

- **1889** — Larrow Valley farmers meet at Larrow Green; organize the Larrow Valley Cooperative Creamery Association; Gideon Vose elected first president (served until 1911, 22 years) (r02).
- **1889–1892** — Minute book from this period was later lost when the office moved in 1938 (r02); "governs everything" Effie Loomis can write about the earliest years.
- **1891** — Main creamery built at Larrow Green, on Larrow Creek; same building still standing as of 1941 (r02). Before this, no common test/price existed; each farmer's own arithmetic ruled.
- **1892** — Article VII adopted at the annual meeting (quoted in full below); written by Gideon Vose, who died in 1917 (r02).
- **1896, 4 May** — Ostrey Hollow skimming station opens; Ansel Keddie begins keeping the test book that day, first leaf: "Ostrey Hollow station. Begun this 4th day of May, 1896. Glass borrowed of Larrow Green until our own comes. A. Keddie." (quoted in r03 and r07, Item 3). Testing glass initially borrowed from the Ashlin creamery per r03 ("Ashlin creamery" — note: r03 says borrowed from Ashlin creamery, while the book's own first leaf and r07's Item 3/Item 2 say borrowed from Larrow Green — an internal inconsistency about which creamery lent the original glass).
- **1896** average test recorded: 3.80 (the station's first, partial season).
- **1897** — "part year" in the summary averages. New equipment (own outfit) arrives "the spring following" 1896, i.e., 1897, in a crate from Ammon & Sons Dairy Supply, Tarnet — invoice dated 14 April 1897, total $77.93 (6 dozen pipettes at 17.6 c.c. blown mark, .35 each = $25.20; 12 dozen test bottles, 8% neck, .22 each = $31.68; 1 acid measure = $0.90; 1 hand tester, 4-bottle = $18.00; freight $2.15) — quoted exactly in r07, Item 2; corroborated in r04 ("an invoice that came to seventy-seven dollars and ninety-three cents for the whole outfit"). In use at the station from 1 May 1897 (r07).
- **1897** — Ansel Keddie's separate farm weigh book (Item 4 in r07) begun 1 January 1897 at Hessel Bottom, kept by one household throughout (attribution confusingly swapped in r07 — see "Oddities" below).
- **1898** — recorded average drops to 3.61 and (per the station's own summary leaf, quoted identically in r03 and r07 Item 3) "3.61 or within three hundredths of it every year since, save the nine weeks of 1911... when it stood at 3.80."
- **1898–1922** (25 full years) — the station took in, per Loomis (r02) and confirmed by the curator (r07, Item 6 curatorial note): 40,000,000 lb of milk total, averaging 1,600,000 lb/year; butterfat price averaged $0.25/lb over the period. **CONFLICT:** Wilbur Teague's own 1948 memoir (r05) instead states the station "over the years the account ran" took in 44,000,000 lb total and gives the average test as 3.16 (not matching the 3.61/3.80 figures used everywhere else) — a clear discrepancy between Teague's memoir and the ledgers/other sources' figures.
- **1902** — complaints first arise at Larrow Green that Ostrey Hollow milk doesn't test with the rest (r03).
- **1905** — Wilbur Teague becomes bookkeeper; invents the "station-gain" ledger line that year (r02, r05).
- **1907** — Merle Strawn takes over the Ordell dairy inspection circuit (fifty-odd plants across four counties). His first visit that year to Ostrey Hollow: "Scales true against my weights. Cold room fair... watched him through two tests. He works slow and clean."
- **1908** — an investigating committee samples Hessel Bottom herds through January–March 1908 and reports (quoted in full in r07, Item 11): "We have sampled the herds of Hessel Bottom through January, February and March. The milk is whole and the scales are honest. It is our opinion that the wet hay of that bottom is what thins the cream, and that the remedy lies with the land and not with the station." (r03 also references this 1908 committee, consistent).
- **1909** — Strawn visits in the fall: scales true, boiler in order, one man cautioned about a rusted seam.
- **1910** — Selby Vose's own Jersey herd (kept at "the home place," inherited from father Gideon) tested 4.56 that year (r08) — this exact figure (4.56) is cited in r04 as an example of a rich-milk herd shorted by 0.24 points due to the pipette error, implying Vose's own family herd was among those undercounted (not explicitly stated as the same herd in r04, but the number matches exactly). Also, per r07 Item 4, the Brant/Keddie farm weigh book's leaf "for 1910" reads: "Year 1910. Delivered to the station, two hundred thousand pounds even, by my own scale and theirs agreeing. Paid on a test of 3.42." (This matches r04's example of a herd recorded at 3.42, shorted 0.18 points.)
- **1911** — the central anomaly. The station's own test measure (pipette) broke on 8 May 1911; a borrowed measure (from Larrow Green, arriving with the cream wagon per r04) was used until the station's own was restored on 10 July 1911. During those nine weeks the recorded average jumped to 3.80 (matching the true, corrected value later established in 1958) and fell back to 3.61 the day the original (short) measure returned. Keddie's own two book entries, quoted in r03: "May 8. Pipette broke this morning. Using a borrowed measure." and "July 10. Our own measure in use again from today. Tests are back where they were. ? — A.K." Keddie left the question mark unanswered for 14 years (as of his 1925 letter). Strawn's inspection that June 1911 found "Scales true. Watched a test through. Nothing here to note" — he did not catch the anomaly (no burette existed yet to test pipette accuracy; those weren't issued to field men until 1921). **CONFLICT/corroboration:** Teague's memoir (r05) independently confirms a measure break "in the spring of 1911," lasting "six weeks" with a borrowed one before their own came back — Teague says six weeks, while Keddie's own dated entries (8 May–10 July) span about nine weeks (matching r03's and r04's explicit "nine weeks" language) — a minor discrepancy in duration between Teague's account and the primary log.
- **1913** — Selby Vose becomes manager of the Association (serves until 1938), succeeding an unnamed predecessor; Strawn's circuit notes for 1913 mention "New manager at the Association this year and he came out with me to the platform, which the last one never did" — corroborating a change in 1913.
- **1915** — Strawn orders a wash-room floor at Ostrey Hollow graded (holding water at the drain).
- **1916** — Strawn's visit notes the tester (Keddie) complaining "his patrons blame him for their figures... he knew it." Separately, this is the year Wilbur Teague says he first raised his suspicions about the station account to Selby Vose and was told to let it alone (r05).
- **1917** — a "bad year for help"; Strawn found two testers doing the work of three men elsewhere in the circuit. Also the year Gideon Vose died (r02).
- **1919** — the accusatory circular/letter. Signed by manager Selby Vose, sent to nine Ostrey Hollow patron families, stating there was "water in the cans" (r01 quotes this framing; r08 confirms and defends authorship: "I defend the circular I sent to the patrons of that station in 1919... It warned the patrons... that added water would be looked for and would be dealt with. It named nobody. It accused nobody."). Vira Toland (r01) recalls her father reading it at the table with his hat on, raining that day, putting it in a drawer where it stayed until his death (later kept by her brother); one woman kept hers pinned inside her trunk lid for decades. Strawn's 1919 circuit entry (r06) states flatly: "The manager of the Association has sent a circular round to the patrons of this station about water in the cans. I have tested for water at this station on every visit I have made to it and I have never found any, and I told him so." — a direct contemporaneous rebuttal to the watering accusation.
- **1920** — Strawn visits twice (spring and fall); scales true both times, no added water; cold room short of ice in fall, remedied before he left the county.
- **1921** — the state begins issuing calibrated burettes (graduated, wooden case, printed instructions) to field inspectors for checking plant glassware — this is the tool that will eventually reveal the pipette flaw.
- **1922** — Strawn does NOT run the Ostrey Hollow glass against the burette this year ("the day being short and the road home being what it was") — explicitly noted by him as a missed opportunity. Also 1922 is the year Ansel Keddie made up the summary leaf at the back of his test book giving station averages (quoted identically in r03 and r07, Item 3): "Average test of all patrons, this station: 1896, 3.80. 1897, part year. 1898, 3.61. And 3.61 or within three hundredths of it every year since, save the nine weeks of 1911 that are noted in their place, when it stood at 3.80."
- **1923, 12 June** — Strawn runs the Ostrey Hollow pipettes against the burette for the first time (having carried it two seasons already) and condemns the entire crate: "Ostrey Hollow. Scales true. Lactometer, no added water, as always here. Ran their milk pipettes against the burette. These measures will not deliver their mark. Condemned the lot, the whole crate of them, and told the manager to order new. Glass is glass and I did not weigh what it has cost them." (quoted in full in both r06 and r07, Item 9). **CONFLICT:** Selby Vose's 1926 address (r08) instead gives the condemnation date as "the twelfth of July, 1923" — a one-month discrepancy from the inspector's own dated entry and the curator's Item 9 photostat, both of which say 12 June.
- **1923, 28 June** — per r07 Item 10, the Association's order for replacement pipettes/bottles to Ammon & Sons is dated this day.
- **1923, 2 July** — Ammon & Sons write back acknowledging the 28 June order, promising shipment that month, declining to comment on the earlier lot (r07, Item 10, not transcribed in full).
- **1923, "in June"** — the condemned crate of pipettes is carried up to the loft over the intake room at the station and left there under a tarpaulin (r04 says "carried up there in June of 1923").
- **1923, July** — new glass in use at the station since this month (r06's October 1923 entry: "New glass in use since July").
- **1923, October** — Strawn runs the new pipettes against the burette; they deliver their mark correctly; scales true (r06).
- **1924** — tests improve. Per Keddie's own book/summary and Strawn's notes, the 1924 average is given as **3.80** (r03's letter: "In 1924 the average of this station was 3.80"; r06: "Their book gives the year at 3.80"). **CONFLICT:** Selby Vose's 1926 address instead states "the average for 1924 stood at 3.90" and describes the rise as "twenty-one hundredths" (0.21) rather than the 0.19 gap (3.61 to 3.80) established elsewhere — Vose's figures for both the new average and the size of the jump disagree with Keddie's own book and the inspector's corroboration.
- **1925, January** — the Ostrey Hollow patrons petition the Association, alleging their milk had never tested as their neighbors' did and asking to be made whole (r02, r08).
- **1925, 17 March – 4 May** — the board of arbitration sits at Ashlin under chairman Alonzo Frick, hearing patrons and officers (r02, r03, r05, r07, r08 all agree on these dates). Ansel Keddie gives his nine-sentence statement to the board on 17 March 1925 (quoted in full in r03; also referenced as "Item 8" in r07, "not transcribed").
- **1925, 30 April** — Ansel Keddie is dismissed/"put out" (r03: "I was put out on the thirtieth of April.").
- **1925, 4 May** — the board's award is recorded (r02, r07, Item 7 — quoted in full): "The board having heard the patrons and the officers, and having had before it the books and appliances of the station, finds no cause established. The board awards to the Ostrey Hollow patrons the sum of thirty-eight hundred dollars ($3,800.00), without admission, to be charged to the station account." Effie Loomis recorded this personally and noted the tension between "finds no cause established" and awarding money anyway. **MAJOR CONFLICT:** Vira Toland's oral account (r01) instead states the settlement was **$3,500**, split nine ways, calling it barely enough for "a hay rake," and frames it as a direct consequence of Keddie being caught stealing cream — a narrative and figure at odds with r02/r05/r07/r08's consistent $3,800 "no cause established" account. r01 also implies the hearing followed soon after the 1919 letter ("the hearing down at Ashlin... that was the winter and spring after [1919]"), whereas all other sources place the petition/hearing in 1925 — a six-year discrepancy in when Vira Toland believes the hearing occurred.
- **1925, fall** — Strawn's circuit note: "New man at the tester's bench this year. He is quick, and quick is not the thing I look for." — Keddie's replacement.
- **1926** — Selby Vose delivers his annual address (r08), marking his 13th year as manager (since 1913), publicly blaming patron water-adding (implicitly, without naming anyone) for the historic low tests, defending the 1919 circular and the $3,800 payment, and dismissing the pipette-wear explanation as mere "housekeeping."
- **1928** — Strawn visits; station "quieter than it was"; loft over intake room already full of odds and ends (where the condemned crate sits, though not identified as such yet); told it's "been full since before the war."
- **1929** — Roy Brant (Emmet's son) receives/keeps the farm weigh book after his father's death (r04, r07).
- **1931** — bad ice year across all four counties on Strawn's circuit.
- **1934** — Ansel Keddie dies (r04). Strawn's visit that year notes a separator bearing ran hot, shut down two days; "The tester's book is kept up, though not in the hand it used to be kept in" (i.e., no longer Keddie's own handwriting).
- **1936, March** — Strawn's last round before retiring; "Nothing to note anywhere and nothing left to say about it." He lays down the Ordell circuit this year and writes his "Extracts from twenty-eight circuit books" covering 1907–1936 (28 pocketbooks, one per year, oilcloth covers).
- **1936** — the state's burette-checking program is discontinued (per Strawn's 1926 entry note — wait, that note is dated under "1926" in the text: "Word from the state office that the burette program is discontinued from this year." (the entry is filed under the year "1926" heading in r06, i.e., program discontinued starting 1926, not 1936 — correcting: re-reading r06, the entry labeled "1926" in the text is actually about the burette program discontinuation, and it says instruments are to be kept by inspectors but no more issued/returned). Strawn notes his own burette "has been used twice since" as of his 1936 writing.
- **1938** — the Association's office is moved; the 1889–1892 minute book is lost in the move (r02). Selby Vose's tenure as manager ends this year (r02).
- **1940** — Effie Loomis lays down the minute book after 39 years as secretary (1901–1940).
- **1941** — Effie Loomis writes "Notes toward a history of the Association" (r02), one year after retiring. Also the year the Alder Corners test book passes from its founder to his daughter (r07, Item 13).
- **1945** — Wilbur Teague retires as bookkeeper after 40 years (1905–1945).
- **1947** — Merle Strawn's 28 circuit notebooks (having gone to the state dairy office on his 1936 retirement) are transferred to the library of the State Agricultural College at Farland — Ivy Keddie's college — where they sit unexamined for 11 years.
- **1948, winter** — Wilbur Teague writes his memoir chapter "The Books of the Association" (r05) at Ashlin, three years after retiring.
- **1958, February** — Ivy Keddie goes up the Hollow to the shuttered station building (used for storage — fence wire and salt, per r01; "odds and ends," per r06's 1928 note) and finds the crate of condemned pipettes under a tarpaulin in the loft over the old intake room, stenciled "AMMON & SONS DAIRY SUPPLY TARNET." Nine of the original pipettes (of six dozen originally purchased) survive unbroken (two others noted elsewhere as chipped at the tip, per r07 Item 5, which says nine survive with two chipped).
- **1958, 9 March** — Ivy Keddie measures the nine pipettes at Farland against a calibrated burette, five runs each. Her sheet (quoted in full, r04 and r07 Item 14): "Nine pipettes from the Ostrey Hollow crate, stems blown 17.6 c.c. Delivered against burette, mean of five runs each: 16.70, 16.71, 16.72, 16.72, 16.72, 16.73, 16.73, 16.74, 16.71. Mean 16.72 c.c. Short 0.88 c.c. That is 95 hundredths of the mark, and the mark is what the neck of the bottle was ruled for." This same year (1958) she also finds the 1923 volume of Strawn's circuit notebooks at the Farland library and copies it; obtains the Alder Corners figures from Rosalie Cudd; borrows the Brant weigh book from Roy Brant (returned same year).
- **1958, also** — a "young woman" from "the college away east" visits Vira Toland (r01) asking about the affair — described as "Hazel Brant's first cousin once removed" — likely the same research trip as Ivy Keddie's, though r01 does not name her as Keddie and describes her college as "away east" rather than Farland (potential inconsistency in geography/identity, unresolved).
- **1958, October** — State Agricultural College Bulletin No. 471 is published (Ivy Keddie and Professor Vaught), concluding (quoted in full in r04 and r07 Item 15): "No milk was watered at Ostrey Hollow and no cream was stolen there. From the first of May, 1897, to the twelfth of June, 1923, the station's tests were low by one part in twenty because its pipettes were short by one part in twenty, and the difference — seventy-six thousand pounds of butter fat — stands credited in the Association's own ledgers, year by year, as station gain."
- **1959** — Ivy Keddie delivers her talk "What the Crate Held" to the Ordell County Historical Society at Ashlin (r04, the retelling itself), with the crate on a table and the borrowed books laid open beside it. The station test book (kept by the Association's office at Ashlin since 1925) comes to the Historical Rooms this year too (r07, Item 3 provenance); the crate itself is given to the Rooms this year as well (r07, Item 5).
- **1962** — the farm weigh book passes to Hazel Brant (r07, Item 4 provenance).
- **1963** — the farm weigh book is received by the Historical Rooms (r07, Item 4).
- **1970** — the Alder Corners test book passes to the Vessey County Historical Society (r07, Item 13).
- **1972** — Halbert Nym writes the accession notes/catalog (r07) for items 1–16 of the Ordell County dairy collection.
- **1979** — Vira Toland's oral history (r01) is taped at her kitchen table at Ostrey Hollow.

## Key documents quoted in full or substantial part

1. **Article VII of the Association by-laws** (adopted 1892, drafted by Gideon Vose), quoted identically in r02 and r07 (Item 1):
   > "Every patron shall be paid for the milk he delivers according to the number of pounds of butter fat contained in it, ascertained by test, and not according to the weight of the milk; and the test of the station where he delivers shall govern."
   Effie Loomis (r02) stresses the final clause — "The test of the station where he delivers shall govern" — as "the hinge of the whole Ostrey Hollow business," since it barred any outside re-testing or appeal.

2. **Ammon & Sons invoice, 14 April 1897** (r07, Item 2):
   > "Sold to the Larrow Valley Co-op. Creamery Assn., for the Ostrey Hollow station: 6 doz. milk test pipettes, 17.6 c.c., at .35 — 25.20; 12 doz. test bottles, 8 per cent neck, at .22 — 31.68; 1 acid measure — .90; 1 hand tester, 4 bottle — 18.00; Freight — 2.15; Total — 77.93."

3. **Station test book, first leaf** (r03, r07 Item 3):
   > "Ostrey Hollow station. Begun this 4th day of May, 1896. Glass borrowed of Larrow Green until our own comes. A. Keddie."

4. **Station test book, summary leaf (made up 1922)** (r03, r07 Item 3):
   > "Average test of all patrons, this station: 1896, 3.80. 1897, part year. 1898, 3.61. And 3.61 or within three hundredths of it every year since, save the nine weeks of 1911 that are noted in their place, when it stood at 3.80."

5. **Keddie's 1911 log entries** (r03):
   > "May 8. Pipette broke this morning. Using a borrowed measure."
   > "July 10. Our own measure in use again from today. Tests are back where they were. ? — A.K."

6. **Farm weigh book, 1910 leaf** (r07, Item 4):
   > "Year 1910. Delivered to the station, two hundred thousand pounds even, by my own scale and theirs agreeing. Paid on a test of 3.42."

7. **Association annual ledger, station account, typical entry (1898)** (r05, r07 Item 6):
   > "Ostrey Hollow: butter fat credited to patrons on station test, 57,760 lb. Butter fat received at Larrow Green in station cream, 60,800 lb. Station gain, 3,040 lb, five and a quarter per cent, as usual. Carried to the general account."

8. **Board of arbitration award, 4 May 1925** (r02, r07 Item 7):
   > "The board having heard the patrons and the officers, and having had before it the books and appliances of the station, finds no cause established. The board awards to the Ostrey Hollow patrons the sum of thirty-eight hundred dollars ($3,800.00), without admission, to be charged to the station account."

9. **Ansel Keddie's statement to the board, 17 March 1925** (r03, quoted in full — nine sentences):
   > "I have kept the test book of this station in my own hand since the station opened, and there is not a figure in it that I did not put there. I have set down the date, the patron, the pounds, the test and the fat, and where I have made an error I have drawn a line through it and put my letters against the line, and I have never written a figure over a figure. I have made the tests in the same order and at the same hour of every working day of every season. I have heard what the patrons say of me and I have heard what the officers say of me. Neither of them has come up that road on a working morning and stood at my elbow and watched the work done. I am asked to account for figures, and the figures are what I read and what I wrote and nothing besides. I am an old man and I have no other trade. I have tested as I was taught and as the state man saw me do it, thirty-one times a week for twenty-nine seasons, and I cannot tell you why the figures are what they are. I have never taken a pound of cream out of that station and there is not a man in this room who thinks I have when he is by himself."

10. **Keddie's unsent letter, spring 1925** (r03) — describes his daily routine in detail (wagons arrive from half-past five; each can weighed and called before anything else; samples taken per patron into numbered jars, made up over ten days, warmed in warm water, mixed, then charged/acid/crank-tested per standard hand-tester procedure); states glass was originally borrowed "off the Ashlin creamery" in [18]96 (**conflicting with the book's own first leaf, which says borrowed of Larrow Green**); states "I did not test a pound in the season of 1925"; recounts 1902 complaints, 1908 committee, 1919 circular; states "I was put out on the thirtieth of April [1925]. They found no cause established and they put me out on the last day of the month, and both of those things are true at once."

11. **1908 investigating committee report** (r07, Item 11, quoted in full):
    > "We have sampled the herds of Hessel Bottom through January, February and March. The milk is whole and the scales are honest. It is our opinion that the wet hay of that bottom is what thins the cream, and that the remedy lies with the land and not with the station."

12. **State Agricultural College Bulletin No. 214 (1911)** (r04, r07 Item 12, quoted):
    > "Bog hay fed as the whole of the roughage depressed the butter fat test by not more than five hundredths of one per cent in winter, and by nothing that could be measured on summer pasture." — Ivy Keddie notes this is "four times too small and... the wrong shape" to explain the observed 0.19 gap, which held flat across all seasons.

Condemned the lot, the whole crate of them, and told the manager to order new. Glass is glass and I did not weigh what it has cost them."

## Quantitative/statistical claims and conflicts (station performance)

- **Total station intake (1898–1922, 25 years):** 40,000,000 lb (r02, corroborated by r07 Item 6 curatorial note) **vs.** 44,000,000 lb per Wilbur Teague's 1948 memoir (r05) — unresolved conflict.
- **Average annual intake:** 1,600,000 lb/year (r02, consistent with the 40M/25-year figure).
- **Average butterfat price over the period:** $0.25/lb (r02, r04, r07).
- **Recorded (flawed) average test, 1898 onward:** 3.61% (per Keddie's own book, r03/r07); **vs.** Teague's memoir instead states the station's overall average test across "the whole period" was 3.16% (r05) — a further, separate conflict from the 3.61 figure used everywhere else.
- **True/corrected average test (per 1958 forensic finding):** 3.80% (r04's calculation: 3.61 ÷ 0.95 = 3.80).
- **Pipette shortfall:** measured mean delivery 16.72 c.c. against a blown/ruled mark of 17.6 c.c.; shortfall of 0.88 c.c., i.e., pipettes delivered 95% of the intended charge (r04, r07 Item 14).
- **Resulting fat discrepancy:** credited fat 1,444,000 lb (at recorded 3.61%) vs. true fat delivered 1,520,000 lb (at corrected 3.80%) over the 25 years/40 million lb; difference = 76,000 lb of butterfat, valued at $19,000 (at $0.25/lb average) (r04, confirmed in r07 Item 6 and Bulletin 471 text).
- **Percentage framing (deliberately dual, per Ivy Keddie's own caveat in r04):** the 76,000 lb "surplus" is 5% of the fat actually delivered (1,520,000 lb) but 5.25% of the fat credited/recorded (1,444,000 lb). The Association's ledgers use the 5.25% figure ("as usual," per Teague's ledger-entry wording in r05/r07) because bookkeepers reckon a surplus against the already-entered (lower) figure. Both percentages are individually correct but describe different denominators — Ivy Keddie explicitly flags this as a source of confusion ("Two percentages have been quoted at me and both are right").
- **Board's 1925 award ($3,800)** represents roughly one-fifth of the calculated true value of the shortfall ($19,000) — explicitly noted by Ivy Keddie (r04): "That is a fifth of it."
- **Vira Toland's competing account (r01):** settlement was $3,500 (not $3,800), split nine ways among patron families, framed as compensation after Keddie was caught stealing cream (not as an unproven-claim arbitration payout).
- **Effect on rich vs. poor milk:** because the error was proportional (a fixed 5% shortfall), richer milk lost more in absolute terms — e.g., a herd recorded at 4.56 was short 0.24 points; a herd recorded at 3.42 was short 0.18 points (r04). (Note: 4.56 matches Selby Vose's own 1910 herd test exactly, per r08, though r04 doesn't explicitly identify whose herd that example refers to.)
- **Larrow Green creamery's own average over the same years:** "a little under two tenths of a point better" than the station's 3.61 (r07, Item 16) — i.e., roughly consistent with the corrected 3.80 figure, serving as independent corroboration.
- **Alder Corners Cheese Company comparison:** over 14 overlapping seasons, the same Hessel Bottom farmers' milk tested about 0.19 points higher at Alder Corners (over in Vessey County) than at the home station (r04) — again matching the calculated true gap almost exactly, and independently corroborating the pipette-error explanation over the wet-hay theory.

## Objects, places, and other details

- **Ostrey Hollow** — valley/hollow, seven miles up from Larrow Green; head of the valley is "Hessel Bottom," a wet meadow with seven farms holding hay/pasture rights, the largest being Emmet Brant's.
- **The skimming station** — a low, whitewashed building at the top of the Hollow where the road bends toward the ridge; cold even in August; smelled of milk, steam, hot iron; had an intake room (scale) and, later, a loft above it used for storage after the station closed (fence wire and salt, per r01; described generally as "odds and ends" by 1928, per r06).
- **Larrow Green** — site of the main creamery (built 1891, on Larrow Creek), the Association's original hub.
- **Ashlin** — county seat/town; site of the Association's office (one room over a harness shop, per r05), the 1925 arbitration hearing, and (later) the Ordell County Historical Society/Rooms.
- **Farland** — location of the State Agricultural College, where Ivy Keddie worked/studied and where Strawn's notebooks and the pipette crate ended up being analyzed.
- **Vessey County / Redlow Ridge / Alder Corners** — neighboring county over the ridge, home to the Alder Corners Cheese Company (Rosalie Cudd, proprietor; Jerome Cudd, her uncle, founder).
- **Mercer's Gap, Bellhaven, Vandermeer, Elkhorn County, Pruett County, Ellerby Playhouse, Sedge County, Cammack Forge, Skiff Crossing, Millstone settlement** — all locations from the unrelated distractor documents, not connected to the Ostrey Hollow narrative.
- **The crate** — wooden, stenciled "AMMON & SONS DAIRY SUPPLY TARNET"; contained the station's condemned milk-test pipettes (originally six dozen bought 1897; nine survived unbroken by 1958, two of those noted as chipped at the tip in r07's Item 5); carried to the station loft in June 1923; found there by Ivy Keddie in February 1958; central physical evidence of the whole mystery.
- **The hand tester** — a 4-bottle crank-driven device (bought 1897 for $18.00) used to spin/separate the fat column for reading.
- **Testing procedure** (per Keddie's unsent letter, r03): scale weighing each can on arrival; sampling into per-patron numbered jars over a 10-day accumulation period; warming jars in warm water before testing; drawing a measured charge via pipette into a bottle with a ruled neck; adding acid; agitating; spinning in the hand tester; reading the fat column against rulings with dividers.
- **The lactometer** — used by state inspector Strawn on every visit specifically to check for added water; consistently found "no added water" at Ostrey Hollow across every recorded year including 1919, 1920, 1922, 1923, 1924, 1925, 1928, 1934, 1936.
- **The burette** — calibrated glass instrument first issued to state field inspectors in 1921 for checking plant measures/pipettes against a sealed standard; this is the tool that (belatedly, in 1923, and again by Ivy Keddie in 1958) revealed the pipette flaw. The state's burette-checking program itself was discontinued a few years after introduction (per r06's note, filed under a "1926" heading in the text, stating instruments were to be kept but no more issued or returned).

## Oddities, internal inconsistencies, and unresolved ambiguities (in addition to conflicts already noted above)

- **r07's Item 3 vs. Item 4 attribution swap:** Item 3 ("Station test book") is captioned as "kept by E. Brant," yet its quoted first leaf is explicitly signed "A. Keddie" and its content matches Ansel Keddie's station test book described in r03/r04. Item 4 ("Farm weigh book") is captioned as "kept by A. Keddie," yet its content ("Delivered to the station... by my own scale and theirs agreeing") reads like a patron/farmer's own delivery record (i.e., a Brant family document), and its provenance trail (begun at Hessel Bottom 1 Jan 1897, kept by "one household," passed to Roy Brant in 1929, then Hazel Brant in 1962) strongly suggests it is the Brant family's own book, not Keddie's. This appears to be a genuine mislabeling/attribution error within the catalog itself (r07), not resolved by any other source.
- **Origin of the first borrowed testing glass:** r03 (Keddie's own letter) says "The glass I began on in ninety-six was borrowed off the Ashlin creamery," while the station book's own first leaf (quoted in both r03 and r07) says "Glass borrowed of Larrow Green until our own comes" — Keddie's letter and his own book's first leaf disagree on which creamery lent the original glass (Ashlin vs. Larrow Green).
- **Identity of the 1958 "young woman" researcher in r01:** described only as being "from the college away east" and "Hazel Brant's first cousin once removed"; not explicitly identified as Ivy Keddie, though the year (1958) and general activity (visiting the shut station, asking about the old affair, "careful, wrote everything down") strongly parallel Ivy Keddie's research described in r04. Not confirmed as the same person by any source — flagged as an ambiguity rather than a stated conflict.
- **Duration of the 1911 measure substitution:** Keddie's dated log entries (8 May–10 July 1911) and r03/r04's explicit "nine weeks" phrasing indicate roughly nine weeks, but Wilbur Teague's memoir (r05) instead recalls "six weeks" — a minor durational conflict.
- **Selby Vose's relationship to Gideon Vose:** "nephew" (r01) vs. "son" (r02, and confirmed in Vose's own first-person words in r08, "My father, who was your first president...") — a direct, unresolved genealogical conflict between Vira Toland's oral account and the documentary/official sources.
- **Condemnation date of the pipettes:** 12 June 1923 (Strawn's own dated circuit entry, r06; also r07 Item 9) vs. "the twelfth of July, 1923" (Selby Vose's 1926 address, r08) — a one-month conflict, with the primary source (inspector's own notebook) favoring June.
- **1924 average test value:** 3.80 (Keddie's book and Strawn's circuit notes, r03/r06) vs. 3.90 (Selby Vose's address, r08) — conflicting; likewise the size of the "rise" is given as roughly 0.19 (3.61→3.80) by the forensic/documentary sources vs. "twenty-one hundredths" (0.21) by Vose.
- **Settlement amount and causal narrative:** $3,800 for "no cause established" (r02, r05, r07, r08 — the institutional/documentary consensus) vs. $3,500 tied explicitly to Keddie being "caught" stealing cream (r01 — the oral/folk account). This is the single largest and most consequential conflict across all eight retellings, since it bears directly on whether Keddie was guilty (r01's belief, later disproven) or innocent (established by 1958 forensic bulletin, corroborated throughout r03, r04, r06, r07).
- **Whether anyone was ever "caught":** r01 flatly states "He was taking the cream. They caught him at it and they put him out," attributing the 1925 dismissal to detected theft. This is contradicted by: the arbitration board's own finding of "no cause established" (r02, r07, r08); Strawn's inspections finding nothing amiss and no added water in every single year on record; and definitively by Bulletin No. 471 (1958): "No milk was watered at Ostrey Hollow and no cream was stolen there." r01's account thus represents a persistent but factually incorrect local belief/folk memory, explicitly overturned by the documentary record compiled in r04/r06/r07.
- **Teague's suspicion vs. the eventual exoneration:** Wilbur Teague's 1948 memoir (r05) maintains to the end (writing decades after the 1958 exoneration would have been possible for him to know about, though it's unclear if he was aware of it) that "a business which is arranged so that a single man can take a little and write down a little less is a business that will sooner or later have a little taken from it" — implying continued suspicion of embezzlement even in his own retrospective account, without acknowledging (or perhaps without yet knowing of) the mechanical pipette explanation. This creates a notable tension between r05's persistent suspicion and r04/r06/r07's forensic exoneration, though it's not necessarily a "conflict" so much as Teague's account possibly predating or being unaware of the 1958 findings (r05 was written 1948, ten years before Bulletin 471).
