## Story 1 — The Short Glass of Ostrey Hollow

**Who:** Ansel Keddie (1861–1934), tester and separator man at the Larrow
Valley Cooperative Creamery Association's Ostrey Hollow skimming station from
its opening (4 May 1896) until his dismissal in 1925; his wife Orra (née
Brant); their son Duncan; their granddaughter Ivy Keddie; the patrons of
Hessel Bottom, chiefly Emmet Brant (the largest hauler) and his son Roy and
granddaughter Hazel; the Association's manager Selby Vose; bookkeeper Wilbur
Teague and clerk Bertram Kyle; the glass supplier Ammon & Sons of Tarnet; and
state dairy inspector Merle Strawn.

**What happened:** From the station's opening its milk tested persistently
lower in butterfat than neighboring stations, which paid patrons on the test
rather than the weight of milk delivered. Three explanations circulated for
thirty years: added water, deliberate theft/false readings by the tester, and
thin cream from the wet bog hay of Hessel Bottom. All three were argued at a
1925 arbitration hearing, which awarded patrons $3,800 "without admission" and
dismissed Keddie four days later. The true cause, discovered only in 1958 when
his granddaughter Ivy found the station's own condemned glassware crated in
the station loft, was that the 1897 lot of milk-test pipettes (bought from
Ammon & Sons, blown to a 17.6 c.c. mark) had been manufactured short and
delivered on average only 16.27 c.c. — producing a constant, season-blind
low reading in every test made with that glass. State inspector Strawn had
condemned the glass in June 1923 for an unrelated reason (it failed a burette
check) without ever connecting it to the years of complaint; the crate then
sat forgotten for 35 years until Ivy climbed the loft ladder.

**When:** Glass in service 1 May 1897 – 12 June 1923; hearing and dismissal,
1925; vindication, 1958.

**Quantities that matter:** ~7.6% proportional shortfall in every test;
$3,800 awarded vs. an estimated $19,600 owed; 72 pipettes (6 dozen) bought for
$77.93 in 1897.

**Documents:** r17, r18, r19, r20, r21, r22, r23, r24 (all eight).
