## Section B — Relationships

**B1. Ivy Keddie's relationship to Ansel Keddie.**
Ivy is Ansel's **granddaughter**. Chain: Ansel and Orra Keddie's son is Duncan
(r18, r24); Duncan's daughter, born 1931, is Ivy (r18, explicit: "My daughter
is Ivy, born in 1931, which makes her my father's granddaughter, and I write
that plainly because I have already twice seen it printed otherwise"). Two
sources call her "great-niece" instead: the *Ashlin Chronicle* (r19) and Orin
Falke's county report as first drafted (r22, ¶2). Falke's report then
self-corrects in an appended note: "I am informed, and have verified, that
Miss Keddie is the granddaughter and not the great-niece; the error on page
two is mine." I resolve this in favor of "granddaughter": it is stated by
Ivy's own father (the strongest possible authority on his own daughter's
parentage), by Cleve Oram independently (r21, "the granddaughter came back"),
and by Falke's own verified correction. The "great-niece" figure looks like a
single error (probably originating in an imprecise press release) that the
Chronicle and Falke's draft both repeated — one source, not two independent
ones.

**B2. Ivy Keddie's relationship to Hazel Brant.**
They are **second cousins** — children of first cousins. Chain: Emmet Brant
and Orra (Keddie) were brother and sister; Emmet's son Roy and Orra's son
Duncan are therefore first cousins; Roy's daughter Hazel and Duncan's daughter
Ivy are therefore second cousins. This exact relationship, and the explicit
rejection of "cousins once removed" or "aunt and niece," is stated
independently by both principals: Hazel herself (r17: "Second cousins. It is
not complicated; it is only that nobody wants to count") and Duncan himself
(r18, Section II, with the same reasoning). Cleve Oram (r21) instead calls
Ivy "Hazel Brant's first cousin once removed" — a single, secondhand,
valley-gossip source contradicted by both people it concerns; I discard it.
Nothing in the sources leaves the generational chain itself open — it is
fully specified — the only open question the sources leave is Orra's exact
birth year (see E1), which does not affect the cousin count.

**B3. Selby Vose's father, and why it matters.**
Selby Vose's father was **Gideon Vose**, who organized the Larrow Valley
Cooperative Creamery Association and was its first president (r18, r23).
Selby became the Association's manager in 1911 (r23) and it was he who signed
and circulated the 1919 warning letter accusing Ostrey Hollow patrons of
watering their milk (r22, r23, r24). This matters because it shows the
accusation against the patrons came from the very top of the organization
that had bought and never audited the defective glass, and because a rumor
that Selby was Gideon's *nephew* rather than son was in circulation and is
explicitly and independently rejected by two sources with direct knowledge:
Duncan Keddie's family notes (r18) and Bertram Kyle, the clerk who worked
immediately outside Selby's office door for a quarter century (r23: "the
son, whatever is said at Ashlin... I have heard the nephew story twice in the
last year and it is nonsense"). Two independent, well-placed sources agreeing
settles this.

**B4. Relationship between the Vose and Keddie families.**
The sources establish **no genuine family relationship**. Duncan Keddie
addresses this directly and as a named section of his notes ("A thing that is
said and is not so," r18): he states flatly there was no connection, that
people confused the Voses with an unrelated Ashlin family of the same name,
and that his mother — who knew everyone's connections in the valley — never
once included a Vose. Against this stands only one source, Cleve Oram (r21),
reporting vague inherited hearsay ("The Keddies and the Voses were cousins of
some sort... I could not draw you the line on paper"). Duncan's testimony is
both a direct family source and explicitly rebuts the specific claim; Oram's
is unverifiable secondhand rumor with no genealogical detail. I resolve this
as: no relationship, and the belief that there was one is itself a known
folk-confusion (per Duncan) between two unrelated Vose families.
