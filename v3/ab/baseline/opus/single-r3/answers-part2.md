# Answer Sheet — Part 2 (Section A, stories 5–8; Section B)

## Section A — Reconstruction (continued)

### Story 5. Over the ridge: the Alder Corners cheese factory (1915–1930)

**Who:** Jerome Cudd, who founded the cheese company at Alder Corners in Vessey County, over the ridge; his daughter Rosalie Cudd; and four of the six Hessel Bottom farmers.

**The factory's dates.**
- Rosalie says it was built in 1913; Rundle and Roy say it opened in 1914.
- These may both be true (built 1913, opened 1914). That reading is my inference; the sources do not settle it.

**The milk.**
- The ridge road is closed from November to April.
- From **1915 to 1930 (16 seasons)**, four Bottom men hauled over the ridge in the summer flush only, June to mid-September. The rest of the year they delivered to the Ostrey Hollow station (Roy, Rosalie, Hazel).
- Hazel's father called it "the only honest arithmetic anybody in that valley ever did without meaning to."

**Cudd's tests.**
- Cudd tested that milk on his own, uncondemned glass. His 1919 figures for the four were **3.79, 3.81, 3.78 and 3.82**.
- His yearly averages ran 3.78–3.82, about **3.80**: the valley's figure, and the same figure the station got on borrowed glass in 1911 and new glass in 1924.
- The cheese yield itself showed there was no water in the milk.
- Jerome never compared his figures with the station's, and no one else did either.
- The same cows, on the same Bottom, in the same seasons, read 3.80 over the ridge and 3.61 at the station. That points to the station's instrument, not the land or the men.
- Because this was summer milk, it also answers the hay theory: Bulletin 214 found no measurable summer effect, yet the station's summer gap was still 0.19.

**How far the checks overlap.**
- Cudd's testing overlaps Keddie's for 1915–1924: **10 seasons**.
- It overlaps the short glass for **8 full seasons (1915–1922)**; the 1923 season was cut by the condemnation on 12 June.
- Ivy's "14 overlapping seasons" matches neither count and is rejected.

**The records.**
- Jerome Cudd died in March 1941. Rosalie (his daughter; the Chronicle's "niece" is wrong) took over the factory and the test book.
- In 1958 she had the figures copied out and sent to Ivy at Farland, "on a stranger's say-so."
- The book has never left Vessey County. It went to the Vessey County Historical Society in 1970 (Duncan's 1970 addition).

**Documents:** Rosalie Cudd's letter (r10); Roy (r09); Hazel (r17); Rundle (r12); Duncan (r18); the Chronicle (r19); Ivy's account.

### Story 6. The office at Larrow Green: the water circular and the "station gain"

**Who:** Selby Vose (manager from 1911 for about a quarter-century; Gideon Vose's son, not his nephew); Wilbur Teague (bookkeeper); Bertram Kyle (clerk at the outer desk for 25 years); and Hobart Sill (buttermaker at Larrow Green, 1893–1934).

**The ledger line.**
- Every year the Ostrey Hollow station account carried three figures:
  - the fat credited to patrons on the station test;
  - the fat received at Larrow Green in the station's cream;
  - the difference, entered as "**station gain** … five and a quarter per cent, as usual," and carried to the general account.
- The column starts in 1897, when the short glass went in. Teague's claim to have invented the line in 1905 is wrong.
- A typical year: 57,760 lb credited against 60,800 lb received, a gain of 3,040 lb. That is 5¼% of the fat credited, and exactly what 1,600,000 lb of milk gives at 3.61% against 3.80%.
- Sill independently saw the same thing in the churn: the Hollow cream "always came down heavy," richer than its sheet. Yet he still blamed water, which cannot make cream heavier.

**Nobody read it.**
- Teague "never asked anybody anything in forty years."
- Vose took each sheet, "put his thumb on the bottom figure, and signed."
- Kyle copied the line every year and read it to the annual meeting. "It was in plain sight with a name on it."

**The 1919 circular.**
- In June 1919 Vose signed, and Kyle typed, a circular warning the Hollow patrons against watering their milk. It went to **nine families** on six farms (Hazel and the other accounts; Orra's diary says eleven).
- Keddie would not mail the circulars. He carried them door to door and stood by while each man read his.
- Hazel's mother burned hers. None of the families answered.
- Emmet Brant came to the Keddies' yard and asked Ansel why he had carried the paper if he did not believe it. He did not set foot in the house again before his death in 1929.

**The office's figures for 1898–1922.**
- Kyle compiled them for the arbitration: **40,000,000 lb** of milk (1.6M lb a year), with fat averaging **25¢**.
- The 1925 award was later charged to the station account.

**Documents:** Kyle (r23); Teague; Sill (r14); Selby Vose; Orra's diary (r24); Hazel (r17); Frick (r13); Vaught (r11); Rundle's press note (r12).

### Story 7. The petition, the arbitration and the dismissal (1924–1934)

**Who:** the Hessel Bottom patrons; Alonzo Frick (Ashlin attorney and chairman of the board); Ansel Keddie; and the Association.

**Before the petition.**
- On the new glass, the station's 1924 average was 3.80: a rise of 0.19, which came **before** the petition.
- Selby Vose's claim that the rise came after the petition is wrong.

**The petition and hearing.**
- In **January 1925** the patrons petitioned, with Emmet Brant's name on the paper.
- A board of arbitration sat from **17 March to 4 May 1925**. The first sitting was at the Cooperative's office in Ashlin. The second and third were in the station weigh room itself (Frick, Oram, Orra, Duncan), with men standing outside in the rain and the condemned crate about twenty feet overhead in the loft.
- Three theories were argued:
  - water (Vose);
  - cream theft (Teague, by insinuation);
  - hay (the 1908 report).

**The four books before the board** (Kyle checked them off; Frick's list agrees):
- the station test book in Keddie's hand, 4 May 1896 to 31 December 1924;
- the receiving book;
- Emmet Brant's weigh book, lent for the purpose;
- Strawn's 1923 notebook, which recorded the condemnation.

Nobody laid the notebook beside the test book, and the crate was never produced.

**Dismissal and award.**
- Keddie was dismissed on **30 April 1925**, four days before the finding.
- On 4 May the board found "no cause established" and awarded **$3,800** "without admission." Vira's and Oram's $3,500 is rejected.
- Hazel's "hearing in '26," Kyle's "award in '24" and the Chronicle's "1908 committee sat again" are all wrong.
- Roy Brant never went to another Association meeting.

**Afterward.**
- The test book was impounded in March 1925 and lay in the Cooperative office at Ashlin until 1959.
- Keddie turned to carpentry: stairs, gates, doors, "a carpenter at sixty-six" in 1927.
- He died at home in **February 1934**. He had once told Orra that "a man cannot be cleared of a thing nobody will name."
- Frick (1946, aged 74) still believed Keddie's denial but got his figures wrong (3.16, 67,000 lb).
- The station closed in 1946 (Oram).

**Documents:** Frick (r13); Kyle (r23); Orra (r24); Duncan (r18); Oram (r21); Hazel (r17); Falke (r22); Selby Vose; Roy (r09).

### Story 8. The crate in the loft: Ivy Keddie and Bulletin 471 (1958)

**Who:**
- **Ivy Keddie**, born 1931, daughter of Duncan (a schoolteacher at Ashlin, born 1894) and granddaughter of Ansel and Orra. She was a graduate student in dairy husbandry at Farland.
- **Professor Leland Vaught**, her co-author.

**The research.**
- In 1958 Roy Brant **lent** her the weigh book; it went to Hazel at his death in 1962 and to the Historical Rooms in 1963.
- In **February 1958** she climbed the ladder into the station loft and found the condemned crate under a tarpaulin and chaff. **Nine** pipettes survived: Oram and Falke say nine, against the Chronicle's and Rundle's eleven.
- She took them to Farland. On **9 March 1958** she measured them against a calibrated burette: mean **16.72 c.c.** against 17.6 marked, so **0.95** of the mark, all in the same proportion.
- She also found Strawn's 1923 notebook in the college library, and Rosalie Cudd sent her the Alder Corners figures.

**Bulletin 471 (October 1958, Vaught and Keddie).**
- True average = 3.61 / 0.95 = **3.80**. The error was proportional, one part in twenty, from 1 May 1897 to 12 June 1923.
- Over the 25 full years 1898–1922, the 40M lb of milk was credited with 1,444,000 lb of fat but actually carried **1,520,000 lb**.
- The **76,000 lb** shortfall, worth **$19,000** at 25¢, sits in the ledgers as "station gain": 5% of the fat delivered, 5¼% of the fat credited.
- The 1925 award was one-fifth of the loss.
- Conclusion: no water, and no theft.
- Vaught's introduction contains slips (16.27 c.c., 118,000 lb, five dozen pipettes, 1898) that his own conclusion contradicts.

**Reception.**
- The Ashlin Chronicle (3 October 1958) announced the result with many errors: "great-niece," 11 pipettes, $19,600, $77.39, Rosalie as "niece."
- Rundle's 1959 reprint retracted the theft theory.
- Falke's 1959 report to the county board corrected "great-niece" to granddaughter and withdrew the hay concession.
- In 1959 the test book and the crate went to the Ordell County Historical Rooms at Ashlin.

**Kinship.** Orra Keddie was Emmet Brant's sister, so Roy Brant and Duncan Keddie were first cousins, and Hazel Brant and Ivy are second cousins. Ansel was cleared 24 years after his death. Orra had died in 1948.

**Documents:** Vaught (r11); the Chronicle (r19); Falke (r22); Duncan (r18); Hazel (r17); Roy (r09); Rundle (r12); Oram (r21); Kyle (r23); Rosalie (r10).

## Section B — Relationships

**B1. Ivy Keddie and Ansel Keddie: granddaughter.**
- Ansel and Orra's son was Duncan (born 1894). Ivy (born 1931) is Duncan's daughter. Duncan states this in his own family notes (r18) because he had "twice seen it printed otherwise."
- Hazel, Oram ("the granddaughter came back") and Falke's correction all agree.
- "Great-niece" appears in the Chronicle (r19) and in Falke's first draft (r22). Both drew on the same college press release and telephone calls, so they count as one source. Falke himself later verified the correction and retracted it.
- A son's written family record outranks a newspaper assembled in a day and a half.

**B2. Ivy Keddie and Hazel Brant: second cousins.**
- Emmet Brant and Orra Keddie were siblings. Emmet's son Roy and Orra's son Duncan were therefore first cousins, and their daughters Hazel and Ivy are second cousins.
- Duncan, Hazel and Rundle all say second cousins. Vira's and Oram's "first cousin once removed" would require a one-generation offset that does not exist, since Hazel and Ivy are both great-grandchildren of the same Brant couple.
- **Left open:** whether Emmet and Orra were full or half siblings.
  - Hazel says Orra was a half-sister "by his father's second wife."
  - Duncan, Orra's own son, says simply "brother and sister … the younger of the two," and he is usually precise.
  - A 12-year age gap (Emmet born 1854, Orra born 1866) fits either account, and no document settles it.
  - If half-siblings, Hazel and Ivy are strictly half second cousins; the generation count is the same.
- **Also left open:** Orra's birth year. The diary preface says 1866 and has her 64 in 1930. Duncan says 1868 but also "eighty-two when she died in 1948," which implies 1866, so 1866 is preferred.
- The mothers of Roy and of Ivy are never named.

**B3. Selby Vose's father: Gideon Vose.**
- Gideon organized the Association in 1889, was its first president until 1911, and wrote its by-laws (Kyle, Duncan). Rundle's "nephew" is Ashlin gossip that both men dismiss.
- **Why it matters:** Gideon's Article VII made the *station's* test govern each patron's pay, with no appeal to another test. His son, manager from 1911, administered that rule for 14 years:
  - He signed the ledger sheets that carried the "station gain" every year without reading them.
  - He sent the 1919 water circular that blamed the patrons.
  - He was on the Association side at the 1925 arbitration, where the award was paid "without admission" and charged to the station account.
- The family that wrote the rule ran the institution that silently kept the 76,000 lb. The son had every reason to defend the father's system and to look for the fault in the farmers rather than in the station.

**B4. The Vose and Keddie families: no relationship established, and the best evidence says none.**
- Oram (r21) repeats that "the Keddies and the Voses were cousins of some sort." He had it from his mother's mother and admits he "could not draw you the line."
- Duncan (r18) states flatly that there was no connection. The rumor that Orra and Mrs. Gideon Vose were cousins comes from confusing Gideon's family with the unrelated Ashlin Voses, and Orra, who "could not pass a headstone without giving you the connection," never once named a Vose.
- A family member's direct knowledge outranks third-hand gossip that admits it cannot name the link.
- An absence of kinship cannot be proved absolutely, but nothing in the sources supports one.
