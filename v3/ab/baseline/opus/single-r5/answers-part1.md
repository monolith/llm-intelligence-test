## Section A — Reconstruction

### Story 1. Article VII and the opening of the Ostrey Hollow station (1889–1896)

**About:** the Larrow Valley Cooperative Creamery Association, its founder Gideon Vose, and its first tester, Ansel Keddie.

**Founding and Article VII.**
- The Association was organized in 1889 by valley farmers meeting at Larrow Green. It built its main creamery there in 1891 and kept its office at Ashlin.
- Gideon Vose was president from 1889 to 1911 and wrote the by-laws. He died in 1917.
- Article VII pays each patron on the pounds of butterfat his milk contains by test, not on the weight of the milk. It also says that "the test of the station where he delivers shall govern." Those words left a patron no appeal to any other test, and they are the hinge of everything that follows.
- The year Article VII was adopted **cannot be determined**:
  - Rundle gives 1891, Effie 1892 and Grigg "the 1893 meeting."
  - The printed by-laws say only "adopted at the annual meeting," and the early minute book was lost (r22).
  - It was in force well before 1896.

**The station.**
- In the winter of 1895–96 the Association built a skimming station at Ostrey Hollow, 7 miles up the valley. It served the six farms of Hessel Bottom, a wet meadow along the creek. The 1908 report counts six farms, as do Roy, Hazel and Kepp; Effie's "seven" is rejected.
- The station **opened 4 May 1896**, and the test book begins that day (r13 exhibit list; r22). Vira's and Hazel's "went up in '95" is a conflation with the building winter.
- The station worked like this:
  - Milk was weighed in at the intake platform and sampled.
  - The sample was tested for butterfat with a pipette charge of 17.6 c.c. into a bottle with a graduated neck.
  - The milk was separated. The cream was hauled 7 miles down to Larrow Green (Cleve Oram's father drove the wagon), and the skim went back to the farmer.

**Ansel Keddie.**
- Ansel (1861–1934) was tester and separator man from the opening day for 29 seasons, 1896 through 1924.
- In 1889 he had married Orra Brant, sister of Emmet Brant, the biggest patron on Hessel Bottom.

**The first season.**
- The station used **borrowed glass**. Ansel's first leaf says it was "borrowed of Larrow Green," and Orra's diary for 1 May 1897 says "done borrowing of Larrow Green." Kyle agrees.
- Sill, the Larrow Green buttermaker from 1893, says it was his own glass and came back chipped the next spring. That is compatible: it was the creamery's glass, in his care.
- Ansel's unsent letter says "the Ashlin creamery." That is a slip, since no creamery at Ashlin appears anywhere else.
- The 1896 milk was carried on the creamery's books, and no station figure survives (r07, r23). The first season's intake is therefore **unknown**: Sill's 900,000 lb and Kyle's 1,100,000 lb are unsupported memories.
- Because the 1896 glass was sound, the first season is not part of the loss.

**Documents:** r02, r03, r07, r12, r14, r15, r22, r23, r24, r21, r01, r17.

### Story 2. The Ammon outfit: seventy-two short pipettes (1897–1923)

**About:** Ammon & Sons of Tarnet, the station's own testing outfit, and state inspector Merle Strawn.

**The purchase.**
- Ammon's invoice is dated **14 April 1897** and reproduced in facsimile in r20:

  | Item | Amount |
  |---|---|
  | 6 doz. milk test pipettes, 17.6 c.c., at $0.35 | $25.20 |
  | 12 doz. test bottles, 8% neck, at $0.22 | $31.68 |
  | Acid measure | $0.90 |
  | Four-bottle hand tester | $18.00 |
  | Freight | $2.15 |
  | **Total** | **$77.93** |

- The items add to $77.93. Junia's prose and the *Chronicle* give $77.39, a transposition. Twenty-five dollars twenty at 35 cents means **72 pipettes**, so "five dozen" (r11, r21) is wrong.
- The outfit went into use on **1 May 1897** (invoice date; r20; Orra's diary; Ivy). Vaught's and Kyle's 1898 is rejected.

**Why it mattered.**
- The pipettes were marked 17.6 c.c. but, as measured in 1958, delivered on average **16.72 c.c.: 0.88 c.c. short, 95 per cent of the mark**.
- A short charge in a bottle ruled for a full charge reads low in the same proportion. Every test the station made therefore recorded 95 per cent of the true fat:
  - the true 3.80 read 3.61;
  - richer milk lost more, so a true 4.80 read 4.56.
- The error was consistent from pipette to pipette. Junia's inference is that a reground mold ran the whole lot short, and a spot-check cannot find an error that is always the same. Ammon's works books burned in 1931, so this is **inference, not evidence**. The *Chronicle*'s charge that Ammon sold a short lot knowingly is unsupported.

**Inspection and condemnation.**
- Strawn rode the circuit from 1907 to 1936. Burettes were issued to field men in 1921, and Strawn skipped the burette check in 1922.
- On **12 June 1923** he ran the station's pipettes against his burette and condemned the whole crate.
- This date comes from Strawn's own 1923 notebook. It is also confirmed by the order date: the Association ordered six dozen pipettes and six dozen bottles on 28 June, and Ammon replied on 2 July ("your order of the 28th ultimo… Our books of the nineties are not such that we can tell you anything"). Vose's and Rundle's "12 July" is impossible, and Hazel's 1921 and Kyle's 1922 are wrong.
- New glass was in use from July 1923, and the 1924 average was **3.80**.
- The condemned crate went up into the loft over the intake room. No one, Strawn included, asked what the glass had been doing for 26 years. The burette program was dropped in 1926.

**Documents:** r20, r19, r11, r04, r06, r22, r23, r24, r18, r08, r12, r21, r17.

### Story 3. The 1908 committee and the hay theory (1902–1926, withdrawn 1959)

**About:** Nyle Grigg's investigating committee, Bulletin 214, and Dr. Roland Kepp.

**The complaint and the committee.**
- Complaints that Ostrey Hollow milk tested low began about **1902**. Orra's March 1902 diary records the talk at the store and Ansel's "it is my figures they will look at."
- In January 1908 the board named a committee of three, chaired by **Nyle Grigg of Larrow Green**, who was not a patron of the station. Orra saw them arrive in Grigg's sleigh and go first to Emmet Brant's farm, the largest.
- The committee:
  - visited all **six** farms and sealed its own samples;
  - checked the station scales against a known weight and the farm scales against the station's;
  - asked about water;
  - stood by while the test was made.
- It found **no dishonesty of any kind**. The report says it sampled **January, February and March only**, and Orra's diary has the men gone by March.
- It concluded that the wet sedge hay of Hessel Bottom "thins the cream" and that "the remedy lies with the land and not with the station." The report closed the question for eleven years.

**The science.**
- The State Agricultural College at Farland published **Bulletin 214 in 1911**, a controlled feeding trial with bog hay. Its summary, quoted in full by Falke, found the test depressed by **not more than 0.05 in winter and by nothing measurable on summer pasture**.

**The theory repeated.**
- In 1919 Grigg reprinted the report with a cover letter that misstates both his own work ("through the whole of 1908, every month") and the Bulletin ("two tenths in the winter feeding").
- Kepp's 1926 paper to the Ordell County Farm Club repeated the "two tenths." It blamed sour, lime- and phosphate-poor soil for "a fifth of a point," used the wrong gap (0.21) and the wrong price (27c), and dismissed the 1911 weeks as "six weeks" of summer flush.
- At the 1925 arbitration the hay report was one of the three theories.

**Why it fails.**
- The true gap was **0.19, the same in every month**, February and July alike (r09, r17, r22). A winter-sampling committee could not have seen that the gap persisted in summer, when the cows were off the hay.
- 0.05 cannot carry 0.19 in winter, and nothing cannot carry it in summer.
- The same Hessel Bottom milk tested about 3.80 at Alder Corners in summer.
- The station test rose in 1911 and again after July 1923 with no drainage at all.
- Falke conceded the hay as a "partial factor" in 1959, then withdrew the concession on reading Bulletin 214's figures.

**Documents:** r15, r16, r22, r11, r24, r09, r13, r02, r04.

### Story 4. The nine weeks of 1911

**About:** Ansel Keddie's test book in the summer of 1911, the one natural experiment in the record.

**What happened.**
- On **8 May 1911** the station's own measuring glass broke. Orra's diary for that day: "A. home cross about a broken measure."
- A borrowed measure was used until **10 July 1911**, which is **nine weeks** (63 days). Teague's and Kepp's "six weeks" is wrong.
- Where the borrowed measure came from is not agreed. Most accounts say Larrow Green; Roy says the state man left his own. Either way it was a sound measure from outside the station's lot.

**The result.**
- For those nine weeks the Ostrey Hollow tests rose to about **3.80**, the true level of the milk and the figure later seen at Alder Corners and after 1923.
- When the station went back to its own glass on 10 July, in midsummer, the tests fell to the old low level at once.
- Nothing else changed: the same tester, the same herds and patrons, the same scales, acid, bottles and procedure.
- Ansel entered the figures and wrote a **"?"** in the test book. At home he said, twice, that it was "something in the glass." Duncan, then seventeen, remembered it. Ansel never put that in the book, because he could not prove it and "would not put down what he could not prove."
- Strawn inspected the station in June 1911, in the middle of the interval, and noted nothing.

**Later readings.**
- Kepp waved the weeks away as a summer fluke. That fails because the gap was constant across seasons and because the tests fell back in July, not in autumn.
- Bulletin 471 called the interval a natural experiment that "lay unread for forty-seven years" (1911 to 1958).
- Falke's 1959 report calls it decisive and uses it against the theft theory: the same man made the tests, under no different supervision, and the figures rose.

**The quantities.**
- Borrowed measure: about 3.80. Own glass: about 3.61. Difference: 0.19, which is 5 per cent.
- This matches the 0.95 ratio measured in 1958.

**Documents:** r03, r04, r11, r18, r22, r24, r16, r05, r09, r06, r12.
