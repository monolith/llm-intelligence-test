# Comprehensive Retention Notes: Retellings 1-8 (Ostrey Hollow Milk Testing Case)

## ENTITIES & RELATIONSHIPS

### PEOPLE - Core Actors
- **Ansel Keddie** (1867?-1934): Tester at Ostrey Hollow skimming station from 1896-1924 (29 seasons). Kept meticulous test book by hand. Dismissed April 30, 1925. Died 1934, exonerated 24 years later (1958).
- **Orra Keddie**: Ansel's wife, first cousin of old Mrs. Vose. Sister of Emmet Brant (major patron). Outlived Ansel, had stigma her whole life.
- **Emmet Brant**: Largest dairy farmer on Hessel Bottom, most cows, most hay ground, largest patron of Ostrey Hollow station every year on record. Brother of Orra. After 1919 circular, did not speak to Ansel for rest of life—lived within sight of each other's chimneys but never said good morning.
- **Vira Toland** (b. 1912): Narrator of retelling 1. Born top of Ostrey Hollow, daughter of milk hauler, lived there her whole life except 2 years in Ashlin when first married. Witnessed the scandal as child of 7 (1919).
- **Selby Vose** (Gideon's nephew): Manager of Association 1913-1938. Signed annual station accounts (knew of station gain every year). Sent 1919 circular warning about water. Defended both circular and $3,800 settlement in 1926 address. In 1926 address, claimed water was deliberately added to cans and stopped only when scrutiny began; tests rose 21 hundredths in 1924 (to 3.90).
- **Gideon Vose** (old): First president Association 1889-1911 (22 years, longest tenure). Founded Association. Drafted by-laws. Father of Selby. Wrote Article VII. Died 1917.
- **Effie Loomis**: Secretary Association 1901-1940. Kept minute book in own hand. Secretary age 14 in 1889, office age 27 in 1901.
- **Wilbur Teague**: Bookkeeper Association 1905-1945 (40 years). Created "station gain" accounting line in 1905 to track discrepancy. Reported to Selby Vose in 1916, told to let alone.
- **Hobart Sill**: Buttermaker at Larrow Green creamery.
- **Merle Strawn**: State dairy inspector, took Ordell circuit 1907, laid down 1936 (29 years). Condemned the defective pipettes June 12, 1923. Noted in 1923 entries that station tests improved after new glass ordered.
- **Ivy Keddie**: Ansel's granddaughter (Duncan Keddie's daughter). Duncan Keddie was Ansel's son, taught school at Ashlin. Ivy gave 1959 historical society presentation, 1972 accession notes. Found crate February 1958 at station loft. Measured defective pipettes March 9, 1958 (mean 16.72cc vs. 17.6cc mark).
- **Roy Brant**: Emmet Brant's son. Kept and lent Hessel Bottom weigh book to Ivy Keddie 1958.
- **Hazel Brant**: Related to family. First cousin once removed of Vira Toland. Received weigh book from Roy Brant 1962.
- **Alonzo Frick**: Attorney, chairman of board of arbitration 1925.
- **Duncan Keddie** (Ansel's son): Teacher at Ashlin. Father of Ivy.
- **Rosalie Cudd**: Jerome Cudd's niece, runs Alder Corners Cheese Company in Vessey County (over Redlow Ridge). Provided copies of test figures for overlapping years.

### ORGANIZATIONS & PLACES
- **Larrow Valley Cooperative Creamery Association**: Founded 1889 at Larrow Green. Main creamery built 1891 on Larrow Creek. Office at Ashlin (one room over harness shop). Members: 9 families hauling milk from Ostrey Hollow (names not given).
- **Ostrey Hollow Station**: 7 miles up valley from Larrow Green, at head of Hollow. Opened before 1896 (began operations May 4, 1896). Skimming station: received milk, separated it, sent cream down to Larrow Green, gave skim back to farmers. Served 9 families of Ostrey Hollow and Hessel Bottom (especially 7 farms with rights on Hessel Bottom).
- **Hessel Bottom**: Wet meadow below hamlet of Ostrey Hollow. Seven farms had hay/pasture rights. Best milk came from here. Most prominent: Emmet Brant's farm.
- **Larrow Green**: Village where creamery and Association office located.
- **Ashlin**: County seat. Site of Association office, board of arbitration hearings (March 17-May 4, 1925).
- **Alder Corners Cheese Company**: Factory in Vessey County, over Redlow Ridge. Test book never left Vessey County; passed founder to daughter 1941, to Vessey County Historical Society 1970.
- **Ordell County Historical Rooms/Society**: Where items catalogued 1972 by curator Halbert Nym. Received crate of pipettes and documents 1959.
- **State Agricultural College at Farland**: Ivy Keddie measured defective pipettes there March 9, 1958. Merle Strawn's circuit notebooks in library since 1947.

## TIMELINE OF EVENTS

**1891**: Main creamery built at Larrow Green on Larrow Creek.

**1892**: Article VII adopted at annual meeting (year after creamery opened): "Every patron shall be paid for the milk he delivers according to the number of pounds of butter fat contained in it, ascertained by test, and not according to the weight of the milk; and the test of the station where he delivers shall govern." This replaces weight-based payment with butterfat-based payment at tested station's test (no appeal).

**April 14, 1897**: Ammon & Sons Dairy Supply (Tarnet) ships to Larrow Valley Co-op for Ostrey Hollow station: 6 doz pipettes (17.6 cc), 12 doz bottles (8% neck), acid measure, hand tester (4-bottle), freight. Total invoice $77.93.

**1898-1924**: Test average 3.61 (or within 0.03), consistent across all years except 1911.

**1902**: Complaints begin. First said Ostrey Hollow milk didn't test with rest of valley.

**1908**: Committee sampled Hessel Bottom herds January-March. Reported: "The milk is whole and the scales are honest. It is our opinion that the wet hay of that bottom is what thins the cream, and that the remedy lies with the land and not with the station."

## THE PIPETTE DEFECT: MATHEMATICAL EVIDENCE

**Defect discovered**: June 12, 1923, by Merle Strawn. Pipettes (blown for 17.6 cc) only delivered 16.72 cc (mean of 9 measured March 9, 1958).

**Ratio**: 16.72 / 17.6 = 0.95 exactly = 95 hundredths of mark.
**Shortfall**: 17.6 - 16.72 = 0.88 cc per 17.6 cc charge.

**Consequence for testing**: Every test made with defective glass read 95% of the truth. Or equivalently, readings were systematically short by 5%.

**Applied to recorded averages**:
- Recorded average (with defective glass): 3.61% butterfat (1898-1922)
- True average: 3.61 / 0.95 = 3.80% butterfat
- Gap: 3.80 - 3.61 = 0.19% (nineteen hundredths)
- 0.19 is 5% of 3.80
- 0.19 is 5.25% of 3.61 (the recorded average)

**Tonnage over 25 full years (1898-1922)**:
- Station intake: 40,000,000 pounds of milk (average 1.6 million pounds/year)
- Recorded (with defective glass): 1,444,000 pounds of butterfat at 3.61%
- True (as delivered): 1,520,000 pounds of butterfat at 3.80%
- Missing butterfat: 76,000 pounds
- At average price 25 cents/pound: $19,000 total value
- Board awarded: $3,800 (one-fifth of actual loss)

**Distribution of loss**: Error proportional, hit hardest on richest milk.
- Example: herd recorded at 4.56% was short 0.24 points (shorted most)
- Example: herd recorded at 3.42% was short 0.18 points (shorted less)

**1911 anomaly explained**: 
- Measure broke May 8, 1911
- Borrowed measure used May 8-July 10 (nine weeks) had correct specs (17.6 cc)
- Tests jumped to 3.80 immediately with borrowed measure
- Returned to 3.61 same day own (defective) measure returned July 10
- No water, no theft, no intentional fraud—just defective measuring glass

**Evidence from other sources**:
- Alder Corners Cheese Company (Vessey County): tested milk from 4 Hessel Bottom farmers during overlapping seasons (14 years). That same milk from Hessel Bottom tested approximately 0.19% higher at Alder Corners than at Ostrey Hollow station. Confirms systematic station-test shortfall.

**Timeline of discovery**:
- Defect present: May 1, 1897 - June 12, 1923 (26 years, 1 month, 12 days)
- Condemned: June 12, 1923
- Root cause scientifically established: March 9, 1958 (35 years later)
- Ansel exonerated in print: October 1958, Bulletin 471 (33 years after dismissal, 24 years after death)

## ACCOUNTING LEDGER: THE "STATION GAIN"

**Wilbur Teague's invention (1905)**: Created "station gain" line in annual ledger to track discrepancy between butterfat the station credited to patrons (on its own test) vs. butterfat actually received at Larrow Green in cream from that station.

**Typical annual entry** (e.g., 1898):
"Ostrey Hollow: butter fat credited to patrons on station test, 57,760 lb. Butter fat received at Larrow Green in station cream, 60,800 lb. Station gain, 3,040 lb, five and a quarter per cent, as usual. Carried to the general account."

**Pattern**: Gain ran consistently at 5.25% of the fat actually in the cream (or equivalently, 5% of the fat credited).

**Two mathematically correct ways to express the same 5% error**:
1. **76,000 lb is 5% of 1,520,000 lb** (fat actually delivered/true value)
2. **76,000 lb is 5.25% of 1,444,000 lb** (fat credited/recorded value)

Bookkeeper Teague reckoned surplus against figure already entered (method 2). Neither is wrong; each measures against a different denominator. But only the first (5%) connects to the glass.

**Selby Vose's knowledge**: Teague says he reported the discrepancy to manager Vose in 1916 and was told to "let it alone." Vose signed off on annual sheets every year. Both Effie Loomis and Wilbur Teague state Vose knew what the account was doing (signed sheets every spring).

**Discrepancy across 40 years of accounts (1898-1938+)**: The "station gain" line appeared in every annual ledger for 25+ years with the phrase "as usual," indicating it was so consistent and unremarkable that neither the bookkeeper nor the manager flagged it as worth investigation.

## THE ARBITRATION AWARD (May 1925): INTERNAL CONTRADICTION

"The board having heard the patrons and the officers, and having had before it the books and appliances of the station, **finds no cause established.** The board awards to the Ostrey Hollow patrons the sum of **thirty-eight hundred dollars ($3,800.00), without admission,** to be charged to the station account."

**The contradiction**: 
- "Finds no cause established" = board found no evidence of wrongdoing
- "$3,800 without admission" = Association paid to settle anyway without admitting fault
- Both true simultaneously: Award was generous settlement of unproved claim

**Effie Loomis's observation**: "I have written many things into that book with more pleasure. *Finds no cause established*, and then an award of thirty-eight hundred dollars: I took it down as it was given to me and it was not my place to ask how both halves of a sentence could be true at once."

**Crate of measures never mentioned in hearing**: State inspector had condemned pipettes June 12, 1923. Crate set aside. Yet no mention of the defective glass in any of the three hearings (March 17-May 4, 1925). Award's phrase about "books and appliances of the station" is form of words, not a detailed list.

**Effie's conclusion**: "Forty years of taking down what men say has taught me that the interesting part is generally what none of them thought worth saying."

## INTERPRETATION CONFLICT: WATER vs. GLASS

**Selby Vose's 1926 address interpretation** (post-award, to members):
- "Water was going into cans at the head of that valley and it stopped when the Association began to look."
- Points to 1924 rise in tests (3.61 to 3.80, a rise of 0.19) as proof water was deliberately added and removed.
- "Nothing was done to those cows. Nothing was done to that bottom land. Nobody changed their feed and nobody changed their pasture. The tests came up when the looking began."
- Defends 1919 circular and 1925 settlement as necessary because of water fraud.
- Dismisses the state inspector's June 12, 1923 condemnation as routine equipment maintenance: "Apparatus wears... it is the state man's business to say so before they fail him, and ours to replace them."

**Ansel Keddie's 1925 statements**:
- "I have never taken a pound of cream out of that station and there is not a man in this room who thinks I have when he is by himself."
- Careful documentation of testing methodology shows no mention of water
- The nine-week 1911 anomaly with borrowed measure proves the glass, not water, causes variance
- "I have tested as I was taught. I cannot tell you why the figures are what they are." (Professing inability to explain, but implying honesty)

**Ivy Keddie's 1958-1959 scientific evidence**:
- "No milk was watered at Ostrey Hollow and no cream was stolen there. From the first of May, 1897, to the twelfth of June, 1923, the station's tests were low by one part in twenty because its pipettes were short by one part in twenty..."
- Pipette defect explains 1911 anomaly perfectly (same 5% proportional loss when defective measure swapped for correct one)
- Pipette defect explains consistent 0.19% gap across all seasons (wet and dry, winter and summer) as proportional error
- State Agricultural College Bulletin 214 (1911) explains only 0.05% via wet hay theory (insufficient to account for 0.19% gap)
- Alder Corners data confirms systematic shortfall (same milk tested higher elsewhere)

**Wilbur Teague's 1948 testimony**:
- "Cream is portable, and that station was in one pair of hands for twenty-nine seasons."
- System allowed one man to take a little and write down a little less
- "No set of books ever made can prevent it. The remedy is two men or a lock, and the Association had neither."
- Reports defect to Vose 1916, told to let alone
- Doesn't state what person did what day, only says structural vulnerability existed

**Merle Strawn (state inspector) 1907-1936 circuit books**:
- Consistently found scales true, no added water at every visit
- June 12, 1923: discovered and condemned pipettes as not delivering their mark
- October 1923: confirmed new pipettes deliver their mark correctly
- 1924-1925: noted tests "improved" after new glass installed
- Never mentions water contamination in 29 years of visits
- His 1919 entry: "The manager of the Association has sent a circular round to the patrons of this station about water in the cans. I have tested for water at this station on every visit I have made to it and I have never found any, and I told him so."

## ODDITIES & CONFLICTS

1. **Arbitration board contradiction**: Finds "no cause established" yet awards $3,800 "without admission"
2. **1919 circular interpretation**: Selby Vose claims deliberate water addition; state inspector Merle Strawn never detected water in 29 years of visits; Ansel's unsent letter makes no mention of water; Ivy Keddie's science proves glass defect, not water
3. **1911 anomaly timing**: Measure breaks May 8, tests jump to 3.80 immediately; measure returns July 10, tests drop to 3.61 same day. Ansel marks with question mark, gives no explanation, "let it stand 14 years." Later scientific work shows this is the smoking gun for glass defect (borrowed measure was correct; own measure was defective)
4. **Station gain consistency**: Gain runs at exactly 5.25% year after year for 25+ years, so consistent Wilbur Teague writes "as usual" in the entry and stops looking. Selby Vose signs off every year without intervention
5. **Wilbur Teague's report 1916**: Teague reported discrepancy to manager Vose in 1916, was told to let alone. No action taken until 1925 arbitration
6. **Crate not mentioned in arbitration**: State inspector condemned defective glassware June 12, 1923. Hearing March-May 1925, almost 2 years later. Award mentions "books and appliances" but no testimony about the condemned crate. Effie Loomis notes: "The interesting part is generally what none of them thought worth saying."
7. **Article VII wording**: "the test of the station where he delivers shall govern"—no appeal clause. Hinge of Ostrey Hollow problem because it locked stations into their own tests, preventing re-testing or averaging
8. **Loss distribution**: Error proportional, so richest milk hurt most. Farmers with best cows (higher butterfat content) were cheated more than farmers with poor cows. Emmet Brant's milk would have been most affected
9. **Post-exoneration**: Ansel dead 24 years (1934-1958) before Bulletin 471 published. Orra outlived him and carried stigma her whole life ("station is quieter than it was" per Merle Strawn 1928 entry). Both died long before vindication
10. **Investigative committee 1908**: Sampled only January-February-March (winter 3 months). Bulletin 214 (1911) says wet hay depression at most in winter. But glass defect was proportional and consistent year-round, winter and summer, wet year and dry year. The 1908 committee missed the real cause

## SUMMARY OF RESOLUTION

**The case is solved by intersecting five historical documents that were never in one room before 1958**:

1. **Station test book** (kept by Ansel Keddie 1896-1924): Shows anomalous 1911 (measure break), 25-year 3.61% average
2. **Wilbur Teague's ledgers** (1905-1945): Shows "station gain" line at 5.25% for 25+ years
3. **Merle Strawn's circuit notebooks** (1907-1936): June 12, 1923 condemnation, October 1923 confirmation, 1919 note of no water found, 1924 note of improved tests
4. **Ammon & Sons invoice** (April 14, 1897): Documents original order of 6 doz pipettes blown 17.6 cc
5. **The crate itself** (found February 1958, measured March 9, 1958): 9 surviving pipettes measured at mean 16.72 cc (short 0.88 cc, 95 hundredths of mark)

**The evidence chain**:
- Glass ordered 1897 at 17.6 cc specification
- Glass in use May 1, 1897 - June 12, 1923 (26 years, 1 month, 12 days)
- Systematic 5% underreading of all tests during this period (proportional, hit richest milk hardest)
- May 8-July 10, 1911: substitution of borrowed measure (correct spec) causes tests to jump to true value 3.80; return to defective measure causes tests to drop back to false 3.61
- June 12, 1923: condemned as not delivering their mark
- June 28, 1923: new glass ordered
- July 1923: new glass in use
- October 1923: new glass confirmed correct

**The human cost**:
- Ansel Keddie: Dismissed April 30, 1925 (age ~58). Died 1934, still stigmatized.
- Orra Keddie: Never spoke to her brother Emmet from 1919 until both dead. Outlived Ansel. Entire community looked at her "a certain way in church" for rest of her life.
- Emmet Brant: Did not speak to Ansel/Orra for rest of life. Lived within sight of each other's chimneys but "never said good morning."
- Nine families of Ostrey Hollow: Paid $3,800 out of $19,000 actual loss ($15,200 shortfall remains uncompensated)
- Farmers with richest milk (like Emmet Brant's herd): Lost proportionally more
- Selby Vose: Defended and doubled down on interpretation of deliberate water fraud in 1926 address. Never acknowledged possibility of instrument error.
- Wilbur Teague: Reported issue 1916, told to let alone. Carried the knowledge 40 years in silence.
- Association management: Signed off on "station gain" line every year without intervention or investigation.

**The exoneration**:
- Ansel Keddie officially exonerated 33 years after dismissal (1925-1958)
- Exoneration published 24 years after his death (1934-1958)
- Ivy Keddie's scientific and documentary work restored his memory and vindicated his lifetime of careful work
