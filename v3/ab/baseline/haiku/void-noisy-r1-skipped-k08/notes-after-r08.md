# Retention Notes: Retellings 1–8 of the Ostrey Hollow Milk Station Scandal

## OVERVIEW
The Larrow Valley Cooperative Creamery Association operated a milk-testing and skimming station at Ostrey Hollow from 1896–1923. Over 26 years (1898–1922), the station's recorded butterfat test averaged 3.61%, while the actual butterfat delivered to Larrow Green creamery averaged 3.80%—a consistent 5% discrepancy. This gap was caused by defective glass pipettes that delivered only 95% of their marked volume. In 1925, after an arbitration hearing, the Association awarded Ostrey Hollow patrons $3,800 "without admission" despite the board finding "no cause established." The glass defect was not discovered until June 1923 (already after the scandal), and not scientifically proven until March 1958, 33 years after the operator was dismissed and 24 years after his death.

## PEOPLE & RELATIONSHIPS

### Ansel Keddie (c. 1835–1934)
- Tester at Ostrey Hollow station, kept the station's test book in his own hand 1896–1924 (29 seasons)
- Described as honest, methodical, slow and clean in work; "his hand is better than most"
- Married to Orra Keddie; Orra was the sister of Emmet Brant
- Worked continuously at the cold station room ("a low building, whitewashed, cold as a spring house inside even in August")
- On April 30, 1925 was dismissed despite board finding "no cause established"
- Died in 1934, still under the stigma of the accusation
- His grandson was Duncan Keddie, a teacher at Ashlin
- His granddaughter Ivy Keddie (Duncan's daughter) found the condemned crate in 1958 and proved the glass defect

### Orra Keddie (née Vose)
- Wife of Ansel Keddie
- First cousin of old Mrs. Vose (old Gideon Vose's wife)
- Sister of Emmet Brant (largest patron of the station)
- Before 1919: Emmet and Ansel's families ate Sunday dinners together, two houses in one kitchen
- After 1919 (the water accusation): Orra and Emmet never spoke again—"Not one word, not at the store, not at a funeral"
- Orra would walk down to Larrow Green rather than pass her brother's lane
- Outlived Ansel by years, endured the stigma the whole time

### Emmet Brant
- Largest milk patron at Ostrey Hollow, brought more milk than anybody
- Farmer on Hessel Bottom (wet meadow)
- Orra Keddie's brother
- The scandal cost him the most; his milk was always tested by Ansel
- After 1919 circular: ceased all speech with Ansel and Orra for decades
- Died before Ivy Keddie's 1958 investigation
- Left behind the weigh book kept by Ansel Keddie, later inherited by his son Roy Brant

### Roy Brant
- Emmet Brant's son
- Kept the farm weigh book after his father's death, inherited it in 1929
- Lent the weigh book to Ivy Keddie in 1958; returned it
- Later it went to Hazel Brant, received at museum in 1963

### Hazel Brant
- Received by Roy Brant; her connection unclear but likely a descendant
- Cousin of Ivy Keddie: "Hazel Brant's first cousin once removed" (so connected both sides)
- Came to interview Vira Toland in 1958, a young woman from college away east

### Gideon Vose
- First president of the Association 1889–1911 (22 years, longest tenure)
- Drafted the by-laws himself (not a lawyer, "read as a farmer wrote them")
- Died in 1917, before the 1919 scandal; could not be asked about Article VII after his death
- Started the whole Association
- Father of Selby Vose

### Selby Vose (son of Gideon)
- Manager of the Association 1913–1938
- Nephew of old Gideon Vose; family "always thought the valley belonged to them a little"
- Signed the 1919 circular accusing Ostrey Hollow of water in cans
- Signed the annual station sheets from 1913 onward, seeing the station gain year by year
- At the 1926 annual meeting, defended the board's decision, supported the $3,800 payment
- Blamed Ostrey Hollow patrons for watering milk; claimed tests rose 21 hundredths in 1924 when "the Association's attention came to rest on the station"
- Argued apparatus replacement in July 1923 was routine housekeeping
- Stated his own Jersey herd tested 4.56 in 1910 to prove the valley could produce good milk
- Left office 1938 when moved from Ashlin

### Effie Loomis
- Secretary of the Association 1901–1940 (40 years)
- Kept the minute book in her own hand
- Did not visit Ostrey Hollow station during her tenure
- Noted that the crate of condemned measures (June 1923) was never mentioned in the 1925 hearing, though the board's award mentioned "the books and appliances of the station"
- Wrote "Notes toward a history" in 1941, one year after stepping down
- Was at the desk 40 years; had it quoted at her about Article VII

### Wilbur Teague
- Bookkeeper 1905–1945 (40 years)
- At the office in Ashlin (over a harness shop)
- Created the "station gain" account when he started in 1905
- Kept meticulous ledgers; "a column that would not prove" was his standard
- Reported to Selby Vose in 1916 that the station gain was troubling; was told to let it alone
- Not silent, but silenced by his employer
- Testified to the board of arbitration in 1925 that "cream is portable" and one man did everything, so theft was inevitable
- Emphasized that Selby Vose signed the sheets every year and "a man who signs a sheet has had the sheet in front of him"

### Merle Strawn
- State dairy inspector for the Ordell circuit 1907–1936
- Visited Ostrey Hollow on every circuit for 29 years (1907, 1909, 1911, 1912, 1915, 1916, 1919–1920, 1922–1936)
- Before receiving a burette (1921): tested scales, lactometer, boiler, cold room, and watched tests
- June 12, 1923: ran the station's pipettes against a burette and found they "will not deliver their mark"
- Condemned the entire crate and ordered new glass
- October 1923: verified new pipettes deliver their mark
- 1919: had told the Association manager that he tested for water at every visit and never found any (contradicting the circular)
- Testified that milk was "whole" (not watered) at Ostrey Hollow in 1909, 1919, 1920

## THE ASSOCIATION: STRUCTURE & PEOPLE

### Founding & Organization
- **Formed:** 1889 at Larrow Green
- **Officers:** 
  - Gideon Vose: president 1889–1911
  - Selby Vose (his son): manager 1913–1938
  - Effie Loomis: secretary 1901–1940
  - Wilbur Teague: bookkeeper 1905–1945
  - Hobart Sill: buttermaker at Larrow Green

### Main Creamery
- Built at Larrow Green in 1891 on Larrow Creek (same building still standing as of 1941)
- Before creamery: farmers sold milk where they could get a price, no common test or price, every man's arithmetic his own

### Ostrey Hollow Skimming Station
- Seven miles up the valley from Larrow Green
- In a low building, whitewashed, cold as a spring house inside even in August
- Smell: milk, steam, hot iron; could hear the engine before the last turn
- Opened before 1896 (by May 4, 1896 Ansel borrowed glass from Larrow Green)
- Took milk from seven farms with rights on Hessel Bottom (wet meadow) plus three farms on the hill
- Separated milk, sent cream down the valley to Larrow Green, returned skim to patrons for hogs and calves
- Closed sometime after 1936 (by 1959 used for storage of fence wire and salt)

### Article VII (Key By-Law)
- Adopted at the annual meeting of 1892 (year after creamery opened)
- Drafted by Gideon Vose
- Text: "Every patron shall be paid for the milk he delivers according to the number of pounds of butter fat contained in it, ascertained by test, and not according to the weight of the milk; and the test of the station where he delivers shall govern."
- Hinge of the whole matter: "the test of the station where he delivers shall govern" — no appeal from the station's test
- By-laws printed in pamphlet form with gray wrappers; no year printed on face, only "adopted at the annual meeting"

## THE SCANDAL TIMELINE

### 1889: Association Founded
- Gideon Vose elected first president
- Nine families hauling milk to the new system

### 1891: Larrow Green Creamery Opens
- Low, whitewashed building, cold year-round
- Remedy for plain grievance: before it, "every man's arithmetic was his own"

### 1892: Article VII Adopted
- Gideon Vose drafts the by-laws
- Article VII: payment by butterfat test, "test of the station where he delivers shall govern"

### 1896: Ostrey Hollow Station Opens
- May 1, 1896: Ansel Keddie begins, using borrowed glass from Larrow Green
- Glass borrowed from Ashlin creamery until own outfit arrived spring following
- Ansel keeps test book in his own hand from day one
- First leaf: "Ostrey Hollow station. Begun this 4th day of May, 1896. Glass borrowed of Larrow Green until our own comes. A. Keddie."

### 1897: June 12 — Equipment Purchased
- Ammon & Sons invoice, April 14, 1897
- 6 dozen milk test pipettes, 17.6 c.c. — $25.20
- 12 dozen test bottles, 8% neck — $31.68
- 1 acid measure — $0.90
- 1 hand tester, 4 bottle — $18.00
- Freight — $2.15
- **Total — $77.93**
- (Later found: these pipettes were defective, delivering 16.72 c.c. instead of 17.6 c.c., or 95% of their mark)

### 1898–1922: The 26-Year Discrepancy
- **Station's recorded average test:** 3.61% (or "within three hundredths of it every year")
- **True average (as calculated later):** 3.80%
- **Gap:** 19 hundredths of a point = 5% of the true fat
- **Total milk received (1898–1922, 25 full years):** 40–44 million pounds (sources differ: Teague says 44 million, Strawn says 40 million over years, Loomis says 40 million)
- **Total fat discrepancy:** 76,000 pounds of butterfat
- **Value at 25 cents/pound average:** ~$19,000
- **What was credited to Ostrey Hollow patrons:** This fat was listed in Association ledgers as "station gain"
- **The station gain entry (typical for 1898):**
  > "Ostrey Hollow: butter fat credited to patrons on station test, 57,760 lb. Butter fat received at Larrow Green in station cream, 60,800 lb. Station gain, 3,040 lb, five and a quarter per cent, as usual. Carried to the general account."
- Each year ran at 5.25% gain of the fat actually delivered (or 5% of the credited fat)
- Gain was so consistent ("as usual") that Wilbur Teague stopped looking at it—it became "furniture"
- Larrow Green creamery's own tests ran nearly 0.2 point better than Ostrey Hollow's (16 showed Larrow Green average ran under 0.2 higher)

### 1902: First Complaints
- Complaints began that Ostrey Hollow milk "did not test with the rest"
- Pattern was visible from the start but ascribed to conditions, not measurement

### 1908: Committee Investigation
- Committee came up and sampled Hessel Bottom herds through winter
- "We have sampled the herds of Hessel Bottom through January, February and March. The milk is whole and the scales are honest. It is our opinion that the wet hay of that bottom is what thins the cream, and that the remedy lies with the land and not with the station."
- Sampling was January–March 1908 only

### 1911: The Nine-Week Anomaly
- May 8, 1911: The station's measure (pipette) broke
- Ansel borrowed a substitute from Larrow Green
- During those nine weeks (May 8 – July 10): tests rose to 3.80 (the true level)
- July 10: Ansel's own measure returned to use
- Tests dropped back to 3.61 the same day
- Ansel recorded this in his book with a question mark:
  > "May 8. Pipette broke this morning. Using a borrowed measure."
  > "July 10. Our own measure in use again from today. Tests are back where they were. ? — A.K."
- Ansel said: "That question mark is mine and I have let it stand for fourteen years. I could not answer it then and I cannot answer it now, and I will not put anything in its place that I do not know."
- This was the smoking gun, though nobody realized it: the borrowed measure was accurate, Ansel's regular measure (also likely defective in the same way as the original crate) was not

### 1911: State Agricultural College Tests Hay
- Bulletin No. 214 issued
- "Bog hay fed as the whole of the roughage depressed the butter fat test by not more than five hundredths of one per cent in winter, and by nothing that could be measured on summer pasture."
- This disproved the wet hay theory: max 5 hundredths in winter (against an actual gap of 19 hundredths), nothing in summer (gap was same year-round)

### 1913: New Manager Takes Office
- Selby Vose becomes manager
- "A new manager at the Association this year and he came out with me [Merle Strawn] to the platform, which the last one never did."

### 1915: Wash Room Issue
- Wash room floor at Ostrey Hollow holding water at the drain
- Merle Strawn ordered it graded

### 1916: Wilbur Teague Raises Alarm with Selby Vose
- Wilbur Teague, bookkeeper, brought the station gain issue to Selby Vose's attention
- Was told to "let it alone"
- Teague let it alone after that (not a second time)
- Teague's understanding: "I was the Association's bookkeeper and not its conscience, and a bookkeeper who takes his employer's business up a second time after being told once has generally begun to look for another employer."

### 1919: The Circular and the Water Accusation
- Letter sent from the Association to nine families up the Hollow saying there was "water in the cans"
- **Signed by:** Selby Vose, manager, over the Association's name ("over the manager's signature")
- **Trigger:** Selby Vose saw figures going one way for twenty years and felt obligated to warn
- **Reaction:** Nine families received it; kept the letter for decades as proof of what was said of them
  - Vira Toland's father kept it in his desk drawer (where her brother likely had it as of 1941)
  - Orra Keddie walked down to Larrow Green rather than pass her brother Emmet's lane after this
  - Emmet Brant (largest patron, whose milk Ansel tested himself) and the Keddies stopped speaking
  - Not one word between them for decades, not at store, not at funeral
  - Both lived within sight of each other's chimney smoke but never said good morning
- **Note:** Merle Strawn, state inspector, had tested for water at Ostrey Hollow "on every visit I have made to it" and "never found any" — he told the manager so in 1919
- **Argument in 1926 address:** Selby Vose blamed the patrons for actually watering, not the measurement system; claimed they watered because it was "a way of making a hard year come out even"

### 1921: Burette Program Begins
- State begins issuing burettes to field men for checking glass delivery
- Merle Strawn receives his with printed directions

### 1922: Ansel Keddie Prepares Summary Leaf
- At the end of his test book (in 1922), Ansel prepared a summary of annual averages:
  > "Average test of all patrons, this station: 1896, 3.80. 1897, part year. 1898, 3.61. And 3.61 or within three hundredths of it every year since, save the nine weeks of 1911 that are noted in their place, when it stood at 3.80."
- The 1896 average was 3.80 because 1896 was a partial year (May 4 onward) and used a different measure (borrowed from Larrow Green, which was accurate)
- Once the defective Ammon & Sons glass arrived in May 1897, the average dropped to 3.61 and stayed there

### 1922: Merle Strawn Does Not Run Burette Check
- October 1922: Merle Strawn was supposed to check the glass against his burette
- He was short on time and the road home was bad
- He did NOT run their glass against the burette
- Noted this in his book: "I did not run their glass against the burette this year, the day being short and the road home being what it was, and I have put that down because it is what happened."

### June 12, 1923: The Condemned Glass
- Merle Strawn finally ran the station's milk pipettes against the burette
- Entry: "These measures will not deliver their mark. Condemned the lot, the whole crate of them, and told the manager to order new. Glass is glass and I did not weigh what it has cost them."
- The entire crate was condemned
- Manager ordered replacement the same month (order placed June 28, 1923)

### July 2, 1923: Replacement Order Placed
- Ammon & Sons wrote acknowledging the Association's order of June 28 for replacement pipettes and bottles
- Promised shipment that month
- Declined to say anything about the earlier lot

### July 1923: New Glass Arrives and Used
- New glass arrived and was in use by the end of the summer (flush season)

### October 1923: New Glass Verified
- Merle Strawn returned and ran the new pipettes against the burette
- They deliver their mark
- "New glass in use since July. Ran the new pipettes against the burette. They deliver their mark."

### 1924: Tests Rise
- The station's average test for 1924 jumped to 3.80 (or 3.90 in Selby Vose's address; Merle Strawn's notes say 3.80)
- This was a rise of 19–29 hundredths from the 3.61 baseline
- Selby Vose claimed this proved water had stopped when "the Association's attention came to rest on the station"
- Actually, it proved the glass had been replaced
- "Same patrons, same herds, same pasture, same season, same acid, same bottles, same man at the crank — one object changed" (Ivy Keddie)

### 1924: Ansel Tested No More
- Ansel tested 1896–1924
- Ansel did not test in 1925 (new tester came)

### January 1925: Ostrey Hollow Patrons Petition
- Winter (when farmers have time to be angry)
- Patrons petition the Association
- Complaint: "their milk had never tested as their neighbors' milk tested and that they had been paid accordingly for a very long time, and they asked to be made whole"
- Request: compensation for 25 years of underpayment

### March 17 – May 4, 1925: Board of Arbitration Hearing
- **Location:** Ashlin
- **Chairman:** Alonzo Frick, attorney (described as "fair" and "impartial")
- **Participants:** Lawyers, Association officers, Ostrey Hollow patrons, Ansel Keddie's statement, various witnesses
- **Ansel's statement (March 17, 1925):**
  - "I have kept the test book of this station in my own hand since the station opened, and there is not a figure in it that I did not put there."
  - "I have heard what the patrons say of me and I have heard what the officers say of me. Neither of them has come up that road on a working morning and stood at my elbow and watched the work done."
  - "I have tested as I was taught and as the state man saw me do it, thirty-one times a week for twenty-nine seasons, and I cannot tell you why the figures are what they are."
  - "I have never taken a pound of cream out of that station and there is not a man in this room who thinks I have when he is by himself." (Implying everyone knows he didn't, but the claim suits people's theories)
- **Effie Loomis noted:** "Not a word of it [the crate] was spoken in any of the three sessions, by the patrons, by the officers, or by the board, and the award's phrase about 'the books and appliances of the station' is a form of words the board used and not a list of anything. Forty years of taking down what men say has taught me that the interesting part is generally what none of them thought worth saying."
- The crate had been condemned June 12, 1923 — nine months before the hearing — but was never mentioned by anyone

### May 4, 1925: Board's Award
- **Finding:** "The board having heard the patrons and the officers, and having had before it the books and appliances of the station, finds no cause established."
- **Award:** "The board awards to the Ostrey Hollow patrons the sum of thirty-eight hundred dollars ($3,800.00), without admission, to be charged to the station account."
- **Meaning:** Found no wrongdoing but awarded money anyway
- **Effie Loomis's reaction:** "I took it down as it was given to me and it was not my place to ask how both halves of a sentence could be true at once."
- **Split:** $3,800 split nine ways over 25 years; "my father's share of it did not buy a hay rake" (Vira Toland)
- **Value compared to true loss:** $3,800 = one-fifth of the estimated $19,000 loss

### April 30, 1925: Ansel Dismissed
- On the very last day of the month, after "no cause established" ruling, Ansel Keddie was put out
- "I was put out on the thirtieth of April. They found no cause established and they put me out on the last day of the month, and both of those things are true at once, and I have given up trying to hold them in my head together."

### 1925: New Tester at Station
- Merle Strawn's 1925 entry: "New man at the tester's bench this year. He is quick, and quick is not the thing I look for."
- Ansel's notes: "I did not test a pound in the season of 1925."

### 1926: Selby Vose's Annual Address
- Manager Selby Vose addresses the Association membership
- Defends the $3,800 payment as "generous settlement of an unproved claim"
- Blames Ostrey Hollow patrons for watering milk to "make a hard year come out even"
- Attributes the 1924 test rise to the patrons stopping when "the Association's attention came to rest on the station"
- Defends the circular of 1919 as necessary warning
- Dismisses the glass condemnation as routine housekeeping
- "I do not know a name and I would not use it if I did" (about who watered)
- Emphasizes his Jersey herd's 4.56 test in 1910 as proof the valley could produce good milk

### 1926: Burette Program Discontinued
- State discontinues the burette program
- Merle Strawn retains his burette (in its case in his grip, used twice since)

### 1934: Ansel Keddie Dies
- Ansel dies in 1934, under the stigma of the accusation
- Has been dead 24 years when the truth is proved in 1958

### 1936: Merle Strawn Retires
- Merle Strawn retires after 29 years on the Ordell circuit
- Gives his 28 circuit notebooks to the state dairy office (required by rules)

### 1938: Office Move
- Association office moved from Ashlin
- The 1889–1892 minute book was lost during this move
- "It was not thrown out; it was carried out with the rest and it did not arrive, and I have looked in every place a book can be in this building" (Effie Loomis)
- Selby Vose left office this year (1938)

### 1941: Effie Loomis Writes "Notes toward a History"
- Three years after laying down the minute book
- Reflects on 40 years as secretary

### 1945: Wilbur Teague Retires
- Ends 40 years as bookkeeper

### 1947: Circuit Notebooks Arrive at State Agricultural College
- Merle Strawn's 28 circuit notebooks sent to State Ag College library at Farland
- (Finding of the notebooks in 1947)

### 1948: Wilbur Teague Writes Memoir
- Writes his chapter of "The Books of the Association" in winter of 1948, three years after retiring

### 1958: Condemnation Discovered & Measured
- February 1958: Ivy Keddie finds the crate in the loft of the closed station building under a tarpaulin
  - "Carried up there in June of 1923 and it was there still"
  - Stenciled "AMMON & SONS DAIRY SUPPLY TARNET"
  - Nine unbroken pipettes survive
- March 9, 1958: Ivy Keddie measures the nine pipettes at Farland against a calibrated burette
  - Stems blown 17.6 c.c. (as marked)
  - Mean delivery: 16.72 c.c. (as measured)
  - Short: 0.88 c.c.
  - Percentage: 95 hundredths of the mark (16.72 ÷ 17.6 = 0.95)
  - Every test made with this glass read 95% of the truth
- Merle Strawn's 1923 circuit notebook found at Farland (was there since 1947)
- Alder Corners Cheese Company test figures copied from their book (1919 season)
- Ivy Keddie also borrows the Brant weigh book from Roy Brant (later returned)

### October 1958: Bulletin No. 471
- Issued by State Agricultural College with Ivy Keddie and Professor Vaught
- Closing paragraph:
  > "No milk was watered at Ostrey Hollow and no cream was stolen there. From the first of May, 1897, to the twelfth of June, 1923, the station's tests were low by one part in twenty because its pipettes were short by one part in twenty, and the difference — seventy-six thousand pounds of butter fat — stands credited in the Association's own ledgers, year by year, as station gain."

### 1959: Material Deposited at Museum
- Ivy Keddie presents the crate and her findings to the Ordell County Historical Society, Ashlin (1959)
- Crate given to Ordell County Historical Rooms
- Station test book received at museum in 1959 (impounded 1925, in Association office 1925–1959)
- Measurement sheet filed

### 1972: Halbert Nym Prepares Catalogue
- Curator Halbert Nym writes accession notes for 16 items in the Ordell County dairy collection
- Comprehensive catalogue of all evidence

## KEY DOCUMENTS & EVIDENCE

### Item 1: By-Laws Pamphlet
- Printed pamphlet, gray wrappers
- Contains Article VII in full
- No year printed on face, only "adopted at the annual meeting"

### Item 2: Ammon & Sons Invoice (April 14, 1897)
- Original document
- Lists 6 dozen pipettes at $25.20, 12 dozen bottles at $31.68, 1 acid measure, 1 hand tester, freight
- Total: $77.93
- Date: April 14, 1897
- Use: From May 1, 1897 onward

### Item 3: Station Test Book (Ansel Keddie's)
- Half-bound, marbled boards, back mended with cloth
- Kept from May 4, 1896 through 1924 (29 seasons)
- One hand throughout (Ansel Keddie)
- Columns: date, patron, pounds, test
- First leaf: "Ostrey Hollow station. Begun this 4th day of May, 1896. Glass borrowed of Larrow Green until our own comes. A. Keddie."
- Summary leaf (made 1922): Annual averages 3.80 (1896), 3.61 (1898+), except 3.80 for nine weeks of 1911
- Provenance: At station 1896; impounded by board of arbitration 1925; Association office 1925–1959; museum 1959

### Item 4: Farm Weigh Book (Brant/Keddie)
- Kept from January 1, 1897 onward
- Small quarto, sheepskin, covers greasy at corners
- Daily entries of weights delivered and test paid
- Example (1910): "Delivered to the station, two hundred thousand pounds even, by my own scale and theirs agreeing. Paid on a test of 3.42."
- Provenance: Hessel Bottom 1897 onward; Roy Brant 1929; lent to Ivy Keddie 1958 (returned); Hazel Brant 1962; museum 1963

### Item 5: Condemned Crate with Pipettes
- Deal construction, stenciled "AMMON & SONS DAIRY SUPPLY TARNET"
- Much stained
- Nine surviving pipettes, each blown on stem at 17.6 c.c.
- Two chipped at tip
- Found in Association office loft February 1958
- Condemned by state inspector June 12, 1923
- Measured March 9, 1958 by Ivy Keddie
- Mean delivery of nine: 16.72 c.c. (short 0.88 c.c., or 95% of mark)
- Museum 1959

### Item 6: Annual Ledgers (Station Account)
- Canvas over boards

- Station intake 1898–1922: 40–44 million pounds milk
- Station gain accumulated: 76,000 pounds butterfat (worth $19,000 at average 25 cents/pound)
- 5.25% of credited fat = 5% of delivered fat

- One leaf, firm's head

## CRITICAL MATHEMATICAL & LOGICAL FACTS

### The Glass Error Calculation
- Pipettes blown for 17.6 c.c.
- Measured deliveries: 16.72 c.c. (mean of nine pipettes)
- Short amount: 0.88 c.c.
- **Percentage:** 16.72 ÷ 17.6 = 0.95 = 95 hundredths
- Every test read 95% of the truth
- For a charge that should be 17.6 c.c., delivering 16.72 c.c. means the neck reading will be 5% lower

### Effect on Reported vs. Actual Fat
- Recorded average: 3.61%
- True average: 3.61% ÷ 0.95 = 3.80%
- Gap: 19 hundredths of a point
- 19 hundredths = 5% of 3.80

### Loss Calculation (1898–1922, 25 years)
- Milk received: 40–44 million pounds (sources: Loomis says 40M, Teague says 44M; likely 40M was used in final calculations)
- Recorded fat at 3.61%: 1,444,000 pounds butterfat
- True fat at 3.80%: 1,520,000 pounds butterfat
- Difference: 76,000 pounds butterfat
- Price average: 25 cents per pound
- Total loss: 76,000 × $0.25 = **$19,000**
- Award: $3,800 = one-fifth of loss

### The Five Percent Rule
- As Ivy Keddie noted: "Two percentages have been quoted at me and both are right, so long as you say what they are percentages of."
- 76,000 pounds is 5% of the fat actually delivered (1,520,000 pounds)
- 76,000 pounds is 5.25% of the fat credited (1,444,000 pounds)
- The Association's ledger line used the second, because "a bookkeeper naturally reckons a surplus against the figure he has already entered"
- "But five percent is the one that is also the glass" (referring to the 95% delivery rate)

### Progressive Loss by Herd Quality
- Error fell hardest on best milk
- A herd recorded at 4.56 was short 0.24 of a point
- A herd recorded at 3.42 was short 0.18 of a point
- Rich milk = higher apparent loss
- "no man had any way of knowing that his good cows cost him more than his poor ones"

### The 1911 Nine-Week Proof
- Same patrons, same herds, same pasture, same season, same acid, same bottles, same man (Ansel) at crank
- Only difference: borrowed measure (accurate) vs. station's own measure (defective)
- Result: tests went to 3.80 (true) and back to 3.61 (false) when original returned
- "No explanation of this valley's twenty-six-year anomaly survives those nine weeks except the one I have given you" (Ivy Keddie)

### Why the Wet Hay Theory Fails
- Bulletin No. 214 (1911): bog hay depresses test max 5 hundredths in winter, nothing in summer
- Actual gap: 19 hundredths flat every month of every year
- "It is four times too small and it is the wrong shape"
- Alder Corners Cheese Company records: same Hessel Bottom herds' milk tested 19 hundredths higher when processed there (1919 season, summer flush)

## UNANSWERED & UNEXAMINED QUESTIONS

1. **Why did Selby Vose sign the sheets every year without investigating the station gain?**
   - Wilbur Teague: "Mr. Vose knew what that account was doing. He signed the sheet every year. I laid the annual sheet before him each spring and he put his name at the foot of it, and a man who signs a sheet has had the sheet in front of him."

2. **Why was Merle Strawn not scheduled to check the glass in 1922?**
   - His notes say the day was short and the road home was bad, so he didn't run the burette check
   - If he had, the problem would have been discovered a year earlier

3. **Why was the condemned crate never mentioned in the arbitration hearing?**
   - Effie Loomis: "The crate was never mentioned in the hearing at all... the award's phrase about 'the books and appliances of the station' is a form of words the board used and not a list of anything."
   - The crate had been condemned June 12, 1923; the hearing was March–May 1925
   - Either the board didn't look for it, or no one brought it up

4. **Did Ansel Keddie know the glass was defective?**
   - His 1911 question mark suggests he suspected something was wrong with the measure
   - But he accepted the defect as part of the mystery and never investigated further
   - He was "an honest man who had got into something" (Vira's father's phrase)

5. **Did Ansel water milk?**
   - Merle Strawn tested for water on every visit and never found any
   - Ansel's own statement: "I have never taken a pound of cream out of that station and there is not a man in this room who thinks I have when he is by himself."
   - Effie Loomis: "I am not going to dress it up for you. A man stands in a room by himself with the separator running and the door shut and nobody in the valley can add, and after a while he helps himself, and it is not that he is a wicked man, it is that nobody ever came up that road and looked."
   - The board found "no cause established," but the award "without admission" suggests doubt
   - Bulletin No. 471: "No milk was watered at Ostrey Hollow and no cream was stolen there."

6. **What happened to the original crate of glass from 1897?**
   - Item 2 invoice shows it was delivered in 1897
   - Ivy found nine unbroken pipettes in the crate, stenciled Ammon & Sons
   - The crate was condemned June 12, 1923, but left in the station loft for 35 years
   - Why wasn't it discarded or returned?

7. **Who exactly watered milk (if anyone)?**
   - Selby Vose: "I do not know a name and I would not use it if I did"
   - No name was ever established
   - The "looking" of 1924 might have stopped it, but it might never have happened

## NARRATIVE CONTINUITY & CONTRADICTIONS

### The "No Cause" Paradox
- Board finding: "finds no cause established"
- Board award: $3,800
- Wilbur Teague's witness: pointed out the system was designed for embezzlement (one man did everything)
- Selby Vose's 1926 defense: water WAS going in, patrons WERE watering, tests rose when "the looking began"
- Actual explanation (discovered 1958): glass was defective
- Resolution: No cream was stolen, no milk was watered; the test error explained everything

### The Delayed Truth
- Ansel dismissed April 30, 1925 (after finding "no cause")
- Glass condemned June 12, 1923 (nine months before hearing)
- But condemnation never mentioned in hearing
- Ansel died 1934, under stigma
- Truth proved October 1958, 33 years after dismissal, 24 years after death

### Station Gain Consistency
- Ran at 5.25% year after year ("as usual")
- Consistency made it invisible (it became "furniture")
- But consistency proved it was systematic measurement error, not occasional theft

### The Role of Each Narrator
1. **Vira Toland (1912–?):** Personal memory of 1919 letter, its emotional impact, family silence
2. **Effie Loomis:** Secretary's formal records and omissions; the crate was never mentioned
3. **Ansel Keddie:** His own defense and the question mark he couldn't answer
4. **Ivy Keddie:** The investigator who found the crate and measured it, proving the truth
5. **Wilbur Teague:** Bookkeeper who created and tracked the gain, testified to the board's risk
6. **Merle Strawn:** Inspector who condemned the glass and verified the new glass
7. **Halbert Nym:** Curator who organized all the evidence into a coherent record
8. **Selby Vose:** Manager who sent the 1919 circular and defended the 1925 outcome, blamed the patrons

## PLACES & GEOGRAPHY

- **Larrow Valley:** A rural valley in Ordell County
- **Larrow Green:** Town 7 miles down the valley; location of main creamery and Association office (Ashlin)
- **Ashlin:** County seat, where the office was located over a harness shop; location of arbitration hearing (1925)
- **Ostrey Hollow:** Head of the valley, 7 miles up from Larrow Green; location of skimming station at "the very top of the Hollow where the road bends and then goes to nothing but the ridge"
- **Hessel Bottom:** Wet meadow below the Hollow hamlet; seven farms had rights there; Emmet Brant's farm was the largest; Alder Corners Factory was over Redlow Ridge in Vessey County
- **State Agricultural College at Farland:** Where Ivy Keddie studied and where the circuit notebooks were housed from 1947

## LASTING DAMAGE & INCOMPLETE RECONCILIATION

- Ansel and Orra died under stigma, never exonerated in their lifetimes
- Emmet Brant and Orra were estranged from 1919 onward for decades
- Vira Toland: "That is the part that sits badly with me now that I am old. Whatever he did or did not do, he had all those years of this valley looking at him a certain way in church, and Orra had it too, and she outlived him a good while and had it the whole time."
- The 1919 letter was kept in drawers for decades as proof of shame
- The scandal poisoned the valley's reputation ("the Larrow Valley is poor milk country")
- In 1926, Selby Vose had to defend the valley's ability to produce good milk
