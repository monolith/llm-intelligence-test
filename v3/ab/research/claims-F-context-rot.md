# Testable claims extracted from F1–F10 (context-rot research)

Source: [`2026-07-17-context-rot-research.md`](/home/anatoly/context-governance-plugin/research/2026-07-17-context-rot-research.md) (findings F1–F10, 25 adversarially verified claims, 0 refuted).
Exact numbers pulled where the report was vague from [`2026-07-17-source-claims-appendix.md`](/home/anatoly/context-governance-plugin/research/2026-07-17-source-claims-appendix.md).
Plugin mechanisms quoted from the "What it does" table in [`README.md`](/home/anatoly/context-governance-plugin/README.md).

Harness assumed throughout: headless `claude -p` sessions with Read/Write; sessions cuttable at chosen points or allowed to compact naturally; plugin loaded or omitted per arm; scoring by hidden tests or an answer key; models haiku / sonnet / opus; **5 runs per cell**.

**Standing note on n.** Five runs per cell is small for run-level scoring. Where a finding's literature effect is a percentage-point gap in accuracy, the practical move is to plant many scorable items per run (15–25 answer-key facts, or 15–25 hidden tests) so the unit of analysis is the item, with run as a random effect. Item-level n per arm = 5 runs × items × 3 models. Run-level n stays 5 per cell, 15 pooled across models.

## F1 — The seed article's citations are real and accurately characterized

- **Claim.** Every load-bearing citation in the seed article ("Governed Context: Managing Context Rot in Claude Code") survived 3-vote adversarial verification against primary sources: intrinsic rot / attention-budget limits and pre-limit gradual degradation (NoLiMa, Adobe Research + LMU, ICML 2025), U-shaped position bias (Liu et al., "Lost in the Middle", TACL 2024), single-distractor harm (Chroma context-rot 2025, direction corroborated by Shi et al., ICML 2023), and multi-turn early commitment (Laban et al., "LLMs Get Lost in Multi-Turn Conversation", ICLR 2026 best paper). One claim — (e), that models rarely flag their own degradation — was covered by no surviving verified claim and stays UNVERIFIED. Coverage is 2023–mid-2025 models plus the Claude 4 / GPT-4.1 / o3 generation.
- **Confidence.** CONFIRMED · **high** · 3-0 unanimous. Claim (e) within it: **UNVERIFIED**.
- **Plugin mechanism it motivates.** None — background. It is a provenance audit of the design basis, not a behavioral claim. Its one live constraint is negative: "do not build self-diagnosis features that depend on claim (e) without separate evidence," which is why the README hands the drift check to a human — `/summary` "Shows both what would be handed over (the ledger) and what the session is thinking right now — your window to catch drift," evidenced as "G13: the model can't reliably self-report degradation, so you check."
- **Testable prediction if the plugin works.** Not directly testable against the plugin. F1 asserts nothing about session behavior — it says the bibliography is sound. The only derivative that is testable belongs to claim (e) and is a *negative* prediction the plugin already assumes: on any of the substrates below, a model asked mid-session "is your context degraded?" will show near-zero correlation between its self-report and its measured accuracy drop, so an arm gated on self-report will not outperform an arm gated on external fill measurement. That tests the model, not the plugin.
- **Substrate and measure.** If run anyway as a manipulation check: any long non-lexical retrieval task (below), with a periodic self-assessment probe injected. Manipulate fill; count the correlation between self-reported degradation and answer-key accuracy. Effect size in the literature: **unknown** — the report explicitly has none, since claim (e) was never verified in either direction.
- **Feasibility on a headless harness.** Easy but low value — inject the probe as a scripted turn in a `claude -p` session and log the answer alongside the accuracy score; costs a full arm's budget to test something the plugin does not do.
- **Caveats the report states.** Claim (e) remains untested in either direction. Several seed citations (Levy arXiv:2402.14848, Shi arXiv:2302.00093, GSM-Symbolic, tool-space confusion arXiv:2505.10570, Breunig, Letta Recovery-Bench) appear only as corroboration inside verifier notes, not as independently verified claims. Verifiers added a qualification that the U-shape attenuates or reshapes in 2025–26 frontier models.

## F2 — Degradation begins far below the advertised limit; effective context is typically ≤ half of claimed

- **Claim.** NoLiMa (ICML 2025): 11 of 13 models claiming ≥128K fell below 50% of their sub-1K baseline at just 32K tokens; effective lengths (last length holding ≥85% of base score) were GPT-4o 8K of 128K claimed, Claude 3.5 Sonnet 4K of 200K, Llama 3.1 405B 2K of 128K; GPT-4o 99.3→69.7 and Claude 3.5 Sonnet 87.6→29.8 at 32K. RULER (COLM 2024, NVIDIA): GPT-4 128K→64K effective, Yi-34B 200K→32K, Mixtral-8x22B 64K→32K, only half of 17 models satisfactory at 32K. Decline is monotonic from 1–4K on — a slope, not a cliff. NoLiMa repo updates through 2025 (GPT-4.1, Llama 4, Gemini 2.5) still show <1K–4K effective on this non-lexical task.
- **Confidence.** CONFIRMED · **high** · 3-0 (6 merged claims).
- **Plugin mechanism it motivates.** The fill monitor and its budget basis — README: "UserPromptSubmit hook … injects a fill advisory when a tier (30/50/75% of budget) is newly crossed — once per tier, no nagging"; and the `window_tokens` / `model_windows` config that decides what the percentage is measured against. Report's implication: "govern by conservative effective-context budgets, not the advertised window … warn at tens-of-K tokens, far before Claude Code's late auto-compact trigger."
- **Testable prediction if the plugin works.** On a non-lexical multi-file synthesis task where 20 answer-key facts are planted across a corpus large enough to push the session past 50% of a 200K budget, the plugin-loaded arm (advisories at 30/50/75%, plus `/handoff` available) will recover **more** planted facts than the no-plugin arm, and the gap will widen with fill tier reached: no difference below the 30% tier, a directional gap at 50%, the largest gap at 75%. Falsified if the gap is flat or reversed across tiers.
- **Substrate and measure.** Substrate: a reading/synthesis task over a planted corpus — 30–60 files, 20 answer-key facts, questions worded to share minimal vocabulary with the source sentences (the NoLiMa regime, per F7). Manipulate: corpus size (so peak fill lands at ~25%, ~55%, ~80% of budget) × plugin on/off. Count: fraction of the 20 planted facts correctly reported; secondarily, peak fill reached and whether the session pruned or handed off. Minimum n: the literature gap here is large (Claude 3.5 Sonnet 87.6→29.8 at 32K), so a run-level d well above 1 — 5 runs per cell suffices for the raw degradation curve; for the *plugin* delta, which is an unmeasured second-order effect, use item-level scoring (20 items × 5 runs × 3 models = 300 per arm) rather than trusting 5 run scores.
- **Feasibility on a headless harness.** Fully feasible — generate the corpus, run `claude -p` with and without the plugin, score against the answer key; fill tiers are controlled by corpus size, so no session cutting is needed.
- **Caveats the report states.** Era-sensitivity: the 32K collapse and the 2–8K effective lengths come from 2023–mid-2025 models, and one verifier estimate has the input length sustaining 80% accuracy growing >250× in nine months, so absolute thresholds are moving targets. Task transfer: NoLiMa is deliberately hard non-lexical retrieval; no surviving study measures context rot in coding agents, so magnitudes should not be quoted as universal effect sizes.

## F3 — Input length ALONE degrades performance; even empty filler is not free

- **Claim.** Du et al. (Findings of EMNLP 2025, 5 LLMs including GPT-4o and Claude 3.5 Sonnet) show that even when models perfectly retrieve all relevant information, math/QA/coding performance drops **13.9%–85%** as input grows within claimed windows, with onset within ~7,500 tokens; degradation persists when filler is whitespace (25,000 inserted spaces still produce wrong answers despite correct extraction; ~48% drop on VarSum/Llama-3.1-8B, ~30% on GSM8K/Mistral-v0.3-7B at 30K filler) and even when irrelevant tokens are fully attention-masked (≥7.9% drop at 30K masked tokens). NoLiMa's ablation supports the mechanism: same questions at 32K score 98.5% with lexical overlap vs 56.2% without. Chroma's repeated-words task shows the same across 18 frontier models.
- **Confidence.** CONFIRMED (stronger than the seed article states) · **high** · 3-0 (4 merged claims).
- **Plugin mechanism it motivates.** Token discipline as such — README: "UserPromptSubmit hook … injects a fill advisory when a tier … is newly crossed," evidenced "G9/G10: prune stale reads"; plus the `governing-context` skill's "content-rot hygiene." Report's implication: "prune even inert content, push verbose tool output into subagents, keep the main thread short; a fuller window is intrinsically a less reliable window."
- **Testable prediction if the plugin works.** On a fixed reasoning task (a hidden-test coding fix, or a 15-item math/QA set) held constant in *content*, with inert filler injected to push the session from ~5K to ~60K tokens of prior context, the plugin-loaded arm will lose **less** accuracy per 10K tokens of added filler than the no-plugin arm, because the advisory prompts a prune/handoff before the filler is carried into the reasoning turn. Falsified if the slopes are indistinguishable — which would mean the advisory changes talk but not tokens.
- **Substrate and measure.** Substrate: a long session that accumulates verbose tool output (many full-file Reads of files irrelevant to the final question), ending in a fixed reasoning task scored by hidden tests or an answer key. Manipulate: filler volume (0 / 15K / 30K / 60K tokens of irrelevant Read output) × plugin on/off. Count: task score, and — as the mechanism check — actual tokens carried into the final turn (the plugin's claim is that it reduces this, so measure it, don't assume it). Minimum n: the literature's mid-range effect is a ~30% relative drop, d well over 1 on a continuous score — ~8–10 runs per arm at run level, or 15 items × 5 runs × 3 models = 225 per arm at item level. The plugin's incremental effect will be smaller than the raw length effect, so favor item level.
- **Feasibility on a headless harness.** Feasible — filler is injectable as scripted Read calls on generated files; the final task is scored by hidden tests. The one thing to instrument is per-turn token count, which the harness must capture rather than infer.
- **Caveats the report states.** The attention-masking ablation covers only 2 open models. The 85% worst case comes from 7–8B models, not frontier ones. Task transfer again: no coding-agent-specific measurement exists. Note also the appendix records a *mitigation* from the same paper that competes with the plugin's: prompting the model to recite retrieved evidence before solving improved GPT-4o up to 4% on RULER — a cheap baseline arm worth including so the plugin is not compared only against doing nothing.

## F4 — Position bias is U-shaped, behaviorally and mechanistically

- **Claim.** Liu et al. (TACL 2024): GPT-3.5-Turbo lost **>20 percentage points** on multi-document QA when the gold document moved to the middle (75.8→55.4 at 20 docs; 73.4→50.5 at 30), the worst case falling below its 56.1% closed-book baseline — mid-positioned relevant context was worse than no context. Hsieh et al., "Found in the Middle" (Findings of ACL 2024): measured attention weights are U-shaped by position and the pattern persists under random document shuffling (position-dependent, not content-dependent); subtracting the positional term recovers **6–15 percentage points** of RAG accuracy on NaturalQuestions, which is causal support, not correlation.
- **Confidence.** CONFIRMED · **high** · 3-0 (4 merged claims).
- **Plugin mechanism it motivates.** The end-of-prompt goal anchor — README `/goal [text]`: "Sets the session goal — a **continuity ledger** (`.governor/ledger.md`) that the hook re-shows at the end of every prompt so a long session can't drift off it," evidenced "F4: the end of the window is the best-attended position; keep it tiny (F10)"; and the UserPromptSubmit hook: "Re-anchors the goal at the end of every prompt (tiny, cache-safe)." Also the skill's "placement guidance (F4 U-shape)."
- **Testable prediction if the plugin works.** On a long build task whose binding constraints are stated once in the opening prompt and then buried by ~40K tokens of intervening tool output, the arm with `/goal` set (anchor re-injected at the end of every prompt) will violate **fewer** of those constraints than the arm with the plugin loaded but no goal set, and both will beat the no-plugin arm. Constraints are the measure: e.g. "never edit `config/*.py`", "all new functions typed", "use the existing logger, not print" — each a hidden test. Falsified if constraint violations are equal with and without the anchor.
- **Substrate and measure.** Substrate: a multi-step build task with 5–8 machine-checkable standing constraints, in a repo big enough that satisfying the task requires ~40K tokens of reading. Manipulate: constraint position (opening prompt only / opening + end-anchored via `/goal`) × session length (short vs long) × plugin on/off. Count: constraint violations per run (hidden tests), plus whether the final answer cites a mid-context fact correctly. Minimum n: a >20pp gap at ~50–75% baseline needs roughly 85 runs per arm at run-proportion level — infeasible at 5 runs per cell, so the design **must** score 5–8 constraints per run (5 runs × 8 constraints × 3 models = 120 constraint-observations per arm), and the anchor effect must be reported at that level with run as a random effect.
- **Feasibility on a headless harness.** Feasible; the anchor is a plugin hook, so the arms differ only in whether `/goal` was issued. The awkward part is holding session length fixed across arms — the anchor may itself change how much the model reads, so log tokens as a covariate.
- **Caveats the report states.** Attention was measured directly only on 7B open models (Vicuna, Tulu-2). 2025–26 work shows the shape is model- and fill-dependent: recency can dominate near the window limit, and frontier models attenuate simple-retrieval position effects — so a null result on opus does not refute the finding, it may confirm the attenuation. Cross-report: G8 (enhancement pass) reframes the driver as *arrangement* — identical distractors clustered near the target scored ~98% vs ~1% scattered — which makes placement even more load-bearing but shifts the mechanism.

## F5 — Multi-turn conversation itself degrades performance; no in-conversation recovery

- **Claim.** Laban et al. (Microsoft Research / Salesforce; 200,000+ simulated conversations; ICLR 2026 oral, Best Paper per verification): all 15 tested top open- and closed-weight LLMs scored worse multi-turn than single-turn, averaging a **39% drop** across six generation tasks (~90% single-turn vs ~65% sharded; GPT-4.1 97.9→61.8, Gemini 2.5 Pro 100.1→64.5, Claude 3.7 Sonnet 100.4→65.9). The drop decomposes into ~16% aptitude loss plus a **112% increase in unreliability** (the 90th–10th percentile spread more than doubles). Models assume early, commit prematurely, over-rely, and do not recover within the conversation. Replicated by arXiv:2602.07338 (~30% degradation including GPT-5.2-era models), which recovers **+20.3 points** via an external mediator that rewrites and consolidates the input — recovery requires outside intervention, not more turns.
- **Confidence.** CONFIRMED · **high** · 3-0 (3 merged claims).
- **Plugin mechanism it motivates.** `/handoff` — README: "Writes a ground-truth-verified handoff brief, then you `/clear`; the next session is offered the brief automatically," evidenced "F5: models don't recover in-conversation — external consolidation does; F9: ~300 focused tokens beat ~113K of history." Supported by the SessionStart hook: "Offers the newest unconsumed handoff brief (<48h)."
- **Testable prediction if the plugin works.** On a build task delivered as sharded, underspecified turns (Laban's protocol) where the model is steered into a wrong approach at turn 3, the arm that runs `/handoff` then `/clear` and resumes from the brief will score **higher** on hidden tests than the arm that continues the same session with corrective turns, at matched total turn count — and the gap will be larger in *variance reduction* (worst-case run) than in mean. Falsified if continuing in-place matches or beats the handoff arm.
- **Substrate and measure.** Substrate: a multi-session build task with hidden tests, driven by a scripted sharded user simulator that withholds requirements until later turns and plants one wrong-approach nudge. Manipulate: recovery strategy (continue-with-corrections / plugin `/handoff` + `/clear` + resume / naive `/clear` with no brief — the third arm is essential, or the test cannot separate "clearing helps" from "the plugin's brief helps"). Count: hidden tests passed; separately the 90th–10th percentile spread across runs; separately turns-to-first-passing-test. Minimum n: for the 39% mean drop on a continuous score (d ≈ 2.5), **4–5 runs per arm** is enough — the budget covers this comfortably. For the unreliability claim, variance comparisons need ~20–30 runs per cell; at 5 runs per cell the spread measure is pooled across models (15) at best, so report it as directional only.
- **Feasibility on a headless harness.** The best-fitting finding for this harness — it needs exactly the two capabilities the harness has (cut a session at a chosen point; resume with or without a brief). The user simulator is the only build cost.
- **Caveats the report states.** Scope is sharded, underspecified instructions with an LLM user-simulator — not naturally-occurring developer conversations. The replication disputes the causal attribution. "Do not recover" means no *spontaneous* recovery; external consolidation demonstrably does recover performance, which is exactly the intervention the plugin provides — so the finding predicts the plugin's mechanism class works, but says nothing about whether `/handoff`'s specific brief format achieves it. Mitigation evidence generally is thin: no measured effect sizes survived for automated compaction, memory files, or subagent isolation.

## F6 — Context rot persists in current frontier models, Claude 4 family included

- **Claim.** Chroma's July 2025 report (Hong, Troynikov, Huber; open code and data): across all 18 models tested — Claude Opus 4, Claude Sonnet 4, Sonnet 3.7/3.5, Haiku 3.5, GPT-4.1, o3, Gemini 2.5 Pro, Qwen3 — performance degrades non-uniformly with input length even on deliberately simple tasks, with degradation appearing around **2.5–10K words** on the simplest (repeated-words) task, far below 200K–1M advertised windows. Corroborated into 2026 (arXiv:2601.15300; 200K-token benchmark roundups showing 30–60-point advertised-vs-effective gaps).
- **Confidence.** CONFIRMED · **high** · 3-0 (2 merged claims).
- **Plugin mechanism it motivates.** None — background; it is the scope justification for the plugin existing at all on this model generation ("governance is warranted for the Claude 4 family specifically — 'newer models fixed long context' is not supported by evidence"). No README row cites F6.
- **Testable prediction if the plugin works.** Not directly testable against the plugin — F6 makes a claim about models, not about governance. Its role in the experiment is as the **manipulation check that licenses every other test**: on the harness's own substrate, with plugin omitted, accuracy on planted items must fall measurably as fill rises, on at least sonnet and opus. If that does not reproduce, the plugin's arms cannot show a benefit and the whole design is uninterpretable. So run it first, as arm zero.
- **Substrate and measure.** Substrate: the simplest possible length-only task, so task difficulty is held flat — e.g. reproduce a list of N planted tokens, or answer 20 trivially-retrievable questions, at 5K / 25K / 60K / 120K of context. Manipulate: input length only. Count: accuracy by length, per model. Minimum n: the Chroma effect is large and monotonic; **5 runs per cell × 3 models** suffices to see a slope, and this is the one cell where 5 is clearly enough.
- **Feasibility on a headless harness.** Trivially feasible and cheap on haiku; run it across all three models since the report's whole point is that model choice changes the onset, not the existence.
- **Caveats the report states.** Vendor caveat: Chroma sells retrieval/RAG infrastructure and the report is not peer-reviewed — though code and data are open and the pattern replicates in peer-reviewed work, and no tested condition showed degradation-free scaling. Onset points are explicitly "moving targets as frontier long-context handling improves," so the harness's own onset may sit far right of Chroma's 2.5–10K words.

## F7 — Simple needle-in-a-haystack scores mask real degradation

- **Claim.** RULER (COLM 2024): 17 models achieved near-perfect vanilla NIAH accuracy yet almost all showed large drops as context grew on 13 more realistic tasks — "only half of them can maintain satisfactory performance at the length of 32K." NoLiMa was built to remove lexical shortcuts: minimal question-needle word overlap forces latent-association inference, and the ablation shows literal-match presence alone moves 32K accuracy from **56.2% to 98.5%**.
- **Confidence.** CONFIRMED · **high** · 3-0 (2 merged claims).
- **Plugin mechanism it motivates.** None — background, but it is **binding on the experiment's measurement design**, which is why it matters here more than most: "never calibrate thresholds from vendor NIAH marketing. Coding-agent work — tracing behavior across files where the question and the answer rarely share words — sits in the non-lexical regime, so the pessimistic (NoLiMa-like) curves are the relevant reference class."
- **Testable prediction if the plugin works.** Not directly testable against the plugin — it is a claim about benchmarks, not about governance. Its testable consequence is a design constraint with a check attached: on the same corpus and the same fill levels, a lexical-overlap task (question repeats the source's exact words) will show a **smaller** plugin benefit than a non-lexical task (question shares no content words with the source), because the lexical version does not degrade enough to leave room. Falsified if the plugin's benefit is identical across the two phrasings — which would mean the harness's task is not actually discriminating.
- **Substrate and measure.** Substrate: the same planted-fact corpus rendered twice — once with questions that reuse source wording, once paraphrased to zero content-word overlap. Manipulate: lexical overlap (high/low) × fill × plugin on/off. Count: recovered facts, reported separately for the two phrasings. Minimum n: the NoLiMa overlap gap is enormous (56.2 vs 98.5), so **5 runs per cell** detects the overlap main effect easily; the interaction with the plugin is the small term and needs item-level scoring.
- **Feasibility on a headless harness.** Feasible and cheap — it doubles the question set, not the run count, if both phrasings are asked in the same session. Ask them in separate sessions if order effects are a worry.
- **Caveats the report states.** Task transfer: NoLiMa is *deliberately* hard non-lexical retrieval and its magnitudes should not be read as universal effect sizes for coding work — the report only asserts that coding "plausibly sits in the harder regime." So a non-lexical substrate is the right reference class but will likely overstate the real-world gap.

## F8 — Three independent benchmarks converge on ~16–32K tokens as the degradation knee (2024-era models)

- **Claim.** Databricks (Leng et al., arXiv:2411.03538, 20 LLMs on RAG workloads): most models improved only up to **16–32K tokens** then declined; Llama 3.1 405B declines after 32K, GPT-4-0125-preview after 64K, and 13 of 20 could not hold accuracy above 64K. NoLiMa's 32K collapse (11/13 below half baseline) and RULER's "half unsatisfactory at 32K" converge on the same region. Failure modes shift qualitatively with length: Claude 3 Sonnet copyright-refusal failures rose 3.7%→21%→49.5% at 16K→32K→64K; DBRX instruction-following failures 5.2%→50.4% from 8K→32K.
- **Confidence.** CONFIRMED for 2024-era · **medium** · 3-0 (2 merged claims). Explicitly graded down because the numbers are cohort-bound.
- **Plugin mechanism it motivates.** The tier defaults and their tunability — README config: `"tiers": [0.30, 0.50, 0.75]`, `"window_tokens": 200000`, `model_windows`; report's implication: "ship thresholds as tunable config with these numbers as defaults (e.g., first advisory around 30–40% fill of a 200K window), and pair token thresholds with behavioral signals rather than hardcoding constants that will age out." The README already disavows the fixed knee: "for 2026 frontier models the old fixed-token knee is obsolete."
- **Testable prediction if the plugin works.** On a fixed non-lexical synthesis task run at a sweep of fill levels, the arm using the shipped tiers (30/50/75% of 200K = advisories at 60K/100K/150K) will **not** outperform an arm with tiers set to fire at the measured knee for that model on that task, and may underperform it — the report's own position is that the defaults are proxies. The strong version, which is what the experiment should try to falsify: *tier placement does not matter; what matters is whether an advisory fires at all before the seam*. Test by comparing tiers=[0.30,0.50,0.75] vs tiers=[0.15,0.30,0.45] vs tiers=[0.60] vs plugin-off on the same task.
- **Substrate and measure.** Substrate: the non-lexical planted-fact corpus at 5 fill levels spanning 10%–90% of budget. Manipulate: tier configuration (4 arms above) × model. Count: accuracy on planted items, plus fill-at-first-advisory and whether the session acted (pruned / handed off) after it. Also count qualitative failure-mode shifts, which is F8's under-used half: refusals, abstentions, and dropped instructions by fill level. Minimum n: **unknown** for the plugin delta — the report gives knee locations, not intervention effect sizes. Treat the knee sweep as descriptive (5 runs per cell is fine to locate a knee to within a fill level) and the tier comparison as underpowered unless scored at item level.
- **Feasibility on a headless harness.** Feasible without code changes — tier arms are a `.governor/config.json` edit per arm, which is exactly what the plugin's own `tests/ab/combinations.json` is built for.
- **Caveats the report states.** Cohort-bound: 2025–26 frontier models hold longer. The Databricks design "partly conflates model degradation with retriever noise." Only the qualitative convergence across three task families is called solid. And the plugin's own follow-up research supersedes this finding — G8 (PredicateLongBench, NVIDIA July 2026) holds context fixed at 128K and shows failure driven by search-space cardinality, adversarial near-miss decoys, and *arrangement* (clustered distractors ~98% vs scattered ~1%), which "retires the false 'you're degrading at X%' advisory framing." Any experiment that tests tier placement as if it marked a degradation point is testing a claim the research already withdrew.

## F9 — Pruning context has a directly measured benefit — as a CEILING measurement

- **Claim.** Chroma on LongMemEval: every model family tested (Claude, GPT, Gemini, Qwen; 18 models) scored significantly higher given **~300-token focused prompts** containing only relevant content than given the **full ~113K-token** conversation history containing the same relevant content plus irrelevant context — "Across all models, we see significantly higher performance on focused prompts compared to full prompts." Claude models showed the most pronounced gap, attributed to conservative abstention under ambiguity in long contexts. Secondary coverage cites accuracy drops of ~30%; the report itself states no headline percentage.
- **Confidence.** CONFIRMED · **medium** · 3-0. Single vendor source, and a ceiling measurement.
- **Plugin mechanism it motivates.** The whole handoff/conclude/merge workflow — README `/handoff` ("F5 … F9: ~300 focused tokens beat ~113K of history"), `/conclude [type]` ("Distils an investigation to a standalone file … dead ends become one-line lessons, details dropped" — "F9, F10"), `/merge <slug>` ("Loads one conclusion back into a clean session — the distillate, never the noise" — "F9"), and the SessionStart hook, whose compaction nudge is evidenced "F9 caveat: compaction quality is unmeasured."
- **Testable prediction if the plugin works.** On a task whose second half depends on findings established in the first half, with a session cut at the midpoint, three arms resumed from: (A) `/handoff` brief written by the plugin, (B) Claude Code's own natural compaction, (C) full transcript carried forward — arm A will pass **more** hidden tests than B, and A will be at least as good as C at a fraction of the tokens. Falsified if A ≤ B, which would mean the plugin's brief adds nothing over the compactor. This is the single most important prediction in the set, because F9 is the only *measured* mitigation and the plugin's core value claim rests on it.
- **Substrate and measure.** Substrate: a two-phase investigation-then-build task with hidden tests — phase 1 establishes 8–10 facts (which module owns X, which two approaches fail and why), phase 2 requires them. Manipulate: what crosses the seam (plugin brief / natural compaction / full transcript / nothing, as the floor). Count: hidden tests passed in phase 2; facts correctly carried across the seam (answer key on the brief itself); tokens at phase-2 start; and re-derivation events, where the session re-reads something the brief already contained — the plugin's break-even model calls this `f`, the chance dropped context gets re-read, and it is the quantity B7's rule turns on. Minimum n: **unknown as a percentage** — the report explicitly says effect sizes are graphical only; secondary sources cite ~30%. If ~30% relative on a hidden-test proportion, item-level scoring with 10 tests × 5 runs × 3 models = 150 per arm is the workable design; run-level 5 is not.
- **Feasibility on a headless harness.** Feasible and central — this is the test the harness was described for. The one constraint: arm B requires letting a session compact naturally, which means sizing phase 1 to actually trip auto-compact rather than cutting at a chosen point, so phase-1 length is not fully controllable across arms A and B. Log tokens at seam for both.
- **Caveats the report states.** **The critical one:** focused prompts were oracle-pruned from dataset labels, so F9 measures the **ceiling** of pruning benefit, not the realized performance of any automated compaction or summarization tool. The plugin's brief is not an oracle, so F9 predicts the *sign* of the effect, not its size — a small or absent effect does not refute F9, it refutes the brief. Single vendor source (Chroma, RAG vendor, not peer-reviewed). The report's open question 2 states it directly: "Do automated, lossy compaction/summarization handoffs retain the oracle-pruning benefit … No measured effect sizes exist for any mitigation as actually implemented." Claude's outsized gap is attributed to abstention, so score abstentions separately from wrong answers or the mechanism will be invisible.

## F10 — A single topically-related-but-wrong distractor measurably reduces performance; four compound it

- **Claim.** Chroma (18 models, 8 input lengths, three conditions — needle-only, needle + 1 randomly positioned distractor, needle + 4): "Even a single distractor reduces performance relative to the baseline (needle only), and adding four distractors compounds this degradation further." A distractor is defined as topically related to the needle but not answering the question. Distractor impact is non-uniform across distractors; GPT models hallucinate most while Claude Opus 4 / Sonnet 4 abstain conservatively. Direction independently corroborated by Shi et al. (ICML 2023): a single irrelevant sentence degrades GSM8K reasoning. **No numeric effect size exists** — Chroma states percentages only in figures.
- **Confidence.** CONFIRMED · **medium** · 3-0. Downgraded because the quantification is single-source, graph-only, not peer-reviewed.
- **Plugin mechanism it motivates.** Content-rot hygiene across three pieces — README `/log [decision|ruled-out|note]` ("ruled-out lines ride in the anchor"; "F10: name what's disproven so a future session can't relearn it"), `/conclude` ("dead ends become one-line lessons, details dropped"; "F9, F10: dead-end details are distractors"), and the goal anchor's size discipline ("keep it tiny (F10)"). Report's implication: "compaction should DROP dead ends rather than summarize them in."
- **Testable prediction if the plugin works.** On a debugging task where the session first pursues and abandons two plausible-but-wrong hypotheses, the arm that used `/log ruled-out` and resumed via `/handoff` will re-attempt an already-disproven approach **less often** than the arm resuming from a natural compaction that summarizes the dead ends in, and will pass more hidden tests. Measure the repeat-attempt count directly — it is the cleanest behavioral readout of F10 available, and it is falsified if the ruled-out arm re-attempts at the same rate.
- **Substrate and measure.** Substrate: a debugging task with a planted bug and two seeded near-miss hypotheses (a wrong-but-plausible module, a stale error message pointing elsewhere) — this is the "long debugging session accumulates exactly this material" case the report names. Manipulate: distractor count (0 / 1 / 4 seeded dead ends) × what survives the seam (ruled-out lines / full dead-end narrative / nothing). Count: repeat-attempts on disproven approaches; hidden tests passed; and — because of the Claude-abstains finding — abstentions and hedged non-answers, scored separately from wrong answers. Minimum n: **unknown** — the report is explicit that Chroma states no percentages and the effect sizes live only in figures. Design for a large expected effect on the behavioral measure (repeat-attempts is a count, not a proportion, so it is more sensitive), and treat the accuracy measure as underpowered.
- **Feasibility on a headless harness.** Feasible — dead ends are seeded by scripting the first phase's turns, so every run enters the seam with the same distractor set. The scoring of "re-attempted a disproven approach" needs a rubric or a grep-able signature (e.g. did the session re-Read the decoy module), which is worth defining before the runs, not after.
- **Caveats the report states.** Single-source, graph-only, non-peer-reviewed quantification; only the *direction* has peer-reviewed corroboration (Shi et al.). Vendor caveat applies (Chroma). And the plugin's follow-up research sharpens the target: G8 found that scattered near-miss decoys, not merely present decoys, are the collapse condition (~1% scattered vs ~98% clustered), so an experiment that seeds decoys without controlling their arrangement is testing a weaker version of the claim than the current evidence supports.

## Findings ranked by how directly the plugin's value depends on them

1. **F9 — pruning beats carrying history.** The only *measured* mitigation in the whole set, and the direct evidence for `/handoff`, `/conclude`, and `/merge` — the plugin's three most substantial commands. It is also the finding whose caveat is most dangerous to the plugin: it measures an oracle's ceiling, so the plugin's real brief could deliver anywhere from all of that benefit to none.
2. **F5 — multi-turn degrades, only external consolidation recovers.** Supplies the *why now* for handing off rather than continuing, and is the finding whose effect size (39% drop, +112% unreliability) is large enough to be visible at 5 runs per cell. If F5 does not reproduce on the harness's substrate, the plugin's central workflow has no occasion to fire.
3. **F10 — distractors, including one, hurt.** Justifies the `ruled-out` ledger line and the "drop dead ends, don't summarize them" rule that distinguishes `/conclude` from ordinary compaction. Direction is solid; the magnitude is unmeasured, so the plugin is betting on a sign.
4. **F4 — U-shaped position bias.** The sole evidence for the plugin's most constantly-running mechanism, the end-of-prompt goal anchor injected on every turn. Strong mechanistically, but measured on 7B models, and frontier attenuation means the anchor may be solving a problem that has partly receded.
5. **F3 — length alone degrades, even inert filler.** Supports "prune anyway" as a standing policy and makes token discipline worth something independent of content quality. It motivates behavior more than any single command, which makes it hard to attribute a plugin effect to.
6. **F2 — effective context is half or less of claimed.** The reason the fill monitor measures against a conservative budget rather than the advertised window. Load-bearing for the monitor's existence, not for where its tiers sit.
7. **F8 — the 16–32K knee.** Set the shipped tier defaults, and the plugin's own README and G8 have since disavowed the fixed-knee framing. It now supports "fire an advisory sometime before the seam," which is a much weaker dependency than it looks.
8. **F6 — rot persists in the Claude 4 family.** Scope justification: if this were false the plugin would be unnecessary. Nothing in the plugin is built on it, but it licenses every other test as the manipulation check.
9. **F7 — NIAH masks degradation.** Binds the *experiment's* measurement design (use non-lexical tasks) more than the plugin's design. Getting it wrong invalidates the test; getting it right changes nothing in the plugin.
10. **F1 — the seed article's citations hold.** Pure provenance. Its only forward-acting content is a prohibition the plugin already obeys (build no self-diagnosis on unverified claim (e)), which is why `/summary` puts a human in the loop rather than asking the model how it feels.
