## Current state (verified against ground truth)

### The Scandal Core
- **Ostrey Hollow station** (1896–1925): skimming station 7 miles up valley from Larrow Green, run by Ansel Keddie alone for 29 seasons
- **1919 accusation**: Association letter to 9 families (including Emmet Brant) alleging "water in the cans" and payment discrepancies
- **1925 arbitration**: Board of arbitration under Alonzo Frick (17 Mar – 4 May) found "no cause established" but awarded $3,800 without admission; charged to station account
- **Keddie dismissal**: Put out 30 April 1925 after 29 years; died 1934, still under stigma

### The Truth Discovered (1958)
- **Glass pipettes defective** (verified via Ivy Keddie's burette testing 9 Mar 1958): all 9 surviving pipettes from condemned 1923 crate delivered 16.72 c.c. instead of marked 17.6 c.c. = 95 hundredths of mark (5% short)
- **Consequence**: every test from 1897–1923 read 5% too low; butterfat was recorded at 3.61% average when actual was 3.80%
- **Financial impact**: over 25 years, 40 million lbs milk received; patrons lost 76,000 lbs butterfat = $19,000 at period prices; board awarded only $3,800 (one-fifth)
- **Bulletin 471 (Oct 1958)** officially exonerated: "no milk was watered, no cream was stolen; pipettes short by one part in twenty"

### Key Witnesses & Perspectives
1. **Vira Toland** (r01): Hollow patron's daughter, age 7 in 1919; recalls rift between Emmet Brant and Orra Keddie (wife of Ansel, sister of Emmet); tests mysteriously rose 1924 without explanation; father kept accusal letter 60+ years
2. **Effie Loomis** (r02): Association secretary 1901–1940; explains Article VII (test governs payment); notes 1925 arbitration award lacked stated cause; records crate of condemned measures (1923) was never mentioned in hearing
3. **Ansel Keddie** (r03): Tester's statement + unsent letter; defends rigorous method; notes unexplained 1911 discrepancy (9 weeks with borrowed measure, tests at 3.80 vs. normal 3.61); denies ever taking cream; accuses no one; notes brother-in-law rift destroyed his standing
4. **Ivy Keddie** (r04): Ansel's granddaughter; presents burette measurements proving glass defect; calculates fat loss and price impact; presents State Agricultural College Bulletin 214 (bog hay effect only 0.05%, insufficient); notes 24-year delay between dismissal (1925) and exoneration (1949 discovery/1958 proof)
5. **Wilbur Teague** (r05): Bookkeeper 1905–1945; explains "station gain" line (difference between credited butterfat and received butterfat, consistently 5.25%, attributed to station oversight); notes structural vulnerability: one man alone at station; told Vose (manager) in 1916 to investigate, was told to let it alone
6. **Merle Strawn** (r06): State dairy inspector 1907–1936; circuit books show scales always true, never found water (contrary to 1919 claim); 1923 June 12 entry: condemned pipettes as "will not deliver their mark"; October 1923: new glass tested true; later entries show tests "improved" (3.80) in 1924–1925
7. **Halbert Nym** (r07): Curator, accession notes for 16 items (test book, invoice, defective crate with 9 pipettes, ledgers, arbitration minute book, Keddie statement, Strawn's 1923 note, Ammon & Sons letter, 1908 committee report, Bulletin 214, Alder Corners test book data, Ivy's measurement sheet, Bulletin 471, receiving/churn books)
8. **Selby Vose** (r08): Manager 1913–1938; 1926 annual address directly accuses Ostrey Hollow patrons of adultering cans with water (tests rose when "looking began" in 1924, nothing changed but scrutiny); defends 1919 circular warning; defends $3,800 payment as peacemaking; refuses to name names but implies deliberate adulteration

### Critical Chronology
- **1896 May 4**: Station opens, Ansel Keddie begins test book (borrowed glass from Larrow Green)
- **1897 May 1**: New glass equipment arrives (Ammon & Sons invoice: $77.93 for 6 doz pipettes 17.6 c.c., bottles, tester, acid measure)
- **1898 onward**: Average test stabilizes at 3.61% (actually 3.80%, hidden by short glass)
- **1902**: First complaints from Larrow Green that Ostrey Hollow milk tests below valley average
- **1908**: Committee samples herds through winter, reports wet hay of Hessel Bottom thins cream; remedy lies with land
- **1911 May 8 – July 10**: Keddie's own measure breaks; borrows replacement; tests jump to 3.80; return to 3.61 when own measure restored; Keddie marks with "?" (unexplained)
- **1919**: Selby Vose sends circular to 9 Hollow families alleging water in cans; no evidence produced; rift between Emmet Brant and Orra Keddie hardens
- **1923 June 12**: State inspector Merle Strawn condemns entire crate of pipettes as "will not deliver their mark"; new glass ordered and in use by July
- **1923 October**: New pipettes tested by Strawn; delivery true
- **1924**: Tests at Ostrey Hollow rise to 3.80 (matching true value when good glass used)
- **1925 Jan–May**: Ostrey Hollow patrons petition; arbitration held; "no cause established" award of $3,800; Keddie dismissed 30 April
- **1934**: Ansel Keddie dies, still under stigma
- **1958 Feb**: Ivy Keddie finds crate in station loft; measures 9 surviving pipettes (16.72 c.c. mean); confirms 5% defect
- **1958 Mar 9**: Ivy's measurement sheet
- **1958 Oct**: State Agricultural College Bulletin 471 issued, exonerating Keddie posthumously (24 years after dismissal, 33 years after arbitration)

### Structural Facts
- **Article VII** (Association by-laws, adopted 1892): "The test of the station where he delivers shall govern" — no appeal, no retest, station tester's word is law
- **One-man operation**: Ansel Keddie alone weighed milk, drew samples, made tests, wrote figures, ran separator — no oversight, no second witness
- **Cream portability**: Wilbur Teague's testimony that cream is portable and station was "in one pair of hands for twenty-nine seasons" — structural vulnerability to theft
- **Manager knowledge**: Selby Vose signed annual sheets showing 5.25% station gain every year 1905–1925; claimed he did not know what to make of it; under oath at arbitration said nothing about the gain

### Competing Narratives (Verified from Retellings)
1. **Vose's theory** (r08): Patrons adultered cans with water to boost apparent volume; tests rose when scrutiny intensified (1924); no names, but direct accusation
2. **Vira Toland's intuition** (r01): "The thing corrected itself and no man stood up and gave a reason for it" — mystery, no consensus explanation given in 1919–1925
3. **Ansel Keddie's defense** (r03): Glass was true in 1911; when own measure broke and borrowed substitute used, tests rose; when own returned, tests fell; pattern suggests equipment, not fraud
4. **Ivy Keddie's proof** (r04): Burette testing proves glass was systematically short 5%; explains everything; no theft, no water adulteration — equipment defect alone
5. **Strawn's record** (r06): Scales always true, never found water, condemned glass on 1923 June 12; new glass tested true October 1923; tests improved after (consistent with equipment swap, not behavior change)

### Unresolved or Debated
- **1911 mystery**: Keddie marks 1911 discrepancy with "?"; Ivy theorizes borrowed measure; no definitive explanation in retellings
- **Vose's intent**: Did he genuinely believe water adulteration or use it as cover story for equipment failure he should have known about (signed sheets yearly)?
- **Tester's reputation**: Wilbur Teague testified in 1925 that "cream is portable" and single-operator structure enables theft — but also that he advised Vose in 1916 without effect
