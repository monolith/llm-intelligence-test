**r09: Roy Brant, farmer at Hessel Bottom (1955 account for county farm bureau)**
- [verified] Keeper of father Emmet Brant's milk ledger (started 1898, two columns: own scale vs. station's)
- [verified] Two scales always agreed; 17-hundredth-point gap between Hollow and Larrow Green tests was **consistent year-round**, not seasonal
- [verified] **1911 mystery**: Nine weeks when station's own measure was broken; borrowed measure used; tests rose from 3.61 to 3.80 for all Hollow patrons; fell back to 3.61 when own measure returned
- [verified] Family connection: Orra Keddie was Emmet Brant's sister; Ansel Keddie married her (Brant's brother-in-law); rift after 1919 water warning circular
- [verified] Postscript 1958: borrowed book for Ivy Keddie (Duncan Keddie's daughter, Hazel's second cousin) for college work

**r10: Rosalie Cudd, cheesemaker at Alder Corners (1958 letter to Ivy Keddie)**
- [verified] Four Hessel Bottom farmers hauled milk over Redlow Ridge to Alder Corners (summer only, 1915–1930, Jun–Sep each year)
- [verified] **Same Hessel Bottom milk tested 3.78–3.82** (average 3.80) at Alder Corners over 16 summers, consistently good
- [verified] Cudd's father Jerome tested his own glass, never compared with Hollow, had no business with Ostrey Hollow figures
- [verified] Cream knowledge: watered milk reveals itself in cheese-making; curd doesn't knit, weak body, lost yield — visible next morning, not hidden
- [verified] **Critical point**: Never one complaint from Jerome Cudd about those four men's milk in 16 summers; never docked one cent; wished he had more

**r11: Leland Vaught, professor of dairy husbandry, Bulletin 471 introduction (Oct 1958)**
- [verified] **Scientific explanation**: Pipettes delivered 16.27 c.c. instead of marked 17.6 c.c. = 0.9244 ratio = 5% short
- [verified] Error is **proportional not additive** — removes fixed fraction, not fixed number of points
- [verified] Error is **precise** — 16.27 c.c. every time for 25 years, January to July, careless or careful operator
- [verified] Error is **invisible from inside plant** — pipette is the standard, cannot be caught by measuring with itself; only burette from outside catches it
- [verified] **1911 experiment**: Borrowed measure used for 9 weeks; tests rose 3.61 → 3.80; returned to 3.61 when own measure came back; recorded contemporaneously with "?"
- [verified] Nine surviving pipettes from condemned 1923 crate measured via burette: mean delivery 16.27 c.c. (marked 17.6 c.c.)
- [verified] Station glass in use May 1, 1898 – June 12, 1923 (condemned by state inspector)
- [verified] **Impact**: 40 million lbs milk received; 118,000 lbs butterfat recorded low; true average 3.905 vs. recorded 3.61; **76,000 lbs butterfat uncredited**, matches station gain ledger

- [verified] 1919: manager circulated warning against water in milk; six-year silence between ends of valley

- [verified] 1959 reprint note: Ivy Keddie at college studying the Hollow matter; 11 pipettes from condemned crate still exist

- [verified] Ledger entry format: "Butter fat credited to patrons on station test, 57,760 lb. Butter fat received at Larrow Green in station cream, 60,800 lb. Station gain, 3,040 lb, five and a quarter per cent"

**r14: Hobart Sill, buttermaker (retired), remarks on leaving (1934)**

**r15: Nyle Grigg, 1908 committee chairman, republishing report with 1919 covering letter**
- [verified] Committee in January 1908; sampled Hessel Bottom herds every month that year
- [verified] No interest in outcome (Grigg hauls to Larrow Green, not Hollow)

- [verified] Wet meadow is distinct chemistry: acid soil, poor lime/phosphates; sweet grasses don't hold; sedge/rush/coarse grasses take over

- [verified] **College Bulletin 214**: Bog hay depresses butterfat by **two tenths** (0.2%) in winter
