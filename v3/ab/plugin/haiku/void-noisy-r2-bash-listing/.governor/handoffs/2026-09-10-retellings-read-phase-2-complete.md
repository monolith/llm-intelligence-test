## The retellings' unified narrative (consolidated core story for future questions)

**The scandal:** ~1896–1924, milk from Ostrey Hollow tested ~19/100 points lower than milk from other valley locations. Patrons blamed wet hay, testers, office, weather. The 1925 arbitration board found "no cause established" but awarded $3,800 without admission. Ansel Keddie (tester, honest, meticulous) was dismissed four days before the award and died in 1934, never vindicated.

**The truth (discovered 1958):** The station's pipettes were systematically short by 5% (delivered 16.72cc instead of 17.6cc). Every test read 95% of truth. This explains:
- Why 3.61 recorded was actually 3.80 true (3.61 ÷ 0.95 = 3.80)
- Why patrons lost 76,000 lbs butterfat worth ~$19,000 over 25 years
- Why the board awarded only $3,800 (one-fifth the actual loss)
- Why tests improved 0.21 points in 1924 — not because patrons stopped adding water (Vose's accusation), but because the inspector condemned the glass 12 June 1923
- The 1911 nine-week interval: when a borrowed measure was used (station's pipette broken), tests rose from 3.61 to 3.80 and held there; when station's own glass returned, figures fell back to 3.61 — the only variable changed, yet no one recognized it until Vaught's 1958 analysis

**Supporting evidence from across retellings:**
- Emmet Brant's ledger (Roy Brant, r09): both scales agreed; Hollow milk consistently tested 0.17 points lower
- Alder Corners factory (Rosalie Cudd, r10): four Hessel Bottom farmers' milk tested ~3.80 (good milk), consistent over 16 summers, never watered
- Hobart Sill's observation (r14): Hollow cream arrived "heavy" (more fat than credited) on platform scales; suspects deliberate water-adding ~1920s
- Larrow Green's books (Rundle, r12 "Note Added While in Press"): "station gain" entries show 3,040 lb per year (5.25% of butterfat delivered but not credited)

**Competing theories (all disproven or incomplete):**
- Water-adding accusation (Vose, r08, 1919; Sill, r14): refuted by Rosalie Cudd's independent test data (r10) and the 1911 interval
- Cream theft (Teague theory, r05; Frick hearing, r13): refuted by "station gain" ledger entries showing fat arriving at Larrow Green *heavier* than credited, not lighter
- Wet hay (Grigg committee r15, 1908; Kepp r16, 1926; College Bulletin 214): explains ~0.2 points depression; insufficient to explain the full 0.21–0.19 gap alone; becomes irrelevant once pipette defect discovered

**Key participants:**
- Ansel Keddie: tester, meticulous, testified honestly, couldn't explain gap, dismissed 1925, died 1934 without vindication
- Emmet Brant (Roy's father): largest patron, lost most to underweighting, never spoke to his sister Orra after 1919; his careful ledger proved scales agreed
- Orra Keddie (Ansel's wife, Emmet's sister): lived 33 years estranged from her brother, outlived Ansel
- Selby Vose: manager, accused patrons of fraud (1926), unaware machinery was condemned (1923)
- Merle Strawn: inspector, caught defect, condemned glass 12 June 1923, left evidence in circuit books
- Ivy Keddie: Ansel's granddaughter, measured crate's pipettes (1958), proved the gap, vindicated grandfather 24 years after death; her college work triggered Vaught's Bulletin 471 and Rundle's 1959 footnote
