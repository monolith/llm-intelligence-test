## The core narrative across all retellings

**Timeline of events:**
- **1889** — Larrow Valley Cooperative Creamery Association founded (Gideon Vose, president)
- **1896–1923** — Ansel Keddie operates Ostrey Hollow skimming station with defective pipettes
  - Tests systematically ~5% low (0.88 cc short per 17.6 cc pipette)
  - Affects 40 million pounds of milk; patrons underpaid ~$19,000
- **1902–1919** — Complaints about low testing; various false explanations offered
- **1911** — Anomaly: borrowed measure used May–July; tests return to "normal" (3.80 vs. usual 3.61), then drop back when own measure returns—suggests the glass, not cream or cattle
- **1919** — Manager Selby Vose sends circular accusing Ostrey Hollow patrons of watering milk
- **June 12, 1923** — State inspector Merle Strawn condemns entire crate of pipettes: "will not deliver their mark"
- **Oct 1923** — New glass confirmed working; tests at station improve to 3.80
- **1925** — Arbitration hearing led by Alonzo Frick; board awards $3,800 "without admission"; patrons receive ~1/5 of actual loss; Ansel dismissed
- **1934** — Ansel Keddie dies, still under the shame of dismissal
- **1958** — Ivy Keddie (granddaughter) finds condemned crate, measures the 9 surviving pipettes: 16.72 cc mean vs. 17.6 cc marked (95% = 5% error)
- **Oct 1958** — State Agricultural College Bulletin 471 published, definitively stating: "the station's tests were low by one part in twenty because its pipettes were short by one part in twenty"

## The eight retellings and their narrators

**r01 — Vira Toland** (Hessel Bottom patron's daughter, recorded 1979)
- Eyewitness to 1919 letter arriving; father kept it in drawer for life
- Knew the families and the rift between Emmet Brant and his sister Orra (Ansel's wife)
- Recalls the strange 1924 rise in tests: "the thing corrected itself and no man stood up and gave a reason"
- Notes a young woman from college came asking about it in 1958 (likely Ivy Keddie)

**r02 — Effie Loomis** (Association secretary 1901–1940, wrote 1941)
- Official source: kept minutes the whole time
- Explains Article VII (pay on butterfat test, not weight) and its last clause: "the test of the station where he delivers shall govern" (no appeal)
- 1925 arbitration: board "finds no cause established" yet awards $3,800 anyway
- **Critical observation**: the condemned crate of measures was NEVER mentioned in the hearing, despite being inspected in June 1923

**r03 — Ansel Keddie** (tester, statement to board 1925 + unsent letter)
- Defends 29 years of meticulous record-keeping: no blots, proper procedure, no figure written over another
- Describes exact testing protocol: warming sample, mixing, charge, acid, crank speed, reading with dividers
- Marks a question mark against the 1911 anomaly in his own hand: "I could not answer it then and I cannot answer it now"
- Notes complaints began 1902, 1919 circular; split with brother-in-law Emmet; dismissal came despite "finding no cause"
- "I have tested as I was taught. I cannot tell you why the figures are what they are."

**r04 — Ivy Keddie** (granddaughter, talk to Historical Society 1959)
- **The smoking gun**: measured 9 pipettes from the crate; mean delivery 16.72 cc (short 0.88 cc from 17.6 cc mark)
- Arithmetic: 16.72 ÷ 17.6 = 0.95 = 95 hundredths = **every test read 5% low**
- Calculates: 40 million pounds intake × (3.61% recorded vs. 3.80% true) = 76,000 pounds of butterfat underpaid
- At average 25¢/lb: **$19,000 lost** to patrons; board awarded $3,800 (1/5 of the loss)
- 1923–1958: 35 years to find the cause; Ansel died 1934 (24 years before vindication)
- **Key insight**: defective glass caused 5% systematic loss; the 1911 borrowed measure had no defect → tests were normal that summer

**r05 — Wilbur Teague** (bookkeeper 1905–1945, memoir 1948)
- Created the "station gain" ledger line: difference between butter fat credited to patrons vs. received at Larrow Green
- Ran consistently at 5.25% of received fat (= 5% of credited fat); "furniture" that didn't change year to year
- Warned Vose in 1916; told to let it alone and did
- 1925 arbitration testimony: "cream is portable; one man did every step at that station"
- Doesn't accuse anyone; notes Vose signed the annual sheet every year

**r06 — Merle Strawn** (state dairy inspector, circuit notebooks 1907–1936)
- Official annual inspection records for Ostrey Hollow station
- **June 12, 1923 entry**: "Ran their milk pipettes against the burette. These measures will not deliver their mark. Condemned the lot, the whole crate of them, and told the manager to order new."
- **October 1923**: "New glass in use since July. Ran the new pipettes against the burette. They deliver their mark."
- **1924**: "Tests improved. Their book gives the year at 3.80."
- Proves the equipment change, not management change or patron behavior, caused the 1924 rise

**r07 — Halbert Nym** (curator, accession notes 1972)
- Documents all 16 items in the collection: invoices, test books, weigh books, ledgers, crate with 9 pipettes, arbitration minute book, Strawn's 1923 circuit entry, measurement sheet from 1958, Bulletin 471
- **Item 5**: The crate itself (deal wood, stenciled "AMMON & SONS"); condemned June 12, 1923; 9 pipettes survive, mean 16.72 cc
- **Item 14**: Ivy Keddie's measurement sheet (March 9, 1958): nine pipettes, mean 16.72 cc, short 0.88 cc, "95 hundredths of the mark"
- **Item 15**: Bulletin 471 closing paragraph: "No milk was watered at Ostrey Hollow and no cream was stolen there... the station's tests were low by one part in twenty because its pipettes were short by one part in twenty"

**r08 — Selby Vose** (manager, annual address 1926)
- Defends the $3,800 payment as a "generous settlement" to keep peace
- **But then accuses the patrons**: "Water was going into cans at the head of that valley and it stopped when the Association began to look"
- Points to the 1924 rise in tests (3.90, "as good a figure as any station") as proof the water was there all along
- Dismisses the June 1923 apparatus condemnation as routine wear: "Apparatus wears... it is the state man's business to say so before they fail him"
- Does not name the tester; calls for "bring it as it left the cow"
- **This false narrative (patrons watered milk) persisted for 33+ years until Ivy Keddie found the crate**
