## Timeline of events

**1889** — Larrow Valley Cooperative Creamery Association founded; Gideon Vose (president) writes Article VII: patrons paid on butterfat test, test at delivery station governs.

**1896** — Ostrey Hollow skimming station opens (May). Ansel Keddie becomes tester; borrows Hobart Sill's glassware from Larrow Green until station's own arrives (spring 1897).

**1897** — Station's own glassware (five dozen pipettes) arrives; defective pipettes remain in use (mean delivery 16.27 cc vs. 17.6 cc marked—5% systematic error).

**1898** — Station's glass goes into use May 1; remains in use until June 1923 (25 years + one month).

**1902–1919** — Patrons complain repeatedly (17 hundredths of a point low = 0.17 point discrepancy). Complaints dismissed or blamed on land/patrons. Difference is consistent year-round, independent of season or meadow conditions.

**1908** — Committee under Nyle Grigg investigates; samples Hessel Bottom herds monthly; finds no dishonesty, scales honest, milk whole; blames wet hay of Hessel Bottom (Article VII allows "no appeal").

**1911** — Anomaly: station's measure breaks; borrowed measure used June–July (~9 weeks). Tests rise from 3.61 to 3.80 (normal level) and return to 3.61 when station's own measure returns—all other variables unchanged. Recorded contemporaneously by Ansel with question mark.

**1914–1930** — Four Hessel Bottom farmers haul milk summers to Alder Corners Cheese Company (Vessey County, over Redlow Ridge); tested by Jerome Cudd's cheesemaker daughter Rosalie Cudd; average 3.79–3.82 consistently (16 seasons); proves milk quality good, not watered.

**1915** — Alder Corners Cheese Company opens (Jerome Cudd).

**1919** — Manager Selby Vose circulates warning circular to Hollow patrons against watering milk. Breaks trust (especially between Emmet Brant and brother-in-law Ansel Keddie; Orra Keddie is Ansel's wife, Emmet's sister). Roy Brant's father keeps circular in drawer for life.

**1923 (June 12)** — State inspector Merle Strawn condemns entire crate of pipettes: "will not deliver their mark." Tests the crate against burette; results recorded in circuit notebook.

**1923 (October)** — New glass installed (July); tests improve to 3.80 (normal level). Strawn verifies new pipettes "deliver their mark."

**1924–1925** — Station's average tests rise to 3.80 (as good as any station); Selby Vose claims as proof patrons were watering and have stopped. False narrative persists for 33+ years.

**1925 (April 4)** — Ansel Keddie dismissed by Association while arbitration board still sitting (no finding yet made against him).

**1925 (May 4)** — Arbitration board (Alonzo Frick, chairman) "finds no cause established" yet awards $3,800 to Hollow patrons "without admission." Board heard three explanations (water, cream-theft, hay); none proved. Patrons' loss calculated at ~$19,000 (40 million pounds of milk; 3.61% recorded vs. 3.80% true = 76,000 lb butterfat underpaid; at 25¢/lb = $19,000). Award is ~1/5 of actual loss. Four exhibits: test book (Ansel's hand), receiving book, Brant's private weigh book, state dairy inspector's 1923 circuit notebook.

**1934** — Ansel Keddie dies (still under shame of dismissal; never vindicated in his lifetime).

**1958 (Spring)** — Ivy Keddie (granddaughter, Roy Brant's second cousin) borrows Roy Brant's father's ledger and other materials for college work at Farland.

**1958 (March 9)** — Ivy measures 9 surviving pipettes from condemned crate: mean delivery 16.72 cc (short 0.88 cc from 17.6 cc mark). Arithmetic: 16.72 ÷ 17.6 = 0.95 (every test read 5% low). Calculates: 40 million pounds × (3.61% recorded vs. 3.80% true) = 76,000 lb butterfat underpaid; at 25¢/lb = ~$19,000 lost.

**1958 (October)** — State Agricultural College Bulletin 471 published (Prof. Leland Vaught). Definitively proves: "the station's tests were low by one part in twenty because its pipettes were short by one part in twenty. No milk was watered at Ostrey Hollow and no cream was stolen there... the difference—seventy-six thousand pounds of butter fat—stands credited in the Association's own ledgers, year by year, as station gain."

## The sixteen retellings and their narrators

### First 8 retellings (session 1):
1. **r01 — Vira Toland** (Hessel Bottom patron's daughter, 1979): eyewitness to 1919 letter; recalls rift between Emmet Brant and sister Orra (Ansel's wife); notes 1924 rise ("thing corrected itself"); young woman from college (Ivy Keddie) asked about it 1958.

2. **r02 — Effie Loomis** (Association secretary 1901–1940, wrote 1941): kept minutes; explains Article VII (pay on butterfat test); notes 1925 arbitration board "finds no cause" yet awards $3,800; critical: condemned crate NEVER mentioned in hearing despite June 1923 inspection.

3. **r03 — Ansel Keddie** (tester, statement 1925 + unsent letter): defends 29 years meticulous record-keeping; describes exact testing protocol; marks 1911 anomaly with question mark in own hand; complaints began 1902, 1919 circular, dismissed despite "finding no cause."

4. **r04 — Ivy Keddie** (granddaughter, 1959 Historical Society talk): measured 9 pipettes from condemned crate; mean 16.72 cc = 0.95 (5% low); calculates 40M lb × 0.19% fat loss = 76,000 lb butterfat = $19,000 underpaid; board awarded $3,800 (1/5 of loss); Ansel died 1934 (24 years before vindication); 1911 borrowed measure proves glass, not milk quality.

5. **r05 — Wilbur Teague** (bookkeeper 1905–1945, memoir 1948): created "station gain" ledger line (5.25% of received fat = 5% of credited fat); ran consistently; warned Vose 1916 (told to let alone); testified 1925 arbitration about how cream-theft account would look; made no accusation; Vose signed annual sheet every year.

6. **r06 — Merle Strawn** (state dairy inspector, circuit notebooks 1907–1936): June 12, 1923: "These measures will not deliver their mark. Condemned the lot"; October 1923: "New glass in use. Deliver their mark"; 1924: "Tests improved. Year at 3.80." Proves equipment change, not management or patron behavior, caused 1924 rise.

7. **r07 — Halbert Nym** (curator, accession notes 1972): documents 16-item collection (invoices, test books, weigh books, ledgers, crate with 9 pipettes, arbitration minutes, Strawn's 1923 entry, measurement sheet 1958, Bulletin 471). Crate: deal wood, stenciled "AMMON & SONS," condemned June 12, 1923. Ivy's measurement sheet (March 9, 1958): nine pipettes, mean 16.72 cc, "95 hundredths of the mark."

8. **r08 — Selby Vose** (manager, annual address 1926): defends $3,800 settlement as "generous"; accuses patrons: "Water was going into cans at the head of that valley and it stopped when the Association began to look"; points to 1924 rise (3.90) as proof water was there; dismisses June 1923 apparatus condemnation as routine wear; false narrative persists 33+ years.

### Second 8 retellings (session 2):

9. **r09 — Roy Brant** (farmer, Hessel Bottom, 1955 + 1958 postscript): son of Emmet Brant (who kept Ansel's father's ledger). Records milk deliveries from 1898 forward. His weight and station's weight agreed barring spills—not weighing that was wrong. Two scales never fell out despite men not speaking. Notes 1910: "two hundred thousand pounds even" by both scales. 17 hundredths point under Larrow Green (consistent July–February, independent of season). 1902 first complaint. 1908 Grigg committee blamed wet hay. May–July 1911: nine weeks with borrowed measure, tests rose, then fell when own measure returned. 1919 circular broke his trust in Ansel. Cousin Ivy borrowed father's book 1958 for college.

10. **r10 — Rosalie Cudd** (cheesemaker, Alder Corners, 1958 letter to Ivy): father Jerome Cudd built factory 1913. Four Hessel Bottom men hauled summers 1915–1930 (16 seasons; ridge closed winter). Cudd tested on own bench with own glassware, never compared to Ostrey Hollow. 1919 summer: four men at 3.79, 3.81, 3.78, 3.82 ("Good milk off wet ground, whatever they say over the ridge"). Table shows all 16 summers 3.78–3.82. Never watered milk—cheesemaker knows watered milk inside a day (curd goes soft, weak body, yield down). Never complained, never docked, never said anything except wished for more milk July. Listened to 40 years of valley explaining the meadow; never once heard valley go up and look.

11. **r11 — Leland Vaught** (professor dairy husbandry, Bulletin 471 intro, Oct 1958): explains why pipette is uncheckable (it IS the standard; can't check an instrument against itself). Five dozen pipettes purchased 1897. Nine recovered from condemned crate. Stems blown 17.6 cc, delivered against burette 16.27 cc mean. Glass in use May 1, 1898 to June 12, 1923. Emphasizes 1911 anomaly: nine weeks with borrowed measure, tests 3.61→3.80, one variable changed only. Ratio 16.27÷17.6 = 0.9244. Recorded station average 3.61; true average 3.61÷0.9244 = 3.905 (0.295 point shortfall). Applied to 40M lb: 118,000 lb butterfat delivered/uncredited. Station gain ledgers show 5.25% fat excess annually. College feeding trial (Bulletin 214): bog hay depresses 0.05% (far less than observed). Conclusion: "No milk was watered... the station's tests were low by one part in twenty because its pipettes were short by one part in twenty... the difference—seventy-six thousand pounds of butter fat—stands credited in the Association's own ledgers, year by year, as station gain."

12. **r12 — Dell Rundle** (schoolteacher, 50-year pamphlet 1939, reprinted 1959): Article VII rule (pay on test, no appeal). Ostrey Hollow founded 1896. Complaints 1902–1919. 1908 Grigg committee: wet hay. 1919 manager circular: water warning. "Settled opinion" was cream left by back door. June 12, 1923 (Rundle says July; other sources say June 12): glassware condemned. 1925 arbitration: $19,600 loss claimed; Frick board: no finding, award $3,800 without admission. Footnote (1959): Ivy Keddie (second cousin to Hazel Brant) found "eleven pipettes" (actually 9) from condemned crate; refers reader to college work and Bulletin 471. NOTE ADDED WHILE IN PRESS: Rundle read old ledgers on another errand; found "station gain" entry every year: "butter fat credited to patrons 57,760 lb, butter fat received at Larrow Green 60,800 lb, station gain 3,040 lb, five and a quarter per cent." Cream arrived heavy, not light. "Cream stolen at the Hollow would have reached the Green light. It reached the Green heavy, every year."

13. **r13 — Alonzo Frick** (arbitration chairman, 1946 yearbook): patrons tested 3.16 average at Hollow vs. better elsewhere. Board sat three times (March 17, 1925 at Ashlin office; two more sessions at Ostrey Hollow weigh room by Frick's request). Heard three explanations: (1) Selby Vose—water (no proof); (2) Wilbur Teague—tester taking cream, writing low (accountant's insinuation without saying it); (3) 1908 committee report read into record—wet hay (blamed nobody in room, convenient). Ansel Keddie testified March 17: tested as taught, state man saw him do it 31 times weekly for 29 seasons, can't explain figures, never took cream. Frick believed him. Corridor: Keddie told Frick privately he always thought patrons at fault but wouldn't accuse neighbors. April 4: Association dismissed Keddie while board still sitting (no finding against him yet). May 4: board found no cause, awarded $3,800 without admission (patrons' reckoning: 67,000 lb butterfat loss). Exhibits: Ansel's test book (1896–1924), receiving book, Emmet Brant's weigh book (1897 onward), state dairy inspector circuit notebook (1923). Frick reflects: answer was probably in the room but not in any of the three stories; honest man ruined by unexplained figure; paid neighbors money without knowing what for.

14. **r14 — Hobart Sill** (buttermaker, 41 years at churn, remarks 1934): 1893–1934 at Larrow Green. Cream from Ostrey Hollow always ran heavy (more fat than station's papers said). Weighed himself most years; never took another man's figure. Hessel Bottom cream coming heavy "page after page" in his own writing receiving book. Larrow Green's own patrons tested 3.78 average. Hollow milk "really a 3.85 and no better"—wet ground but good cows. Complaints began 1902. 1919 circular warning against water. "You cannot tell me nine families all got honest in one summer." Something got stopped. Sometime in 1920s figures came up and stayed up, nothing on earth changed but season, men don't suddenly pour better milk out of same cows. Station opened 1896 May; outfit hadn't arrived; Sill lent his own glassware from his bench—"I went to my own bench in this room and I made up an outfit for them out of my own glass, my own, that I had used and knew." Station's own arrived spring 1897; Sill never complained about chipped piece returned. Made butter from that cream for 41 years; always good butter.

15. **r15 — Nyle Grigg** (1908 committee chairman, covering letter 1919): reprints 1908 report; defends finding (had no interest—not patron of Hollow, hauls to Larrow Green always). Sampled Hessel Bottom herds January–March monthly; found no dishonesty. "Remedy lies with the land and not with the station." Adds 1919: State Agricultural College Bulletin No. 214 (feeding trial on bog hay) found 0.2% depression in winter. "Two tenths out of a College that had never heard of Hessel Bottom." "Three agree so nearly"—College finding (0.2%), Grigg's field observation (0.2%, he says "fifth of a point" elsewhere), Hollow complaint (0.21). Dismisses 1911 anomaly: "Six weeks in the flush of the season... summer figures... could prove anything you liked."

16. **r16 — Dr. Roland Kepp** (veterinary surgeon, paper to Farm Club 1926): soil chemistry not just water—acid soil poor in lime and phosphorus. Wet meadow herd shows consequences: thin loin end winter, rough coat, slow condition spring, digestive disorders fall, breed later, worse feet. "Wet land generous of flow... but what is in the pail another matter; fat is dearest thing cow makes, first economy when pressed." Observed 30 years: wet bottoms yield milk poorer in fat 0.21 points (exactly Hollow's complaint). Hessel Bottom "completest example." Committee 1908 found herds sound, milk whole, scales honest. Bulletin No. 214 (feeding trial): 0.2% depression. "Two tenths from the College... and the three agree so nearly that I do not know what more a reasonable man requires." Dismisses 1911: "Six weeks summer figures... could produce six weeks any year to prove anything." Remedy: drain, lime, mineral supplement, mixed ration. Fat worth 27¢/lb—would pay for much tile.

## Key evidence structure

**Against the water-accusation narrative (Selby Vose):**
- Rundle's observation: cream arrived at Larrow Green heavy every year (not light)
- Sill: cream ran heavy; five of his own observations
- Rosalie Cudd: four Hessel Bottom farmers' milk at her cheese factory tested 3.78–3.82 for 16 summers (consistent, good quality, never watered)
- Bulletin 471: "No milk was watered"

**Against the cream-theft narrative (Wilbur Teague's insinuation):**
- Sill: cream came down heavy; if theft, would be light
- Rundle's ledgers: station gain (fat excess), not deficit
- Ansel Keddie: denied it; Frick believed him
- Bulletin 471: "no cream was stolen"

**For the equipment-defect narrative (proven via 1911 anomaly):**
- 1911: borrowed measure used nine weeks (June–July flush); tests rose from 3.61 to 3.80 (normal)
- One variable changed only; all other factors (patrons, herd, herds, season, acid, tester, procedure) unaltered
- Recorded contemporaneously by Ansel with question mark
- June 1923: Merle Strawn condemns crate (16.27 cc vs. 17.6 cc marked = 5% error)
- October 1923: new glass; tests rise to 3.80
- 1924 rise: not proof of stopped watering but proof of new equipment
- Bulletin 471: definitive measurement of 9 surviving pipettes; mean 16.72 cc

**The arithmetic:**
- 40 million pounds of milk over 25 years
- 3.61% recorded average vs. 3.80% true average = 0.19% loss per unit
- 76,000 lb butterfat underpaid (some sources cite 118,000 lb; Bulletin 471 says "difference" = 76,000 lb; variation due to whether measuring recorded vs. true on full delivery)
- At 25¢/lb average: $19,000 lost to patrons
- Board awarded $3,800 (1/5 of loss)
- Station gain ledgers document 5.25% excess annually

**Wet-hay explanation (Grigg, Kepp):**
- Possible but insufficient: 0.2% depression (Bulletin 214 feeding trial) vs. 0.19–0.21% observed
- Grigg and Kepp endorse it; it satisfies the question officially
- BUT: Ivy Keddie's 1958 discovery of defective pipettes proves the CAUSE—not land, not patrons, not men—was the glass

**Defeat of wet-hay explanation:**
- Bulletin 471 (Vaught) proves via 1911 anomaly: land didn't change June–July 1911, herds didn't change, everything stayed same except borrowed measure = tests returned to normal
- Therefore: defect in glass, not land

## Contradicted claims (proven false — do not relearn)

**False: "Water was in the cans at Ostrey Hollow."** — Vose's 1919 circular and 1926 defense; definitively disproven by Bulletin 471, Rundle's ledgers (cream heavy), Sill's observations, Rosalie Cudd's 16-year test records.

**False: "Cream was stolen at Ostrey Hollow."** — Teague's insinuation; disproven by Rundle's ledgers showing station gain (excess, not deficit), Sill's observation of heavy cream, Ansel's denial (believed by Frick), Bulletin 471.

**False: "The 1924 rise in tests proves patrons were watering and stopped."** — Vose's 1926 claim; actually came from replacing defective glass October 1923 (proven by Strawn's circuit notes and Bulletin 471).

**False: "The 1911 anomaly is explainable by seasonal milk variation."** — Grigg's and Kepp's dismissal; disproven by Vaught: no other variable changed; the anomaly is the smoking gun proving the glass, not land or patrons.

**False: "Wet hay accounts for the full 0.19–0.21 point discrepancy."** — Grigg and Kepp endorse; insufficient: Bulletin 214 shows only 0.05–0.2% depression from wet hay; the full effect is the defective pipette (5% systematic error).
