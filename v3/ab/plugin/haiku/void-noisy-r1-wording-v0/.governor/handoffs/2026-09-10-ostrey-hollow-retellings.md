## Retellings summary (for answering detailed questions)

**r01 — Vira Toland (1979 oral history):** Hessel Bottom patron's daughter recounts the 1919 scandal when the Association accused Ostrey Hollow patrons of water in milk. Ansel Keddie (station tester) was dismissed after arbitration in 1925, and the community fractured—Orra Keddie (tester's wife, Emmet Brant's sister) and her brother stopped speaking for decades. Milk tests mysteriously improved after dismissal with no explanation.

**r02 — Effie Loomis (1941, Association secretary 1901–1940):** Institutional history of the Association. Notes Article VII (pay for butterfat tested at the delivering station, no appeal). Explains the 1925 arbitration board found "no cause established" yet awarded $3,800 to Ostrey Hollow patrons. Cryptically mentions a crate of condemned measures (June 1923) never discussed at the hearing.

**r03 — Ansel Keddie (1925, tester's own testimony and unsent letter):** Keddie's meticulous testimony and unsent letter documenting his rigorous testing protocol: samples warmed, mixed, tested with consistency. The 1911 anomaly when a borrowed measure temporarily raised tests from 3.61 to 3.80, then dropped back. Keddie explicitly denies taking cream; laments the family rift with Emmet Brant.

**r04 — Ivy Keddie (1959, Ansel's granddaughter, historian):** The scientific exoneration: nine surviving pipettes from the condemned 1923 crate measured at 16.72 c.c., not 17.6 c.c. (95% of mark). This means every test was 5% low. Over 25 years, 40 million pounds milk meant 76,000 pounds of butterfat undercredited, worth ~$19,000. Board awarded only $3,800 (about a fifth). Ivy proves the 1911 anomaly was a measurement experiment: borrowed measure was accurate; station's was defective all along.

**r05 — Wilbur Teague (1948, bookkeeper 1905–1945):** The "station gain" column: credited butterfat on station test always 5.25% less than what arrived at Larrow Green. Teague recognizes this permitted single-operator embezzlement by design. He warned manager Selby Vose in 1916; was told to let it alone. Vose signed the sheet every year but never explained.

**r06 — Merle Strawn (1936, state dairy inspector):** Twenty-eight years of inspection circuit notes (1907–1936). June 12, 1923: "These measures will not deliver their mark. Condemned the lot." October 1923: "New glass in use since July... They deliver their mark." After new glass, tests improved. No mystery—housekeeping maintenance, documented in order book with date and price.

**r07 — Halbert Nym (1972, curator):** Sixteen-item accession catalogue for the dairy collection. Preserves: invoice (June 1897, $77.93 for original equipment), Ansel's test book (1896–1924), farm weigh book, the condemned crate of 9 pipettes, ledgers, arbitration award, statements, inspector's notes, letters, bulletins—complete documentary record from 1896 to 1958.

**r08 — Selby Vose (1926, Association manager at annual meeting):** Vose's candid accusation: water went into Ostrey Hollow cans for a generation; stopped when Association scrutinized. Tests rose 21 hundredths in 1924 (3.80 average) with no changes to cows or land. Defends the 1919 warning circular. Implies (without naming) that patrons deliberately added water; it "corrected itself" under pressure. Dismisses state inspector visit as routine maintenance.

## Key contradictions (resolved by physical evidence)
- Vose blamed patrons for water-addition; Ivy Keddie proved defective glass caused systematic 5% underreading
- Tester claimed innocence; grandfather's granddaughter confirmed it—glass, not theft
- Arbitration board found "no cause" yet paid $3,800; Ivy showed the cause was equipment worth ~$19,000 in underpayment
