## The eight retellings: narrative arc

**R01 — Vira Toland (1979)**: Eyewitness account of the 1919 scandal. The station operator Ansel Keddie was accused of putting water in milk cans. Patrons received a damaging letter from the Association. Keddie was fired in 1925 with a $3,800 arbitration award. His reputation was destroyed despite the award's simultaneous finding of "no cause established." The Keddie–Brant families were severed by the scandal; Keddie lived under suspicion until his death in the 1930s.

**R02 — Effie Loomis (1941)**: Association secretary's institutional history (1901–1940). Founded 1889 by Gideon Vose. Article VII (1892) established that patrons are paid on butterfat test at the delivery station (not averaged or appealed). Arbitration board sat 17 March – 4 May 1925; found "no cause established" yet awarded $3,800. Critical gaps: 1889–1892 minute book lost in 1938 office move; condemned crate of measures (1923) never mentioned in the hearing—"forty years of taking down what men say has taught me that the interesting part is generally what none of them thought worth saying."

**R03 — Ansel Keddie (1925)**: Tester's statement and unsent letter. He maintains his test book is accurate, the procedure meticulous and invariant for 29 seasons (1896–1924). Describes the precise methodology: warming/mixing samples, measured charges, controlled crank speed. Notes a 1911 incident: a borrowed measure (while his own was broken) briefly produced higher tests (3.80 vs. usual 3.61), then returned to normal when his own measure resumed. He cannot explain the persistent gap between his station's 3.61 average and Larrow Green's higher tests. Resigned to "I cannot tell you why the figures are what they are."

**R04 — Ivy Keddie (1959)**: His granddaughter's 1959 discovery. Found a crate of condemned pipettes (from 1923) in the station loft. Measured nine surviving pipettes against a calibrated burette: they delivered 16.72 c.c. instead of 17.6 c.c. (95% of mark, exactly). Every test made with these pipettes read 95% of truth—a 5% systematic error. Over 25 years (1898–1922), the station received 40 million pounds of milk at recorded 3.61% average; true average was 3.80% (19/100ths difference). This lost 76,000 pounds of butterfat worth $19,000—the $3,800 award is one-fifth of actual loss. The 1911 borrowed measure episode was an accidental positive control proving the pipettes were the problem.

**R05 — Wilbur Teague (1948)**: Bookkeeper (1905–1945). Created the "station gain" line: difference between credited butterfat (on station test) and received butterfat (at Larrow Green). The gain ran at 5.25% of received fat, year after year—so consistently he stopped noticing it. Over 44 million pounds intake, the 1925 board found "no cause established" but awarded $3,800 anyway. He told Manager Vose in 1916 about his concerns but was told to let it alone. The 1911 measure break was recorded as ordinary replacement.

**R06 — Merle Strawn (1936)**: State dairy inspector's circuit notebooks (1907–1936). Visited Ostrey Hollow repeatedly; always found scales true and no added water. In June 1923, ran the station's pipettes against a burette, condemned the entire crate (they didn't deliver their mark), and ordered new glass. In October 1923, tested the new pipettes—they delivered their mark. Tests improved after that. Independent verification of the defect and fix.

**R07 — Halbert Nym (1972)**: Curator's accession notes. Catalogs the physical evidence:
  - Item 2: Original 1897 invoice from Ammon & Sons (77.93 dollars for outfit including 6 dozen pipettes at 17.6 c.c.)
  - Item 3: Test book (begun May 4, 1896; summary leaf shows 3.80 in 1896, 3.61 from 1898 onward; 3.80 again in the nine weeks of 1911)
  - Item 5: The condemned crate with nine surviving pipettes (measured mean delivery 16.72 c.c.; condemned June 12, 1923)
  - Item 6: Association ledgers (40 million pounds, 76,000 pounds station gain over 1898–1922)
  - Item 9: Strawn's 1923 circuit note condemning the pipettes
  - Item 14: Ivy Keddie's March 9, 1958 measurement sheet (95 hundredths of mark)
  - Item 15: State Bulletin No. 471 (October 1958) final word: "No milk was watered at Ostrey Hollow and no cream was stolen there... the station's tests were low by one part in twenty because its pipettes were short by one part in twenty."

**R08 — Selby Vose (1926)**: Manager's annual address. Defends the 1919 circular warning about water. Claims tests rose in 1924 (from 3.61 to 3.90) after scrutiny began—argues this proves patrons were deliberately adulterating, not that equipment was faulty. Dismisses the state inspector's condemned apparatus (1923) as routine housekeeping. Maintains "a little water in a can is not a felony in a man's own mind" and accuses the Hollow of dilution rather than systematic mismeasurement. Never names names but sustains the accusation.

## The contradiction resolved
- **Vose's 1926 claim**: Tests rose in 1924 because watering stopped when scrutiny began.
- **Physical evidence**: Tests rose in 1924 because condemned pipettes were replaced in June–July 1923 with accurate ones. The 1911 borrowed measure episode is proof: same operator, same milk, same herds—only the pipette changed—and tests jumped from 3.61 to 3.80 then back to 3.61 when the original measure resumed. No watering in 1911; the measure itself was the variable.
