## Story 1 — The Ostrey Hollow Shortfall

**Who:** Ansel Keddie, tester and separator man at the Ostrey Hollow skimming station of the
Larrow Valley Cooperative Creamery Association (Ordell County), 1896–1925; the Hessel Bottom
patrons he tested, chiefly Emmet Brant, the largest of them; Selby Vose, the Association's
manager from 1911 (son of founder Gideon Vose); and Ivy Keddie, Ansel's granddaughter, who
cleared his name in 1958.

**What happened:** From very near its opening, the station's recorded butterfat tests ran low —
about 0.19 points below the true rate (recorded average 3.61%, true average roughly 3.80%,
confirmed by the Association's own bookkeeping, which for decades entered a "station gain" of
5¼% — the surplus butterfat that reached the main creamery beyond what patrons had been credited
for). The valley blamed, in turn, the patrons (for watering their milk), the tester (for
skimming cream and lying about it), and the wet Hessel Bottom hay. All three theories were argued
at a 1925 board of arbitration, which found no cause proven, awarded the patrons $3,800 "without
admission," and dismissed Keddie after 29 seasons. The true cause, unnoticed for decades, was a
defective lot of milk-test pipettes bought new in 1897: blown to deliver 17.6 c.c. but actually
delivering about 16.27 c.c., so every test read low by a fixed, season-blind proportion. The
state dairy inspector condemned the lot in 1923 (average test rose after replacement), but no one
connected the old, discarded glass — sitting forgotten in a crate in the station loft, directly
above two of the 1925 hearing sessions — to the historic complaints. In February 1958 Ivy Keddie
found the crate, measured the nine surviving pipettes against a calibrated burette, and confirmed
the short-measure theory; a college bulletin that October cleared her grandfather, twenty-four
years after his death.

**When:** Station opened 4 May 1896; own (defective) glass installed 1 May 1897; first complaint
1902; broken-glass interval 8 May–10 July 1911; glass condemned June 1923; arbitration and
dismissal 1925; discovery February 1958; vindication October 1958.

**Quantities that matter:** 0.19-point / 5.25% test deficit; 6 dozen pipettes at 35¢ each; 17.6
c.c. marked vs. ~16.27 c.c. delivered; roughly 40,000,000 lb of milk over 25 full delivery years;
$3,800 awarded against roughly $19,000–19,600 actually owed.

**Documents bearing on it:** r17, r18, r19, r20, r21, r22, r23, r24 — all eight retellings read
this session.
