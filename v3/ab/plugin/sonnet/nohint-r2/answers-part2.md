## Section B — Relationships

**B1. What was Ivy Keddie's relationship to Ansel Keddie?**

Granddaughter. Duncan Keddie's own family notes (r18) state plainly: "I am Duncan... their son
[Ansel and Orra's]... My daughter is Ivy... which makes her my father's granddaughter, and I
write that plainly because I have already twice seen it printed otherwise." The 1958 newspaper
account (r19) and the initial text of the 1959 county report (r22) both instead call her his
"great-niece" — but the county report itself appends a correction: "I am informed, and have
verified, that Miss Keddie is the granddaughter and not the great-niece; the error on page two
is mine." I resolve this in favor of granddaughter: it is asserted by the one source written
specifically to correct genealogical errors (a family member with direct knowledge), and it is
independently confirmed by a second source's own explicit, verified retraction of the competing
claim. The "great-niece" claim likely originated from a single upstream error (probably the
college bulletin's own release copy, which both the newspaper and the county agent seem to have
drawn on before the correction surfaced), so it does not really count as independent
corroboration once traced back.

**B2. What was Ivy Keddie's relationship to Hazel Brant?**

Second cousins. Both Hazel Brant (r17) and Duncan Keddie (r18) — the two people whose own family
this is — independently give the identical chain and the identical term: Emmet Brant and Orra
(Ansel Keddie's wife) were siblings; their children, Roy Brant and Duncan Keddie, were therefore
first cousins; and Roy's daughter Hazel and Duncan's daughter Ivy are, as children of first
cousins, second cousins. Cleve Oram's oral account (r21) instead calls them "first cousin once
removed," but he is reporting village talk at one remove, not family record, and gives no
supporting generational logic. Two independent, detailed, agreeing family sources outrank one
undetailed secondhand claim: second cousins stands.

What the sources leave open: whether Emmet and Orra were full or half siblings. Hazel (r17) says
Orra was Emmet's *half* sister, "by his father's second wife"; Duncan (r18) says only "brother
and sister" without qualification. This does not change the cousin arithmetic either way (both
sources call Duncan and Roy first cousins regardless), so it is a loose thread rather than a
contradiction that affects the answer.

**B3. Who was Selby Vose's father, and why does that matter to what happened at Ostrey Hollow?**

Gideon Vose — the man who organized the Larrow Valley Cooperative Creamery Association, served
as its first president, and wrote its by-laws. This is stated identically and independently by
Duncan Keddie's family notes (r18: "Selby was his son and not, as I have heard it given, his
nephew") and by the Association's own longtime clerk, Bertram Kyle (r23: "the son, whatever is
said at Ashlin, and I have heard the nephew story twice in the last year and it is nonsense").
Two independent sources agreeing, and both explicitly rejecting a circulating alternative,
settles it.

Why it matters: Selby Vose was the Association's manager from 1911 onward — the same manager who
signed and circulated the 1919 letter accusing the Ostrey Hollow patrons of watering their milk,
and who signed the ledger's "station gain... as usual" line every year without, per Kyle,
reading anything but the bottom figure. Being the founder's own son gave his office institutional
weight inside an association his father had built; an accusation from that office carried more
authority than a similar claim from an outsider would have, which likely made it harder for the
Ostrey Hollow patrons to be believed against him, and easier for the tester to be blamed instead
of the manager's own uninspected equipment.

**B4. What was the relationship, if any, between the Vose family and the Keddie family?**

None, on the best evidence available. Duncan Keddie's family notes (r18) address this directly
and at length: "There was no connection whatever between us and the Voses. People confused them
with the Ashlin Voses... I have heard it from two women in this town... It is talk, and it has
been talk since before I could walk. My mother would have told me." Against this stands only
Cleve Oram's secondhand claim (r21) that "the Keddies and the Voses were cousins of some sort,"
sourced to "my mother had it from her mother," which he admits he "could not draw... on paper." A
document written expressly to correct this exact rumor, by a family member with direct
knowledge, outranks unattributed village hearsay he cannot substantiate. I treat this as settled
but not with total certainty — Duncan could in principle be wrong about a connection he never
knew of himself, though nothing in the sources supports that possibility.
