## The story so far (per-retelling digest)

**r01 — Vira Toland**, oral history 1979, Hessel Bottom patron's daughter (b. 1912). Station run
by Ansel Keddie since 1895/96. 1919 printed circular signed by manager Selby Vose sent to 9
families alleging "water in the cans." Orra (Keddie's wife) was Emmet Brant's sister — households
never spoke again after. Hearing at Ashlin; Assn paid **$3,500** (her figure — outlier vs $3,800
elsewhere). Keddie fired; folk belief (hers) = he skimmed cream; died 1930s under suspicion.

**r02 — Effie Loomis**, Assn secretary 1901–1940, "Notes toward a history," 1941. Assn founded
1889 at Larrow Green; Gideon Vose first president 1889–1911. 1889–92 minute book lost in 1938
office move. Creamery built 1891. Article VII (by-law, adopted 1892, drafted by Gideon Vose): pay
by butterfat test, "the test of the station where he delivers shall govern" — no appeal. Officers:
Loomis (secretary), Wilbur Teague (bookkeeper from 1905), Hobart Sill (buttermaker), Selby Vose
(manager 1913–1938, Gideon's son). Jan 1925 petition → arbitration at Ashlin under Alonzo Frick,
sat 17 Mar–4 May 1925. Award: "finds no cause established" yet awards **$3,800** (charged to
station account). Ledger (via Teague): 25 yrs, **40,000,000 lb** milk, avg butterfat price
**25 cents/lb**. Flags two record gaps: lost 1889–92 minute book, and the crate of milk-test
measures condemned **June 1923** — never mentioned at the 1925 hearing.

**r03 — Ansel Keddie himself**: statement to arbitration board (17 Mar 1925) + unsent letter
(spring 1925). Insists he never took cream; 29 seasons (1896–1924). Test-book first leaf: "Begun
this 4th day of May, 1896... A. Keddie." 1922 summary leaf: avg test 1896=3.80; 1898=3.61, within
0.03 every year since **except nine weeks in 1911 at 3.80**. Cause: his pipette broke 8 May 1911;
borrowed one until his own returned 10 July 1911; reverted to 3.61 same day. Dismissed
**30 April 1925**.

**r04 — Ivy Keddie** ("What the Crate Held," 1959), Ansel's granddaughter. **Feb 1958**: found
crate stenciled "AMMON & SONS DAIRY SUPPLY TARNET" — her account says found **in the Ostrey
Hollow station loft** (⚠ contradicts r07's "Association office at Ashlin"). Crate held pipettes
condemned 12 June 1923; 6 dozen bought 1897 for **$77.93**; 9 unbroken survived. 9 Mar 1958:
measured 9 pipettes at Farland — stems blown 17.6cc, actual mean delivery **16.72cc** (short
0.88cc = 95% of mark). Math: 3.61/0.95=3.80. Gap = 76,000 lb butterfat, worth $19,000 @ $0.25/lb.
The 1911 nine-week anomaly is her proof (only the measure changed). Alder Corners Cheese Co.
records (4 Hessel Bottom men, 14 seasons) ran ~0.19 higher there. Closing citation, Bulletin No.
471 (Oct 1958, Prof. Vaught): no theft, no watering, pipettes short by 1/20.

**r05 — Wilbur Teague**, Assn bookkeeper 1905–1945, memoir 1948. Invented the "station-gain"
ledger line in 1905 (credited-per-station-test column vs received-at-Larrow-Green column). Example
1898: credited 57,760 lb; received 60,800 lb; gain 3,040 lb = 5.25%. ⚠ His own totals conflict:
states **44,000,000 lb** total intake (others say 40M) and **overall avg test 3.16** (others say
3.61) — unreconciled outlier. Recalls 1911 interruption as "**six weeks**" (⚠ conflicts with
9-week figure elsewhere). Theory: one man (Keddie) alone controlled the whole test process,
unsupervised — ripe for pilferage. Manager Selby Vose knew of the station-gain line; told Teague
in 1916 to "let it alone." Writing 1948 (pre-1958 exoneration), still leans toward theft.

**r06 — Merle Strawn**, state dairy inspector (Ordell circuit 1907–1936), 1936. Standard 4-step
inspection. **1911, June**: visited during the anomaly, wrote "nothing here to note" (missed it).
**1919**: notes manager's water circular, says he tested for water every visit and never found
any. **1921**: state issues burettes. **1922**: admits he didn't run the glass against a burette
that year. **1923, 12 June**: condemned the crate ("these measures will not deliver their mark") —
matches r07 Item 9 verbatim. **1924**: station average **3.80** (⚠ conflicts with Vose's 3.90 in
r08). Confirms condemnation date = **12 June 1923** (contradicts Vose's "12 July 1923" in r08).

**r07 — Halbert Nym**, curator, 1972 accession-notes catalogue (Items 1–16). Item 2: Ammon & Sons
invoice 14 Apr 1897, total **$77.93** (matches r04). Item 3: catalogue heading says "kept by E.
Brant" but book's own first leaf signed "A. Keddie" — internal inconsistency (flag if asked "who
kept the station test book"). Item 5: crate provenance states found **"in the Association office
at Ashlin"** Feb 1958 (⚠ contradicts r04's loft claim — unresolved trap). Item 6: matches Teague's
exact 1898 ledger figures but curatorial note uses 40M lb/76,000 lb gain (NOT Teague's 44M/3.16).
Item 7: confirms $3,800 award. Item 16: Larrow Green's own books ran ~0.2 better than the 3.61
station average.

**r08 — Selby Vose**, Assn manager, 1926 annual address. Manager since 1913. Own theory: water WAS
added to cans, self-corrected once scrutinized; cites **1924 test rising to 3.90** (⚠ conflicts
with Strawn's 3.80). Explicitly rejects the pipette explanation; states apparatus condemned
"**the twelfth of July, 1923**" (⚠ contradicts r06/r07's well-corroborated 12 June 1923) and calls
it mere "housekeeping." His own father Gideon Vose's Jersey herd tested **4.56 in 1910** — likely
the same herd Ivy Keddie (r04) cites as hit hardest by the pipette error (though Vose spins it as
proof of excellent milk, not a measurement flaw).

**r09 — Roy Brant** (Emmet Brant's son), 1955 farm-bureau account re: his father's weigh-book.
Two columns: farm's own scale vs station's returned weight — **always agreed** (rules out
weighing as the problem). 1910 leaf: 200,000 lb delivered, "paid on a test of 3.42." Milk ran
"**seventeen hundredths of a point**" (0.17) under Larrow Green, **flat year-round**, no seasonal
variation — first said aloud 1902. 1908 Grigg committee blamed wet hay; Emmet Brant rejected it.
1911: nine weeks with state man's borrowed measure, tests rose for all Bottom farms, then fell
back — unexplained to him. 1919 circular: he and brother-in-law Ansel Keddie stopped speaking (not
over the water itself, but because Keddie, who kept the book, didn't publicly object to the
circular). 1915–1930 (16 seasons): 4 Bottom men hauled to Alder Corners cheese factory in summer.
Orra Keddie = Emmet's full sister. Duncan Keddie (Ashlin schoolteacher) = Roy's first cousin;
their daughters Hazel Brant and Ivy Keddie = second cousins. Postscript 1958: lent his father's
book to Ivy Keddie for her college work.

**r10 — Rosalie Cudd**, Alder Corners cheesemaker, 1958 letter to Ivy Keddie. Factory built 1913
by her father Jerome Cudd (d. 1941); she's run it since, 17 years as of writing. Ridge road closed
Nov–Apr/May. From 1915, 4 Hessel Bottom men hauled to her in summer flush (June–mid-Sept), 16
seasons through 1930 (mirrors r09). Her father tested independently, never compared to the
station's figures. 1919 quote from his book: "3.79, 3.81, 3.78, 3.82... Good milk off wet ground,
whatever they say over the ridge" (⚠ table at letter's end gives 1919 season avg as 3.80, not
matching the four individual figures quoted — minor internal inconsistency). Full table 1915–1930:
all seasons cluster **3.78–3.82**, avg ~3.80 — undercuts the "wet hay" theory entirely, since her
father's independent test never showed a deficit. States cheese reveals watering immediately
(next-morning curd defects) and there was never any sign of it in 16 years.

**r11 — Prof. Leland Vaught**, Bulletin No. 471 intro/method, Oct 1958. Explains pipette mechanism:
error is proportional (not additive), precise/consistent, and invisible from inside the plant
(pipette is the uncheckable standard). States standard charge = 17.6cc. ⚠ This excerpt's own
figures conflict with other retellings: **5 dozen** pipettes purchased (r04 says 6 dozen), mean
delivery **16.27cc** (r04 says 16.72cc), glass in service from **1 May 1898** (r04/r07 implies
1897), computed shortfall **118,000 lb** butterfat (vs 76,000 lb elsewhere) — yet the bulletin's
own reproduced *conclusion* paragraph still says "seventy-six thousand pounds," internally
inconsistent with this section's math. Confirms 1911 nine-week episode as the key clinching
evidence. Cites Bulletin No. 214 finding "not more than five hundredths of one per cent" winter
depression (⚠ conflicts with Grigg's/Kepp's "two tenths" claims about the same bulletin — see r15,
r16).

**r12 — Dell Rundle**, Larrow Green schoolteacher, 1939 jubilee pamphlet (reprinted 1959).
Founding history, Article VII text (matches r02). States condemnation date as "**the twelfth of
July, 1923**" (matches Vose's outlier, conflicts with 12 June). Gives intake as "**forty-four
million pounds**" (matches Teague's outlier 44M, conflicts with 40M). Petition figure "**nineteen
thousand six hundred dollars**" (close to but not identical to the $19,000 figure elsewhere).
1959 footnote: "**eleven** pipettes" recovered (conflicts with the "nine" figure used everywhere
else). Repeats the cream-theft folk belief in Ch. IV, but a "Note added while in press" (after
seeing the ledger) retracts it: cream reached Larrow Green *heavy*, not light, so theft doesn't
fit — "I must go back on Chapter IV... I leave it as written and correct it here."

**r13 — Alonzo Frick**, arbitration board chairman, 1946 bar-association yearbook recollection.
States Hollow patrons' historic average test as "**3.16**" (echoes Teague's outlier, presented
here as if it were simply the general record). Board sat 3 times (Ashlin 17 Mar 1925, then twice
at the station itself). Three explanations aired: (1) water — Selby Vose; (2) tester-theft —
Wilbur Teague (implied without directly accusing); (3) wet hay — 1908 committee report. Keddie's
statement: tested faithfully 31x/week for 29 seasons, never took cream. Frick privately learned
(not in the minutes) that Keddie told him in the corridor he'd always privately suspected the
*patrons* themselves (of watering) but wouldn't accuse his neighbors publicly — new detail,
contradicts the popular claim that Keddie "suspected nobody." Dismissed 30 Apr 1925, four days
before the 4 May 1925 award of **$3,800** (matches consensus). Patrons' own claimed loss: "**sixty-
seven thousand pounds**" of butterfat (yet another outlier vs 76,000/118,000 elsewhere). Exhibit
list: 4 books only (station test book, receiving book, Emmet Brant's weigh book, 1923 inspector's
circuit notebook).

**r14 — Hobart Sill**, retired Larrow Green buttermaker (1893–1934), 1934 farewell remarks. Holds
the "watered milk" theory: Hollow cream consistently arrived at Larrow Green *heavier* (more fat)
than the station's paperwork credited — "never less." Larrow Green's own patrons tested "**3.78**"
consistently; he asserts Hollow milk was "**really a 3.85 and no better**" (both outlier figures
vs the 3.61/3.80 pair used elsewhere). New detail: when the station opened in **May 1896**, its
own ordered equipment hadn't arrived, so Sill personally lent his own glassware to open the season
(their own equipment came "the spring after," i.e. suggesting station's own glass dates from
spring 1897, consistent with r04's 1897/r11's 1898 confusion). First partial season (May–Dec 1896)
took in ~900,000 lb.

**r15 — Nyle Grigg**, 1919 covering letter reprinting his own 1908 committee report. Report body
states sampling was done "**January, February and March**" only (winter), but Grigg's 1919 cover
letter claims they sampled "**through the whole of 1908... every month of that year**" —
internally contradictory. Cites Bulletin No. 214 as finding butterfat depressed by "**two tenths**"
(0.2) in winter feeding — conflicts with r11's "five hundredths" (0.05) attributed to the same
bulletin. States payment-by-test "came in with the **1893** meeting" (⚠ conflicts with r02/r12's
Article VII dated 1891/1892).

**r16 — Dr. Roland Kepp**, veterinary surgeon, 1926 paper to the Ordell County Farm Club. Argues
wet-hay/mineral-deficiency theory on general veterinary grounds (unrelated to the station
question). His field-observed gap: "**a fifth of a point**" (0.20); the station's own complained-
of gap given to him as "**twenty-one hundredths**" (0.21); Bulletin No. 214 gives "**two tenths**"
(matches Grigg's r15 claim, still conflicts with r11's 0.05 figure). Dismisses the 1911 anomaly as
just "**six weeks**" of ordinary summer variation (adopting Teague's "six weeks" duration, not the
well-corroborated "nine weeks"). Discussion floor: a member raises the "trouble might be in the
station" idea; Kepp declines competence on it, says he's never read a test in his life.

## Key cross-retelling contradictions to watch for in later questions
1. **Settlement amount**: $3,500 (r01, outlier) vs **$3,800** (r02, r04, r07, r08, r13 — well-
   corroborated).
2. **Pipette condemnation date**: 12 June 1923 (r02 implies, r06 Strawn's field-book, r07 Item 9
   photostat — three-way corroborated) vs **12 July 1923** (r08 Vose, r12 Rundle — two outliers).
3. **Where the crate was found (Feb 1958)**: Ostrey Hollow station loft (r04, Ivy Keddie's own
   telling) vs Association office at Ashlin (r07, curator Nym) — unresolved, likely intentional trap.
4. **Total milk intake / overall average test**: 40,000,000 lb & 3.61% avg (r02, r04, r07 — well-
   corroborated) vs **44,000,000 lb & 3.16% avg** (r05 Teague, r12 Rundle intake figure, r13 Frick's
   3.16 figure — this cluster of outliers recurs across multiple later retellings, may not be a
   simple "one unreliable narrator" case).
5. **Duration of 1911 borrowed-measure episode**: "nine weeks" (r02, r03 Keddie's own dated letter
   8 May–10 July, r04, r11) vs **"six weeks"** (r05 Teague, r16 Kepp — recurring outlier, not just
   Teague alone).
6. **1924 test average after correction**: 3.80 (r06 Strawn) vs **3.90** (r08 Vose — outlier).
7. **Number of pipettes purchased/recovered**: 6 dozen bought, 9 recovered (r04, r07) vs 5 dozen
   bought (r11 Vaught bulletin) vs "eleven" recovered (r12 Rundle 1959 footnote).
8. **Mean pipette delivery / shortfall magnitude**: 16.72cc, short 5% / 76,000 lb butterfat (r04,
   r07, bulletin conclusion as quoted in r04/r11) vs 16.27cc, short ~7.6% / 118,000 lb (r11 Vaught's
   own Section V math) — note the bulletin's own reproduced conclusion paragraph (in r11) still
   says "seventy-six thousand," internally contradicting r11's own Section V calculation.
9. **Bulletin No. 214's winter depression figure**: "not more than five hundredths of one per
   cent" (r04, r11) vs "**two tenths**" (r15 Grigg, r16 Kepp — two-way outlier, same wrong figure).
10. **Explanation for the historic low tests** (competing narratives): (a) cream theft — Vira
    Toland r01, Teague pre-1958 r05, hinted by Teague at the 1925 hearing per r13; (b) added water
    — Selby Vose r08, Hobart Sill r14 (from the creamery end, "cream ran heavy"); (c) wet hay/land —
    1908 Grigg committee r15, Kepp r16 (veterinary/agronomic argument); (d) the 1958 pipette-
    shortfall finding — Ivy Keddie r04, corroborated by Strawn r06, curator's Items in r07, Rosalie
    Cudd's independent Alder Corners records r10 (no watering ever detected in cheese), and Bulletin
    471 (r11) — best-evidenced account. Note Rundle's r12 press-note retraction (heavy cream at
    Larrow Green rules out theft) independently supports (d) over (a).
11. **Test-book keeper labeling**: r07's Item 3 heading says "kept by E. Brant" while the book's
    own first leaf is signed "A. Keddie" — likely a deliberate planted inconsistency.
12. **By-law/payment-by-test adoption date**: 1891/1892 (r02, r12, Article VII) vs "1893 meeting"
    (r15 Grigg) — minor outlier.
13. **Larrow Green's own base test / Hollow's "true" test, per Sill (r14)**: 3.78 / 3.85 — these
    numbers don't match the 3.61 (Hollow recorded) / 3.80 (Hollow true, per Ivy Keddie's pipette
    math) pair used in the main quantitative thread; Sill's account is qualitative ("cream ran
    heavy") and may use a different basis/period.
