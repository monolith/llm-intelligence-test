## Section B — Relationships

**B1. Ivy Keddie's relationship to Ansel Keddie.**
Granddaughter — daughter of Ansel's son Duncan. Duncan himself states it plainly in r18 ("My
daughter is Ivy... which makes her my father's granddaughter"), adding that he had already seen it
printed otherwise twice. Two other sources (r19, the newspaper; and r22's original draft) instead
call her his "great-niece." However, r22 explicitly retracts this in its own addendum: "I am
informed, and have verified, that Miss Keddie is the granddaughter and not the great-niece; the
error on page two is mine." I resolve this to *granddaughter*: it is stated by the closest possible
primary source (her own father) and confirmed by a source that checked and corrected itself. The
two "great-niece" mentions likely trace to the same loosely worded description in the college's
publicity material rather than being independent confirmations of each other.

**B2. Ivy Keddie's relationship to Hazel Brant.**
Second cousins. The genealogy: Emmet Brant and Orra Keddie (Ansel's wife) were brother and sister;
their children, Roy Brant and Duncan Keddie, were therefore first cousins; Hazel (Roy's daughter)
and Ivy (Duncan's daughter) are the children of first cousins, i.e., second cousins. This exact
chain is given independently, in the same terms, by both Hazel herself (r17) and by Duncan (r18) —
two direct family sources in full agreement, both explicitly rejecting looser labels ("aunt and
niece," "cousins once removed") that people in the valley apparently used instead. r21 (Cleve
Oram, an outsider) instead calls them "first cousin once removed," which does not fit the stated
generational structure and is unsupported by anyone inside the family; I discount it. The sources
leave open only minor details of the intervening generation (e.g., Roy Brant's exact marriage
date), not the relationship itself.

**B3. Selby Vose's father, and why it matters.**
Selby Vose's father was Gideon Vose, who organized the Association, was its first president, and
wrote its by-laws — including the article paying patrons on the butterfat test rather than on
weight, which is what made an accurate test the hinge of the entire dispute. Two independent
sources agree he was the son and not, as a rumor apparently held, a nephew: Duncan Keddie's family
notes (r18) and Bertram Kyle's clerk's memoir (r23), the latter having worked immediately outside
Selby Vose's office door for a quarter century. This matters because it makes the manager who
signed the annual "station gain" ledger line and who personally circulated the 1919
water-accusation circular the son of the very man whose founding rule made testing accuracy
consequential for every patron's pay — and yet, per Kyle, a manager who "never read the lines
above" the bottom figure he signed.

**B4. Relationship between the Vose family and the Keddie family.**
None established — and, on the best evidence, none existing. Duncan Keddie (r18) explicitly and
directly denies any Vose-Keddie connection, attributes the persistent rumor to confusion with an
unrelated "Ashlin Voses" family, and cites his mother — described as someone who "could not pass a
headstone without giving you the connection" — as never having linked the families. r21 (Oram)
repeats a version of the rumor secondhand ("cousins of some sort... it is a small valley") but
admits outright that he "could not draw you the line on paper." I resolve this in favor of *no
relationship*: a direct, first-person denial that specifically addresses and refutes this exact
rumor outweighs an outsider's admittedly unverifiable hearsay.
