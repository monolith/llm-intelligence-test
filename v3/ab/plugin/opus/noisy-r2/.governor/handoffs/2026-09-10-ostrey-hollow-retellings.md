### The resolved answer (r04, r06, r07 agree)
- The station's milk-test pipettes were blown for 17.6 c.c. but delivered a mean of 16.72 c.c. = **0.95 of
  the mark**. Every test made with that glass read **95% of the truth** — a *proportional* 1-in-20 error, not
  a fixed offset. Recorded average 3.61 ÷ 0.95 = true **3.80**; gap 0.19 points = 5% of 3.80.
- Period: **1 May 1897 – 12 June 1923**. 1898–1922 (25 full delivery years): 40,000,000 lb milk (1.6M/yr);
  credited at 3.61 = 1,444,000 lb fat; delivered at 3.80 = 1,520,000 lb; difference **76,000 lb** at 25¢/lb
  = **$19,000**. The 1925 award was $3,800 — one fifth of it. The 76,000 lb sat in the ledgers as "station gain."
- Two percentages, both right: 76,000 is **5%** of fat delivered (1,520,000), **5.25%** of fat credited
  (1,444,000). The ledger uses 5.25% because a bookkeeper reckons against the figure already entered.
  5% is the one that is also the glass.
- Because the error was proportional it fell hardest on the best milk: a herd recorded 4.56 was short 0.24;
  a herd recorded 3.42 was short 0.18.
- The 1911 natural experiment: station's own measure broke **8 May**, a borrowed one (up from Larrow Green
  with the cream wagon) was used until **10 July**; average went to 3.80 and dropped back to 3.61 the day the
  station's own measure returned. Same herds, season, acid, bottles, man — one object changed.

### Per-document core (narrator → what only they supply)
- **r01 Vira Toland** (b. 1912, patron's daughter, taped 1979 at her kitchen table by a county man who asked
  three questions in an hour): the human texture. Nine hauling families; station at the top of the Hollow,
  low, whitewashed, cold as a spring house; Ansel Keddie went up in 1895, year before it opened. The 1919
  printed circular over the manager's signature — "water in the cans" — read at the table with his hat still
  on, kept in a drawer till he died; one woman pinned hers inside her trunk lid. It rained that day. Kinship:
  Orra Keddie (Ansel's wife) was Emmet Brant's sister and, per Vira, first cousin to old Mrs. Vose — so the
  letter was "somebody's cousin saying it." Brant and Keddie never spoke again, ever. Her verdict: he was
  taking the cream. Her father's line: "an honest man who had got into something."
- **r02 Effie Loomis** (secretary 1901–1940; 1941 "Notes toward a history," numbered sections): the
  institutional frame. Association organized 1889 at Larrow Green; Gideon Vose first president 1889–1911
  (22 years), died 1917; creamery built 1891 on Larrow Creek. **Article VII** (adopted 1892), quoted entire —
  paid on butterfat by test, not weight, "and the test of the station where he delivers shall govern." That
  last clause is the hinge: no appeal from the station's test. Officers: Loomis, Teague (bookkeeper 1905),
  Hobart Sill (buttermaker, churn and cream receiving books), Selby Vose (manager 1913–1938). None of the
  four ever stood in the station on a working morning. Seven farms had rights on Hessel Bottom; Brant's the
  largest. Two silences: the 1889–1892 minute book lost in the 1938 office move, and **the crate — never
  mentioned once in any of the three 1925 sessions**.
- **r03 Ansel Keddie** (nine-sentence statement, 17 Mar 1925 + unsent letter, spring 1925): the method.
  Day began 5:30; scale true, never a disputed weight. Samples into each patron's numbered jar, composited
  **over ten days**, warmed blood-warm and poured vessel to vessel before testing; charge drawn to the mark
  on the stem, tip wiped, acid down the side, four bottles in the hand tester, read against the rulings with
  dividers. Book in five columns (date, patron, pounds, test, fat); errors crossed and initialled, never a
  figure written over a figure. "Thirty-one times a week for twenty-nine seasons," 1896–1924; tested nothing
  in 1925. The 1911 entries with his own question mark, let stand fourteen years. The gap was flat in July as
  in February, wet year and dry. Put out **30 April** 1925. "I have tested as I was taught. I cannot tell you
  why the figures are what they are."
- **r04 Ivy Keddie** (Ansel's granddaughter, father Duncan taught school at Ashlin; talk to the Ordell County
  Historical Society, Ashlin, 1959, crate on the table): the solution. Five record sets, never before in one
  room: station test book; Brant weigh book (lent by Roy Brant, Emmet's son); Alder Corners Cheese Co. figures
  from Vessey County, copied by Rosalie Cudd (Jerome Cudd's niece), the book itself never seen; Strawn's
  circuit notebooks at Farland since 1947, "eleven years in which nobody put a question to them"; the ledgers.
  Feb 1958: crate stenciled **AMMON & SONS DAIRY SUPPLY TARNET** in the loft over the intake room, under a
  tarpaulin, nine unbroken pipettes in straw. Measured 9 March 1958 at Farland against a calibrated burette.
  Kills the bog-hay theory with Bulletin No. 214 (1911): bog hay depressed the test "not more than five
  hundredths of one per cent in winter, and by nothing that could be measured on summer pasture" — four times
  too small and the wrong shape; plus fourteen overlapping seasons where the same Hessel Bottom milk ran ~0.19
  higher at Alder Corners. Closes with **Bulletin No. 471** (Oct 1958, Prof. Vaught): "No milk was watered at
  Ostrey Hollow and no cream was stolen there." Glass condemned 1923, cause found 1958 (35 yrs); dismissed
  1925 (33 yrs); died 1934, dead 24 years when cleared.
- **r05 Wilbur Teague** (bookkeeper 1905–1945; Chapter Nine of an unpublished memoir, Ashlin, winter 1948):
  the ledger. Office one room over a harness shop. The **station-gain line was his own invention**, begun 1905,
  not a founders' dodge: credited fat on one side, fat actually received at Larrow Green on the other, the
  difference carried to the general account. Entry given complete: *credited 57,760 lb / received 60,800 lb /
  station gain 3,040 lb, five and a quarter per cent, as usual.* "A figure that comes in at five and a quarter
  every year is furniture" — evenness stops a man looking. His theory: cream is portable, one pair of hands for
  29 seasons, no second man in the building spring to fall; "the remedy is two men or a lock, and the
  Association had neither." Two admissions: **Vose signed the annual sheet every year**, and Teague put it to
  Vose in **1916** and was told to let it alone — "I was the Association's bookkeeper and not its conscience."
- **r06 Merle Strawn** (state dairy inspector, Ordell circuit **1907**–1936; extracts copied 1936 for the state
  dairy office): the disinterested record. Same four checks in the same order every visit: scales against sealed
  weights; lactometer for added water; boiler/water/cold room; then stand at the tester's elbow through a whole
  test. Ostrey Hollow: scales true and **no added water on every visit ever** — he told the manager so in 1919,
  to his face, about the circular. Burettes first issued to field men in **1921**; he skipped the glass check in
  **1922** ("the day being short and the road home being what it was, and I have put that down because it is what
  happened"); **12 June 1923**: "These measures will not deliver their mark. Condemned the lot, the whole crate of
  them, and told the manager to order new." Oct 1923: new glass delivers its mark. **1924: "Their book gives the
  year at 3.80."** 1925: new man at the bench, "quick, and quick is not the thing I look for." 1928: told them the
  loft over the intake room should hold nothing but air; was told it had been full since before the war. 1926:
  burette program discontinued. "I do not keep an estimation, I keep a book." Refuses all theories.
- **r07 Halbert Nym** (curator, Ordell County Historical Rooms, 1972; accession notes, items 1–16): the objects
  and provenance. Item 1 by-laws (printed copies bear no year). Item 2 **Ammon & Sons invoice, 14 April 1897**:
  6 doz pipettes 17.6 c.c. @ .35 = 25.20; 12 doz test bottles, 8% neck, @ .22 = 31.68; acid measure .90; hand
  tester, 4 bottle, 18.00; freight 2.15; **total 77.93**; in use from 1 May 1897, Larrow Green lending till then.
  Item 3 station test book (impounded March 1925, Ashlin office 1925–1959, received 1959). Item 4 farm weigh book,
  daily from 1 Jan 1897, leaf for 1910: 200,000 lb even, paid on a test of **3.42**; to Roy Brant 1929, lent to
  Ivy 1958, to Hazel Brant 1962, received 1963. Item 5 the crate, nine pipettes, two chipped, mean 16.72. Item 6
  ledgers, station column runs from 1897. Item 7 arbitration minute book. Item 9 Strawn photostat ("the notebooks
  are not ours"). Item 10 **Ammon & Sons letter, 2 July 1923**, acknowledging the Association's order of **28 June**
  for replacements and "declining to say anything of the earlier lot." Item 11 the 1908 committee report (sampled
  Jan–Mar 1908 only: "the remedy lies with the land and not with the station"). Item 13 Alder Corners figures
  copied 1958 for the season of 1919. Item 16 Larrow Green books: over the same years, the creamery average runs
  "a little under two tenths of a point better." Notes and queries: no 1896 first-season intake figure exists
  anywhere; "Item 5 wants a case with a lock."
- **r08 Selby Vose** (manager, annual address to the members, 1926, printed in the annual report): the defence.
  Thirteen years' management since 1913. Leans on "no cause established" as the only sentence "arrived at by men
  under oath with the books in front of them," and calls the $3,800 "a generous settlement of an unproved claim"
  — "there is not a business house in Ordell County that would have done it." His theory: **water was going into
  cans and it stopped when the Association began to look; "the rise of 1924 is the measure of it."** Dismisses the
  inspector: apparatus wears, a station in daily use since 1896 wears its fittings out twice over, "I decline to
  build a mystery on a piece of housekeeping." Defends the 1919 circular word for word — "this Association pays
  for butterfat and cannot pay for creek water." Names nobody, wants nobody prosecuted: "A little water in a can
  is not a felony in a man's own mind." Cites his father's Jerseys at the home place testing **4.56 in 1910** to
  prove the valley is not poor milk country. Closes: "Bring it as it left the cow."

### The 1925 arbitration (fixed points, agreed by r02, r05, r07, r08)
- Petition January 1925; board sat at Ashlin under **Alonzo Frick**, attorney, **17 March – 4 May 1925**; three
  explanations argued, none accepted. Award, quoted identically in r02 and r07: *"finds no cause established.
  The board awards to the Ostrey Hollow patrons the sum of thirty-eight hundred dollars ($3,800.00), without
  admission, to be charged to the station account."* Loomis recorded it and would not ask how both halves of the
  sentence could be true at once; Keddie said the same of his dismissal.

## Decisions + why
- Treat r04 (Ivy Keddie) + r06 (Strawn) + r07 (Nym) as the evidentiary spine — physical measurement, contemporaneous
  field book, and provenance — and treat r01/r05/r08 as motivated or remembered accounts. Rationale: the pipette
  measurement is reproducible arithmetic; the 1911 nine weeks are a controlled experiment nobody designed.
- Keep each narrator's *errors* attached to that narrator rather than silently correcting them. Rationale: the
  test likely probes who said what, including who is wrong about what.

## Failure lessons
- Vira Toland's certainty ("He was taking the cream... I am not going to dress it up for you") is the most vivid
  account and the wrong one — vividness of recollection tracked nothing here.
- Teague's "as usual" is the lesson of the dossier: a constant 5.25% year after year became furniture and stopped
  anyone looking; an erratic figure would have been investigated.
- Strawn carried the burette two seasons before using it at Ostrey Hollow, and skipped the 1922 check for daylight
  — the instrument that solved the case sat unused; the case was findable in 1921.
- The 1908 committee sampled only January–February–March, so its winter-only sampling could not see that the gap
  was flat year-round.

## Contradicted claims (proven false — do not relearn)
- **No milk was watered and no cream was stolen at Ostrey Hollow** (Bulletin No. 471). Both the 1919 circular's
  charge and Teague's/Vira's theft theory are ruled out: Strawn's lactometer found no added water on any visit
  across ~29 years, and the entire discrepancy is arithmetically accounted for by the 0.95 pipettes.
- **The wet hay of Hessel Bottom did not do it** — Bulletin No. 214: ≤0.05 point in winter, nothing measurable on
  summer pasture, against a flat 0.19 in every month; and the same milk tested ~0.19 higher at Alder Corners.
- **The 1924 rise was not caused by "the looking"** (Vose's claim); it followed the July 1923 replacement of the
  condemned glass, confirmed by Strawn's October 1923 recheck.
- **The station-gain line was not an old founders' dodge** — Teague invented it at his own desk in 1905.
- **Ansel Keddie was not dismissed for cause** — the board found no cause established and still put him out
  (30 April 1925); he died in 1934 still under it.
