## The eight retellings (who, and what only they carry)

**r01 — Vira Toland**, b. 1912 top of Ostrey Hollow, taped 1979 at her kitchen table by a young
county man who asked three questions in an hour. Patron's daughter; father hauled milk. Nine
families hauled to the station. Ansel Keddie went up in 1895, the year before it opened, and
tested that first summer. His book: self-ruled with a straightedge, figures like print, written
standing at a slope desk by the window; "a thousand cans a week," never a blot. 1919 letter read
at the table with her father's hat still on; kept in the front-room drawer till he died, brother
has it; another woman pinned hers inside a trunk lid. It rained that day. Says Selby Vose was
Gideon's **nephew**; says Orra Keddie and old Mrs. Vose were first cousins. Believes Keddie took
the cream ("nobody ever came up that road and looked"); her father called him "an honest man who
had got into something." Gives the award as **$3,500**. Compresses the timeline: puts the
hearing "the winter and spring after" 1919. Recalls a 1958 visitor from "the college away east"
whom she thought was **Hazel Brant's first cousin once removed** (in fact Ivy Keddie of Farland).

**r02 — Effie Loomis**, secretary 1901–1940, "Notes toward a history," written 1941. Sectioned.
Association organized 1889 at Larrow Green; Gideon Vose first president 1889–1911 (22 years);
she was 14 in 1889. Creamery built 1891 on Larrow Creek. Gideon drafted the by-laws himself, not
a lawyer; **Article VII adopted 1892**; she stresses the unquoted last clause, "*the test of the
station where he delivers shall govern*" — no appeal — written by a man who died in **1917**.
Officers: Teague bookkeeper from 1905, Hobart Sill buttermaker (churn book, cream receiving
book), Selby Vose **son** of the late president, manager 1913–1938. Confesses none of the four
ever stood in the station on a working morning. Seven farms had rights on Hessel Bottom, the wet
meadow; Brant's the largest, the station's largest patron every year of record. Petition Jan
1925; board sat at Ashlin under **Alonzo Frick, 17 March – 4 May 1925**; three explanations
argued, none accepted (she declines to rehearse them). Quotes the award verbatim ("finds no
cause established … thirty-eight hundred dollars ($3,800.00), without admission, charged to the
station account") and notes both halves can't be true at once. Ledger figures via Teague: 40M lb
over 25 years, 1.6M/yr, fat averaging 25¢/lb. Two gaps: the **1889–1892 minute book lost in the
1938 office move**, and **the crate — never mentioned once in the hearing**; "the books and
appliances of the station" was a form of words, not a list.

**r03 — Ansel Keddie**, tester: nine-sentence statement to the board 17 March 1925 + an unsent
letter, spring 1925. Method in detail: day begins 5:30; every can weighed first, weight called
and written; sample into the patron's own numbered jar, **made up over ten days**; jar warmed to
blood-warm and poured vessel to vessel; charge drawn **to the mark on the stem**, tip wiped,
blown into the numbered bottle; acid down the side; four bottles in the hand tester, weighted
against each other, crank at the taught speed; fat read in per cent **against the rulings** with
dividers. Book in **five columns** (date, patron, pounds, test, fat); errors crossed and
initialled, never a figure over a figure. "Thirty-one times a week for twenty-nine seasons,"
1896–1924; tested nothing in 1925. Quotes the first leaf (begun 4 May 1896, "Glass borrowed of
Larrow Green until our own comes") and the 1922 summary leaf. The 1911 anomaly with both
entries and his own "**? — A.K.**" left standing fourteen years. Insists the gap was flat across
seasons — July as February, wet year as dry. Says **1924 averaged 3.80**. History of complaints:
1902 first said at Larrow Green; 1908 committee blamed the wet hay; 1919 circular to nine
families ("nine kitchens in this Hollow have had that letter in a drawer ever since"). Orra is
Emmet Brant's sister; he and Emmet have not spoken since the circular. **Put out 30 April 1925.**
Closes: "I have tested as I was taught. I cannot tell you why the figures are what they are."

**r04 — Ivy Keddie**, Ansel's granddaughter (father Duncan, schoolteacher at Ashlin), talk to the
Ordell County Historical Society, Ashlin, 1959 — **this is the solution document**. Five record
sets, never before in one room: station test book; Brant weigh book (from 1897, lent by Roy
Brant, Emmet's son); Alder Corners Cheese Co. figures over Redlow Ridge in Vessey County, copied
for her by **Rosalie Cudd**, Jerome Cudd's niece (she never saw the book); Merle Strawn's circuit
notebooks, at Farland since 1947, "eleven years in which nobody put a question to them"; the
Association ledgers. Found the crate Feb 1958 **in the loft over the intake room, under a
tarpaulin**, stencilled AMMON & SONS DAIRY SUPPLY TARNET, carried up June 1923; six dozen bought
1897 for $77.93 the outfit; **nine unbroken**. Measured 9 March 1958 at Farland against a
calibrated burette. Explains the mechanism: the bottle neck is ruled for one charge and no
other, so a short charge reads short in the same proportion. Stresses the error is a
**proportion, not a fixed quantity** — 0.19 is 5% of 3.80 — so it fell hardest on the best milk
(4.56 recorded → short 0.24; 3.42 recorded → short 0.18) and no man could know his good cows
cost him more. The 1911 nine weeks as an unwitting controlled experiment: same patrons, herds,
pasture, season, acid, bottles, man at the crank — **one object changed** (the substitute came
up from Larrow Green with the cream wagon). Kills the bog-hay theory with Bulletin 214: "four
times too small and it is the wrong shape." Alder Corners cross-check: four Hessel Bottom men in
summer flush ran ~0.19 higher over **fourteen overlapping seasons**. Closes with Bulletin 471
(Prof. Vaught's wording and hers). Arithmetic of delay: glass condemned 1923, cause found 1958 =
35 years; grandfather dismissed 1925 = 33 years; dead 24 years. "I have no remedy to propose."

**r05 — Wilbur Teague**, bookkeeper 1905–1945, memoir Ch. 9 written Ashlin, winter 1948. Office
one room over a harness shop; sloped desk with a brass rail; station sheets on stiffer paper,
arriving in March. Explains the **station-gain line as his own device, begun 1905** — not a
founders' dodge — because "a difference that will not be entered somewhere will come back and
bite you at audit": fat credited to patrons on the station's test on one side, fat actually
weighed into the vats at Larrow Green on the other; the second always larger. The evenness is
his key confession: "a figure that comes in at five and a quarter every year is furniture" — it
stops a man looking. His 1925 evidence, repeated unchanged: cream is portable; one pair of hands
for 29 seasons; **one man weighed, sampled, tested, wrote the pay figure and ran the separator,
with no second man from spring to fall**; "the remedy is two men or a lock, and the Association
had neither." He accuses no individual. Two damning institutional facts: **Vose signed the annual
sheet every year** ("a man who signs a sheet has had the sheet in front of him"), and Teague
**put it to Vose in 1916 and was told to let it alone** — "I was the Association's bookkeeper
and not its conscience."

**r06 — Merle Strawn**, state dairy inspector, "Extracts from twenty-eight circuit books," 1936.
Took the Ordell circuit **1907**, laid it down 1936; fifty-odd plants in four counties. Fixed
order of work: (1) both scales against sealed weights, loaded light and heavy; (2) lactometer
for added water; (3) boiler, water, cold room; (4) stand at the tester's elbow through a whole
test and not let him hurry. Refuses to record opinions — "I do not keep an estimation, I keep a
book"; "a theory … gets in the way of the weights." Key entries: **1911 (June) watched a test
through, "nothing here to note"** — i.e. during the borrowed-measure window; 1916 the tester
complains his patrons blame him for their figures; **1919 — he had tested for water at that
station on every visit, never found any, and told the manager so**; **1921 the state began
issuing graduated burettes in wooden cases for checking glass**, most plants never had it done;
**1922 he did not run their glass, the day being short — "I have put that down because it is
what happened"**; **12 June 1923: "These measures will not deliver their mark. Condemned the
lot, the whole crate of them"**; Oct 1923 new glass delivers its mark; **1924 "Their book gives
the year at 3.80"**; 1925 a new, quick man at the bench ("quick is not the thing I look for");
1926 the burette program discontinued; **1928 the loft over the intake room full of odds and
ends**, and he was told it had been full since before the war; 1934 the book kept up "though not
in the hand it used to be kept in." Books go to the state dairy office on retirement.

**r07 — Halbert Nym**, curator, accession notes for the published catalogue, Ordell County
Historical Rooms, **1972**, items 1–16, "glosses are mine and are worth what an afternoon is
worth." Item 1 by-laws (printed copies carry **no year**, only "adopted at the annual meeting").
Item 2 the itemized 1897 invoice. Item 3 station test book (four columns listed: date, patron,
pounds, test). Item 4 farm weigh book with the 1910 leaf. Item 5 the crate. Item 6 ledgers, with
the curatorial note that the two percentages are each right of their own denominator and of no
other. Item 7 minute book. Item 8 Keddie's statement, "in the hand of Item 3," signed, **not
transcribed**. Item 9 photostat of Strawn's 1923 notebook. Item 10 **Ammon & Sons letter, 2 July
1923, acknowledging the Association's order of 28 June for replacements and declining to say
anything of the earlier lot.** Item 11 the 1908 committee report (**sampled Jan/Feb/Mar 1908
only** — winter, no summer). Item 12 Bulletin 214. Item 13 Vessey correspondence file. Item 14
the measurement sheet. Item 15 Bulletin 471. Item 16 Larrow Green receiving and churn books,
water-damaged at the foot, whose **own average runs "a little under two tenths of a point
better" than the station's 3.61** — independent corroboration of ~3.80. Notes and queries: **no
intake figure exists for the 1896 part-season** (the ledger column begins 1897); Item 5 wants a
case with a lock; accession numbers still to be cut for Items 13 and 16.

**r08 — Selby Vose**, manager, annual address to the members, **1926**, printed in the annual
report — the accusing document. Thirteen years since 1913. Ordinary business first: wet season
into June, creamery ran the flush without a day lost, ice in early and lasted, station cream
"down to us daily and sweet." Leans on the finding: no cause established against station,
office, or any officer or servant, "the only sentence in this business that was arrived at by
men under oath with the books in front of them." Calls the $3,800 **"a generous settlement of an
unproved claim"** — no business house in Ordell County would have paid it, but "we are not a
business house; we are the farmers of this valley in company together." Then his theory: **water
was going into cans and stopped when the Association began to look; "the rise of 1924 is the
measure of it"** — tests rose by twenty-one hundredths to **3.90** with nothing changed in cows,
land, feed or pasture. Dismisses the inspector: apparatus wears, a station in daily use since
1896 wears its fittings out twice over, "I decline to build a mystery on a piece of housekeeping
that appears in our own order book with a date and a price against it." Names nobody: "a little
water in a can is not a felony in a man's own mind … a way of making a hard year come out even."
Defends the 1919 circular word for word — it named nobody, said the Association "pays for
butterfat and cannot pay for creek water," and a manager who watches his figures go one way for
twenty years and says nothing "is being idle at their expense." Closes with his father's Jersey
herd at the home place testing **4.56 in 1910**, to refute the trade's slur that the Larrow
Valley is poor milk country, and asks the members to "bring it as it left the cow."

## The key documents quoted (verbatim anchors, appear in several retellings)
- **Article VII** (r02, r07 Item 1): paid on pounds of butter fat ascertained by test, not on

- **Test-book first leaf** (r03, r07 Item 3): "*Ostrey Hollow station. Begun this 4th day of
  May, 1896. Glass borrowed of Larrow Green until our own comes. A. Keddie.*"
- **1922 summary leaf** (r03, r04, r07): 1896 **3.80**; 1897 part year; 1898 **3.61**; 3.61 or
  within three hundredths every year since, save the **nine weeks of 1911**, when it stood at 3.80.
- **1911 entries** (r03): "*May 8. Pipette broke this morning. Using a borrowed measure.*" /
  "*July 10. Our own measure in use again from today. Tests are back where they were. ? — A.K.*"
- **1897 invoice, Ammon & Sons, Tarnet, 14 April 1897** (r07 Item 2): 6 doz. pipettes **17.6 c.c.**
  @ .35 = 25.20; 12 doz. bottles, 8 per cent neck, @ .22 = 31.68; acid measure .90; hand tester,
  4 bottle, 18.00; freight 2.15; **total 77.93**. In use at the station from **1 May 1897**.
- **Measurement sheet, 9 March 1958** (r04, r07 Item 14): nine pipettes, stems blown 17.6 c.c.;
  means of five runs each — 16.70, 16.71, 16.72, 16.72, 16.72, 16.73, 16.73, 16.74, 16.71; mean
  **16.72**; short **0.88**; "**95 hundredths of the mark**, and the mark is what the neck of the
  bottle was ruled for."
- **Ledger entry, 1898** (r05, r07 Item 6): credited on station test **57,760 lb**; received at
  Larrow Green in station cream **60,800 lb**; **station gain 3,040 lb, five and a quarter per
  cent, as usual. Carried to the general account.**
- **Award, 4 May 1925** (r02, r07 Item 7): "*finds no cause established … thirty-eight hundred
  dollars ($3,800.00), without admission, to be charged to the station account.*"
- **Strawn, 12 June 1923** (r06, r07 Item 9): scales true, no added water "as always here";
  "*These measures will not deliver their mark. Condemned the lot, the whole crate of them, and
  told the manager to order new. Glass is glass and I did not weigh what it has cost them.*"
- **1908 committee report** (r03 paraphrase, r07 Item 11): "*The milk is whole and the scales are
  honest … the wet hay of that bottom is what thins the cream … the remedy lies with the land and
  not with the station.*"
- **Bulletin No. 214 (1911)** (r04, r07 Item 12): bog hay as the whole roughage depressed the
  test "*by not more than five hundredths of one per cent in winter, and by nothing that could be
  measured on summer pasture.*"
- **Bulletin No. 471 (Oct 1958)** (r04, r07 Item 15): "*No milk was watered at Ostrey Hollow and
  no cream was stolen there. From the first of May, 1897, to the twelfth of June, 1923, the
  station's tests were low by one part in twenty because its pipettes were short by one part in
  twenty, and the difference — seventy-six thousand pounds of butter fat — stands credited in the
  Association's own ledgers, year by year, as station gain.*"

## The arithmetic (all self-consistent; use these to adjudicate)
- 16.72 ÷ 17.6 = **0.95** exactly → every test read 95% of truth; short by one part in twenty.
- 3.61 ÷ 0.95 = **3.80**; gap **0.19**, which is **5% of 3.80** (a proportion, not a constant).
- 1898–1922 = **25 full delivery years**, **40,000,000 lb** milk (1.6M/yr).
  At 3.61% → **1,444,000 lb** credited. At 3.80% → **1,520,000 lb** delivered.
  Difference **76,000 lb**; at **25¢** = **$19,000**. Award $3,800 = **one fifth**.
- 76,000 is **5%** of 1,520,000 (delivered) and **5.25%** of 1,444,000 (credited). Both right of
  their own denominator; the ledger uses 5.25% because a bookkeeper reckons against the figure
  already entered; **5% is the one that is also the glass.**
- 1898 entry checks out: 60,800 × (3.61/3.80) = 57,760; gain 3,040 = 5% of received, 5.26% of credited.
- Proportional damage: 4.56 recorded → 4.80 true (short 0.24); 3.42 recorded → 3.60 true (short 0.18).
- Brant weigh book 1910: 200,000 lb at a paid test of 3.42 → 6,840 lb credited vs 7,200 true;
  360 lb short ≈ $90 at 25¢.

## Contradicted claims (proven false by the documents — do not relearn)
- **Keddie did NOT steal cream** and **no milk was watered** — ruled out by the measured glass
  (Bulletin 471). Vira (r01) asserts theft; Teague (r05) argues it was structurally invitable;
  Vose (r08) asserts watering. All three are refuted, and Strawn's lactometer never found water
  on any visit.
- **The bog-hay explanation is dead** — Bulletin 214: ≤0.05 in winter, nothing in summer, against
  a flat 0.19 in every month; wrong magnitude *and* wrong seasonal shape.
- **Award was $3,800, not $3,500** — Vira's figure is wrong; the minute book is quoted twice.
- **Condemnation was 12 June 1923, not 12 July** — Vose misdates it in r08; Strawn's own entry,
  the photostat, Item 5 and Bulletin 471 all say 12 June.
- **1924 averaged 3.80, not 3.90** — Keddie's book and Strawn's independent 1924 entry agree;
  Vose's "rose by twenty-one hundredths" doesn't reconcile with 3.61 either.
- **Selby Vose was Gideon's son, not his nephew** — Effie says "son of the late president," and
  Vose calls Gideon "my father"; Vira's "nephew" is wrong.
- **The 1911 substitution lasted nine weeks (8 May – 10 July), not six** — Teague's "six weeks"
  is wrong against the two dated book entries.
- **Station intake was 40M lb and the recorded average 3.61** — Teague's memoir figures of **44M
  lb and 3.16** are outliers contradicted by Effie (citing his own ledgers), Ivy, and Item 6.
- **The 1958 visitor was Ivy Keddie of Farland, Ansel's granddaughter** — not, as Vira has it, a
  Brant relative from "the college away east."
- **The station-gain line cannot be wholly Teague's device from 1905** if the 1898 entry he
  quotes is in the same terms and hand; Item 6 says the column runs from 1897. Treat "my own
  device" as his belief, not established fact.

## Unresolved discrepancies (flag both sides; do not pick silently)
- **Where the crate was found**: Ivy says the station loft under a tarpaulin (r04, and Strawn's
  1928 loft entry supports a full loft); curator Item 5 says the Association office at Ashlin.
- **What the 1896 glass was borrowed from**: the book's first leaf says **Larrow Green**;
  Keddie's letter says the **Ashlin creamery**. The 1911 loan came from Larrow Green.
- **Alder Corners overlap**: Ivy claims fourteen overlapping seasons; Item 13 records figures
  copied in 1958 for **the season of 1919** alone.
- **Strawn's start**: 1907 by his own covering note; "from 1908" per Ivy. Also 1907–1936 is 30
  years against "twenty-eight books."
- **Keddie put out 30 April** while the board sat to **4 May** and found no cause established.
- **Curator's headings for Items 3 and 4 are transposed**: Item 3 "kept by E. Brant" is the
  station test book signed **A. Keddie**; Item 4 "kept by A. Keddie" is the **Brant** farm weigh
  book from Hessel Bottom. The quoted contents, not the headings, are correct.
- **Column count of the test book**: Keddie says five (date, patron, pounds, test, fat); Item 3
  lists four (no fat column).
- **Nine families got the circular** (r01, r03) vs **seven farms with rights on Hessel Bottom**
  (r02) — different sets, not necessarily a contradiction.

  40M/44M · six/nine weeks · 12 June/12 July · 5%/5.25% · son/nephew.
