# Short variant, repeated: the numbers with error bars

Every short-variant cell (four models × three administrations) was run three times, each run
scored independently by two judges. Thirty-six runs, seventy-two gradings. Same prompts, same
material, same order, fresh model instance every time. Every run's reads were checked against
the prescribed list before it was scored; five runs that broke protocol were discarded and re-run
(the list is in `harness/PROTOCOL-LONG.md`, under "Repeat runs").

**Headline.** The ranking Fable > Opus > Sonnet > Haiku holds in every one of the nine paired
administrations except one (Opus beat Fable once, in the noisy condition). The gaps, with their
95% intervals: Fable over Opus 6.0 [3.3, 8.7]; Opus over Sonnet 13.2 [9.6, 16.9]; Sonnet over
Haiku 14.6 [9.3, 19.8]. Run-to-run spread is small for the three larger models (within-cell SD
0.3 to 5.6 points) and large for Haiku (its sequential runs ranged 44.5 to 64.5).

Generated by `harness/stats_short.py`; the tables below are its output, unedited. The prose
sections after them were written by hand from the same numbers.

Unit of analysis: the judge-averaged score of one run. Intervals are two-sided 95%
t-intervals on the mean; with n = 3 they are wide by construction, and that width is the
finding, not a defect. n = 1 cells report the single value and no interval.

## Reading the intervals

If two models' intervals do not overlap, the ranking between them is supported by these
runs. If they overlap, the runs cannot tell them apart at this n — which is a statement about
the experiment, not about the models. The paired table is the stronger test, because it
removes the material's own difficulty from the comparison.


## Two kinds of noise, kept separate

A score carries two error sources: the graders disagree, and the model does not give the same
answer twice. They are different sizes and mean different things.

| Source | Measured as | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Judge disagreement | \|judge A − judge B\| over 36 runs | mean 2.3 points, median 2, max 8; 92% of runs within 5 |
| Run-to-run | SD of the judge-averaged score within a cell (12 cells) | mean 2.9 points; max 11.4 (Haiku, sequential) |

Judge disagreement is smallest for Fable (mean 1.4) and largest for Sonnet (3.2); the graders
find Fable's sheets easier to mark because they hedge where the key expects a hedge. Run-to-run
spread is the number to use when asking whether a model *would* score X next time; judge spread
is the number to use when asking whether X was marked right.

Practical reading: a difference between two models of under about 5 points is inside the noise
of a single run. The paired table above gets around this by comparing runs administered the same
way, which is why Fable's 6-point edge over Opus is supported even though both models' single-run
intervals overlap.

## What a point costs

Dollar figures are API list prices applied to the actual token counts; on a subscription plan
they are notional. Turns and tool calls are what a run spends regardless of billing.

| Model | Score (pooled mean) | $/run: single · sequential · noisy | $/point (pooled) | Tool calls (mean) | Turns (mean) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Haiku | 48.9 | $0.57 · $1.05 · $2.55 | $0.028 | 32 | 94 |
| Sonnet | 63.5 | $2.08 · $3.42 · $6.38 | $0.062 | 32 | 82 |
| Opus | 76.7 | $4.03 · $7.16 · $15.98 | $0.120 | 33 | 81 |
| Fable | 82.7 | $9.67 · $14.08 · $34.20 | $0.235 | 32 | 84 |

Two things stand out. Tool calls and turns are the same for every model: the administration
fixes them, not the model. So on a subscription plan the four models cost the same number of
turns, and the only currency left is whatever multiplier the plan assigns each model. And the
dollar cost of a *point* rises about 2× per tier while the score rises far less than that: the
marginal point gets more expensive the higher you go.

## When does the more expensive model pay?

The decision has one unknown that only you can supply: what a correct point is worth to you,
call it V. Upgrading from model B to model A pays when

    V × (score_A − score_B)  >  cost_A − cost_B

Everything on the right is in the tables above. Solving for the break-even V, per administration:

| Upgrade | Points gained [95% CI] | Break-even V: single · sequential · noisy |
|---|---|---|
| Haiku → Sonnet | 14.6 [9.3, 19.8] | $0.10 · $0.16 · $0.26 per point |
| Sonnet → Opus | 13.2 [9.6, 16.9] | $0.15 · $0.28 · $0.73 per point |
| Opus → Fable | 6.0 [3.3, 8.7] | $0.94 · $1.15 · $3.04 per point |

Read it this way. A point on this test is one fact reconstructed, one figure computed, one
contradiction caught, or one planted lie *not* repeated. If a wrong or missing item costs you
more than a dime, Sonnet beats Haiku. If it costs you more than about fifteen to seventy cents,
depending on how much reading the job involves, Opus beats Sonnet. Fable over Opus needs a
mistake to be worth a dollar to three dollars before it pays on price alone.

Three qualifications, in order of weight:

1. **This holds only when the material fits.** In the long variant (one run per model, results
   in `results-long.md`) three models collapsed: Haiku 45 → 6, Sonnet 60 → 23, Opus 77 → 26, and
   Haiku with everything in view beat Opus after compaction. Fable 5.1 held: 83 → 67 (68 & 66),
   at about $1,040 for the run, $15.33 per point, with handover notes four to five times the size
   of the others'. The first dollar still goes to making the material fit. If it cannot be made
   to fit, only one of the four produced an answer sheet worth reading, and that is the price.
2. **The ceiling matters more than the slope near the top.** Fable's worst run (80.5) is above
   Opus's mean (76.7); Opus clears 80 in two runs of nine, Fable in nine of nine. If the task has
   a pass mark, the question is not cost per point but which models clear it reliably.
3. **Points are not fungible.** The deductions section of every score file shows *what* was
   lost. Across all models the dominant loss is the same: asserting a planted error as fact.
   Fable's edge over Opus is mostly in Section A reconstruction and in hedging where the sources
   do not settle a question, which is exactly the failure a reader of the output cannot see.

Worked example. A team reviews contract summaries where a missed clause costs, say, $50 of
rework. That is V = $50 per point, far above every break-even above: use the best model. A
classroom exercise where a wrong fact costs nothing but a red mark: V is near zero, and Haiku's
$0.57 run is the right buy, provided the material fits in one context.

## The long variant, and what the repeats do not cover

The long variant was not repeated (it costs $40 to $1,040 per run and a full day). Its numbers are
n = 1 and the direction is the finding, not the digits.

| Model | Short (first run) | Long, read once | Long $/point | Handover notes at the end |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Haiku | 45 | 6 (8) | $6.83 | 14,700 words |
| Sonnet | 60 | 23 (24) | $3.58 | 18,200 |
| Opus | 77 | 26 (24) | $9.42 | 15,900 |
| Fable 5.1 | 83 | 67 (68 & 66) | $15.33 | 75,500 |

Fable 5.1's run, which an earlier Fable version refused outright, needed two harness workarounds
(notes written in parts once they passed the output ceiling; two hand-sized segments over the last
noise document), both recorded in `harness/PROTOCOL-LONG.md` with the handover size after every
segment. Its batch-by-batch profile is the same shape as the others', only higher: 29/34, then
16/35 with seven points of deductions, then 23/31.

## Caveats a reviewer should hold on to

- Nine runs per model is enough to rank the models and to bound the gaps to a few points; it is
  not enough to say anything about the tails.
- The two judges are Opus instances working from the key. They agree with each other and with a
  hand-scored sample, but a human panel could shift the absolute level by a few points.
- One task family: synthesis from contradictory sources with planted errors. Rankings on other
  work may differ.
- Five runs were discarded and re-run because the reader broke the reading protocol (four
  skipped the last unrelated document before a write; one spawned a helper agent). Haiku and
  Fable made no such slip; the discards are kept beside the cells they belong to.

## Reproduce

    v2/harness/.venv/bin/python v3/harness/stats_short.py \
        --trees v3/runs v3/runs-r2 v3/runs-r3 --prices v2/harness/prices.json --out v3/QUANT.md

Each tree holds one repeat; each cell holds `answers.md`, the transcript, and `score.json` /
`score-2.json` from the two judges. Add a fourth tree and the intervals tighten on their own.
