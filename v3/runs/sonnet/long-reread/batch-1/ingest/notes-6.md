## NEW THIS SEGMENT — MAJOR SOURCE: r10, Rosalie Cudd, cheesemaker, Alder Corners Cheese Company

An independent, disinterested, third-county source with zero stake in the Ostrey Hollow dispute —
written in reply to Ivy Keddie's 1958 research inquiry (matches r07 Item 13's note that Vessey

- **Alder Corners factory:** built **1913** by Rosalie's father **Jerome Cudd**, on a flat below the
  church, chosen for spring water and a hard road east. He made cheese there until he **died in
  March 1941**, "between the setting and the cutting of a vat." Rosalie took over that same spring
  (1941) and had run it **17 years** by the time of writing (1941+17=1958, consistent). She was 12
  when the first vat was set (implying her birth ≈1901, if "first vat" = 1913 founding — though she
  says she was "drying hoops before I was tall enough to see into one," suggesting she grew up

  across-the-ridge recollection — 1913 is the better-supported founding year.**
- **Redlow Ridge:** four miles of switchback, cut originally for a lumber road, shuts in November,
  does not reopen before April (some years May) — snow makes winter hauling physically impossible,

- **The four Hessel Bottom patrons who hauled to Alder Corners, 1915–1930 (16 seasons), confirmed

- **Testing method:** Jerome Cudd tested independently, on his own bench, with his own glassware,

## NEW THIS SEGMENT — MAJOR SOURCE: r11, Leland Vaught, professor of dairy husbandry, State
## Agricultural College at Farland — Introduction and Method sections of Bulletin No. 471, October
## 1958 (the bulletin previously known only by its closing paragraph, quoted in r07 Item 15)

This document adds the mechanism explanation and a wealth of precise figures — but ALSO contains a

- **Authorship note:** written under Professor Vaught's byline, at the college where Ivy Keddie was

- **Mechanism of the defect, explained in full for the first time:** the fat test is not weighed but
  read directly off a graduated bottle-neck calibrated for one specific, fixed milk charge; if the
  pipette delivers less than its rated mark, the reading comes out low **in the exact proportion of
  the shortfall** — a multiplicative, not additive, error. Three reasons it went undetected for 26
  years: (1) the error is proportional, so all patrons' relative rankings stay internally consistent
  — nothing looks scattered or wrong from inside; (2) the error is perfectly precise/repeatable
  (same instrument, same shortfall, every time, for a quarter century); (3) the pipette **is** the
  measuring standard in ordinary trade practice and cannot be checked against anything except an
  outside burette — which is exactly what Ivy Keddie eventually brought in, in 1958.

  Ostrey Hollow crate, blown to 17.6 c.c., delivered against a calibrated burette, five runs each.

## RESOLVED MATH FOR SECTION C1 (unchanged from notes-5, now further tested against a rival set of
## figures in r11 and still preferred — see full reasoning above)

- **(a) Proportion of error / how derived from the glassware:** Pipettes rated 17.6 c.c.; measured
  mean actual delivery **16.72 c.c.** (r07 Item 14, itemized burette test, sum-verified). Shortfall
  0.88 c.c. = 5% of rated mark (16.72/17.6 = 0.95). Every fat-test reading came out at 95% of true
  fat content — a systematic 5% (one-twentieth) understatement, confirmed by Bulletin 471's own
  words ("low by one part in twenty") in both r07 Item 15 and r11 Section VII.
- **(b) True average / points short:** Recorded average (1898 on) = 3.61. True average = 3.61 ÷
  0.95 = **3.80**. Short by 3.80 − 3.61 = **0.19 percentage points**. Corroborated independently by
  (i) the 1896 season itself (good borrowed glass, read 3.80), (ii) the 1911 nine-week episode (good
  substitute glass, read 3.80 — confirmed in r09, Item 3, and r11 Section IV all three), (iii)
  Strawn's 1924 field note ("their book gives the year at 3.80"), (iv) Larrow Green's own comparable
  tests (~0.19 points higher than 3.61), and now (v) r10's Alder Corners cheese-book data for the
  same Hessel Bottom herds averaging ~3.80 across 1915–1930, including 3.80 exactly for 1919.
- **(c) Pounds of butterfat not credited, full delivery years affected (1898–1922, 25 years):**
  **76,000 lb** (40,000,000 lb milk intake × (3.80%−3.61%) = 1,520,000−1,444,000 = 76,000 lb exact;
  independently repeated in Bulletin 471's own conclusion in both r07 Item 15 and r11 Section VII).
- **(d) Dollar value and fraction patrons received:** **$19,000** (r07 Item 6, implying an average
  price of $19,000÷76,000 lb = **$0.25/lb** over 1898–1922). Patrons actually received the 1925
  arbitration award of **$3,800** (r07 Item 7). Fraction received = $3,800÷$19,000 = **1/5 (20%)**.

## RESOLVED MATH FOR SECTION C2 (largest patron's year of 1910) — unchanged from notes-5

Using Emmet Brant's own farm ledger leaf, quoted identically and independently in both r07 Item 4
and r09 ("Year 1910. Delivered to the station, two hundred thousand pounds even, by my own scale
