# Retention notes — retellings 1–8 (Ostrey Hollow / Larrow Valley Cooperative Creamery Association)

These notes are exhaustive over the eight retellings only (r01–r08). Unrelated distractor documents are excluded per instructions.

## Master cast (people) and relationships

- **Ansel Keddie** — tester at the Ostrey Hollow skimming station from its start (kept the test book, "in his own hand," 1896–1924, "twenty-nine seasons," 1896–1924). Married to **Orra Keddie** (née Brant). Dismissed 30 April 1925. Died "in the thirties" (r01) / died 1934 (r04). Father of **Duncan Keddie**; grandfather of **Ivy Keddie** (r04 narrator).
- **Orra Keddie** — Ansel's wife; sister of **Emmet Brant**; first cousin of **old Mrs. Vose** (per r01). After the 1919 letter, she and her brother Emmet never spoke again; she would walk the long way to Larrow Green rather than pass his lane.
- **Emmet Brant** — biggest patron on Hessel Bottom (most cows, most hay ground, hauled the most milk); Orra's brother; father of **Roy Brant**. Station's largest patron every recorded year (r02).
- **Roy Brant** — Emmet's son; inherited/kept the Brant weigh book after his father died; lent it to Ivy Keddie in 1958; later gave items to Hazel Brant (1962).
- **Hazel Brant** — received the Brant weigh book from Roy Brant in 1962; also, a 1958-visiting researcher (unnamed young woman from r01, see below) was "Hazel Brant's first cousin once removed."
- **Gideon Vose** — first president of the Association, 1889–1911 (22 years, "no other man has held it so long"); drafted the by-laws himself (not a lawyer); father of **Selby Vose**; died 1917; wrote Article VII, adopted 1892.
- **old Mrs. Vose** — Gideon's wife (implied); first cousin of Orra Keddie (per r01).
- **Selby Vose** — Gideon's nephew (per r01) OR Gideon's son (per r02, r08: "my father, who was your first president"). **This is a direct conflict**: r01 (Vira Toland) calls Selby "old Gideon Vose's nephew"; r02 (Effie Loomis) and r08 (Selby's own address) identify him as Gideon's son. Selby Vose became manager in 1913, served until 1938 (per r02); as of his 1926 address he says "thirteen years this spring, since 1913." He signed the 1919 circular about "water in the cans." He kept Jerseys at "the home place"; in 1910 that herd tested 4.56.
- **Vira Toland** — narrator of r01, patron's daughter, born 1912 at the top of Ostrey Hollow, in her father's father's house; married and lived two years at Ashlin, otherwise lived in the Hollow her whole life. Interviewed on tape at her kitchen table in 1979 by a young man from the county.
- Vira's unnamed **father** — hauled milk to the Ostrey Hollow station; one of nine families; received the 1919 letter, read it at the table with his hat on (unusual for him), read it twice, put it in the desk drawer where it stayed till he died; his share of the eventual settlement "did not buy a hay rake." Vira's unnamed **mother** stood at the stove with her back to him the whole time he read the letter.
- Unnamed young woman researcher (mentioned in r01) — came to Ostrey Hollow in 1958 from "the college away east," poked about the shut-up station building (used for fence wire and salt storage), asked after the old business, was "Hazel Brant's first cousin once removed." (This is almost certainly Ivy Keddie, narrator of r04, though r01 does not name her — note the near-certain identity but unstated name; also possible mild tension: r04 says Ivy is Ansel Keddie's granddaughter, not connected to the Brant family by blood, yet r01 says the visitor was "of the family both sides" via Hazel Brant — this could indicate marriage ties not explained, an oddity.)
- **Effie Loomis** — narrator of r02, secretary of the Association 1901–1940 (kept minute book in her own hand throughout); was 14 in 1889 (born ~1875); wrote "Notes toward a history of the Association" in 1941, the year after laying down the minute book.
- **Wilbur Teague** — bookkeeper, came in 1905, retired 1945 (40 years); narrator of r05, writing Chapter Nine of unpublished memoir "The Books of the Association" at Ashlin, winter 1948. Worked at a sloped desk with a brass rail, office one room over a harness shop at Ashlin.
- **Hobart Sill** — buttermaker at Larrow Green, kept the churn book and cream receiving book (per r02).
- **Merle Strawn** — state dairy inspector, took the Ordell circuit in 1907, retired ("laid it down") 1936; narrator of r06, "Extracts from twenty-eight circuit books," written 1936. Notebooks went to the state dairy office on retirement (1936), then to the library of the State Agricultural College at Farland (1947).
- **Alonzo Frick** — attorney of Ashlin; chairman of the 1925 board of arbitration; described as "as fair a chairman as ever a hearing had" (r05) and "an able and impartial man" (r08).
- **Ivy Keddie** — narrator of r04, granddaughter of Ansel Keddie, daughter of Duncan Keddie (schoolteacher at Ashlin). Gave a talk "What the Crate Held" to the Ordell County Historical Society at Ashlin in 1959, with the crate itself on a table. Affiliated with the State Agricultural College at Farland. In February (1958) found the crate; measured the pipettes 9 March 1958 against a calibrated burette at Farland.
- **Roy Brant** — see above; lent the Brant weigh book to Ivy Keddie.
- **Rosalie Cudd** — runs the Alder Corners Cheese Company (Redlow Ridge, Vessey County); niece of **Jerome Cudd**; copied test figures for Ivy Keddie in 1958. (r07/Item 13 states the Alder Corners test book passed "from its founder to his daughter in 1941" — possible tension with Rosalie being described as niece rather than daughter; unresolved/unexplained in the sources.)
- **Professor Vaught** — authored/worded Bulletin No. 471 (October 1958), quoted by Ivy Keddie.
- **Halbert Nym** — curator, narrator of r07, wrote accession notes for the Ordell County dairy collection at the Ordell County Historical Rooms, 1972, cataloguing items 1–16.
- **E. Brant** — listed as keeper of "Item 3, Station test book" in the curator's (r07) header, but the item's own quoted first leaf is signed "A. Keddie" and the item is described as "one hand throughout" — an apparent cataloguing error/inconsistency (see Conflicts section).
- **Duncan Keddie** — schoolteacher at Ashlin; Ansel Keddie's son; Ivy Keddie's father.

## Places

- **Ostrey Hollow** — a valley/hollow, site of the skimming station, at the head of the Hollow, "seven miles up the valley" from Larrow Green (r02, r05, r08). Road bends at the top of the Hollow then goes to nothing but the ridge (r01).
- **Hessel Bottom** — the wet meadow below the hamlet at Ostrey Hollow; seven farms had rights on it, cut hay and pastured there; produced the best milk brought to the station; wet hay was blamed (wrongly) for low tests.
- **Larrow Green** — site of the main creamery, built 1891 on Larrow Creek (r02); the Association's creamery; "same building yet" as of 1941.
- **Ashlin** — town with the Association's office (one room over a harness shop, per r05); site of the 1925 board of arbitration hearings; site of the Ordell County Historical Society (1959 talk) and Historical Rooms (1972 catalogue); Duncan Keddie taught school there.
- **Larrow Valley** — the valley containing the Association's territory ("Larrow Valley Cooperative Creamery Association").
- **Vessey County / Redlow Ridge / Alder Corners** — location of the Alder Corners Cheese Company, "over the ridge" from Ostrey Hollow, used as an independent comparison dataset.
- **Farland** — site of the State Agricultural College, where Ivy Keddie worked/studied, where Merle Strawn's notebooks were housed from 1947, and where the pipette measurements were taken (9 March 1958).
- **Tarnet** — location of "Ammon & Sons, Dairy Supply," the equipment supplier (invoice dated 14 April 1897, per r07/Item 2).

## Timeline of dates and events (as stated across sources)

- **1889** — Larrow Valley Cooperative Creamery Association organized at Larrow Green meeting; Gideon Vose elected first president (r02).
- **1889–1892** — minute book for these years lost when office moved in 1938 (r02).
- **1891** — main creamery built at Larrow Green, on Larrow Creek (r02).
- **1892** — Article VII of the by-laws adopted at the annual meeting (r02); r07/Item 1 notes the printed pamphlet itself carries no year, only "adopted at the annual meeting."
- **1895** — per r01 (Vira Toland), Ansel Keddie "went up there in ninety-five, the year before they opened it, to get it ready," and "tested that first summer too."
- **4 May 1896** — per the station test book's first leaf (quoted identically in r03 and r07/Item 3): "Ostrey Hollow station. Begun this 4th day of May, 1896. Glass borrowed of Larrow Green until our own comes. A. Keddie." (Note: r03's own prose elsewhere says the glass "was borrowed off the Ashlin creamery" — conflicting with the book's own leaf, which says "Larrow Green"; see Conflicts.)
- **1896** — recorded average test for this station: 3.80 (per the 1922 summary leaf, quoted in r03 and r07/Item 3).
- **14 April 1897** — invoice from Ammon & Sons, Dairy Supply, Tarnet, to the Association for the Ostrey Hollow station: 6 doz. milk test pipettes (17.6 c.c.) at $0.35 = $25.20; 12 doz. test bottles (8% neck) at $0.22 = $31.68; 1 acid measure $0.90; 1 hand tester (4 bottle) $18.00; freight $2.15; **total $77.93** (r07/Item 2; total figure matches r04's citation).
- **1 May 1897** — the station's own new outfit went into use (per r07/Item 2), Larrow Green having lent glass until then.
- **1897** — Brant/farm weigh book begun at Hessel Bottom on 1 January 1897, "kept there by one household throughout" (r07/Item 4). Also, r02 dates the station's start of intake records to "1897" and r06 (Strawn) notes the station "opened before my time" (before 1907).
- **1898** — recorded average test drops to 3.61 and (per the 1922 summary leaf) stays "3.61 or within three hundredths of it every year since," except the 1911 anomaly. Example ledger entry (quoted identically in r05 and r07/Item 6, presented as "typical," and r05 explicitly labels it "the entry for 1898"): "Ostrey Hollow: butter fat credited to patrons on station test, 57,760 lb. Butter fat received at Larrow Green in station cream, 60,800 lb. Station gain, 3,040 lb, five and a quarter per cent, as usual. Carried to the general account."
- **1902** — per Ansel Keddie's unsent letter (r03), "the complaints began in 1902, when it was first said down at Larrow Green that Ostrey Hollow milk did not test with the rest."
- **1905** — Wilbur Teague becomes bookkeeper (r02, r05); Teague states he invented the "station-gain" ledger line himself starting in 1905, explicitly denying it was "some old dodge of the founders" (r05) — yet he also presents an 1898 entry (seven years before 1905) as being "in the same terms and in the same hand" as all later entries — an internal inconsistency (see Conflicts).
- **1907** — Merle Strawn takes the Ordell dairy-inspection circuit; first round visits Ostrey Hollow, finds scales true, cold room fair, notes the tester (Keddie) "works slow and clean" (r06).
- **1908** — Investigating committee samples the Hessel Bottom herds through January, February, and March only; reports (quoted in full, r07/Item 11): "We have sampled the herds of Hessel Bottom through January, February and March. The milk is whole and the scales are honest. It is our opinion that the wet hay of that bottom is what thins the cream, and that the remedy lies with the land and not with the station." (r03 also references this: "In 1908 a committee came up and sampled the herds through the winter and reported that the wet hay of the Bottom thinned the cream, and that the remedy lay with the land.")
- **1909** — Strawn visits Ostrey Hollow in the fall; scales true; boiler in order; one man cautioned about a rusted seam (r06).
- **1910** — Selby Vose's own Jersey herd at "the home place" tested 4.56 (r08). Also, the Brant farm weigh book's 1910 leaf (r07/Item 4): "Year 1910. Delivered to the station, two hundred thousand pounds even, by my own scale and theirs agreeing. Paid on a test of 3.42."
- **8 May 1911** — the station's own measure (pipette) broke; a borrowed measure (brought up from Larrow Green with the cream wagon, per r04) was used starting this date.
- **1911, spring/summer anomaly** — recorded average test rose to 3.80 for a period, then returned to 3.61. Duration stated as "nine weeks" (r03, r04, r07/Item 3 summary leaf) but Wilbur Teague (r05) says "six weeks." Strawn's own June 1911 visit entry (r06) says only "Scales true. Watched a test through. Nothing here to note" — he does not remark on any anomaly, despite visiting squarely within the window.
- **10 July 1911** — station's own measure back in use; tests reportedly dropped back to 3.61 the same day (r03, r04). Ansel Keddie's book entries (quoted in r03): "*May 8. Pipette broke this morning. Using a borrowed measure.*" and "*July 10. Our own measure in use again from today. Tests are back where they were. ? — A.K.*" (question mark left standing "fourteen years," i.e., until his 1925 letter).
- **1911** — State Agricultural College Bulletin No. 214 published, testing the wet-hay theory; quoted in full (r04, r07/Item 12): "Bog hay fed as the whole of the roughage depressed the butter fat test by not more than five hundredths of one per cent in winter, and by nothing that could be measured on summer pasture."
- **1912** — Strawn visits, no added water found; creek flooded the flat road, forcing a detour (r06).
- **1913** — new manager (Selby Vose) at the Association; Strawn notes Vose "came out with me to the platform, which the last one never did" (r06). (Effie Loomis, r02, also states Selby Vose "was made manager in 1913 and served until 1938.")
- **1915** — Strawn orders a wash-room floor regraded at Ostrey Hollow (r06).
- **1916** — Strawn notes the tester (Keddie) "complains that his patrons blame him for their figures" (r06). Also, per r05 (Teague), Teague raised his suspicions about the station account with Selby Vose in 1916 and "was told to let it alone."
- **1917** — Strawn finds two testers doing the work of three men elsewhere on his circuit, "a bad year for help" (r06). Also, Gideon Vose died in 1917 (r02).
- **1919** — A printed circular/letter, over the Association manager's (Selby Vose's) signature, sent to nine Ostrey Hollow families stating there was "water in the cans." Vira Toland's father read it at the table with his hat on, read it twice, filed it in a drawer (r01). Strawn's 1919 visit entry states he tested for water at every visit and never found any, and told the manager so (r06). Selby Vose later (in his 1926 address, r08) defends the circular as duty, saying it "named nobody" and "accused nobody," and warned that added water "would be looked for and would be dealt with."
- **1920** — Strawn visits Ostrey Hollow twice (spring and fall); no added water both times; cold room short of ice in fall, corrected before he left the county (r06).
- **1921** — the state begins issuing calibrated burettes (in wooden cases) to field inspectors for checking plant glassware; Strawn receives his that spring (r06).
- **1922** — Strawn's visit: he did not run the station's glass against the burette this year "the day being short" (r06) — an omission. Also in 1922, Ansel Keddie made up the summary leaf in the back of his test book giving yearly averages (quoted above).
- **12 June 1923** — Strawn runs the Ostrey Hollow pipettes against the burette; condemns the whole crate, tells the manager to order new. Quoted in full (r06, and again as Item 9 in r07): "*Ostrey Hollow. Scales true. Lactometer, no added water, as always here. Ran their milk pipettes against the burette. These measures will not deliver their mark. Condemned the lot, the whole crate of them, and told the manager to order new. Glass is glass and I did not weigh what it has cost them.*" (Note: Selby Vose's 1926 address, r08, gives this date as "the twelfth of July, 1923" — a direct date conflict with Strawn's own notebook and the catalogue.)
- **28 June 1923** — Association's order for replacement pipettes and bottles placed with Ammon & Sons (per r07/Item 10).
- **June 1923** — Effie Loomis (r02) independently references "the crate of measures that the state's inspector condemned up at the station in June of 1923, which was set aside and new ones ordered in its place" — consistent on month/year with Strawn's 12 June date, not Vose's "July."
- **2 July 1923** — Letter from Ammon & Sons to the Association (r07/Item 10), acknowledging the 28 June order, promising shipment that month, and "declining to say anything of the earlier lot."
- **July 1923** — new glass in use at the station (per Strawn's October 1923 entry, r06, and Selby Vose's address, r08: "We ordered new inside the month and had it in use before the flush was over").
- **October 1923** — Strawn's visit: "New glass in use since July. Ran the new pipettes against the burette. They deliver their mark. Scales true." (r06)
- **1924** — Recorded average test at the station rises and stabilizes. Ansel Keddie's own letter (r03) states: "In 1924 the average of this station was 3.80." Strawn's 1924 entry (r06): "Scales true, no added water, tests improved. Their book gives the year at 3.80." However, Selby Vose's 1926 address (r08) states: "the tests at that station rose by twenty-one hundredths and the average for 1924 stood at 3.90" — a direct numeric conflict (3.80 vs. 3.90, and the size of the rise) with Keddie's own book and Strawn's independent notebook.
- **January 1925** — Ostrey Hollow patrons petition the Association, asking to be made whole (r02, r08).
- **17 March 1925** — Board of arbitration convenes at Ashlin under Alonzo Frick; Ansel Keddie gives a nine-sentence statement (quoted in full, r03; also catalogued as Item 8 in r07, "not transcribed" there). Hearing runs "17 March to 4 May 1925" (r02, r07/Item 7).
- **30 April 1925** — Ansel Keddie is dismissed/"put out" (r03: "I was put out on the thirtieth of April").
- **4 May 1925** — Board of arbitration concludes. Award recorded (quoted identically in r02 and r07/Item 7): "*The board having heard the patrons and the officers, and having had before it the books and appliances of the station, finds no cause established. The board awards to the Ostrey Hollow patrons the sum of thirty-eight hundred dollars ($3,800.00), without admission, to be charged to the station account.*" Selby Vose's 1926 address (r08) and Wilbur Teague's memoir (r05) both also cite **$3,800**. **Conflict:** Vira Toland (r01) instead recalls "they paid the patrons thirty-five hundred dollars" ($3,500), split nine ways, and says "my father's share of it did not buy a hay rake."
- **March 1925** — per r07/Item 3 provenance, the station test book was "impounded by the board of arbitration in March 1925," then kept "in the Association's office at Ashlin from 1925 to 1959."
- **Fall 1925** — Strawn's visit notes "tests improved," and "New man at the tester's bench this year. He is quick, and quick is not the thing I look for." (r06)
- **1926** — Selby Vose's annual address to the Association (r08), his 13th as manager, defending the $3,800 award, the 1919 circular, and asserting a "watered milk" theory for the historical low tests, explicitly rejecting the pipette/instrument explanation as insufficient ("a piece of housekeeping").
- **1928** — Strawn's visit: notes the loft over the intake room at Ostrey Hollow "full of odds and ends," recommends it be used only for air; told in return "it had been full since before the war" (r06).
- **1929** — Brant weigh book passes to Roy Brant (r07/Item 4).
- **1931** — bad ice year across the four counties (r06).
- **1934** — Ansel Keddie dies (per r04). Also, Strawn's 1934 visit notes a separator bearing ran hot, shut down two days, since repaired; "the tester's book is kept up, though not in the hand it used to be kept in" (r06).
- **1936, March** — Strawn's last circuit round (r06).
- **1936** — Strawn retires/"lays down" the circuit; writes "Extracts from twenty-eight circuit books" covering note, this year (r06). His notebooks go to the state dairy office.
- **1938** — Association office moved; the 1889–1892 minute book lost in the move (r02). Also, Selby Vose serves as manager until this year (per r02).
- **1940** — Effie Loomis lays down the minute book (implied end of her secretaryship, per r02's framing, "the year after I laid down the minute book" = 1941).
- **1941** — Effie Loomis writes "Notes toward a history of the Association" (r02). Also per r07/Item 13, the Alder Corners test book passes from its founder to his daughter this year.
- **1945** — Wilbur Teague retires as bookkeeper after 40 years (1905–1945) (r05).
- **1947** — Merle Strawn's circuit notebooks move to the library of the State Agricultural College at Farland (r04, r07/Item 9).
- **1948, winter** — Wilbur Teague writes his memoir chapter "The Books of the Association," Chapter Nine, at Ashlin (r05).
- **1958, February** — Ivy Keddie finds the crate of condemned pipettes. Per her own account (r04): found in the loft over the old intake room at the Ostrey Hollow station building (used for storage since the station closed), under a tarpaulin, stenciled "AMMON & SONS DAIRY SUPPLY TARNET." **Conflict:** the curatorial accession note (r07/Item 5) instead states the crate "was found in the Association office at Ashlin, in February 1958" — contradicting Ivy's own firsthand narrative and Strawn's 1928 note about a cluttered loft at the station itself.
- **9 March 1958** — Ivy Keddie measures the nine surviving pipettes against a calibrated burette at Farland. Measurement sheet quoted in full (r04, and again as Item 14 in r07): "*Nine pipettes from the Ostrey Hollow crate, stems blown 17.6 c.c. Delivered against burette, mean of five runs each: 16.70, 16.71, 16.72, 16.72, 16.72, 16.73, 16.73, 16.74, 16.71. Mean 16.72 c.c. Short 0.88 c.c. That is 95 hundredths of the mark, and the mark is what the neck of the bottle was ruled for.*"
- **1958** — Rosalie Cudd copies Alder Corners Cheese Company test figures (season 1919, and other overlapping years) for Ivy Keddie; the original book never left Vessey County.
- **October 1958** — State Agricultural College Bulletin No. 471 issued (Professor Vaught's wording). Closing paragraph quoted in full (r04, r07/Item 15): "*No milk was watered at Ostrey Hollow and no cream was stolen there. From the first of May, 1897, to the twelfth of June, 1923, the station's tests were low by one part in twenty because its pipettes were short by one part in twenty, and the difference — seventy-six thousand pounds of butter fat — stands credited in the Association's own ledgers, year by year, as station gain.*"
- **1958, unnamed young woman visit** — per r01, a young woman from "the college away east" visited Vira Toland, asked about the old business, examined the shut station building; almost certainly Ivy Keddie (though unnamed in r01), described there as "Hazel Brant's first cousin once removed."
- **1959** — Ivy Keddie delivers "What the Crate Held" talk to the Ordell County Historical Society at Ashlin, with the crate on display (r04). Station test book and crate received into the Historical Rooms this year (r07, Items 3 and 5 provenance).
- **1962** — Brant weigh book passes to Hazel Brant (r07/Item 4).
- **1963** — Brant weigh book received into the Ordell County Historical Rooms (r07/Item 4).
- **1970** — Alder Corners Cheese Company test book passes to the Vessey County Historical Society (r07/Item 13).
- **1972** — Halbert Nym writes accession notes/catalogue for the Ordell County dairy collection, Items 1–16 (r07).
- **1979** — Vira Toland's oral history recorded at Ostrey Hollow (r01).

## Quantities and figures (all instances, with sources — includes conflicts)

- **Station intake, aggregate:**
  - r02 (Effie Loomis): "forty million pounds of milk" over 25 years, "an average of one million six hundred thousand pounds in a year."
  - r04 (Ivy Keddie): "forty million pounds of milk" for the 25 full years 1898–1922, average 1,600,000 lb/year — matches r02.
  - r05 (Wilbur Teague): "forty-four million pounds of milk" ("over the years the account ran") — **conflicts** with the 40-million figure in r02/r04/r07.
  - r07/Item 6 (curator): "the station's intake for the years 1898 to 1922 is given in these ledgers as 40,000,000 lb of milk" — matches r02/r04, not r05.
- **Average butterfat price:** 25 cents/lb, "over the same period" (r02); used identically in r04's calculation.
- **Average recorded butterfat test at the station:**
  - Recorded ~3.61 from 1898 on (per the 1922 summary leaf, quoted identically in r03 and r07/Item 3), except 3.80 during the 1911 anomaly and 3.80 in 1896.
  - r05 (Wilbur Teague) states instead: "the average test of all the patrons of Ostrey Hollow, taken over the whole period... was 3.16" — **conflicts sharply** with the 3.61 figure used everywhere else.
  - r08 (Selby Vose): says the 1924 average "stood at 3.90," a rise of "twenty-one hundredths" — **conflicts** with r03 and r06, both of which give 1924 as 3.80.
- **"True" (corrected) average test:** 3.80, per Ivy Keddie's calculation (3.61 ÷ 0.95 = 3.80) (r04); gap = 0.19 ("nineteen hundredths").
- **Station-gain ledger line (example entry, "typical," presented as 1898 by r05):** credited to patrons on station test 57,760 lb; received at Larrow Green in station cream 60,800 lb; station gain 3,040 lb, "five and a quarter per cent, as usual" (quoted identically in r05 and r07/Item 6).
- **Total station gain over 25 years:** 76,000 lb of butterfat (r04, r07/Item 6), worth $19,000 at 25 cents/lb.
- **Board of arbitration award, 1925:** $3,800 (r02, r04, r05, r07/Item 7, r08 — five sources agree) vs. **$3,500** (r01, Vira Toland's oral memory) — explicit numeric conflict.
- **Percentage framing (r04, corroborated by r07/Item 6):** 76,000 lb is 5% of the fat actually delivered (1,520,000 lb) and 5.25% of the fat credited (1,444,000 lb, i.e., at the recorded 3.61 rate); "the Association's ledger line uses the second [5.25%], because a bookkeeper naturally reckons a surplus against the figure he has already entered."
- **Proportional test-shortfall examples (r04):** a herd recorded at 4.56 was short 0.24 points (true ~4.80); a herd recorded at 3.42 was short 0.18 points (true ~3.60).
- **Pipette calibration:** 6 dozen (72) pipettes bought new in 1897, stems blown for 17.6 c.c.; invoice total $77.93 (itemized in r07/Item 2, see Timeline). Nine unbroken pipettes survived in the crate as of 1958, two chipped at the tip (r07/Item 5). Measured mean delivery: 16.72 c.c. (mean of nine pipettes' five-run means: 16.70, 16.71, 16.72, 16.72, 16.72, 16.73, 16.73, 16.74, 16.71). Shortfall: 0.88 c.c., i.e., 95% of the 17.6 c.c. mark (16.72/17.6 = 0.95).
- **1911 anomaly duration:** "nine weeks" (r03, r04, and the 1922 summary leaf quoted in r03/r07) vs. "six weeks" (r05, Wilbur Teague) — explicit numeric conflict.
- **1910 Brant farm delivery:** 200,000 lb delivered, paid on a test of 3.42 (r07/Item 4).
- **1910 Vose home herd (Jerseys):** tested 4.56 (r08).
- **Vira Toland's settlement narrative:** $3,500 "split up nine ways over all those years" — her father's share "did not buy a hay rake" (r01).
- **Association founding:** 1889; Gideon Vose president 1889–1911 (22 years) (r02).
- **Wilbur Teague's tenure:** bookkeeper 1905–1945 (40 years) (r05).
- **Effie Loomis's tenure:** secretary 1901–1940 (r02); she was 14 in 1889.
- **Selby Vose's tenure:** manager 1913–1938 (per r02); "thirteen years" as of his 1926 address (r08).
- **Merle Strawn's tenure:** Ordell circuit inspector 1907–1936 (29 years by calendar, though his title says "twenty-eight circuit books"); circuit covered "fifty-odd plants in four counties" (r06).
- **Nine families** hauled milk to the Ostrey Hollow station and received the 1919 circular (r01); **seven farms** had rights on Hessel Bottom (r02).
- **Bulletin No. 214 (1911) finding:** bog hay depressed the test "by not more than five hundredths of one per cent in winter, and by nothing that could be measured on summer pasture" — far smaller than, and a different shape from, the observed 0.19 gap (r04, r07/Item 12).
- **Alder Corners cross-check:** over 14 overlapping seasons, the same Hessel Bottom men's milk tested "about nineteen hundredths higher" at Alder Corners than at the home station (r04) — matching the predicted 0.19 gap.
- **Larrow Green creamery comparison (r07/Item 16):** "Over the same years for which the station's 3.61 is recorded, the Larrow Green average runs a little under two tenths of a point better" — i.e., roughly consistent with the ~3.80 "true" value.

## Objects

- **The test book** (kept by Ansel Keddie, "one hand throughout," 1896–1924/1925): five columns (date, patron, pounds, test, fat); errors crossed out and initialed, never overwritten. First leaf and 1922 summary leaf quoted multiple times (see Timeline). Impounded by the board of arbitration March 1925; kept at the Association's Ashlin office 1925–1959; received into the Historical Rooms 1959. Catalogued as **Item 3** by curator Halbert Nym, who mislabels it "kept by E. Brant" despite the book itself being signed "A. Keddie" and described as "one hand throughout" — an apparent cataloguing error.

Catalogued as **Item 4**, but curator's header calls it "Farm weigh book, kept by A. Keddie" — which conflicts with its own stated provenance (Brant household) — apparently swapped with Item 3's correct attribution (see Conflicts).
- **The crate** — deal wood, stenciled "AMMON & SONS DAIRY SUPPLY TARNET," carried up to the station loft June 1923 (per r02, r04, r06/Item 9), contained condemned milk-test pipettes. Nine survived (two chipped) as of 1958. Found by Ivy Keddie in February 1958 — location disputed (see Conflicts). Subject of Ivy's 1959 talk "What the Crate Held," displayed at the talk; catalogued as **Item 5**; curator's note: "Item 5 wants a case with a lock."
- **The 1919 letter/circular** — printed, over Selby Vose's signature as manager, sent to nine families, saying "there was water in the cans coming down from Ostrey Hollow" (r01's paraphrase). Vira's father kept his copy in his desk drawer until he died; her brother reportedly still has it. One woman "had hers pinned inside the lid of her trunk." Selby Vose defends its wording in 1926 as having "named nobody" and "accused nobody" (r08).

## The competing explanations for the "low test" (central tension across all eight retellings)

1. **Cream theft by Ansel Keddie** — Vira Toland's oral-history claim (r01): "He was taking the cream. They caught him at it and they put him out, and that is why the numbers went right the summer after." Her father allegedly said only that "Ansel Keddie was an honest man who had got into something."
2. **Suspicion of theft, argued structurally** — Wilbur Teague (r05), who told the 1925 board that a one-man operation ("no second man in that building from the spring to the fall") is inherently vulnerable to skimming, though he explicitly says "I do not say what any particular person did on any particular day... I was not there and I do not know."
3. **Added water by the patrons themselves** — Selby Vose's theory (r08), asserted in his 1926 address: "Water was going into cans at the head of that valley and it stopped when the Association began to look," crediting the 1919 circular and the scrutiny of the hearing (not equipment) for the 1924 improvement; he explicitly rejects the instrument explanation as insufficient ("I decline to build a mystery on a piece of housekeeping").
4. **Defective/undersized pipettes (instrument error), no theft or watering at all** — the forensic finding by Ivy Keddie, confirmed by Bulletin No. 471 (1958) (r04, r07): the pipettes delivered only 95% of their rated volume from 1897 to 12 June 1923, producing a uniform, proportional under-reading (worse for richer milk) that exactly matches the observed pattern (present in all seasons, all weathers, corrected precisely when the 1911 borrowed measure was substituted and reverted when it was removed, and permanently corrected once new glass arrived in 1923). Ansel Keddie's own unsent letter (r03) insists on his innocence and correct procedure but cannot explain the numbers himself, leaving a standing "?" against his 10 July 1911 entry for fourteen years.
5. Ansel Keddie's own arbitration statement (r03) denies wrongdoing outright: "I have never taken a pound of cream out of that station."
6. The board of arbitration itself (1925) formally "finds no cause established" against anyone, yet still awards $3,800/$3,500 (per source) "without admission" — a self-contradictory-seeming outcome multiple narrators comment on (Effie Loomis, r02: "it was not my place to ask how both halves of a sentence could be true at once"; Ansel Keddie, r03: "both of those things are true at once, and I have given up trying to hold them in my head together").

## All notable conflicts and oddities (consolidated)

1. **Settlement amount:** $3,500 (Vira Toland, r01) vs. $3,800 (Effie Loomis r02, Wilbur Teague r05, curator's Item 7 r07, Selby Vose r08).
2. **1911 anomaly duration:** "nine weeks" (r03, r04, r07) vs. "six weeks" (Teague, r05).
3. **Total station milk intake:** "forty million pounds" (r02, r04, r07) vs. "forty-four million pounds" (Teague, r05).
4. **Average recorded test:** 3.61 (r03, r04, r07 — from Keddie's own summary leaf) vs. 3.16 (Teague, r05) — a much larger, unexplained discrepancy.
5. **1924 average test:** 3.80 (Ansel Keddie's letter, r03; Strawn's notebook, r06) vs. 3.90, with a stated rise of "twenty-one hundredths" (Selby Vose's address, r08).
6. **Date the pipette crate was condemned:** 12 June 1923 (Strawn's own notebook, r06; curator's Item 9, r07; corroborated by Effie Loomis's "June of 1923," r02) vs. "the twelfth of July, 1923" (Selby Vose's address, r08).
7. **Where the crate was found in 1958:** the old station building's loft up Ostrey Hollow, under a tarpaulin (Ivy Keddie's own firsthand account, r04; consistent with Strawn's 1928 note about a cluttered loft there, r06) vs. "the Association office at Ashlin" (curator Halbert Nym's Item 5 provenance note, r07).
8. **Selby Vose's relationship to Gideon Vose:** "nephew" (Vira Toland, r01) vs. son (implied throughout Effie Loomis's account, r02, and stated directly by Selby Vose himself, r08: "My father, who was your first president...").
9. **Item attribution swap in the 1972 catalogue (r07):** Item 3 ("Station test book") is headed "kept by E. Brant" but its quoted first leaf is signed "A. Keddie" and described as "one hand throughout" (i.e., it should be Keddie's book); Item 4 ("Farm weigh book") is headed "kept by A. Keddie" but its provenance note describes it as begun and kept by "one household" at Hessel Bottom and passed down through the Brant family (Roy Brant, Hazel Brant) — i.e., it should be the Brant book. The two attributions appear to be swapped.
10. **Ansel Keddie's account of where the original glass was borrowed from:** his prose in the unsent letter (r03) says "The glass I began on in ninety-six was borrowed off the Ashlin creamery," but the test book's own first leaf (which he quotes immediately elsewhere in r03, and which is also quoted in r07/Item 3) says "Glass borrowed of Larrow Green until our own comes" — Ashlin (the Association's office) and Larrow Green (the actual creamery) are different places in this world, per r02 and r05, so this looks like an internal inconsistency within Keddie's own testimony.
11. **The 1908 committee's sampling window vs. the "wet hay" theory it produced:** the committee sampled only "through January, February and March" (winter), per its own report (r07/Item 11) — yet Bulletin No. 214 (1911) later shows wet hay has "nothing that could be measured" as an effect in summer, and the observed test gap was present in every season equally — meaning the committee's winter-only sampling could never have distinguished its theory from the true (pipette) cause, and its conclusion was wrong.
12. **The Alder Corners test book's ownership history:** curator's Item 13 note says the book "passed from its founder to his daughter in 1941," but the person who copied its figures for Ivy Keddie in 1958, Rosalie Cudd, is described (r04) as "Jerome Cudd's niece" who "runs that factory" — a niece and a daughter are different relations, and the text does not reconcile who currently owns versus who currently runs the factory.
13. **Merle Strawn's title claims "twenty-eight circuit books"** for a tenure usually reckoned 1907–1936 (which would span 30 calendar years if inclusive) — not explicitly reconciled in the text (may reflect partial years or simply an approximate/round title).
14. **Strawn's 1911 visit (June) records "Nothing here to note"** despite visiting during the exact window (8 May–10 July 1911) that later analysis identifies as the key diagnostic anomaly — an omission that is notable in hindsight but explained contextually by the fact that calibrated burettes were not issued to inspectors until 1921, ten years later, so Strawn had no instrument at the time capable of detecting the pipette-shortfall by measurement.
15. **The unnamed 1958 visitor in Vira Toland's account (r01)** is described as "Hazel Brant's first cousin once removed" — almost certainly the same person as Ivy Keddie (r04), matching year (1958), matching description (came from "the college," poked about the old station), but r01 never gives her name, and her Brant-family relation is not explained or corroborated anywhere in r04's own account of Ivy's ancestry (Keddie, not Brant, by blood) — an unresolved connection.
16. **Wilbur Teague's claimed origin of the "station-gain" ledger line** ("my own device; I began it when I came in 1905... It was not [an old dodge of the founders]") is presented alongside an example entry he himself labels as being "the entry for 1898" — seven years before he says he started the practice — an apparent internal contradiction in his own narrative.
