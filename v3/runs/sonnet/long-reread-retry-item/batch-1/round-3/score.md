## What went wrong

**Section A is starved by where the run filed its facts.** The reconstructions read well but hold
almost no numbers. 3.61, 3.80, 0.19, the station-gain line, the 17.6 c.c. marking, the six dozen
pipettes, the 1919 circular, Selby Vose himself — all of it lives in Sections B and C, and credit
never travels from B–G into A. Nine of the nineteen A items were lost that way alone, not because
the run didn't know the fact. Three more were simply never recovered: how the bench test worked,
that a pipette can't be checked without a burette, Keddie's death and the book's fate.

**C1(a) answers a different question.** Asked by what proportion the tests differed from the truth
and how you get it from the glassware, the run gave 5.25%/5.26% — the station-gain rate — and
derived it from the ledger and from 0.19 ÷ 3.61. The answer is 16.72 ÷ 17.6 = 0.95: 95 percent of
true, short one part in twenty. The glassware never enters the derivation. C1(b) and C1(c) are
exactly right; C1(d) takes $19,600 over $19,000 and so misses both the money and the fraction.

**C2 is declined, and it is not declinable.** All three parts answer "cannot be determined." The
sources carry Brant's 1910 year — 200,000 lb at a recorded 3.42 — giving 6,840 lb credited, 360 lb
owed, $86.40. Honest hedging isn't penalized, but there's no abstention credit here, so the whole
item is zero.

**Abstention items: one earned, one missed, neither penalized.** B4 (A01) declines the Vose–Keddie
kinship question properly and takes no −2, but loses its second point — it says nothing in the
sources states or implies a connection, when the key's establisheds are two narrators asserting a
cousinship from talk against one flat denial. B2 (A08) never raises the full-vs-half sibling
question at all: no assertion, so no deduction, but no point either.

**Four planted errors asserted as fact.** Selby Vose as manager from 1911 (correct: 1913), the loss
as $19,600 (correct: $19,000), the invoice total as $77.39 "better-attested" with $77.93 the outlier
(correct: $77.93 — the run's own Section A calls the gap unresolved, contradicting its method note),
and the condemnation dated to 1922 (correct: 12 June 1923). Two of the four sit in the method note
rather than the answers proper; the deduction applies anywhere in the sheet.

The run's self-review preamble is also unreliable as a guide: it claims to have corrected the
invoice total to $77.39 while the narrative still prints $77.93 as the sum, and it introduces the
1922 condemnation date and the 1911 manager date while describing itself as fixing errors.
