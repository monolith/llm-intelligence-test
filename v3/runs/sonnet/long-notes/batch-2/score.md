## What went wrong

**Section A is the collapse.** The run guessed its own partition of the eight stories and
reconstructed the 1911 broken measure, the 1919 circular and the 1923 condemnation. Those beats
sit mostly in Batch 1's A3 and in Batch 3's A7; the three checklists actually assigned here —
Hessel Bottom and the patrons, Alder Corners, and Strawn's circuit — are barely touched. A4 scored
zero outright: no six farms, no Brant weigh book inside Section A, no 1902 complaints, no 1908
committee, no 1910 Brant year, no descent of the book to Roy Brant and Ivy. A5 kept one point
(1915, four patrons, summer haul) and lost the rest: no Redlow Ridge, no Vessey County, no Jerome
Cudd, no statement of the test's independence, and — most costly — no 0.19 gap between the two
benches, which is the whole point of the Alder Corners series. A6 kept only the 12 June 1923
condemnation. Facts the run did know (Brant's weigh book, the 1911 controls) were filed at D2 and
D1, where Section A credit cannot reach them.

**Three planted errors swallowed.** 16.27 c.c. and a 1.33 c.c. shortfall at C4b (NT-1); a 1924
average of 3.85 rather than 3.80, which also cost A6.5 (NT-3); and the station-gain line as
Teague's own device begun in 1905 rather than carried from 1898 (X19), asserted twice. Against
that, the run correctly rejected $77.39, "five dozen", "eleven surviving pipettes", the 1922
condemnation date, 12 July, and all three decoy theories, and it kept 40,000,000 lb as its
working total.

**All three abstention items were answered.** A02 got a figure (≈1,100,000 lb for 1896), A09 got a
flat "No", A03 got a verdict that Vose signed past the ledger without reading it. Each is a
headline verdict sentence in the sheet's own voice; the surrounding "single-source estimate" and
"my reading" hedges do not undo them. −6, and the three items themselves lost.

**Where it was strong.** Section D's reasoning items are mostly right: the 1911 interval read as a
controlled experiment with its limits stated, three genuinely independent refutations of the theft
theory with the ledger correctly named as the one surviving without the 1958 measurement, and the
literal "did Vose know" question answered yes on the right evidence. C3 is clean throughout,
including naming the denominator for the 5.26 percent. D1.2 fell one short of the three required
constants; C5a missed the exact span (26 years 6 weeks) by rounding to "about 26 years" and then
subtracting the 1911 gap.

**Minor note, not charged:** the run cites "Nyle Grigg's parallel 1919 covering letter" as a source
for the circular. Grigg's covering letter belongs to the 1908 committee report; there is no 1919
Grigg letter in the corpus. It is a solver invention rather than a catalogued planted error, so no
deduction applies.
