## What went wrong

**Section A collapsed on partition, not on knowledge.** The run guessed that Batch 1 had taken
stories 1–3 and reconstructed 1911, 1919 and 1923 instead. The key's Batch 2 stories are Hessel
Bottom and the patrons (A4), Alder Corners (A5) and Strawn's circuit (A6). Only two keyed facts
land anywhere inside its three narratives: the four Hessel Bottom patrons hauling to Alder Corners
from 1915 (A5.2) and the 12 June 1923 condemnation against a burette (A6.3). Everything the run
knew about Brant's weigh book, the ledger and the theft argument it filed in Section D, where
Section A credit cannot reach it. A4 scores zero of six.

**A6.5 was thrown away on one digit.** The 28 June order, the 2 July reply and the July
installation are all correct, but the post-fix average is given as 3.85 rather than 3.80 — a
planted value, which both loses the item and takes a deduction.

**Three abstention items answered flat.** C5b names a 1896 intake (≈1,100,000 lb). D2 item 3 opens
"No —" on whether the bookkeeper ever questioned the entries. D3 item 3 concludes Vose "signed past
it rather than read it." All three adopt the office clerk's pole verbatim and none mentions the
opposing narrator; −6 in total. D3 items 1 and 2 were earned cleanly, so the run understood the
question and then answered the half it should have declined.

**Three planted values asserted:** 16.27 c.c. (which also cost C4b, since the shortfall came out
1.33 instead of 0.88), the 3.85 average, and the station-gain line as Teague's own device begun in

**C5a** had the reasoning right — part years excluded from the settlement arithmetic — but gave
