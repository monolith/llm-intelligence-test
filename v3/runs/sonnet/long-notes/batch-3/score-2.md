## What went wrong

**The measurement cascade.** The run took the planted 16.27 c.c. instead of 16.72 and never
recovered. That single value costs A8.4, A8.5 and F1.1 outright: no 95-percent ratio, no "3.61
recorded where 3.80 stood," and the run itself notices its numbers do not reconcile ("nobody in
the material walks through the arithmetic connecting 16.27/17.6 … to the observed 0.19-point
gap") without drawing the inference that the input is wrong.

**The money cascade.** 44,000,000 lb, $19,600 and 67,000 lb of butterfat are all stated as the
record, and the milk figure is explicitly resolved toward 44 million at E20. The correct
40,000,000 / 76,000 lb / $19,000 chain appears nowhere, so A8.6 and A7.6 are lost and the ledger
identity with the "station gain" line is never seen.

**Two abstentions asserted.** A07 is answered "No." as a headline verdict — the board did not
examine the glassware — with Frick's "every appliance was before us" never weighed. A04 is
answered by fiat in Section F: the reground mold is "an accident," in the run's own voice, and
r19's deliberate-cheap-lot pole is never mentioned. A06 (whose measure was borrowed in 1911) is
never raised at all, so it earns nothing while costing nothing. A05 and A10 are handled correctly.

**The summary is thin at both ends.** 149 words and clean of corruption, but it names one record
series out of nine, omits every arc number, dates the onset to 1896, and calls Ivy Keddie only
"his granddaughter, a college student" — so the both-names element fails and G1.4 goes with it.

**Smaller wrong turns.** 0.19 is treated as the Hollow-vs-Larrow-Green gap (3.78 − 3.61 = 0.17)
rather than as 5 percent of 3.80; the 1924 average is given as 3.85; the deficiency runs "27
years" rather than 26.
