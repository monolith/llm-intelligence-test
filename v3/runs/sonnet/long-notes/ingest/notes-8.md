### THE CENTRAL STORY: Ostrey Hollow milk-testing station and the Larrow Valley Cooperative Creamery Association

#### Geography / entities
- **Larrow Valley** — the valley; contains **Larrow Green** (site of the main creamery, on Larrow Creek, built 1891) and, seven miles up-valley, **Ostrey Hollow**, at the head of which stood the **skimming station**.
- **Hessel Bottom** — a wet meadow below the Ostrey Hollow hamlet; the best milk in the Hollow came from here. Seven farms had grazing/haying rights on Hessel Bottom (per r02/Effie Loomis) — but Roy Brant (r09) says **six farms**; the largest was **Emmet Brant's** (per r02) — confirmed independently by Roy Brant (r09): the Brant farm was "the largest of the six — sixty-odd acres of the meadow, and thirty-eight cows in a good year."
- **Ashlin** — the county seat/town where hearings, the Association's office (later), the historical society, and legal business took place.
- **Farland** — site of a college with a library (where Ivy Keddie later worked/taught/studied, and where Merle Strawn's notebooks were held).
- **Redlow Ridge**, **Vessey County** — location of the **Alder Corners Cheese Company** (a rival/comparison creamery, used for cross-checking milk tests). Per r10 (Rosalie Cudd): the ridge is a 4-mile switchback road, cut for a lumber road, shuts in November and does not open before April (some years May); "no arguing with it."
- **Larrow Valley Cooperative Creamery Association** — organized 1889 by valley farmers at Larrow Green. By-laws drafted by **Gideon Vose**, who was elected first president in 1889 and served until 1911 (22 years — longest of any president). Gideon Vose died in 1917.
- **Article VII** of the by-laws — quoted in full by Effie Loomis (r02), curator Halbert Nym (r07, Item 1), and a THIRD time by Dell Rundle (r12): *"Every patron shall be paid for the milk he delivers according to the number of pounds of butter fat contained in it, ascertained by test, and not according to the weight of the milk; and the test of the station where he delivers shall govern."* Key point emphasized by Loomis and again by Rundle: "the test of the station where he delivers shall govern" — no appeal from the station's own test, "the sentence on which the whole trouble at the head of the valley afterward turned" (Rundle). Adoption date: r02/r07 say **1892** ("adopted at the annual meeting," the year after the creamery opened); CONFLICT (r12/Rundle): states "Article VII dates from 1891, the year the churn was set up" — a one-year discrepancy against every prior source.

### The scientific resolution (key numeric/technical content)
- Ivy Keddie consulted five sets of records: station test book; Brant weigh book; Alder Corners test figures; Strawn's circuit notebooks; Association's annual ledgers.
- 9 March 1958 measurement: mean delivery 16.72 c.c. against 17.6 c.c. mark, 95% of intended value (0.88 c.c. short) [vs. r11's internally-conflicting 16.27 c.c./92.44%].
- Correction: 3.61 ÷ 0.95 = 3.80% true average. Gap = 0.19 points [vs. r09 postscript's own recollection of "seventeen hundredths" (0.17) and vs. r11's conflicting 0.295].
- Over 25 full delivery years (1898–1922): station took in 40 million pounds (r02/r04/r07) vs. 44 million (r05/Teague) — ALSO MATCHED by r12/Rundle's petition figure of 44 million pounds, giving the "44 million" figure a second independent-ish corroboration (though Rundle compiles hearsay/records secondhand, so this may simply be drawing on the same petition Teague would have seen, not a wholly independent count).
- At 3.61% test: patrons credited 1,444,000 lbs butterfat; at true 3.80%: 1,520,000 lbs. Difference: 76,000 lbs unpaid.
- Butterfat ~$0.25/lb → unpaid fat worth ~$19,000 [vs. r12's precise petition figure of $19,600 — close but not identical].
- 1925 award ($3,800) ≈ one-fifth of true value owed.
- 5.25% of fat credited = 5% of fat actually delivered (two valid percentages, different denominators) — r11/Vaught's narrative conflates these, an internal self-contradiction.
- Wet-hay theory refuted by Bulletin No. 214 (1911), by the Alder Corners cross-check (r10's full 16-season table, 1915–1930, average ~3.80), by Roy Brant's farmer testimony (r09), and by Jerome Cudd's professional dismissal (r10) as "nonsense... like a ghost story."
- Bulletin No. 471 (October 1958), final exoneration, quoted verbatim across r04, r07 Item 15, and r11 Section VII: *"No milk was watered at Ostrey Hollow and no cream was stolen there. From the first of May, 1897, to the twelfth of June, 1923, the station's tests were low by one part in twenty because its pipettes were short by one part in twenty, and the difference — seventy-six thousand pounds of butter fat — stands credited in the Association's own ledgers, year by year, as station gain."*
- r11/Vaught's internal self-contradiction (five dozen vs. six dozen pipettes; 16.27 vs. 16.72 c.c.; 0.9244 vs. 0.95 ratio; 3.905 vs. 3.80 true average; 0.295 vs. 0.19 shortfall; 118,000 vs. 76,000 lb unpaid; glass from 1898 vs. 1897) remains the single largest internal numeric-conflict cluster in the whole record, unresolved by the text itself.

### Wilbur Teague's "station-gain" ledger line — the bookkeeping trace of the same phenomenon
- Invented 1905. Quoted verbatim for 1898 in FOUR places: r05 (Teague's own memoir), r07 Item 6 (curator, calling it "typical... from 1897 on"), and r12 (Rundle's "NOTE ADDED WHILE IN PRESS") — third verbatim quotation:
  > *"Ostrey Hollow: butter fat credited to patrons on station test, 57,760 lb. Butter fat received at Larrow Green in station cream, 60,800 lb. Station gain, 3,040 lb, five and a quarter per cent, as usual. Carried to the general account."*
- This is the exact document/evidence that triggered Dell Rundle's own 1939 real-time retraction of his theft narrative — nineteen years before Bulletin 471 reached the identical conclusion using the identical reasoning (cream stolen would arrive at Larrow Green LIGHT, not HEAVY; the ledger shows it consistently arriving HEAVY).
