### THE CENTRAL STORY: Ostrey Hollow milk-testing station and the Larrow Valley Cooperative Creamery Association

#### Geography / entities
- **Larrow Valley** — the valley; contains **Larrow Green** (site of the main creamery, on Larrow Creek, built 1891) and, seven miles up-valley, **Ostrey Hollow**, at the head of which stood the **skimming station**.
- **Hessel Bottom** — a wet meadow below the Ostrey Hollow hamlet; the best milk in the Hollow came from here. Seven farms had grazing/haying rights on Hessel Bottom (per r02/Effie Loomis) — but Roy Brant (r09) says **six farms**; the largest was **Emmet Brant's** (per r02) — confirmed independently by Roy Brant (r09): the Brant farm was "the largest of the six — sixty-odd acres of the meadow, and thirty-eight cows in a good year." NEW (segment 11): "six farms" now further confirmed by THREE more independent sources — Nyle Grigg's 1908 committee report (r15): "Hessel Bottom, where six farms cut hay and turn out stock"; Dr. Roland Kepp's 1926 paper (r16): "Six farms cut hay and pasture stock upon it"; and Hazel Brant's 1981 interview (r17): "Six. Hessel Bottom was six farms and no more." "Six farms" is now corroborated by FIVE independent sources (r09, r15, r16, r17, plus the underlying 1908 report itself) against r02/Loomis's lone "seven."
- **Ashlin** — the county seat/town where hearings, the Association's office (later), the historical society, and legal business took place. NEW (segment 11, r17): the Brant weigh book is now held "down the street at the historical rooms in this town" of Ashlin — "where anybody may look at it who wants to, which is nobody."
- **Farland** — site of a college with a library (where Ivy Keddie later worked/taught/studied, and where Merle Strawn's notebooks were held). The State Agricultural College at Farland published Bulletin No. 214 (1911, feeding trial on bog hay) and Bulletin No. 471 (1958, final exoneration).
- **Redlow Ridge**, **Vessey County** — location of the **Alder Corners Cheese Company** (a rival/comparison creamery, used for cross-checking milk tests). Per r10 (Rosalie Cudd): the ridge is a 4-mile switchback road, cut for a lumber road, shuts in November and does not open before April (some years May); "no arguing with it."
- **Tarnet** — NEW (segment 11, r17): per Hazel Brant, the place the pipette crate "came out from... in ninety-nine" (1899) — a new supplier/origin location and date for the crate, in tension with the established 1897 Ammon & Sons invoice date (see new conflicts below).
- **Larrow Valley Cooperative Creamery Association** — organized 1889 by valley farmers at Larrow Green. By-laws drafted by **Gideon Vose**, who was elected first president in 1889 and served until 1911 (22 years — longest of any president). Gideon Vose died in 1917.
- **Article VII** of the by-laws — quoted in full by Effie Loomis (r02), curator Halbert Nym (r07, Item 1), and a THIRD time by Dell Rundle (r12): *"Every patron shall be paid for the milk he delivers according to the number of pounds of butter fat contained in it, ascertained by test, and not according to the weight of the milk; and the test of the station where he delivers shall govern."* Key point emphasized by Loomis and again by Rundle: "the test of the station where he delivers shall govern" — no appeal from the station's own test, "the sentence on which the whole trouble at the head of the valley afterward turned" (Rundle). Adoption date: r02/r07 say **1892** ("adopted at the annual meeting," the year after the creamery opened); CONFLICT (r12/Rundle): states "Article VII dates from 1891, the year the churn was set up" — a one-year discrepancy against every prior source. NEW CONFLICT (segment 11, r15/Nyle Grigg's 1919 letter): Grigg states "Payment by test came in with the 1893 meeting, as I have always understood it" — a THIRD distinct adoption-year variant (1893), now making the date a genuine three-way conflict: 1891 (Rundle) / 1892 (Loomis, curator Nym) / 1893 (Grigg). Attorney Alonzo Frick's 1946 recollections (r13) also describe the "no appeal" rule as central and calls the whole affair "not litigation at all... an arbitration under the by-laws of a creamery association, with no appeal from it, no reported decision, and nothing whatever to cite it for."

#### People and relationships (the core family/social web)
- **Ansel Keddie** — tester at the Ostrey Hollow skimming station. Went up in 1895 to prepare it, opened/began testing 4 May 1896. Tested 29 seasons, 1896–1924 (did not test in 1925). Dismissed ("put out") on **30 April 1925**. Died in **1934**.
  - Internal inconsistency in Keddie's own letter (r03): "borrowed off the Ashlin creamery" vs. book's own first-leaf entry "Glass borrowed of Larrow Green" — Larrow Green corroborated independently at least four times elsewhere (r07 Item 2, r07 curatorial note, r14/Sill's personal account). Ashlin claim remains sole outlier.
  - Segment 10 finding (r14/Sill): buttermaker Hobart Sill personally supplied his own glass/testing outfit from his own bench at Larrow Green when the station opened in May 1896; his equipment went up the valley on the wagon and the station "opened on it." The station's own outfit "came the spring after" (spring 1897) and Sill's personal equipment was returned to him, "and one piece of it was chipped."
  - Segment 10 finding (r13/Frick): Keddie made a formal statement to the 1925 arbitration board on 17 March 1925, stating he had tested "as he was taught and as the state man had seen him do it, thirty-one times a week for twenty-nine seasons," that he could not explain the figures, and that he had never taken a pound of cream from the station. Frick believed him. MAJOR PSYCHOLOGICAL DETAIL: Frick recounts that privately, in the corridor at Ashlin, Keddie confided that he had always personally believed the PATRONS were at fault (privately suspected the water-theory) but would not say so publicly because he had lived among them thirty years and would not accuse his neighbors. Frick explicitly corrects the record: "it has been said since that he went to his grave accusing nobody, which is true, and that he suspected nobody, which is not."
  - NEW (segment 11, r17/Hazel Brant): additional texture — "the two of them [Ansel Keddie and Emmet Brant] argued about drainage for forty years." Possible tension with r09's claim that Emmet and Keddie stopped speaking after the 1919 circular (not a hard contradiction — the drainage arguments may have predated or been a separate channel from general conversation — but flagged as a point worth reconciling).

NEW figure (r17): the 1910 leaf of the Brant book records "two hundred thousand pounds even, delivered to the station," "his scale and theirs agreeing," paid on a test of "three forty-two" (3.42) — a wholly new, specific yearly data point not recorded anywhere else in the corpus.

#### The central mystery/timeline of events
(Numbered items 1–48 carried forward verbatim in substance from segment 6/7; segment 8/9 added nothing; segment 10 additions layered in; segment 11 additions/refinements appended as lettered sub-notes at the relevant items, plus new items for the r15/r16/r17 retellings.)
1. **1889** — Association organized at Larrow Green; Gideon Vose first president. Per r12 (Rundle): they met in the schoolhouse in cold weather; "it went on very late and... somebody's horse got loose in the middle of it."
2. **1891** — Main creamery built at Larrow Green; churn set up; valley "began to be paid in cash once a month" (r12). Per r14/Sill: Sill came to Larrow Green in "Eighteen ninety-three," two years after the creamery/churn was set up.
3. **1892** (per r02/r07) or **1891** (per r12/Rundle) or **NEW: 1893** (per r15/Grigg) — Article VII adopted (pay by butterfat test, station's test governs, no appeal). Now a three-way date conflict. Per r12: "there were men who left the Association over it."
4. **1895** — Ansel Keddie goes up to Ostrey Hollow to prepare the station. Confirmed again (r17/Hazel Brant): "Ansel Keddie went up to that station in ninety-five."
5. **1896** (4 May) — Station opens. Per r14/Sill: confirms May 1896 opening, station's own equipment not yet arrived, Sill's personal glass used; station's first partial season (May–Dec 1896) took in "about nine hundred thousand pounds" of milk.
6. **1897** — Station's own outfit arrives (per Ammon & Sons invoice, dated 14 April 1897; in use from 1 May 1897 per r07's provenance note). r11/Vaught's "1898" claim remains a self-contradictory outlier. Per r14/Sill: confirms the station's own outfit "came the spring after" (spring 1897). **NEW POSSIBLE CONFLICT (segment 11, r17/Hazel Brant)**: Hazel states "The crate came out from Tarnet in ninety-nine [1899]" — a new, two-years-later date and a new named place of origin ("Tarnet") for what appears to be the same crate/glass, in tension with the established 1897 invoice date. Possibly a different/later crate, or a memory conflation by Hazel (interview conducted 60+ years after the events); flagged as unresolved.
7. **1898** — Recorded average settles at 3.61. Per r13/Frick (segment 10): a second "3.16" variant recorded. Per r14/Sill (segment 10): Sill's own impressionistic true-value estimate of 3.85 (vs. Bulletin 471's calculated 3.80); Larrow Green's own (non-Hollow) baseline average given as 3.78.
8. **1902** — Complaints first arise (r09, r12, r14 all confirm). NEW (segment 11, r16/Kepp): Kepp's 1926 paper describes the complaint as running "for something like a quarter of a century" as of 1926 — consistent with an 1902 start (24 years).
9. **1907** — Merle Strawn takes over the Ordell dairy-inspection circuit.
10. **1908** — Committee visits under **Nyle Grigg of Larrow Green** (named firsthand in r09's postscript, corroborated by r12/Rundle), blames wet hay of Hessel Bottom. Sampling was winter months only. Per r13/Frick: confirms the 1908 committee report was read into the 1925 arbitration record. **NEW (segment 11, r15)**: the full 1908 committee report is now recovered VERBATIM (see "Objects and Documents Quoted" below), reprinted by Grigg himself in a 1919 covering letter. The report specifies: appointed January 1908; sampled the six Hessel Bottom farms "through January, February and March" (winter months, now precisely dated); visited each farm "some of them three and four times"; drew and sealed their own samples; tried station scales against a known weight and against patrons' own scales; found "no dishonesty of any kind, in any quarter"; concluded the wet hay of the bottom "thins the cream" and "the remedy lies with the land and not with the station." Signed "NYLE GRIGG, chairman, and the committee" (other members never named anywhere in the corpus).
11. **1909** — Strawn visits, all in order.
12. **8 May–10 July 1911** (nine weeks, per r03/r04/r09/r11 — sole outlier is r05/Teague's "six weeks") — station's own measure breaks; substitute used; recorded average rises to 3.80, then reverts to 3.61. **NEW (segment 11, r16/Kepp)**: Kepp's 1926 paper independently references "the summer of 1911" testing being "broken off for six weeks" — using the SAME "six weeks" figure as r05/Teague, now a second source for that specific variant against the "nine weeks" consensus. Kepp dismisses the episode's evidentiary value as unrepresentative summer data ("Six weeks in the flush of the season... I am astonished that anybody should found an argument on six weeks of summer figures").
13. **1912–1918** — Strawn's routine annual visits; Selby Vose becomes manager 1913; Jerome Cudd builds Alder Corners factory 1913, opens 1914; from 1915, four Hessel Bottom farmers begin hauling summer milk to Alder Corners for 16 seasons (1915–1930). **NEW (segment 11, r17/Hazel Brant)**: independently confirms "Four of those six men hauled over the ridge in the summer flush, to the cheese factory on the far side, and brought their milk back down to our own station the rest of the year" — a fresh corroboration of the "four of six/seven" Alder-Corners-hauling arrangement, with the added color that her father Roy called it "the only honest arithmetic anybody in that valley ever did without meaning to."
14. **1919** — Printed circular over manager Selby Vose's signature sent to nine families, alleging "water in the cans." Per r09: Emmet Brant and Ansel Keddie stopped speaking. Per r12/Rundle: "after that the two ends of this valley did not speak civilly to one another for six years" (1919+6=1925). **NEW (segment 11, r17/Hazel Brant)**: independently confirms "nine families" and explicitly reconciles the recurring "nine families vs. six/seven farms" tension for the first time in the corpus: "Nine families, six farms, if you are counting — a farm can hold more than one family and generally did." ### THE THREE EXPLANATIONS BEFORE THE 1925 BOARD (r13, segment 10 — unchanged)
1. **Water theory** — Selby Vose.
