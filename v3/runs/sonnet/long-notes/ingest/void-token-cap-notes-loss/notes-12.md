### RETELLING 18 — FULL CONTENT (Duncan Keddie's family memoir)

**Frame:** Duncan Keddie, retired schoolteacher, age 74 (so writing in 1968, implying birth 1894 — consistent with his stated birth year below), writes this for his daughter Ivy and her children. Purpose: to set the family record straight because "nobody has ever put the family right on paper," and to record where the family's documentary books went before they're all scattered. He states he has "not tried to make anybody in these pages better than he was."

**Section I — The people:**
- **Ansel Keddie** — Duncan's father. Born 1861, died 1934. Was tester and separator man at the **Ostrey Hollow skimming station** of the **Larrow Valley Cooperative Creamery Association**, from the year it opened. Described as "a careful man in small things to a degree that other people found trying" — Duncan says this precision "was the making of the record he left and... the unmaking of him."
- **Orra Brant** — Duncan's mother. Born 1868. Married Ansel in 1889. She was **Emmet Brant's** sister (the younger of the two siblings).
- **Emmet Brant** — Orra's brother, Duncan's uncle. Farmed the largest place on **Hessel Bottom** and hauled the most milk into the station of any man on the creek. Duncan notes this fact "sat in the middle of our family for a generation and hardly anyone outside it ever noticed" — significant because when Ansel was accused in 1925 of "shorting the patrons," the patron he allegedly shorted worst was Emmet — his own wife's brother, who had been a guest at their table every Sunday for 36 years.
- **Duncan Keddie** — the narrator/author. Born 1894. Ansel and Orra's son. Taught school at Ashlin most of his life (arithmetic among other subjects).
- **Ivy** — Duncan's daughter, born 1931. Ansel's granddaughter. Duncan stresses this plainly because he's seen it "printed otherwise" and heard it misstated aloud in town.
- **Roy Brant** — Emmet's son. Farmed the Hessel Bottom place after Emmet's death; kept his father's weigh book. Roy and Duncan are **first cousins** (children of a brother and sister — Emmet and Orra).
- **Hazel Brant** — Roy's daughter. Hazel and Ivy are **second cousins** (children of first cousins) — explicitly NOT "cousins once removed" (a different relation/generation) and NOT "aunt and niece" (a mistake people make because it's shorter to say).
- **Selby Vose** — managed the (Larrow Valley Cooperative Creamery) Association. Son (NOT nephew, as sometimes wrongly reported) of **Gideon Vose**.
- **Gideon Vose** — organized the Association, was its first president, wrote its by-laws.
- **Rosalie Cudd** (of Vessey County) — ran the (Alder Corners) cheese company over the ridge after her father. Daughter of **Jerome Cudd**.
- **Jerome Cudd** — founded the Alder Corners cheese/test company.
- **Merle Strawn** — the state's dairy inspector on this circuit. Explicitly "kin to nobody in the valley whatever — not to us, not to the Brants, not to the Voses, not to anybody on the creek." Duncan flags this as important because his notebooks "turned out to matter" and it matters the notebook-keeper was disinterested/unrelated to everyone.

**Section II — Relationships recap** (explicitly "set out so that they can be copied"): Emmet Brant and Orra (Duncan's mother) = brother and sister. Roy Brant (Emmet's son) and Duncan = first cousins. Hazel Brant (Roy's daughter) and Ivy (Duncan's daughter) = second cousins. Selby Vose = Gideon Vose's SON (not nephew — corrects a common error). Rosalie Cudd = Jerome Cudd's daughter. Merle Strawn = unrelated to everyone in the valley.

**Section III — "A thing that is said and is not so":** No connection whatsoever between the Keddie/Brant family and the Voses. People confuse the (Gideon) Voses with the unrelated "Ashlin Voses." A rumor (heard from two women in town) claims Duncan's mother Orra and Gideon Vose's wife were cousins — one gossip even claims first cousins. Duncan calls this pure talk, "since before I could walk," and notes his mother — who could recite every family connection in the valley ("could not pass a headstone without giving you the connection") — never once included a Vose in her account. This is presented as a **debunked rumor / conflict between town gossip and the family record**.

**Section IV — "My father":**
- Ansel Keddie was **dismissed on 30 April 1925**, four days *before* the arbitration board handed down its official finding — Duncan's dry observation: "the finding found nothing and the dismissal said everything."
- Ansel died in 1934, nine years after dismissal, with the accusation ("the imputation") still on him among the men whose milk he'd weighed since before their fathers died.
- **Summer 1911** (Duncan was 17): Ansel came home saying "it was something in the glass" (referring to testing glassware), and "he never dared put it in the book." He said it twice — at supper and again by the door — unusual behavior for him. In the test book, instead of a figure, he wrote a question mark.
- Ansel then continued testing/recording for **another 14 years** (1911→1925) using the same (implicitly faulty) glassware, dutifully writing down every figure it gave — and "the figures were what convicted him" in 1925. Duncan frames this as tragic: Ansel had nothing but a suspicion against "the outfit he had been taught to trust and the practice of every station in the county," so he kept faithfully recording data he suspected was wrong, and that data was later used against him.
- Orra (mother) outlived Ansel by 14 years, died **1948 at age 82**. She spoke of "the business" (the scandal) only once a year, in April. She kept every letter and left them to Duncan; he says they contain only weather, prices, and her brother's (Emmet's) health — "nothing anybody would want" — but he's keeping them anyway.

**Section V — "The books, and where they went"** (Duncan's chief purpose in writing — tracking 5 physical historical documents/objects):

1. **Station test book** — kept by Ansel in his own hand from 1896 until he left (1925). Impounded by the board of arbitration in March 1925. Held at the Cooperative office in Ashlin 1925-1959. Moved to the **Ordell County Historical Rooms** in 1959 (current location as of the memoir).
2. **Brant weigh book** — started by Emmet Brant 1 January 1897; kept until his death in 1929; passed to son Roy. Roy **lent** it to Ivy in 1958 (Duncan explicitly stresses the word "lent") and got it back. At Roy's death in 1962, it passed to daughter Hazel. Hazel gave it to the Historical Rooms in 1963.
3. **Strawn's circuit notebooks** — one volume per year Merle Strawn rode the Ordell circuit. Went to the state dairy office when Strawn retired in 1936. Transferred in 1947 to the library of the **State Agricultural College at Farland**. Ivy found the volume for **1923** there in 1958 (11 years after the transfer) — "Nobody had asked for them in the interval."
4. **The Alder Corners test book** — kept by Jerome Cudd from the founding of his company; passed to daughter Rosalie in 1941 when she took over the factory; in 1958 she had the figures copied and sent to Ivy at the college. The physical book itself has never left Vessey County.
5. **The crate** (condemned glassware from the station) — put in the loft over the intake room in 1923; stayed there until Ivy found it in **February 1958**. Sent east to Farland to be measured, then returned; in 1959 deposited with the Historical Rooms alongside the test book. Duncan's ironic aside: "Twenty feet of ladder, thirty-five years, and two of the three sessions of that arbitration sat in the room underneath it" (implying the arbitration hearings happened in a room directly below where the crucial glassware sat hidden in the loft, unexamined, the whole time).

**"Added, 1970" (postscript):** Mrs. Cudd's (Rosalie's) test book has, that year (1970), gone to the **Vessey County Historical Society**. Final line: "the five books are now in three buildings and are none of them ours." (Ordell County Historical Rooms; State Agricultural College library at Farland; Vessey County Historical Society — three separate repositories, none belonging to the family.)

**Key oddity/theme:** The central irony of the memoir is that Ansel's own scrupulous honesty (recording a suspicion as a mere question mark rather than falsifying data, then continuing to record data from equipment he distrusted rather than refusing to use it or stop recording) is what ultimately produced the paper trail used to convict/dismiss him — "the most expensive virtue anybody in that valley ever had."

Barnabas Loftus — had told Chalmers the board needed a plank laid across rather than balancing on the flume edge (narrow, often wet). Expenses certified to the county: Coroner's fee $5.00; 6 jurors @ $1.00 = $6.00; witness fees, 4 @ $0.75 = $3.00; physician's fee $4.00; hall rent $1.00; total $19.75, submitted to Dray County commissioners.

Disposition: body released to brother Wilfred Chalmers, who arrived afternoon of the 14th and arranged burial at the county seat (undertaker's charge not a county expense).

Coroner's note: he wrote separately to Colquitt Bros. asking the recommended rail be installed within 30 days, and asked the township clerk to put the matter on the board's next meeting agenda "that it not be forgotten."
