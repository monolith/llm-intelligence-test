# Retention Notes — Segment 1

These notes cover everything read in segment 1: five retellings (r01, r02, r03-embedded, r04, r05) that together tell one continuous, multi-source story about the Ostrey Hollow milk-testing station, plus the noise/filler content in two "long" assembled documents (r03-long.md and r06-long.md), which is NOT part of the story but is logged briefly for completeness. Distractor documents (unrelated, one-off) are NOT detailed here per instructions — only the retellings matter for exhaustive coverage — but I note their topics in one line each in case they recur.

## THE CENTRAL STORY: Ostrey Hollow milk-testing station and the Larrow Valley Cooperative Creamery Association

### Geography / entities
- **Larrow Valley** — the valley; contains **Larrow Green** (site of the main creamery, on Larrow Creek, built 1891) and, seven miles up-valley, **Ostrey Hollow**, at the head of which stood the **skimming station**.
- **Hessel Bottom** — a wet meadow below the Ostrey Hollow hamlet; the best milk in the Hollow came from here. Seven farms had grazing/haying rights on Hessel Bottom; the largest was **Emmet Brant's**.
- **Ashlin** — the county seat/town where hearings, the Association's office (later), the historical society, and legal business took place.
- **Farland** — site of a college with a library (where Ivy Keddie later worked/taught, and where Merle Strawn's notebooks were held).
- **Redlow Ridge**, **Vessey County** — location of the **Alder Corners Cheese Company** (a rival/comparison creamery, used for cross-checking milk tests).
- **Larrow Valley Cooperative Creamery Association** — organized 1889 by valley farmers at Larrow Green. By-laws drafted by **Gideon Vose**, who was elected first president in 1889 and served until 1911 (22 years — longest of any president). Gideon Vose died in 1917.
- **Article VII** of the by-laws (adopted 1892, the year after the creamery opened), quoted in full by Effie Loomis (r02): *"Every patron shall be paid for the milk he delivers according to the number of pounds of butter fat contained in it, ascertained by test, and not according to the weight of the milk; and the test of the station where he delivers shall govern."* Key point emphasized by Loomis: "the test of the station where he delivers shall govern" — no appeal from the station's own test. This clause is called "the hinge of the whole Ostrey Hollow business."

### People and relationships (the core family/social web)
- **Ansel Keddie** — tester at the Ostrey Hollow skimming station. Went up in 1895 to prepare it, opened/began testing 4 May 1896 (first leaf of his book, quoted twice, once in r03 and again in r04: *"Ostrey Hollow station. Begun this 4th day of May, 1896. Glass borrowed of Larrow Green until our own comes. A. Keddie."*). Tested 29 seasons, 1896–1924 (did not test in 1925). Dismissed ("put out") on **30 April 1925**. Died in **1934**.
  - Note an internal inconsistency in Keddie's own letter (r03): the body text says the glass was "borrowed off the Ashlin creamery," but the book's own first-leaf entry (quoted in the same letter) says "Glass borrowed of Larrow Green." These two statements conflict with each other within the same document.
- **Orra Keddie** (née Brant) — Ansel Keddie's wife; sister of **Emmet Brant**; first cousin of **old Mrs. Vose** (per r01). After the 1919 circular (see below), Orra and her brother Emmet did not speak again — "not one word, not at the store, not at a funeral," per r01. She would walk the long way to avoid passing her brother's lane. She outlived Ansel by "a good while."
- **Emmet Brant** — biggest patron on Hessel Bottom (most cows, most hay ground, most milk hauled). Orra Keddie's brother. His son is **Roy Brant** (see r04), who kept up the Brant weigh book after his father died and lent it to Ivy Keddie in 1958.
- **Selby Vose** — son of Gideon Vose; became manager of the Association in 1913, served until 1938 (per r02); signed the circular/letter of 1919 accusing Ostrey Hollow of "water in the cans" (per r01). Per r05 (Teague), Vose signed the annual station sheet every year and so had seen the "station gain" entries; Teague raised the issue with him in 1916 and was told to let it alone.
- **Gideon Vose** — founder/first president of the Association (1889–1911), drafted the by-laws, died 1917. His widow is referred to as "old Mrs. Vose" in r01, said to be first cousins with Orra Keddie.
- **Effie Loomis** — secretary of the Association, 1901–1940; kept the minute book. Wrote "Notes toward a history of the Association" (r02) in 1941, the year after retiring. Was 14 years old in 1889 (so born ~1875).
- **Wilbur Teague** — bookkeeper of the Association, 1905–1945 (40 years); kept the ledgers at the Ashlin office (one room over a harness shop). Wrote an unpublished memoir, "The Books of the Association," Chapter Nine written at Ashlin, winter 1948 (r05), three years after retiring.
- **Hobart Sill** — buttermaker at Larrow Green; kept the churn book and cream receiving book (mentioned in r02).
- **Alonzo Frick** — attorney of Ashlin; chaired the board of arbitration that heard the Ostrey Hollow patrons' petition (confirmed in r02, r03, r05).
- **Vira Toland** — narrator of r01; born 1912 at the top of Ostrey Hollow; daughter of a Hessel Bottom patron (one of the nine families hauling milk to the station). Interviewed/taped in 1979 at Ostrey Hollow by "a young man from the county."

- **Roy Brant** — Emmet Brant's son; kept up the Brant weigh book after his father's death; lent it to Ivy Keddie.
- **Rosalie Cudd** — runs the Alder Corners Cheese Company (Redlow Ridge, Vessey County); niece of **Jerome Cudd**; copied out that factory's test figures for Ivy Keddie (the original book never left Vessey County).
- **Merle Strawn** — state dairy inspector for the county from 1908; kept circuit notebooks, held at Farland college library since 1947 (unexamined for 11 years until Ivy Keddie's 1958 research).
- **Professor Vaught** — authored (with Ivy Keddie) Bulletin No. 471 (issued October 1958), which is quoted as the final exoneration of Keddie.
- A **young woman from "the college away east"** visited Ostrey Hollow in 1958 to interview old-timers (per r01) — she was "Hazel Brant's first cousin once removed." (Hazel Brant is not otherwise identified but implies a Brant family connection; this researcher may be Ivy Keddie herself, though r01 doesn't name her — plausible link given the year 1958 matches Ivy Keddie's research year, but this is NOT stated explicitly, only inferred — flagging as uncertain.)

### The central mystery/timeline of events
1. **1889** — Association organized at Larrow Green; Gideon Vose first president.
2. **1891** — Main creamery built at Larrow Green.
3. **1892** — Article VII adopted (pay by butterfat test, station's test governs, no appeal).
4. **1895** — Ansel Keddie goes up to Ostrey Hollow to prepare the station.
5. **1896** (4 May) — Station opens; Keddie begins testing, using glass borrowed (per the book's first leaf) "of Larrow Green" — or (per his own later letter text) "off the Ashlin creamery" [internal conflict, see above]. Recorded average test that year: 3.80.
6. **1897** — Station's own outfit (six dozen pipettes etc., invoice $77.93 total for pipettes, bottles, acid measure, hand tester, and freight — per r04) arrives; used from then on. Average this year: "part year" (incomplete data, per Keddie's 1922 summary leaf).
7. **1898** — Recorded average settles at 3.61 (and stays "within three hundredths of it" almost every year after, except the 1911 anomaly).
8. **1902** — Complaints first arise at Larrow Green that Ostrey Hollow milk doesn't test as well as the rest of the valley's.
9. **1908** — A committee visits, samples herds through the winter, and reports that the wet hay of Hessel Bottom thins the cream, blaming the land itself (later shown to be false — see Bulletin No. 214 below).
10. **8 May 1911** — The station's own measuring device (pipette/measure) breaks. A substitute/borrowed measure is used.
    - Per r03 (Keddie) and r04 (Ivy Keddie): the borrowed measure came from Larrow Green, arriving with the cream wagon, used from 8 May to **10 July 1911** (about nine weeks). During this window the recorded average jumped to 3.80 and reverted to 3.61 the day the station's own measure returned.
    - Per r05 (Teague): the break is dated to "spring of 1911," and the borrowed measure was used for "six weeks" before the station's own came up from town — a shorter duration than the ~9 weeks given in r03/r04. **This is a numerical conflict between sources.**
    - Keddie's own book entries, quoted in r03: *"May 8. Pipette broke this morning. Using a borrowed measure."* and *"July 10. Our own measure in use again from today. Tests are back where they were. ? — A.K."* — the question mark is Keddie's own, left standing for 14 years (as of his 1925 letter) because he could not explain it.
11. **1913** — Selby Vose becomes Association manager (serves until 1938).
12. **1919** — A printed circular, over manager Selby Vose's signature, is sent to nine families (the Ostrey Hollow patrons) stating there was "water in the cans" — i.e., accusing them of watering/diluting milk (or at least stating the tests showed as much). Per r01 (Vira Toland): her father received this letter at the table, read it twice with his hat still on, and kept it in a desk drawer until he died; his son (Vira's brother) reportedly still has it. Other families kept theirs for decades too — one woman had hers pinned in her trunk lid.
    - Note: r03 (Keddie's own letter) also references "In 1919 a circular went out from the Association to nine families up here saying there was water in their cans," confirming the 1919 date and the "nine families" detail, and states he bears no grudge against those who signed it.
13. **1922** — Ansel Keddie writes a summary leaf of averages at the back of his test book (quoted identically in both r03 and r04): *"Average test of all patrons, this station: 1896, 3.80. 1897, part year. 1898, 3.61. And 3.61 or within three hundredths of it every year since, save the nine weeks of 1911 that are noted in their place, when it stood at 3.80."*
14. **Per r01 (Vira Toland's account — later shown to be at least partly mistaken/contradicted)**: "All at once, one summer, the figures came up and stayed up" with no explanation given by anyone; this is described as happening BEFORE a hearing that r01 dates to "the winter and spring after" 1919 (i.e., roughly 1919–1920). r01 claims the hearing found Keddie had been "taking the cream" (stealing/skimming), that he was dismissed for this reason, and that this is why the numbers "went right" the following summer. r01 states the patrons were paid **$3,500** total, split nine ways, calling it "settled." r01's narrator explicitly asserts theft: "He was taking the cream. They caught him at it... A man stands in a room by himself with the separator running and the door shut and nobody in the valley can add, and after a while he helps himself..." Her own father reportedly said only that "Ansel Keddie was an honest man who had got into something."
    - **This account is contradicted by every other source in the story** (see Conflicts section below) — the hearing was actually in 1925, not 1919–1920; the award was $3,800, not $3,500; and the board explicitly found "no cause established," with the true cause (revealed in 1958) being faulty measuring equipment, not theft.
15. **June 1923** — A crate of milk-test pipettes/measures at the Ostrey Hollow station is condemned by the state's inspector; new ones are ordered. This crate is set aside and stored (later found in the loft, under a tarpaulin, stenciled "AMMON & SONS DAIRY SUPPLY TARNET"). Per r02 (Effie Loomis), this crate/condemnation was **never mentioned at the 1925 hearing** at all — not by patrons, officers, or the board — despite the board's award referencing "the books and appliances of the station" as a generic phrase.
16. **January 1925** — Patrons of Ostrey Hollow formally petition the Association, claiming their milk had never tested as well as their neighbors' and asking to be made whole.
17. **17 March 1925** — Ansel Keddie gives a nine-sentence statement to the board of arbitration at Ashlin (quoted in full in r03). He asserts he never took cream, tested consistently and correctly for 29 seasons, and cannot explain the figures.
18. **17 March – 4 May 1925** — Board of arbitration sits at Ashlin, chaired by attorney **Alonzo Frick**. Per r02: "Three explanations were argued before it. None of the three was accepted." Per r05 (Teague), Teague testified that the arrangement (one man alone handling weighing, sampling, testing, and running the separator, unsupervised) created opportunity for theft — but did NOT claim to know that theft occurred, and stated "the board found nothing established against anybody."
19. **30 April 1925** — Ansel Keddie is dismissed ("put out") — the last day of the month, despite the board finding "no cause established." Keddie's own letter (r03) juxtaposes these two facts without resolving the contradiction: "I was put out on the thirtieth of April. They found no cause established and they put me out on the last day of the month, and both of those things are true at once, and I have given up trying to hold them in my head together."
20. **The award (1925)**: Per r02 and r05 (both consistent): the board awarded the Ostrey Hollow patrons **$3,800**, "without admission," charged to the station account. Quoted award text (r02): *"The board having heard the patrons and the officers, and having had before it the books and appliances of the station, finds no cause established. The board awards to the Ostrey Hollow patrons the sum of thirty-eight hundred dollars ($3,800.00), without admission, to be charged to the station account."*
    - This conflicts with r01, which gives the award as **$3,500**, split nine ways, and frames it as a settlement following a finding of guilt (theft), not a "no cause established" arbitration award.
21. **1934** — Ansel Keddie dies, his name still under suspicion/cloud.
22. **1938** — Selby Vose's term as manager ends (per r02). Also the year the Association's office was moved and the 1889–1892 minute book was lost in the move (never recovered; per Effie Loomis in r02, "it was carried out with the rest and it did not arrive").
23. **1940** — Effie Loomis retires as secretary (served 1901–1940).
24. **1941** — Effie Loomis writes "Notes toward a history of the Association" (r02).
25. **1945** — Wilbur Teague retires as bookkeeper (served 1905–1945).
26. **1947** — Merle Strawn's circuit notebooks placed in Farland college library (per r04), where they sit unexamined for 11 years.
27. **1948** (winter) — Wilbur Teague writes Chapter Nine of his unpublished memoir, "The Books of the Association" (r05), at Ashlin.
28. **1958 (February)** — Ivy Keddie finds the condemned pipette crate in the station loft (stenciled "AMMON & SONS DAIRY SUPPLY TARNET"), carried up there in June 1923; contains nine unbroken pipettes out of six dozen originally bought.
29. **1958 (9 March)** — Ivy Keddie measures the nine pipettes at Farland against a calibrated burette.
30. **1958 (October)** — Bulletin No. 471 issued (Ivy Keddie and Professor Vaught), exonerating Ansel Keddie and explaining the true cause: undersized pipettes, not theft or bad hay.
31. **1959** — Ivy Keddie delivers "What the Crate Held" to the Ordell County Historical Society at Ashlin (r04), with the crate itself displayed at the meeting.
32. **1979** — Vira Toland's oral history (r01) is recorded — twenty-one years AFTER the 1958/1959 exoneration, yet she still tells the older, disproven "theft" narrative as fact, apparently unaware of (or not crediting) Ivy Keddie's later findings. This is presented without her acknowledging Bulletin No. 471 at all.

### The scientific resolution (the key numeric/technical content, from r04)
- Ivy Keddie consulted **five sets of records**: (1) the station test book (in the Association's office since 1925, delivered to the Historical Society's "Rooms" the year of her talk); (2) the **Brant weigh book** (Hessel Bottom records from 1897 onward, lent by Roy Brant); (3) the **Alder Corners Cheese Company** test figures from Redlow Ridge, Vessey County (copied out by Rosalie Cudd; original book never left Vessey County; Ivy never saw it directly); (4) **Merle Strawn's circuit notebooks** (state dairy inspector's records from 1908, at Farland college library since 1947); (5) the Association's own annual ledgers.
- The crate: found February 1958 in the loft over the old intake room at the station, under a tarpaulin, stenciled "AMMON & SONS DAIRY SUPPLY TARNET." Carried up there in June 1923 (matching the condemnation event Effie Loomis mentioned). Inside, packed in straw: the station's condemned milk-test pipettes — six dozen originally bought new in 1897 (invoice **$77.93** for the whole outfit: pipettes, bottles, acid measure, hand tester, and freight). Only **nine** were unbroken in the crate.
- On **9 March 1958**, Ivy Keddie measured these nine pipettes at Farland against a calibrated burette. Quoted measurement sheet: *"Nine pipettes from the Ostrey Hollow crate, stems blown 17.6 c.c. Delivered against burette, mean of five runs each: 16.70, 16.71, 16.72, 16.72, 16.72, 16.73, 16.73, 16.74, 16.71. Mean 16.72 c.c. Short 0.88 c.c. That is 95 hundredths of the mark, and the mark is what the neck of the bottle was ruled for."*
- Math: the pipettes were supposed to deliver 17.6 c.c. but actually delivered a mean of 16.72 c.c. — 16.72/17.6 = 0.95 (95%). Every butterfat test made with this glass therefore read 95% of the true value.
- Applying this correction to Keddie's own 1922 summary leaf (recorded average 3.61%): 3.61 ÷ 0.95 = **3.80%** true average. The gap (0.19, i.e., "nineteen hundredths") is about 5% of the true 3.80 figure.
- Quantities over 25 full delivery years (1898–1922): station took in **40 million pounds** of milk (average **1,600,000 lbs/year**) — this figure matches Effie Loomis's account in r02 (which independently states the same "forty million pounds... average of one million six hundred thousand pounds in a year," attributed to bookkeeper Teague's annual sheets) but **conflicts with Wilbur Teague's own r05 account**, which states the station took in **44 million pounds** of milk over the years (a different, larger total — internal conflict between two sources, one of which is Teague's own memoir versus the "his figures" cited secondhand by Loomis).
- At the recorded 3.61% test: patrons were credited **1,444,000 lbs** of butterfat. At the true 3.80% test: they had actually delivered **1,520,000 lbs**. Difference: **76,000 lbs of butterfat**, unpaid.
- Butterfat averaged **$0.25/lb** over those years (matching Effie Loomis's r02 figure of "the price paid for butterfat averaged twenty-five cents a pound" for the 25-year claim period) → unpaid fat worth **$19,000**.
- The 1925 arbitration award of $3,800 was therefore "a fifth of" the true value owed ($3,800/$19,000 = 0.2).
- Two valid-but-different percentages exist depending on the denominator: 76,000 lbs is 5% of the actual fat delivered (1,520,000) and 5.25% of the fat credited (1,444,000). Ivy Keddie notes the Association's bookkeeping (per r05, Teague's "station gain" line) used the second denominator ("a bookkeeper naturally reckons a surplus against the figure he has already entered").
- **Key connection**: this 5.25% figure is EXACTLY the same percentage Wilbur Teague's ledger recorded every year as "station gain" (see r05 below) — meaning Teague's own books had been recording physical evidence of the shortfall for decades ("as usual") without anyone identifying its cause.
- Because the test error was proportional (a fixed percentage), richer milk lost more in absolute terms: a herd recorded at 4.56% test was short 0.24 points; one recorded at 3.42% was short only 0.18 points.
- The 1911 anomaly is explained: the substitute/borrowed measure (which, unlike the station's own faulty pipettes, was accurate) was in use 8 May–10 July 1911, causing tests to jump to the true 3.80% and drop back to the biased 3.61% the moment the faulty station measure returned — "one object changed" and the whole average moved, which Ivy Keddie says is the only surviving explanation for "this valley's twenty-six-year anomaly."
- The **wet-hay theory is explicitly refuted**. State Agricultural College tested the claim in 1911 and published **Bulletin No. 214**, quoted: *"Bog hay fed as the whole of the roughage depressed the butter fat test by not more than five hundredths of one per cent in winter, and by nothing that could be measured on summer pasture."* This is "four times too small" and "the wrong shape" compared to the observed year-round 0.19-point gap (present in July as much as February).
- Cross-check: the Alder Corners factory (over the ridge) tested milk from four Hessel Bottom farmers during their summer flush; across 14 overlapping seasons, the SAME milk from the SAME wet ground tested about 0.19 points HIGHER at Alder Corners than at home — proving the milk itself wasn't deficient, only the Ostrey Hollow station's measurement.
- **Bulletin No. 471**, issued October 1958 (Ivy Keddie and Professor Vaught), final exoneration, quoted in full: *"No milk was watered at Ostrey Hollow and no cream was stolen there. From the first of May, 1897, to the twelfth of June, 1923, the station's tests were low by one part in twenty because its pipettes were short by one part in twenty, and the difference — seventy-six thousand pounds of butter fat — stands credited in the Association's own ledgers, year by year, as station gain."*
  - Note: this bulletin dates the faulty-pipette period as "the first of May, 1897" to "the twelfth of June, 1923" — i.e., starting when the station's OWN outfit arrived in 1897 (not the 1896 borrowed glass) and ending when the crate was condemned in June 1923. This is a specific, dated range that should be treated as authoritative for the "true" story, though it doesn't perfectly reconcile with Keddie's own 1922 summary leaf, which shows 1896 (with borrowed glass) ALSO reading high (3.80) and only dropping to 3.61 in 1898 with the station's own (faulty) equipment — consistent with the bulletin's start date of 1897 being when the bad equipment was first used.
- Timeline of vindication, per Ivy Keddie: "The glass was condemned in 1923 and the cause was found in 1958: thirty-five years. My grandfather was dismissed in 1925: thirty-three years. He died in 1934 and had been dead twenty-four years when the sheet was read that clears him."

### Wilbur Teague's "station-gain" ledger line (r05) — the bookkeeping trace of the same phenomenon
- Teague invented this bookkeeping device himself in 1905: one column entered the butterfat credited to patrons on the station's OWN test; the other entered the butterfat actually received and tested at Larrow Green from the station's cream. The second figure was ALWAYS larger. The difference was labeled "station gain" and credited to the Association's general account (i.e., the Association benefited financially from the shortfall, though unknowingly, per the later exoneration).
- Quoted sample entry, for 1898: *"Ostrey Hollow: butter fat credited to patrons on station test, 57,760 lb. Butter fat received at Larrow Green in station cream, 60,800 lb. Station gain, 3,040 lb, five and a quarter per cent, as usual. Carried to the general account."*
- This "five and a quarter per cent" (5.25%) gain recurred so consistently every year that the phrase "as usual" became part of the standard ledger entry — precisely matching the 5.25% figure Ivy Keddie later calculates independently from the pipette measurements (76,000/1,444,000 = 5.25%). Teague's ledgers were unknowingly recording the exact signature of the equipment defect for decades.
- Teague's testimony to the 1925 board argued that the station's staffing arrangement (one man alone doing everything, unsupervised, for 29 seasons) created opportunity for theft, but he explicitly says he does NOT know what, if anything, any particular person did, and confirms "the board found nothing established against anybody."
- Teague states Selby Vose signed the annual station sheet every year (so had seen the figures) and that Teague raised concerns with Vose privately in 1916 but was told to let it alone, and did.

## FULL LIST OF CONFLICTS AND ODDITIES BETWEEN/WITHIN THE RETELLINGS

1. **Date of the hearing**: r01 (Vira Toland) implies the hearing/settlement was in the "winter and spring after" 1919 (i.e., ~1919–1920). r02 (Effie Loomis), r03 (Keddie's own statement), and r05 (Teague) all agree the actual petition was January 1925 and the board of arbitration sat at Ashlin from 17 March to 4 May 1925.
2. **Award amount**: r01 says the patrons were paid **$3,500**, split nine ways. r02, r03 (implicitly, by referencing the same award), and r05 all agree on **$3,800**.
3. **Cause/verdict**: r01 asserts Ansel Keddie was caught stealing/skimming cream and was dismissed BECAUSE of proven theft — explicitly stating "He was taking the cream. They caught him at it." Every other source contradicts this: r02 and r05 both state the board "found no cause established" against anyone; r03 has Keddie flatly deny ever taking cream; and r04's Bulletin No. 471 (1958) declares definitively "No milk was watered at Ostrey Hollow and no cream was stolen there," attributing the entire shortfall to defective pipettes.
4. **Total milk received**: r02 (Effie Loomis, citing bookkeeper Teague's annual sheets) and r04 (Ivy Keddie's independent calculation) both say **40 million pounds** over 25 years (avg. 1.6 million lbs/year). r05 (Teague's own memoir) says **44 million pounds** — a direct conflict with the figures attributed to Teague's own sheets elsewhere.
5. **Average butterfat test figure**: r03 (Keddie's book) and r04 (Ivy Keddie, using Keddie's book) use **3.61%** as the long-term recorded average (with 3.80% in 1896 and during the 1911 anomaly). r05 (Teague) instead states the average test of all Ostrey Hollow patrons over the whole period was **3.16%** — a notably different, lower figure with no reconciliation offered between the sources.
6. **Duration of the 1911 measure substitution**: r03 and r04 both specify precisely 8 May to 10 July 1911 (about nine weeks — matching Keddie's own book entries and his 1922 summary leaf's phrase "nine weeks of 1911"). r05 (Teague) says the substitute was used for "six weeks" before the station's own measure came back — a shorter duration, conflicting with the more precisely dated 9-week figure elsewhere.
7. **Source of the borrowed glass, 1896 vs. 1911**: Keddie's letter (r03) states the FIRST glass (1896) was "borrowed off the Ashlin creamery" in the body text, but the book's own first-leaf entry (also quoted in r03 and again in r04) says "Glass borrowed of Larrow Green until our own comes." Separately, the 1911 substitute measure is stated (r04) to have "come up from Larrow Green with the cream wagon" — so Larrow Green is the consistent source for the 1911 substitute, but the 1896 origin is internally contradictory within Keddie's own account.
8. **The crate's role at the 1925 hearing**: r02 (Effie Loomis) states specifically that the condemned crate of measures (from June 1923) was NEVER mentioned in any of the three sessions of the 1925 hearing — not by patrons, officers, or the board — even though the board's award language referenced "the books and appliances of the station" as a generic phrase not itemizing anything. This means the actual physical cause of the shortfall sat undiscussed and unexamined for 33 more years until Ivy Keddie's 1958 investigation.
9. **Wet-hay explanation**: mentioned as one theory in r03 (a 1908 committee blamed "the wet hay of the Bottom" for thinning the cream) and explicitly and quantitatively refuted in r04 via State Agricultural College Bulletin No. 214 (1911), which found the wet-hay effect "four times too small" and seasonally the "wrong shape" to explain the observed gap.
10. **r01's status as an outdated/unreliable narrative**: Vira Toland's 1979 oral history repeats the theft narrative as settled fact twenty-one years after Ivy Keddie's 1958 exoneration and Bulletin No. 471, without any acknowledgment of that later finding — an oddity/gap in transmission of information within the valley's own oral history, possibly reflecting how thoroughly the original (incorrect) narrative had become entrenched locally even after being formally disproven.
11. **The unnamed 1958 researcher in r01**: Vira Toland recalls a young woman from "the college away east" who visited in 1958, described as "Hazel Brant's first cousin once removed," who "went up and poked about the old station building" and interviewed Vira, taking notes. This is very likely (but not explicitly confirmed in the text) the same person as Ivy Keddie, whose investigation year (1958) matches exactly — but Vira Toland never names her, and Ivy Keddie's own account (r04) does not mention interviewing anyone in Ostrey Hollow, focusing instead on documentary/scientific research (crate, pipettes, ledgers). This is flagged as an unresolved, uncertain possible connection, not a stated fact.
12. **"Nine families" vs. "seven farms"**: r01 and r03 both describe the 1919 circular as going to "nine families." Meanwhile r02 describes "Seven farms had rights on Hessel Bottom" as patrons of the station generally. These are not necessarily contradictory (nine families total hauled to the station; seven farms specifically had Hessel Bottom grazing/haying rights), but the two different small numbers (seven vs. nine) both recur and should not be conflated.
