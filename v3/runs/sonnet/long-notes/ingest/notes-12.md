### THE CENTRAL STORY: Ostrey Hollow milk-testing station and the Larrow Valley Cooperative Creamery Association

#### Geography / entities
- **Larrow Valley** — the valley; contains **Larrow Green** (site of the main creamery, on Larrow Creek, built 1891) and, seven miles up-valley, **Ostrey Hollow**, at the head of which stood the **skimming station**.
- **Hessel Bottom** — a wet meadow below the Ostrey Hollow hamlet; the best milk in the Hollow came from here. Seven farms had grazing/haying rights on Hessel Bottom (per r02/Effie Loomis) — but Roy Brant (r09) says **six farms**; the largest was **Emmet Brant's** (per r02) — confirmed independently by Roy Brant (r09): the Brant farm was "the largest of the six — sixty-odd acres of the meadow, and thirty-eight cows in a good year." "Six farms" is now corroborated by FIVE independent sources (r09, r15/Grigg's 1908 committee report, r16/Kepp, r17/Hazel Brant, plus the underlying 1908 report itself) against r02/Loomis's lone "seven."
- **Ashlin** — the county seat/town where hearings, the Association's office (later), the historical society, and legal business took place. Per r17: the Brant weigh book was held "down the street at the historical rooms in this town" of Ashlin — "where anybody may look at it who wants to, which is nobody." NEW (segment 12, r18/Duncan Keddie): confirmed and expanded — the Brant weigh book went to the "Ordell County Historical Rooms" in Ashlin in 1963 (given by Hazel Brant); the station test book also went there in 1959. "Ordell County" is now given as the formal county name (previously only "Ordell County Bar Association," "Ordell County Farm Club," "Ordell dairy-inspection circuit" had appeared — now explicitly the county containing Ashlin and the Historical Rooms).
- **Farland** — site of a college with a library (where Ivy Keddie later worked/taught/studied, and where Merle Strawn's notebooks were held). The State Agricultural College at Farland published Bulletin No. 214 (1911, feeding trial on bog hay) and Bulletin No. 471 (1958, final exoneration).
- **Redlow Ridge**, **Vessey County** — location of the **Alder Corners Cheese Company** (a rival/comparison creamery, used for cross-checking milk tests). Per r10 (Rosalie Cudd): the ridge is a 4-mile switchback road, cut for a lumber road, shuts in November and does not open before April (some years May); "no arguing with it."
- **Tarnet** — per r17/Hazel Brant, the place the pipette crate "came out from... in ninety-nine" (1899) — a supplier/origin location and date for the crate, in tension with the established 1897 Ammon & Sons invoice date (see conflicts below).
- **Larrow Valley Cooperative Creamery Association** — organized 1889 by valley farmers at Larrow Green. By-laws drafted by **Gideon Vose**, who was elected first president in 1889 and served until 1911 (22 years — longest of any president). Gideon Vose died in 1917.
- **Article VII** of the by-laws — quoted in full by Effie Loomis (r02), curator Halbert Nym (r07, Item 1), and a THIRD time by Dell Rundle (r12): *"Every patron shall be paid for the milk he delivers according to the number of pounds of butter fat contained in it, ascertained by test, and not according to the weight of the milk; and the test of the station where he delivers shall govern."* Key point emphasized by Loomis and again by Rundle: "the test of the station where he delivers shall govern" — no appeal from the station's own test, "the sentence on which the whole trouble at the head of the valley afterward turned" (Rundle). Adoption date: r02/r07 say **1892**; CONFLICT (r12/Rundle): "1891, the year the churn was set up"; THIRD variant (r15/Nyle Grigg's 1919 letter): "came in with the 1893 meeting, as I have always understood it" — a genuine three-way conflict: 1891 (Rundle) / 1892 (Loomis, curator Nym) / 1893 (Grigg). Attorney Alonzo Frick's 1946 recollections (r13) also describe the "no appeal" rule as central and calls the whole affair "not litigation at all... an arbitration under the by-laws of a creamery association, with no appeal from it, no reported decision, and nothing whatever to cite it for."

#### People and relationships (the core family/social web)
- **Ansel Keddie** — tester at the Ostrey Hollow skimming station. Went up in 1895 to prepare it, opened/began testing 4 May 1896. Tested 29 seasons, 1896–1924 (did not test in 1925). Dismissed ("put out") on **30 April 1925**. Died in **1934**. NEW (segment 12, r18/Duncan Keddie, his son): born **1861**, died 1934 (confirms age ~73 at death). Described by his son as "a careful man in small things to a degree that other people found trying, and it was the making of the record he left and, I have sometimes thought, the unmaking of him." NEW (r18): in the summer of 1911 he came home saying "it was something in the glass" — said it twice, at supper and again by the door, "which was not his way" — witnessed firsthand by 17-year-old Duncan. He never put this suspicion in the book; what he wrote instead was a question mark. He kept testing for another 14 years after this private suspicion, writing down every figure the flawed glass gave him — "the figures were what convicted him." Duncan's reflection: "this was the most expensive virtue anybody in that valley ever had."
  - Internal inconsistency in Keddie's own letter (r03): "borrowed off the Ashlin creamery" vs. book's own first-leaf entry "Glass borrowed of Larrow Green" — Larrow Green corroborated independently at least four times elsewhere (r07 Item 2, r07 curatorial note, r14/Sill's personal account). #### The central mystery/timeline of events
(Numbered items 1–70 carried forward verbatim in substance from segment 6/7/10/11; segment 12 additions/refinements appended as lettered sub-notes at the relevant items, plus new items for the r18 retelling.)
1. **1889** — Association organized at Larrow Green; Gideon Vose first president. Per r12 (Rundle): they met in the schoolhouse in cold weather; "it went on very late and... somebody's horse got loose in the middle of it." **NEW (segment 12, r18):** Orra Brant married Ansel Keddie in 1889 — same year as the Association's founding (coincidence noted, not remarked on by any source).
2. **1891** — Main creamery built at Larrow Green; churn set up; valley "began to be paid in cash once a month" (r12). Per r14/Sill: Sill came to Larrow Green in "Eighteen ninety-three," two years after the creamery/churn was set up.
3. **1892** (per r02/r07) or **1891** (per r12/Rundle) or **1893** (per r15/Grigg) — Article VII adopted (pay by butterfat test, station's test governs, no appeal). Three-way date conflict, unresolved by any segment-12 material.
4. **1895** — Ansel Keddie goes up to Ostrey Hollow to prepare the station. Confirmed again (r17/Hazel Brant): "Ansel Keddie went up to that station in ninety-five."
5. **1896** (4 May) — Station opens. Per r14/Sill: confirms May 1896 opening, station's own equipment not yet arrived, Sill's personal glass used; station's first partial season (May–Dec 1896) took in "about nine hundred thousand pounds" of milk.
6. **1897** — Station's own outfit arrives (per Ammon & Sons invoice, dated 14 April 1897; in use from 1 May 1897 per r07's provenance note). r11/Vaught's "1898" claim remains a self-contradictory outlier. Per r14/Sill: confirms the station's own outfit "came the spring after" (spring 1897). POSSIBLE CONFLICT (r17/Hazel Brant): Hazel states "The crate came out from Tarnet in ninety-nine [1899]" — a new, two-years-later date and a new named place of origin ("Tarnet") for what appears to be the same crate/glass, in tension with the established 1897 invoice date. **NEW (segment 12, r18/Duncan Keddie): Duncan's provenance account of "the crate" does not give an arrival/origin date for the crate itself — he describes only that "the station's condemned glassware went up into the loft over the intake room in 1923 and stayed there until Ivy found it in February of 1958" — so this does not directly confirm or refute either the 1897 or 1899 dating, though it is silent on "Tarnet" and offers no corroboration of Hazel's account. Flagged as still unresolved.**
7. **1898** — Recorded average settles at 3.61. Per r13/Frick: a second "3.16" variant recorded. Per r14/Sill: Sill's own impressionistic true-value estimate of 3.85 (vs. Bulletin 471's calculated 3.80); Larrow Green's own (non-Hollow) baseline average given as 3.78.
8. **1902** — Complaints first arise (r09, r12, r14 all confirm). Per r16/Kepp: complaint described as running "for something like a quarter of a century" as of 1926 — consistent with an 1902 start (24 years).
9. **1907** — Merle Strawn takes over the Ordell dairy-inspection circuit.
10. **1908** — Committee visits under **Nyle Grigg of Larrow Green**, blames wet hay of Hessel Bottom. Sampling was winter months only (January–March 1908). Full 1908 committee report recovered verbatim in segment 11 (r15) — see "Objects and Documents Quoted" below.
11. **1909** — Strawn visits, all in order.
12. **8 May–10 July 1911** (nine weeks, per r03/r04/r09/r11 — outliers are r05/Teague's "six weeks" and now r16/Kepp's independent "six weeks") — station's own measure breaks; substitute used; recorded average rises to 3.80, then reverts to 3.61. **NEW (segment 12, r18/Duncan Keddie) — this is the SAME 1911 episode Duncan describes from his father's own mouth** (see Ansel Keddie entry above): Ansel came home in "that summer of 1911" saying "it was something in the glass" — the first-ever firsthand account of Ansel's own private, contemporaneous suspicion of the equipment during this exact episode, 47 years before the crate was found. This is a major new piece of evidence: it shows Ansel Keddie himself suspected the true cause as early as 1911, but suppressed it because he "would not put down what he could not prove."
13. **1912–1918** — Strawn's routine annual visits; Selby Vose becomes manager 1913; Jerome Cudd builds Alder Corners factory 1913, opens 1914; from 1915, four Hessel Bottom farmers begin hauling summer milk to Alder Corners for 16 seasons (1915–1930). Per r17/Hazel Brant: confirms "Four of those six men hauled over the ridge in the summer flush... and brought their milk back down to our own station the rest of the year."

Per r12/Rundle: "after that the two ends of this valley did not speak civilly to one another for six years" (1919+6=1925). Per r17/Hazel Brant: confirms "nine families" and reconciles the "nine families vs. six/seven farms" tension: "Nine families, six farms... a farm can hold more than one family and generally did." Hazel's own mother received one of the nine letters, "read hers twice and put it in the stove." POSSIBLE CONFLICT (r17/Hazel Brant): Hazel states the state man condemned the Ostrey Hollow station's glass "in twenty-one" (1921) — conflicts with the well-established consensus condemnation date of June 1923 (Strawn's log, Bulletin 471, r07 photostat Item 9) or 12 July 1923 (r08/Vose, r12/Rundle). **NEW (segment 12, r18/Duncan Keddie): does not directly date the condemnation, but confirms the crate "went up into the loft over the intake room in 1923" — implicitly corroborating the 1923 consensus date over Hazel's 1921 claim, since the crate could only go to the loft after being condemned.** This tips the weight of evidence further toward 1923 and away from Hazel's 1921.

20. **30 April 1925** — Ansel Keddie dismissed despite "no cause established." Per r13/Frick: "four days before we made our award." **NEW (segment 12, r18): independently reconfirmed by Duncan, his son, in the same words/structure as Frick's account ("four days before").**
