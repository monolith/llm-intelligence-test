### THE CENTRAL STORY: Ostrey Hollow milk-testing station and the Larrow Valley Cooperative Creamery Association

#### Geography / entities
- **Larrow Valley** — the valley; contains **Larrow Green** (site of the main creamery, on Larrow Creek, built 1891) and, seven miles up-valley, **Ostrey Hollow**, at the head of which stood the **skimming station**.
- **Hessel Bottom** — a wet meadow below the Ostrey Hollow hamlet; the best milk in the Hollow came from here. Seven farms had grazing/haying rights on Hessel Bottom (per r02/Effie Loomis) — but Roy Brant (r09) says **six farms**; the largest was **Emmet Brant's** (per r02) — confirmed independently by Roy Brant (r09): the Brant farm was "the largest of the six — sixty-odd acres of the meadow, and thirty-eight cows in a good year." "Six farms" is corroborated by FIVE independent sources (r09, r15/Grigg's 1908 committee report, r16/Kepp, r17/Hazel Brant, plus the underlying 1908 report itself) against r02/Loomis's lone "seven." r19 (Ashlin Chronicle) independently reaffirms "six farms."
- **Ashlin** — the county seat/town where hearings, the Association's office (later), the historical society, and legal business took place. Per r17: the Brant weigh book was held "down the street at the historical rooms in this town" of Ashlin. Per r18/Duncan Keddie: the Brant weigh book went to the "Ordell County Historical Rooms" in Ashlin in 1963 (given by Hazel Brant); the station test book also went there in 1959. r19 confirms "Ordell County" again. The newspaper is **The Ashlin Chronicle**, a county weekly, front page, dated 3 October 1958.
- **Farland** — site of a college with a library (where Ivy Keddie later worked/taught/studied, and where Merle Strawn's notebooks were held). The State Agricultural College at Farland published Bulletin No. 214 (1911, feeding trial on bog hay) and Bulletin No. 471 (1958, final exoneration), co-authored by Professor Leland Vaught and Miss Ivy Keddie.
- **Redlow Ridge**, **Vessey County** — location of the **Alder Corners Cheese Company** (a rival/comparison creamery, used for cross-checking milk tests). Per r10 (Rosalie Cudd): the ridge is a 4-mile switchback road, cut for a lumber road, shuts in November and does not open before April (some years May).
- **Tarnet** — home city of **Ammon & Sons**, "a dairy supply house," per r17/Hazel Brant the place the pipette crate "came out from... in ninety-nine" (1899). Confirmed by r19 as Ammon & Sons' home city, from whom the Association's testing outfit was bought new in 1897 for $77.39 (six dozen pipettes, test bottles, acid measure, hand tester, freight), going into use spring 1897. **NEW Segment 16 (r20):** Junia Ammon, the firm's own historian, writing "Sixty Years of Ammon & Sons" (1949, ch. 9), confirms Tarnet as the firm's home city on the street behind the railroad yard — a four-story building plus basement. She states the house sold nothing into Ordell County before 1900 (Ordell lay behind the ridge and traded east out of Farland; founder Marcus Ammon complained about the freight rate). She reproduces the actual 1897 invoice and the 1923 reply letter verbatim (see Documents Quoted below). This does NOT resolve the Tarnet/1899 vs. established 1897 conflict (Hazel's "1899" claim remains unaddressed by any source), though it strongly reaffirms the 1897 Ammon & Sons transaction independently, with primary-document-level detail.
- **Larrow Valley Cooperative Creamery Association** — organized 1889 by valley farmers at Larrow Green. By-laws drafted by **Gideon Vose**, elected first president in 1889, served until 1911 (22 years). Gideon Vose died in 1917.
- **Article VII** of the by-laws — quoted in full by Effie Loomis (r02), curator Halbert Nym (r07), and Dell Rundle (r12): *"Every patron shall be paid for the milk he delivers according to the number of pounds of butter fat contained in it, ascertained by test, and not according to the weight of the milk; and the test of the station where he delivers shall govern."* Adoption date: three-way conflict — 1891 (Rundle)/1892 (Loomis, curator Nym)/1893 (Grigg) — unresolved through segment 16.

#### People and relationships (the core family/social web)
- **Ansel Keddie** — tester at Ostrey Hollow. Went up 1895 to prepare station, opened/began testing 4 May 1896. Tested 29 seasons, 1896–1924. Dismissed 30 April 1925. Died 1934. Born 1861 (per Duncan Keddie/r18). Described by son Duncan as "a careful man in small things to a degree that other people found trying." In summer 1911 privately said "it was something in the glass" (witnessed by 17-year-old son Duncan) but never wrote the suspicion down — wrote a question mark instead; kept testing another 14 years, recording the very figures that convicted him. Per r13/Frick: privately believed the PATRONS were at fault (water theory) but wouldn't say so publicly out of loyalty to neighbors of 30 years. **NEW Segment 16 (r21, Cleve Oram):** vivid new physical/biographical detail — "a long dry man, very quiet," wore a canvas apron with the strap over one shoulder, had "a way of holding a bottle up to the window with his arm straight out." Cleve "was not frightened of him and most of the boys were." After 1925 dismissal, Keddie kept 4–5 cows on land too small for them and did carpentry — "good carpentry, he hung a barn door for us and it swung true for thirty years." Died "in the thirties"; funeral "well attended and nobody said much."
