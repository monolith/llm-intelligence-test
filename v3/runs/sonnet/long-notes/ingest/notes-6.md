### THE CENTRAL STORY: Ostrey Hollow milk-testing station and the Larrow Valley Cooperative Creamery Association

#### Geography / entities
- **Larrow Valley** — the valley; contains **Larrow Green** (site of the main creamery, on Larrow Creek, built 1891) and, seven miles up-valley, **Ostrey Hollow**, at the head of which stood the **skimming station**.
- **Hessel Bottom** — a wet meadow below the Ostrey Hollow hamlet; the best milk in the Hollow came from here. Seven farms had grazing/haying rights on Hessel Bottom (per r02/Effie Loomis) — but Roy Brant (r09) says **six farms**; the largest was **Emmet Brant's** (per r02) — confirmed independently by Roy Brant (r09): the Brant farm was "the largest of the six — sixty-odd acres of the meadow, and thirty-eight cows in a good year."
- **Ashlin** — the county seat/town where hearings, the Association's office (later), the historical society, and legal business took place.
- **Farland** — site of a college with a library (where Ivy Keddie later worked/taught/studied, and where Merle Strawn's notebooks were held).
- **Redlow Ridge**, **Vessey County** — location of the **Alder Corners Cheese Company** (a rival/comparison creamery, used for cross-checking milk tests). Per r10 (Rosalie Cudd): the ridge is a 4-mile switchback road, cut for a lumber road, shuts in November and does not open before April (some years May); "no arguing with it."
- **Larrow Valley Cooperative Creamery Association** — organized 1889 by valley farmers at Larrow Green. By-laws drafted by **Gideon Vose**, who was elected first president in 1889 and served until 1911 (22 years — longest of any president). Gideon Vose died in 1917.
- **Article VII** of the by-laws (adopted 1892, the year after the creamery opened), quoted in full by Effie Loomis (r02) and again by curator Halbert Nym (r07, Item 1): *"Every patron shall be paid for the milk he delivers according to the number of pounds of butter fat contained in it, ascertained by test, and not according to the weight of the milk; and the test of the station where he delivers shall govern."* Key point emphasized by Loomis: "the test of the station where he delivers shall govern" — no appeal from the station's own test. This clause is called "the hinge of the whole Ostrey Hollow business." Per r07, the printed by-laws pamphlet itself carries no year on its face, only "adopted at the annual meeting" — the 1892 date comes from external sources.

Glass borrowed of Larrow Green until our own comes. A. Keddie."*). Tested 29 seasons, 1896–1924 (did not test in 1925). Dismissed ("put out") on **30 April 1925**. Died in **1934**.
  - Internal inconsistency in Keddie's own letter (r03): body text says the glass was "borrowed off the Ashlin creamery," but the book's own first-leaf entry says "Glass borrowed of Larrow Green." r07's Item 2 provenance note gives a THIRD independent confirmation that Larrow Green (not Ashlin) was the lender, calling this "Ashlin creamery" claim increasingly an outlier.

#### The central mystery/timeline of events
1. **1889** — Association organized at Larrow Green; Gideon Vose first president.
2. **1891** — Main creamery built at Larrow Green.
3. **1892** — Article VII adopted (pay by butterfat test, station's test governs, no appeal).
4. **1895** — Ansel Keddie goes up to Ostrey Hollow to prepare the station.
5. **1896** (4 May) — Station opens; Keddie begins testing, using glass borrowed "of Larrow Green" (book's first leaf) — or "off the Ashlin creamery" (Keddie's own letter body text) [internal conflict]. Recorded average test that year: 3.80.
6. **1897** — Station's own outfit arrives; used from then on. Per r07 Item 2 (Ammon & Sons invoice, dated **14 April 1897**), full itemized breakdown of the $77.93 total:
   > *"Sold to the Larrow Valley Co-op. Creamery Assn., for the Ostrey Hollow station: 6 doz. milk test pipettes, 17.6 c.c., at .35 — 25.20; 12 doz. test bottles, 8 per cent neck, at .22 — 31.68; 1 acid measure — .90; 1 hand tester, 4 bottle — 18.00; Freight — 2.15; Total — 77.93"*
   In use at the station from **1 May 1897**, "Larrow Green having lent until then" (per r07 curatorial note — a THIRD independent confirmation Larrow Green was the 1896 lender). **SEGMENT 6 MAJOR CONFLICT (r11/Vaught):** Vaught's own bulletin introduction states the station's own glass "went into use on **the first of May, 1898**" — one year later than every other source (invoice date 1897, r07 provenance "1 May 1897," and even Vaught's OWN quoted bulletin conclusion in the same document, Section VII, which says "From the first of May, 1897"). This is an internal self-contradiction within r11 alone. Average this year: "part year" (incomplete data, per Keddie's 1922 summary leaf). Per r06 (Strawn), the state's burette-checking program did not exist until 1921, so no independent check of this glass's accuracy took place at delivery. **SEGMENT 6 (r11):** also states **"five dozen" pipettes were purchased** — directly conflicting with the invoice (r07 Item 2) and every other source's "six dozen" (72) figure; five dozen = 60.
7. **1898** — Recorded average settles at 3.61 (and stays "within three hundredths of it" almost every year after, except the 1911 anomaly).
8. **1902** — Complaints first arise at Larrow Green that Ostrey Hollow milk doesn't test as well as the rest of the valley's. **SEGMENT 6 (r09 postscript) independently confirms this date**: "The first men to say so out loud said it in 1902, and they were laughed at, and one of them was my father [Emmet Brant]."
9. **1907** — Per r06, Merle Strawn takes over the newly-formed Ordell dairy-inspection circuit this year (fifty-odd plants, four counties) and makes his first round; visits Ostrey Hollow, finds scales true, cold room fair, and praises the tester's (Keddie's) book-keeping hand and slow, careful testing method.
10. **1908** — A committee visits, samples herds through the winter, and reports that the wet hay of Hessel Bottom thins the cream, blaming the land itself (later shown to be false). **SEGMENT 6 (r09 postscript) NAMES the committee's chairman for the first time: "Nyle Grigg of Larrow Green" headed the 1908 committee.** Per r07 Item 11, the committee's fair copy report is now quoted in full: *"We have sampled the herds of Hessel Bottom through January, February and March. The milk is whole and the scales are honest. It is our opinion that the wet hay of that bottom is what thins the cream, and that the remedy lies with the land and not with the station."* — sampling was winter months only (Jan–Mar 1908), which per Bulletin 214's later finding (wet-hay effect vanishes on summer pasture) means the committee could never have detected the year-round nature of the true gap.
11. **1909** — Per r06, Strawn visits Ostrey Hollow in fall: scales true, boiler in order (safety tried), milk sweet, cans clean; one man cautioned about a rusted seam.
12. **8 May 1911** — The station's own measuring device (pipette/measure) breaks. A substitute/borrowed measure is used.
    - Per r03 (Keddie) and r04 (Ivy Keddie): borrowed measure came from Larrow Green, arriving with the cream wagon, used 8 May–10 July 1911 (about nine weeks). Recorded average jumped to 3.80, reverted to 3.61 the day the station's own measure returned.
    - Per r05 (Teague): break dated to "spring of 1911," borrowed measure used for "six weeks" — a shorter duration than the ~9 weeks given in r03/r04. **Numerical conflict between sources.**
    - Keddie's own book entries, quoted in r03: *"May 8. Pipette broke this morning. Using a borrowed measure."* and *"July 10. Our own measure in use again from today. Tests are back where they were. ? — A.K."*
    - Per r06, Strawn's own inspection visit fell in June 1911 — inside the anomaly window per r03/r04 dating — yet his log entry reads only: *"Ostrey Hollow, June, on my way over to the other side of the county. Scales true. Watched a test through. Nothing here to note."*
    - **SEGMENT 6 (r09 postscript) independently corroborates the 9-week figure from the Brant family's own side**: "There were nine weeks in the summer of 1911 when the station's own measure was out of use, it having been broken. The state man left his own measure that June and that is what they used. Through those nine weeks our tests came up, all of us, every farm on the Bottom; and then the station's own came back into use and the figures went down again... My father noticed the rise and he noticed the fall and he entered both, and he could make nothing of it." (Note: this account says the SUBSTITUTE measure was "the state man['s]" own measure — i.e., attributes the loaner to Strawn/the state inspector, not explicitly to "Larrow Green" as r03/r04 state — a nuance/possible additional wrinkle on the borrowed-measure's source, though not necessarily contradictory since Strawn could have personally carried a measure that happened to come via Larrow Green, or this could be a distinct, minor discrepancy.)
    - **SEGMENT 6 (r11/Vaught) reiterates the episode**: "For nine weeks in the summer of 1911 the station's own pipette was broken and a borrowed measure was used in its place... The station's average test rose from 3.61 to 3.80 and held there for the nine weeks, and returned to 3.61 on the day the station's own measure came back into service." Vaught calls this "worth more than the arithmetic" — an uncontrolled natural experiment that "ran itself... and lay unread for forty-seven years" (1911 to 1958).
13. **1912** — Per r06, Strawn visits: scales true, lactometer shows no added water, notes cream now shipped down the valley the same day it is separated.
14. **1913** — Selby Vose becomes Association manager (serves until 1938). Per r06, Strawn's 1913 visit is to Larrow Green creamery for two days; notes the new manager (Selby Vose) came out to the platform with him, unlike his predecessor. **SEGMENT 6 (r10): this is also the year Jerome Cudd BUILT the Alder Corners factory** (on the flat below the church, spring water on the lot).
15. **1914** — **SEGMENT 5/6 cross-reference:** per r09, the cheese factory over Redlow Ridge "had opened" this year (one year after r10's stated 1913 construction date — likely construction vs. opening, not a contradiction).
16. **1915** — Per r06, Strawn visits Ostrey Hollow: wash room floor holding water at the drain, ordered graded. **SEGMENT 6 (r09 and r10 cross-confirm): from 1915, four Hessel Bottom farmers began hauling milk over Redlow Ridge to the Alder Corners cheese factory each summer flush (about 1 June to mid-September), returning to Ostrey Hollow the rest of the year — for 16 seasons, the last being 1930** (per r10, precise). r09 gives the same "sixteen seasons" figure but does not give the exact end year in the portion read; r10 supplies "The last of those summers was 1930." Reasons per r10: cheese paid better than butter in flush season, factory could use all the milk offered, and the ridge route was a shorter pull for those four farms in season. Roy Brant (r09) states his own family (the largest herd) did NOT go over, calling it "a poor reason and cost us money" in hindsight, since "the biggest herd is the one that cannot afford to be talked about."
17. **1916** — Per r06, Strawn's Ostrey Hollow visit notes: scales true, no added water; "The tester complains that his patrons blame him for their figures. I told him that is the trade and he said he knew it." Same year Teague privately raised the shortfall issue with manager Selby Vose and was told to let it alone.
18. **1917** — Per r06, Strawn visits a cheese factory in the next county, then Larrow Green; "a bad year for help," plants short-handed, testers doing the work of three men.
19. **1919** — A printed circular, over manager Selby Vose's signature, sent to nine families (the Ostrey Hollow patrons) stating "water in the cans." Per r01 (Vira Toland): her father kept it in a desk drawer until he died; other families kept theirs for decades. Per r03 (Keddie): confirms "nine families," Keddie bears no grudge against signers.
    - **r06 (Strawn):** independently and contemporaneously rebuts the circular's premise: *"...I have tested for water at this station on every visit I have made to it and I have never found any, and I told him so."*
    - **r08 (Selby Vose, 1926 address):** DEFENDS the circular as measured, temperate, naming nobody, and something he'd "write... again tomorrow" — at odds with both Strawn's rebuttal and r01's account of devastating fallout.
    - **SEGMENT 6 (r09 postscript) — first-person corroboration of the personal cost, from the OTHER side of the rift**: "In 1919 the manager sent a paper round to the Hollow patrons warning us against putting water in the milk. Nine families at the head of the valley got that paper and nine families read it for what it was. My father [Emmet Brant] was one who never forgave it. He and Ansel Keddie were brothers-in-law, and from 1919 they did not speak." Crucially, Roy Brant clarifies the reason was NOT anger at the accusation itself but that "my father held that the man who kept the test book ought to have stood up in front of that circular and did not" — i.e., Emmet blamed Ansel Keddie for not publicly defending the patrons, a nuance not present in r01's framing.
    - **SEGMENT 6 (r10) — Jerome Cudd's own 1919 test book entry, quoted verbatim, cross-checking the SAME four Hessel Bottom farmers in the SAME year the circular went out**: *"The four Hessel Bottom men, summer of 1919: 3.79, 3.81, 3.78, 3.82. Good milk off wet ground, whatever they say over the ridge."* Average of these four = 3.80 — matching the "true" test value established elsewhere, and giving a THIRD independent, contemporaneous refutation (alongside Strawn's lactometer testimony and, later, Bulletin 471) that nothing was wrong with the milk in exactly the year the accusation was made.
20. **1920** — Per r06, Strawn visits Ostrey Hollow twice (spring and fall): scales true both times, no added water; cold room short of ice in fall, flagged and fixed.
21. **1921** — Per r06: the state begins issuing graduated burettes to field men. Strawn's own burette arrived that spring.
22. **1922** — Ansel Keddie writes a summary leaf of averages (quoted identically in r03, r04, r07/Item 3): *"Average test of all patrons, this station: 1896, 3.80. 1897, part year. 1898, 3.61. And 3.61 or within three hundredths of it every year since, save the nine weeks of 1911 that are noted in their place, when it stood at 3.80."* Per r06, Strawn's 1922 visit notes he did NOT run the burette check this year (logistics) — a near-miss, one year before the fault was caught.
23. **June 1923** — A crate of milk-test pipettes/measures at Ostrey Hollow is condemned; new ones ordered. Per r02 (Effie Loomis), never mentioned at the 1925 hearing at all.
    - **r06 (Strawn) — precise date, "1923, June 12":** *"Ostrey Hollow. Scales true. Lactometer, no added water, as always here. Ran their milk pipettes against the burette. These measures will not deliver their mark. Condemned the lot, the whole crate of them, and told the manager to order new. Glass is glass and I did not weigh what it has cost them."* Matches Bulletin 471's "twelfth of June, 1923" exactly.
    - **r07, Item 9:** photostat of this same 1923 June 12 log entry, independently reproducing the exact text — second physical-document confirmation.
    - **r08 (Selby Vose, 1926) CONFLICT:** gives the condemnation date as **"the twelfth of July, 1923"** — a one-month discrepancy against Strawn's log, the r07 photostat, and Bulletin 471, all of which agree on June 12.
    - Per r06, "1923, October": Strawn returns; new glass in use since July; runs new pipettes against burette, confirms "They deliver their mark."
    - **r07, Item 10:** NEW DOCUMENT — letter, Ammon & Sons to the Association, dated **2 July 1923**, acknowledges the Association's order of **28 June 1923** for replacement pipettes/bottles, promises shipment that month, "declining to say anything of the earlier lot."
24. **January 1925** — Patrons of Ostrey Hollow formally petition the Association.
25. **17 March 1925** — Ansel Keddie gives a nine-sentence statement to the board of arbitration at Ashlin (quoted in full in r03; catalogued but NOT transcribed in r07/Item 8). Key line: *"I have never taken a pound of cream out of that station and there is not a man in this room who thinks I have when he is by himself."*
26. **17 March – 4 May 1925** — Board of arbitration sits at Ashlin, chaired by Alonzo Frick. Per r02: "Three explanations were argued before it. None of the three was accepted." Per r05 (Teague), testified the staffing arrangement created opportunity for theft but did NOT claim theft occurred; "the board found nothing established against anybody." **r08 (Vose, 1926) confirms same dates, chair, and "no cause established" finding**, but frames the episode very differently — asserting deliberate watering as the true cause.
27. **30 April 1925** — Ansel Keddie dismissed ("put out") despite "no cause established." His own letter (r03) juxtaposes these facts without resolving the contradiction.
    - **r06 (Strawn):** Strawn's 1924 entry (year BEFORE dismissal) already records corrected figures: "Their book gives the year at 3.80" — undercutting any theory Keddie personally caused/benefited from the low readings.
    - **r08 (Vose) CONFLICT:** states the 1924 average "stood at 3.90" after a rise of "twenty-one hundredths" — conflicts with r06's 3.80 by 0.10, and is arguably internally inconsistent with Vose's own numbers (0.21 + 3.61 baseline = 3.82, not 3.90).
28. **The award (1925)**: Per r02 and r05: board awarded Ostrey Hollow patrons **$3,800**, "without admission," charged to the station account. Full text quoted in r02, catalogued again in r07/Item 7. **r01 conflicts**: gives award as **$3,500**, split nine ways, framed as a guilt settlement. **r08 (Vose) independently corroborates $3,800**, further weakening r01's figure.
29. **1925, fall** — Per r06, Strawn's post-dismissal visit notes: "New man at the tester's bench this year. He is quick, and quick is not the thing I look for." Only oblique reference to Keddie's replacement; never names him or the hearing.
30. **1926** — Per r06: burette-checking program discontinued statewide. **Also the year of Selby Vose's own annual address (r08)** — his 13th as manager ("thirteen years this spring, since 1913"). In it he asserts deliberate watering was the true cause, defends the 1919 circular, dismisses the equipment explanation as "a piece of housekeeping," and gives the (conflicting) July 12, 1923 condemnation date and 3.90 1924-average figure.
31. **1928** — Per r06, Strawn's visit: "The station is quieter than it was." Notes the loft over the intake room was "full of odds and ends... since before the war" (pre-WWI) — the crate was added to a pre-existing accumulation of junk, not the loft's sole contents.
32. **1930** — **SEGMENT 6 (r10):** the last summer the four Hessel Bottom farmers hauled to Alder Corners (16th and final season of that arrangement, begun 1915).
33. **1931** — Per r06: Strawn visits Larrow Green and Ostrey Hollow same week; bad ice year across all four counties.
34. **1934** — Ansel Keddie dies, name still under suspicion. Per r06, same-year Strawn visit notes a separator bearing ran hot (two-day shutdown, repaired); "The tester's book is kept up, though not in the hand it used to be kept in" — oblique, unnamed acknowledgment Keddie is gone.
35. **1936, March** — Per r06: Strawn's LAST round on the circuit — closes out his 29-year career (1907–1936).
36. **1938** — Selby Vose's term as manager ends. Also the year the Association's office moved and the 1889–1892 minute book was lost (never recovered).
37. **1940** — Effie Loomis retires as secretary (served 1901–1940).
38. **1941** — Effie Loomis writes "Notes toward a history of the Association" (r02). **SEGMENT 6 (r10): also the year Jerome Cudd died** ("in March, between the setting and the cutting of a vat") and Rosalie Cudd took over the Alder Corners factory — matching r07 Item 13's "founder to his daughter in 1941" (see MAJOR CONFLICT RESOLUTION below).

41. **1955 (winter)** — Roy Brant, aged 67, writes his own account (r09) for the county farm bureau's local-history collection, with a promised postscript "added three years afterward" (~1958) — **NOW READ IN FULL as of Segment 6, see Part B.**

That is 95 hundredths of the mark..."* **SEGMENT 6 MAJOR CONFLICT (r11/Vaught): gives the mean delivery as "16.27 c.c." instead of 16.72 c.c.** — see Part B for full discussion of the cascading numeric consequences.

- **Vaught's technical explanation (r11, Section II)**, new descriptive detail on the test method itself: milk is drawn into a pipette and delivered into a test bottle; sulfuric acid dissolves everything but the fat; the bottle is spun in a hand tester; fat rises into the graduated neck; operator reads fat percentage directly off the neck. The neck's graduation assumes a FIXED, KNOWN charge of milk (17.6 c.c., the "standard charge in this state"). If the pipette delivers less than its mark, the reading is low "in the exact proportion by which the charge was short." Three consequences Vaught emphasizes: (1) the error is **proportional, not additive** — removes a fixed fraction, not fixed points, so richer milk loses more absolutely, but herds still rank correctly against each other (only comparison to the outside world is wrong); (2) the error is **precise/consistent** — same short pipette delivers the identical wrong amount every time, for decades, "in January and in July, for a careful operator and a careless one," giving "the appearance of excellent work"; (3) the error is **invisible from inside the plant** — every other element of the test (scales, tester speed, acid strength, bottle graduation) can be checked against an external standard, but "the pipette is the standard" itself, so it can only be caught by an external burette, which reached field men very late (1921, per r06).

### Wilbur Teague's "station-gain" ledger line (r05) — the bookkeeping trace of the same phenomenon
- Teague invented this bookkeeping device himself in 1905: one column entered butterfat credited to patrons on the station's OWN test; the other entered butterfat actually received/tested at Larrow Green from the station's cream. The second figure was ALWAYS larger; the difference was "station gain," credited to the Association's general account.
- Quoted sample entry, for 1898 (independently reproduced verbatim by curator Halbert Nym, r07 Item 6, as "typical" of the ledger form from 1897 on): *"Ostrey Hollow: butter fat credited to patrons on station test, 57,760 lb. Butter fat received at Larrow Green in station cream, 60,800 lb. Station gain, 3,040 lb, five and a quarter per cent, as usual. Carried to the general account."*
- This 5.25% gain recurred so consistently that "as usual" became part of the standard entry — matching Ivy Keddie's independently-calculated 5.25% (76,000/1,444,000).
- Teague's testimony to the 1925 board argued staffing created opportunity for theft, but explicitly did NOT claim to know what, if anything, happened; "the board found nothing established against anybody."
- Teague states Selby Vose signed the annual station sheet every year and that Teague raised concerns privately in 1916 but was told to let it alone.
