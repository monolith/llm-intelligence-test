### THE CENTRAL STORY: Ostrey Hollow milk-testing station and the Larrow Valley Cooperative Creamery Association

#### Geography / entities
- **Larrow Valley** — the valley; contains **Larrow Green** (site of the main creamery, on Larrow Creek, built 1891) and, seven miles up-valley, **Ostrey Hollow**, at the head of which stood the **skimming station**.
- **Hessel Bottom** — a wet meadow below the Ostrey Hollow hamlet; the best milk in the Hollow came from here. Seven farms had grazing/haying rights on Hessel Bottom (per r02/Effie Loomis) — but Roy Brant (r09) says **six farms**; the largest was **Emmet Brant's** (per r02) — confirmed independently by Roy Brant (r09): the Brant farm was "the largest of the six — sixty-odd acres of the meadow, and thirty-eight cows in a good year." "Six farms" is corroborated by FIVE independent sources (r09, r15/Grigg's 1908 committee report, r16/Kepp, r17/Hazel Brant, plus the underlying 1908 report itself) against r02/Loomis's lone "seven." r19 (Ashlin Chronicle) independently reaffirms "six farms."
- **Ashlin** — the county seat/town where hearings, the Association's office (later), the historical society, and legal business took place. Per r17: the Brant weigh book was held "down the street at the historical rooms in this town" of Ashlin. Per r18/Duncan Keddie: the Brant weigh book went to the "Ordell County Historical Rooms" in Ashlin in 1963 (given by Hazel Brant); the station test book also went there in 1959. r19 confirms "Ordell County" again. The newspaper is **The Ashlin Chronicle**, a county weekly, front page, dated 3 October 1958.
- **Farland** — site of a college with a library (where Ivy Keddie later worked/taught/studied, and where Merle Strawn's notebooks were held). The State Agricultural College at Farland published Bulletin No. 214 (1911, feeding trial on bog hay) and Bulletin No. 471 (1958, final exoneration), co-authored by Professor Leland Vaught and Miss Ivy Keddie.
- **Redlow Ridge**, **Vessey County** — location of the **Alder Corners Cheese Company** (a rival/comparison creamery, used for cross-checking milk tests). Per r10 (Rosalie Cudd): the ridge is a 4-mile switchback road, cut for a lumber road, shuts in November and does not open before April (some years May).
- **Tarnet** — home city of **Ammon & Sons**, "a dairy supply house," per r17/Hazel Brant the place the pipette crate "came out from... in ninety-nine" (1899). Confirmed by r19 as Ammon & Sons' home city, from whom the Association's testing outfit was bought new in 1897 for $77.39 (six dozen pipettes, test bottles, acid measure, hand tester, freight), going into use spring 1897. Junia Ammon, the firm's own historian, writing "Sixty Years of Ammon & Sons" (1949, ch. 9), confirms Tarnet as the firm's home city on the street behind the railroad yard — a four-story building plus basement. She states the house sold nothing into Ordell County before 1900 (Ordell lay behind the ridge and traded east out of Farland; founder Marcus Ammon complained about the freight rate). She reproduces the actual 1897 invoice and the 1923 reply letter verbatim (see Documents Quoted below). This does NOT resolve the Tarnet/1899 vs. established 1897 conflict (Hazel's "1899" claim remains unaddressed by any source), though it strongly reaffirms the 1897 Ammon & Sons transaction independently, with primary-document-level detail.
- **Larrow Valley Cooperative Creamery Association** — organized 1889 by valley farmers at Larrow Green. By-laws drafted by **Gideon Vose**, elected first president in 1889, served until 1911 (22 years). Gideon Vose died in 1917.
- **Article VII** of the by-laws — quoted in full by Effie Loomis (r02), curator Halbert Nym (r07), and Dell Rundle (r12): *"Every patron shall be paid for the milk he delivers according to the number of pounds of butter fat contained in it, ascertained by test, and not according to the weight of the milk; and the test of the station where he delivers shall govern."* Adoption date: three-way conflict — 1891 (Rundle)/1892 (Loomis, curator Nym)/1893 (Grigg) — unresolved. r22 (Orin Falke, 1959 county report): states the printed by-laws "carry no date upon their face, only the words 'adopted at the annual meeting,'" and that "the minute book of the Association's first years was lost in an office removal," so Falke's office "is unable to state the year of adoption and does not attempt to." This is a careful non-answer from an official investigator, consistent with (and explaining) the persistent three-way date conflict, but does not resolve it.

Dismissed 30 April 1925. Died 1934 (per r24/Orra's diary: died "this morning, at home, in the forenoon, without much pain," February 1934). Born 1861 (per Duncan Keddie/r18; consistent with r24/Orra's diary, which implies Ansel was 66 in October 1927 — "A carpenter at sixty-six" — matching 1861 birth). After 1925 dismissal, Keddie kept 4–5 cows on land too small for them and did carpentry — "good carpentry, he hung a barn door for us and it swung true for thirty years." #### The central mystery/timeline of events
(Numbered items 1–84 carried forward verbatim in substance from segments 6/7/10/11/12/15/16; corroborating/conflicting detail from segment 17 noted inline below via r22/r23/r24; no new numbered timeline items required in segments 18 or 19 — neither introduced new story retellings.)
1. **1889** — Association organized at Larrow Green; Gideon Vose first president.
2. **1891** — Main creamery built at Larrow Green; churn set up.
3. **1892/1891/1893** — Article VII adopted — three-way conflict, unresolved. Per r22/Falke: by-laws carry no adoption date on their face; minute book of the first years lost in an office removal — explains, but does not resolve, the conflict.
4. **1895** — Ansel Keddie goes up to Ostrey Hollow to prepare the station.
5. **1896 (4 May)** — Station opens. Per r22/Falke: confirms station "built in the winter of 1895 and 1896," opened "on the 4th of May, 1896" — independent corroboration.
6. **1897** — Station's own outfit arrives (Ammon & Sons invoice dated 14 April 1897 per r20; in use from 1 May 1897 per r20's own prose). MAJOR (r24/Orra's diary, entry dated May 1, 1897): "A. says the station's own glass came up and they are done borrowing of Larrow Green. He is pleased about it." This is a DIRECT, DATED, PRIMARY-SOURCE (contemporaneous diary) confirmation of the exact 1 May 1897 date — the single strongest possible corroboration in the corpus, since it was written on or near the actual day, not decades later. CONFLICT (r23/Bertram Kyle): Kyle's clerk memoir instead states the station's own outfit "went into use on the first of May, 1898" — ONE YEAR LATER than the now very strongly corroborated 1897 date (primary invoice + Orra's contemporaneous diary + established consensus). Kyle claims this "from the correspondence, which I handled later," but this is now a clear minority/erroneous claim against overwhelming evidence for 1897. POSSIBLE CONFLICT (r17/Hazel Brant): "The crate came out from Tarnet in ninety-nine [1899]" — remains unresolved and unaddressed by any source.
   - Original outfit pipette count: "six dozen" (established, r19 + r20 invoice) vs. "five dozen" (r21/Oram) — unresolved, carried forward.
7. **1898** — Recorded average settles at 3.61. Per r22/Falke: gives the precise long-run figures: Ostrey Hollow station averaged **3.61**, Larrow Green creamery averaged **3.78** over the same years (an "ordinary figure for this county"); deficiency fixed by the 1958 bulletin at **nineteen hundredths of a point** (0.19), constant in every month of the year, "in the flush of June and in the depth of February alike."
8. **1902** — Complaints first arise. Per r24/Orra's diary, March 1902: "Talk at the store that our milk up here runs poorer than theirs down the valley. A. came home and would not eat," told Orra "it is my figures they will look at." Confirms 1902 as the first-complaint year from a first-person contemporaneous source.
9. **1907** — Merle Strawn takes over the Ordell dairy-inspection circuit.
10. **1908** — Committee visits under Nyle Grigg of Larrow Green, blames wet hay of Hessel Bottom. Per r24/Orra's diary, January and March 1908: "Four men up the road today in Grigg's sleigh"; went to Emmet's farm first as the biggest; "wanted to see the barns and not the station"; committee's word in March was "that it is the wet hay of the bottom that thins the cream." Emmet Brant's response: "the bottom fed his father and it will feed him." Ansel "says nothing at all, which is his way, and is not always kindness to me" (Orra's own aside).
11. **1909** — Strawn visits, all in order.
12. **8 May–10 July 1911** (nine weeks) — station's own measure breaks; substitute used; average rises to 3.80, reverts to 3.61. Same episode as Ansel's private "something in the glass" suspicion. Per r24/Orra's diary, May 8, 1911: "A. home cross about a broken measure" — independent, dated, contemporaneous corroboration of the exact 8 May 1911 date. Per r22/Falke: gives the precise dates "8th of May" to "10th of July," matching established sources exactly, and states the tester "noted the fact in his book and set a question mark against it" — confirming the question-mark detail independently.
13. **1912–1918** — Strawn's routine visits; Selby Vose becomes manager 1913 (established) vs. CONFLICT (r23/Kyle): 1911 (see People section above); Jerome Cudd builds Alder Corners factory 1913, opens 1914; from 1915, four Hessel Bottom farmers begin hauling summer milk to Alder Corners for 16 seasons (1915–1930).
14. **1919** — Printed circular over Selby Vose's signature sent to nine families ("water in the cans") — CONFLICT (r24/Orra's diary, June 1919): states the circular was carried "to eleven families" by Ansel himself — an "eleven" vs. established "nine" families conflict. ### THE THREE (NOW FOUR/FIVE-DOCUMENTED) EXPLANATIONS BEFORE THE 1925 BOARD (r13 — unchanged, joined by additional documented private believers)
1. **Water theory** — Selby Vose. Per r22/Falke: explicitly examines and REJECTS this theory using Strawn's lactometer testing record ("never once found added water at that station" from 1908 forward).
2. **Cream-theft theory** — Wilbur Teague, obliquely; also privately believed by Cleve Oram's (unnamed) father (r21). Per r22/Falke: explicitly examines and REJECTS this theory using the 1911 nine-week broken-measure episode as disproof ("the same man made the tests in that interval, under no different supervision, and the figures rose").
3. **Wet-hay theory** — via the 1908 committee report. MAJOR (r22/Falke): Falke's initial report (paragraph 9) treats this theory as still partially credible — "This office is not prepared to dismiss it... the wet hay of that bottom must be allowed as a real if partial factor, and the 1908 committee was not simply wrong." However, Falke's OWN APPENDED POSTSCRIPT, written after obtaining the actual figures from Bulletin No. 214 (the 1911 feeding trial he had cited without having read), EXPLICITLY RETRACTS this concession: the 1911 trial found bog hay "depressed the butter fat test by not more than five hundredths of one per cent [0.05%] in winter, and by nothing that could be measured on summer pasture" — and Falke concludes, "Five hundredths in winter and nothing in summer will not carry nineteen hundredths in July. I withdraw the concession made on page four." This is the corpus's clearest, most quantitatively explicit debunking of the wet-hay theory — a rare case of a source correcting itself with hard data mid-document.
Frick: "Three were put to us; each of them was urged by a man of standing; each of them would have been believed by half the valley; and not one of them was proved." The eventual TRUE explanation — undersized pipettes — was not discovered until 1958 and was proposed by nobody at the 1925 hearing. r19 restates the central irony: "Nobody watered anything. Nobody stole anything. The scales were true, the acid was right, the cows were ordinary, and a piece of glass out of a crate was wrong." Per r22/Falke: independently reaches the identical final conclusion via his own careful paragraph-by-paragraph elimination: "the bulletin's conclusion is that a short charge of milk into a bottle whose neck is ruled for the full charge yields a reading short in the same proportion... and that no milk was watered and no cream was stolen at Ostrey Hollow."

### r18 — "Duncan Keddie, 'Notes for the family,' written at Ashlin, 1968 (with an addition of 1970)."

Found embedded within `test-input/long/r18-long.md`, Chapter 24 (around lines 5684–5737). Subtitle: *"A retired schoolteacher, seventy-four, sets down for his daughter and her children who everyone was and where every book went; the sections are his own."*

Duncan Keddie — Ansel and Orra's son, born 1894, retired schoolteacher — writes this memoir explicitly to correct the record for his daughter Ivy and her children: "my daughter has spent ten years putting the case right in print and nobody has ever put the family right on paper at all, and because when I am gone there will be no one left who was in the house." Method: "I have kept to what I know. Where I do not know a thing I have said so. I have not tried to make anybody in these pages better than he was, and I have not needed to."

Structured in labeled sections:

**I. The people.** Ansel Keddie (father), born 1861, died 1934. Orra Brant (mother), born 1868, married Ansel 1889, "Emmet Brant's sister, the younger of the two." Duncan himself, born 1894. His daughter Ivy, born 1931.

**II. What the connections are, set out so that they can be copied.** Emmet Brant and Orra were full siblings. Emmet's son Roy Brant kept the weigh book. Roy and Duncan are first cousins. Roy's daughter Hazel and Duncan's daughter Ivy are SECOND COUSINS ("the children of first cousins. Not cousins once removed... and not aunt and niece, which people say because it is shorter"). Selby Vose was Gideon Vose's SON, not nephew. Rosalie Cudd was Jerome Cudd's daughter. Merle Strawn "was kin to nobody in the valley whatever."

**III. A thing that is said and is not so.** Duncan denies any blood connection between his family and the Voses: "There was no connection whatever between us and the Voses. People confused them with the Ashlin Voses, who were no relation to Mr. Gideon either." Reports hearing from "two women in this town" that his mother and Gideon Vose's wife were cousins, calls this "talk... since before I could walk," states his mother "never once put a Vose in it."

**IV. My father.** Dismissal 30 April 1925, "four days before the board handed down what it called its finding, so that the finding found nothing and the dismissal said everything." Lived nine years after, dying 1934 "with the imputation on him." In summer 1911, Ansel came home saying "it was something in the glass" — said twice, "which was not his way" — witnessed by 17-year-old Duncan. Never recorded the suspicion; wrote a question mark instead. "He was a man who would not put down what he could not prove... this was the most expensive virtue anybody in that valley ever had... So he kept on. He kept on for another fourteen years, and wrote down every figure the glass gave him, and the figures were what convicted him." Orra outlived Ansel by 14 years, died 1948 at "eighty-two" (arithmetic implies 79–80 under 1868 birth year — but r24/Orra's own diary implies a 1866 birth year, which makes "eighty-two" exact).

**V. The books, and where they went.** "books go astray and people afterward say that a thing was never written when it was only never found." Full custody chains for FIVE items:
- *Station test book* — kept 1896–1925; impounded March 1925; held at Cooperative office 1925–1959; to Ordell County Historical Rooms 1959.
- *Brant weigh book* — begun 1 Jan 1897 (Duncan's account); kept until Emmet's death 1929; to Roy; lent to Ivy 1958 ("lent, and I want the word"); returned; to Hazel at Roy's 1962 death; to Historical Rooms 1963.
- *Strawn's circuit notebooks* — to state dairy office at 1936 retirement; to Farland college library 1947; Ivy found the 1923 volume there 1958, "eleven years later. Nobody had asked for them in the interval."
- *Alder Corners test book* — kept by Jerome Cudd; to Rosalie 1941; figures copied/sent to Ivy 1958; "the book itself has never been out of Vessey County." 1970: gone to Vessey County Historical Society.
- *The crate* — to the loft 1923; found by Ivy February 1958; to Farland to be measured; back; deposited with Historical Rooms 1959. Closing image: "Twenty feet of ladder, thirty-five years, and two of the three sessions of that arbitration sat in the room underneath it."

**Added, 1970 (postscript):** "Mrs. Cudd's test book has this year gone to the Vessey County Historical Society, and the five books are now in three buildings and are none of them ours."
