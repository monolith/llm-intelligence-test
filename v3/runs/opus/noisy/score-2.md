## Notes on ambiguity

- **A1.5** is the one item where the key's own rules pull two ways: three bolded elements with no
  `REQUIRED:` line. I read it as a compound identity fact (all bold gates), not a list, so the
  missing "Gideon's son" inside Section A loses it. A judge applying the "core fact" clause would
  award it, making the total 82.
- **A2.2** was awarded on the "core fact" clause: Story 1 gives weighed / sampled / tested for fat /
  cream down / skim back, and Story 8 gives the charge-and-graduated-neck mechanism, though
  "composite sample" never appears.
- **A3.5** was awarded on substance: Section A gives Orra as Ansel's wife throughout and Emmet Brant
  as "his brother-in-law", with Emmet the biggest farm on the Bottom.
- **A4.3** survives its gate: the sheet keeps the two gaps apart, deriving Larrow Green's 3.78 from
  the 0.17 Hollow-vs-Green comparison and reserving 0.19 for the shortfall.
- **A1.6** was awarded despite Story 6's "invented in 1905," because the 1898 origin, the shape and
  the general-account destination are all stated there; the inconsistency is charged once, as a
  corruption deduction, not twice.

## Profile

High A with a maxed E and a perfect C: the sheet reconstructed fluently *and* arbitrated by document
rather than by counting. Its losses are almost all one failure mode — facts filed in the pointed
sections and never restated inside the narratives (A4.2, A4.5, A7.6, A8.6, A1.5 are all present and
correct somewhere in B–G). The two real errors of judgment are both over-confidence on abstention
poles that the sheet had the evidence to decline, and a habit of reconciling a planted value into a
new role instead of rejecting it.
