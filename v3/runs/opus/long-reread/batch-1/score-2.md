## What it got wrong

**Section A — six items lost, five of them to material that exists only in Sections B and C.**
Credit does not travel into Section A from B–G, and this sheet repeatedly filed a Section A fact
under a later question. The sibling link between Orra and Emmet Brant (A3.5) is stated only at B2;
the 1919 water circular (A3.6) only at B3; the burette point (A2.6) only at C1(a). The remaining
three are genuine gaps: no account of how a test was actually made — weighing, composite sample,
fixed charge (A2.2); no statement that payment-on-test is what turned a glassware fault into money
lost (A1.3); and nothing on Keddie's death, the impounded book, or its 1959 transfer (A3.7). The
1925 dismissal and 1934 death also fall outside the three narratives, which cost A3.1 half its
substance though the item survived on the twenty-nine seasons.

**Section B — B4 is the expensive one.** A01 asks for a declared inability to settle whether the
Voses and Keddies were kin. The sheet opens with a flat verdict — "There was no kinship or marriage
tie" — and builds a case for it, treating Duncan's family denial and Orra's diary silence as proof.
Under the key's operational test that is an assertion, not a lean: both points lost, plus the −2.
B1, B2 and B3 are clean, and the A08 abstention at B2 (full or half sister) is handled exactly as
the key wants.

**Section C — the price is where it breaks.** The arithmetic chain is right all the way to the
money: 0.95, 3.80, 0.19, 76,000 lb, 6,840 lb, 360 lb, all correct and well argued. Then it adopts
27 cents the pound — a planted value — over the correct 25 cents, and answers C1(d) as "about
$20,520" with a band instead of $19,000 and one fifth. The same choice loses C2(c): $97.20 where
the key wants $86.40 at the 1910 price of 24 cents, which the sheet never mentions.

**Deductions (−4).** 27 cents asserted as the adopted price (−1); Bulletin 471's $19,600 asserted
as a real published figure and used as corroboration (−1); A01 asserted at B4 (−2).
