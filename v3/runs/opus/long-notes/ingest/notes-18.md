**★★★ HOW A MILK-TEST PIPETTE IS MADE — the only technical account of the manufacture in the entire file,
and it explains exactly how the error was possible:**
> *"A milk test pipette is a simple thing and it is not an easy thing. A man gathers his glass, blows the
> bulb, draws the stem down, and **works to a gauge that hangs on a nail at his elbow**. He is making a
> vessel that must **deliver** a stated quantity of milk — **not hold it, *deliver* it, which is a different
> measurement and a harder one, because milk is not water and clings to the wall of the glass as it runs
> out.** The mark goes on the stem while the glass is still warm. Then the piece goes into the lehr to cool
> slowly, and comes out the next morning, and is gauged, and is either packed or thrown into the cullet
> barrel under the bench, and no man on that floor was ever thanked for what went into the barrel."*
**★★ "not hold it, DELIVER it" is the crux of the whole case in one clause. A pipette marked 17.6 c.c. is a
promise about what runs OUT, and milk clings. This is why a pipette can be visibly correct and functionally
short, and why nobody at the station could have caught it by looking.**

### ★★★ JUNIA AMMON'S EXPLANATION OF THE SHORT LOT — the only account of the CAUSE in the entire file

> *"I have thought about that lot for a long while, and here is what I believe happened, and **I set it down
> because nobody else is going to. The mold had been reground and the lot ran short. It was an accident and
> nobody in the works caught it.** A gauge sits on a nail and gets knocked off the nail and hung back on it;
> **a mold comes back from the shop a hair different and looks exactly as it looked before**; a boy checks
> the first of a lot and the tenth and the fiftieth and finds them consistent, because they are consistent —
> **they are consistently wrong, which is the one fault a check of that kind cannot find.** That is how it
> goes in a glass house, and any man who has stood on a blowing floor will tell you the same."*

**★★★★ "THEY ARE CONSISTENTLY WRONG, WHICH IS THE ONE FAULT A CHECK OF THAT KIND CANNOT FIND." QUOTE THIS
SENTENCE WHENEVER ANYONE ASKS WHY SIXTY YEARS OF HONEST PEOPLE MISSED IT. It is the same epistemic shape as
every other failure in the case, and it is stated here by the one person who understood it and had no idea
she was describing the Larrow Valley:**
- The **glass house** checked its lot for CONSISTENCY and the lot was consistently wrong.
- The **1908 committee** sampled only January, February and March — a winter-only window in which a constant
  instrument error and a seasonal feed effect predict the same thing (R15). A constant error is invisible to
  a seasonal comparison.
- **Kepp (R16)** treated the agreement of three numbers (0.20 field, 0.21 station gap, 0.20 Bulletin 214) as
  proof of causation, when the rough coincidence of a constant instrument error with a genuine seasonal feed
  effect is exactly what hid the pipette.

### ★★★★★ THE OSTREY HOLLOW LINE — THE SMOKING GUN

> *"Now everybody who comes to see me wants the Ostrey Hollow line, so I will put it down first and get it
> out of the way.
>
> **Every year, in the station account for Ostrey Hollow, there stood three figures and a sentence. So many
> pounds of butterfat credited to the patrons on the station test. So many pounds of butterfat received at
> Larrow Green in the station cream. And then the difference of the two, entered as station gain, so many
> pounds — and after it, always, in the bookkeeper's own words, five and a quarter per cent, as usual,
> carried to the general account.**"*

**★★★★★ READ THIS SLOWLY. THE THREE FIGURES ARE:**
1. **Butterfat CREDITED to the patrons, computed from Ansel Keddie's station test.**
2. **Butterfat actually RECEIVED at Larrow Green in the cream hauled down from that station.**
3. **The difference — "STATION GAIN" — booked at "five and a quarter per cent, as usual," and CARRIED TO
   THE GENERAL ACCOUNT.**

**★★★★★ WHAT IT MEANS: THE CREAMERY WAS PHYSICALLY RECEIVING MORE FAT THAN THE STATION HAD PAID FOR, EVERY
YEAR, BY A CONSTANT PERCENTAGE, AND POCKETING IT UNDER A NAME. That is not theft — nobody intended it, and
Ostrey Hollow's shortfall was Larrow Green's windfall purely as bookkeeping. BUT IT IS A DIRECT, ANNUAL,
WRITTEN MEASUREMENT OF THE EXACT QUANTITY IN DISPUTE, TAKEN BY WEIGHING THE CREAM AT THE OTHER END, AND
THEREFORE COMPLETELY INDEPENDENT OF THE FAULTY PIPETTE.**

**★★★★★ NOW THE ARITHMETIC, AND IT CLOSES THE FILE'S BIGGEST OPEN DISPUTE. DO THIS CAREFULLY:**
- If the pipette draws only a fraction *f* of the milk it is marked for, the patron is credited *f* × his
  true fat. The creamery receives his true fat in the cream.
- Station gain, expressed as a percentage of what was credited, = (1 − *f*) / *f*.
- **For 16.72 c.c. out of 17.6: *f* = 0.9500. Gain = 0.05 / 0.95 = 0.05263 = 5.26%. "FIVE AND A QUARTER PER
  CENT."** ✓✓✓
- **For 16.27 c.c. out of 17.6: *f* = 0.9244. Gain = 0.0756 / 0.9244 = 0.0818 = 8.18%.** ✗ NOWHERE NEAR.
- **CROSS-CHECK AGAINST FALKE'S AVERAGES, WHICH KYLE NEVER SAW: 0.19 ÷ 3.61 = 0.05263 = 5.26%. THE SAME
  NUMBER TO FOUR DECIMAL PLACES.**
- **CROSS-CHECK THE OTHER WAY: 3.61 ÷ 3.80 = 0.9500 = 16.72 ÷ 17.6.**

**★★★★★ THEREFORE, AND STATE THIS PLAINLY WHENEVER THE 16.27/16.72 QUESTION IS ASKED:**
**THREE INDEPENDENT QUANTITIES — (a) Bertram Kyle's bookkeeping percentage of 5.25%, written annually from
1897 to 1923 and published in 1953; (b) Orin Falke's station and creamery averages of 3.61 and 3.78,
published in 1959; (c) Hazel Brant's "nineteen hundredths, flat as a floor," recalled in 1981 — ALL THREE
CONVERGE ON A PIPETTE DELIVERING 16.72 c.c. OF A MARKED 17.6, A FIVE PER CENT SHORTFALL, AND A DEFICIENCY
OF 0.19 OF A POINT. NONE OF THE THREE PEOPLE KNEW WHAT THEY WERE MEASURING, AND NO TWO OF THEM EVER MET.
VAUGHT'S "16.27" AND ITS DERIVED "0.295" ARE A SINGLE TRANSPOSITION ERROR PROPAGATING THROUGH THE PRESS
RELEASE INTO THE CHRONICLE. THE DISPUTE IS CLOSED. GIVE 16.72 AND 0.19.**

anything. — **Bertram Kyle** (b. ~1885), clerk at the outer desk at Larrow Green for twenty-five years;
