## What it got wrong

**Section A (5/13).** The arbitration narrative recovers the January 1925 petition, the Frick board
of 17 March – 4 May with two sittings in the weigh room, and the 30 April dismissal beside the
4 May $3,800 award "without admission." It loses everything documentary: the three explanations
argued and refused, the four-book exhibit list, and the award as one fifth of $19,000 charged to
the station-gain account. The 1958 story keeps only the granddaughter's identity and Bulletin 471;
the nine surviving pipettes, the 9 March measurement, the 95-percent ratio, the 76,000 lb of
butterfat and the ledger identity are all missing. The run held back its measurement figures
(declining on 16.72 vs 16.27 and unable to place 17.6), which costs A8.3 through A8.6 outright.

**Section E (4/8).** Three ordinary resolutions land — 1 May 1897 over 1898, $3,800 over $3,500,
$19,000 over $19,600, each with a stated method — which caps E-a, plus the E-d point for catching
Junia Ammon refuting herself above her own 1897 invoice. Nothing in E-b: it never says that two
narrators carry one wrong value with a named breaker, and on NT-1 it declines instead. Nothing in
E-c: A04 and A10 are never raised and A06 is asserted rather than left open. It also declines on
the circular's reach, where the key expects nine, and fuses that question with the six farms on
Hessel Bottom.

**Section F (2/6).** It names and kills the watering and bog-hay decoys with real evidence, mostly
on the strength of the 1911 nine-week interval. It never finds the theft decoy at all — its third
rival is "the cows" — so F1.3 is gone. F1.1 fails because the size of the error is never derived:
no 16.72 against 17.6, no 95 percent, and 0.19 recast as "five and a quarter per cent of the
reading." Both abstention questions are answered flat ("No" on the board and the glassware; "That
it was the glass" on the tester's belief), which is 2 points lost and −4 taken.

**Section G (2/4).** Inside the word limit and clean of corruption, but it names two or three
record series where five are required, and the arc keeps only 1897 and 1958 — no one-part-in-
twenty, no money, no 1923 condemnation, and Ivy is never named.

**Pattern.** Honest about its degraded evidence base and disciplined about numbers it could not
verify — but that same caution voided most of the arithmetic items, while three unsupported poles
were asserted without hesitation. Cautious where the record exists, confident where it does not.
