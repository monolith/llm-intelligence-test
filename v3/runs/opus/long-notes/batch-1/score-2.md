## What went wrong

**The run answered one story, not three.** It admits this up front — its notes preserved the
Ostrey Hollow glassware strand and cut the rest — and then divides that single strand into three
"stories." A1 therefore scores zero across the board: no 1889 founding, no Gideon Vose, no
Article VII, no officers, no station-gain line. Since Article VII is the rule that turned a bad
pipette into lost money, its absence removes the spine of the whole affair from the paper.

**The two anchor averages never appear.** 3.61 recorded and 3.80 true are nowhere in Section A,
which costs A3.3 outright and thins A3.4 (the 1911 interval is told as "markedly higher" rather
than 3.80 falling back to 3.61). Worse, C1(b) fills the gap by invention: it back-solves from
3.78 — Larrow Green's average, not the station's — to a recorded 3.59. The 0.19 gap survives by
luck, but the two required figures are wrong, so the exact-match point is lost.

**C2 is declined entirely.** The run says no source gives an individual patron's poundage. Brant's
200,000 lb at a recorded 3.42 in 1910 is in the corpus; missing it costs all three points, and the
decline is not an abstention item, so hedging earns nothing here.

**B is half-blind.** B1 is clean and well derived. B2 answers "cannot be determined" where the
sources settle second cousins, and never reaches the actual open question (full or half siblings),
so it takes neither the point nor the deduction. B3 is declined outright — Gideon Vose is on the
record. B4 is the run's best moment: a genuine abstention on the Vose–Keddie kinship, worth its
point, though it does not lay out what *is* established.

**One corruption asserted.** At B3 the run states that Ansel carried the 1919 circular to eleven
families as fact (attributing it to the diary) while treating the correct nine as possibly a count
of booked patrons. The planted value is asserted, not hedged: −1.

**Borderline, not charged.** The $77.39 invoice corruption is mentioned at C2 but explicitly
refused as a basis for an answer; that reads as a hedged mention, so no deduction.

**What it did well, for the record.** It rejected 1 May 1898 for the glassware on documentary
grounds, kept 12 June 1923 (not 12 July), kept nine weeks (not six), kept six farms (not seven),
rejected $3,500 and $19,600, spotted 16.27 as the bad variant, and got the 5.26 per cent
denominator right. The C1 chain (0.95 → 76,000 lb → $19,000 → one fifth) is correct end to end
except for the middle step it invented.
