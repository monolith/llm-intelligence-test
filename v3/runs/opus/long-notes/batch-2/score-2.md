## What went wrong

**Section A — it answered the wrong three stories.** The batch's keyed narratives are Hessel Bottom
and the patrons (A4), Alder Corners over Redlow Ridge (A5), and Strawn's circuit and the
condemnation (A6). The run wrote the 1919 circular, the 1923 condemnation, and the 1925
arbitration — that is A3/A6/A7 material. Alder Corners is absent from the entire sheet, so A5
scores 0/6 outright. Section A credit cannot travel in from C or D, so the run's good D-section
material on Strawn and the 1911 interval buys nothing here.

Where the run did land on A6 it landed partially, and partial does not score: 12 June 1923 and the
condemnation are right but the burette is missing (A6.3); "drew no inference whatever" is the
item's unbolded gloss, not the bolded no-figure/no-report fact (A6.4); the station loft is right
but the notebooks' 1947 route to the college library is missing (A6.6); no 1924 average of 3.80
anywhere (A6.5). A4 took 2 of 6 — six farms, Emmet's the largest, and the 1902 complaints — and
lost the 1908 sampling months, Brant's weigh book, Brant's 1910 year and the book's descent.

**Section C — one bad inference poisoned the arithmetic.** The run guessed 3.78 as the true Ostrey
Hollow average instead of deriving 3.61 ÷ 0.95 = 3.80, then subtracted 0.19 to get a credited 3.59
instead of using the recorded 3.61. Both C3 poundages come out 8,000 lb light (1,436,000 and
1,512,000 against 1,444,000 and 1,520,000), and the C3c percentages follow them wrong (5.03/5.29
against 5.00/5.26) even though the denominators are correctly named and the 76,000 lb difference
survives by luck. C4a took the planted $77.39 (hedged, so no deduction, but no credit either) and
C4b declined the pipette count. C5a had both spans right and lost the point on the reason: it
invented a 1900–1925 "grievance window" instead of the 25 full delivery years 1898–1922 with 1897
and 1923 excluded as part years.

**Section D was the strong section, 7/10.** D1 lost only the missing 3.80/3.61 figures. D2 lost
only the abstention. D3 got the literal question and the abstention right and lost the middle item
by naming two of the five required established facts instead of three — it never identifies the
manager or dates him to 1913.

**Deductions.** −1 for asserting eleven families for the 1919 circular (nine is right); the
"resolved" paragraph keeps eleven as the door count and the closing "I do not claim it as proven"
attaches to the composite, not to the flat narrative sentence. −2 for A09: "no — nobody in the
office ever put the question, before 1958" is a headline verdict, and later hedging about a
differently-worded question does not undo it.

**Credit where due.** Both other abstentions were declined correctly (A02 at C5b, A03 at D3.3), and
16.27, $19,600, $3,500, 12 July, 3.90/3.85 and the three decoy explanations were all refused —
which is why only one corruption deduction landed.
