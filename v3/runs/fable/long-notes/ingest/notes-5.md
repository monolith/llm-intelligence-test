# Retention notes — Segment 5 (Retellings 01–08; the Ostrey Hollow skimming-station affair)

These notes cover eight retellings encountered so far. All concern the same events: the Larrow Valley Cooperative Creamery Association, its skimming station at Ostrey Hollow, the tester Ansel Keddie, the long-running low butterfat tests there, the 1925 board of arbitration, and the 1958–59 discovery that the station's pipettes were short. Retellings 01–06 (Vira Toland 1979; Effie Loomis 1941; Ansel Keddie 1925; Ivy Keddie 1959; Wilbur Teague 1948; Merle Strawn 1936) are carried forward from the previous notes unchanged. **Two NEW retellings were read this segment: Retelling 07 — Halbert Nym, curator, accession notes for the Ordell County dairy collection (Ordell County Historical Rooms, 1972), and Retelling 08 — Selby Vose, manager, annual address to the Association (1926).** Both are set out in full below (Sources 7 and 8) and merged into the cast, places, timeline, conflicts and objects. A ninth item labelled as a "long retelling" turned out to be generated noise only (a porch-gossip transcript of Huldah Larkspur, Xanthus Yarrow, Araminta Hollister and Increase Ives in "Grayling Falls County"; a nonsense "BrolNabu" code specification; and a second, separate store ledger beginning 1893-11-06 at a running total of $5.70) — it contains NO story material.

**Noise read this segment and earlier (NOT story):** the same country-store ledger continued (daily sales of dry goods — calico, muslin, flannel, ticking, osnaburg, salt pork, molasses, vinegar, lard, sugar, tea, coffee, pepper, rice, currants, raisins, crackers, candles, matches, soap, starch, buttons, nails, spikes, powder, shot, rope, shingles, foolscap, writing paper, liniment, oats, flour, salt, tobacco — with hundreds of customer names, day totals and running dollar totals). The ledger as now read spans **1858–1957** (earlier segments 1858–1867, 1886–1938; this segment 1938-10-31 through 1957-07-15, the running total ending at $821,434.31). Its recurring marginal notes are: "The scale was checked against the county standard and found true," "A stranger passed through and settled his account in coin," "Word came that the mill upriver had shut for want of water," "The peddler came through and traded rather than paid," "Trade was thin, most families holding money back for the fair," "No custom before noon; the bridge was under repair," "The stove smoked all forenoon and the front room was closed," "Store closed at noon for a funeral in the neighborhood," "A committee called about the school tax and stayed talking past closing," "A dispute over an old charge was settled by splitting the difference," "Settled an old account carried over from the spring," "The clerk misfigured a bill and the error was caught before it left the counter," "Rain kept off the hay and trade was brisk," "The roads were bad and few came in," "Nothing of note; an ordinary day." **Caution:** the ledger's dates run straight through the years of the Ostrey Hollow affair (1919 circular, 1923 condemnation, 1925 hearing, 1926 address, 1934, 1936, 1938, 1941, 1947, 1948, 1958 is beyond its end), but none of its entries have anything to do with the story; its "scale was checked… found true" note is a shopkeeper's scale and must NOT be confused with Merle Strawn's "Scales true" inspection entries at Ostrey Hollow or Selby Vose's "tried our scales against his weights and found them true"; its funerals, disputes and settled accounts are not the Keddie/Brant/Vose affairs. Other noise: an 1884 admission ledger of the "Cold Spring Water-Cure near Mercer's Gap" (Dr. Josiah Vantine; patients Cordelia Ashgrove, Ansgar Krell — four weeks for rheumatism —, Prudence and Osric Alderking, Barnabas Quire, Salathiel Purbeck, Zebulon Dabney, Araminta and Theodosia Fennimore, Wendell Corcoran; its "liniment compounded on the premises" is not the store's liniment nor anything in the story); a "SKIFFLINE" ferry-scheduling API specification (Kelmond Harbor Transit Authority, Lowgate, Ferrn Point; error SKF-2201); a "ZephDral"/"TorDral"/"BrolNabu"-style code spec; gibberish paragraphs; porch-gossip transcripts; a combinatorics paper on "saturated lacings" by "R. T. Havelock." None of it bears on the story.

## CAST (consolidated across all eight sources)

**The Keddie family**
- **Ansel Keddie** — tester at the Ostrey Hollow skimming station. Kept the test book in his own hand from the station's opening. Tested 29 seasons, 1896 through 1924; did not test in 1925. Dismissed ("put out") 30 April 1925. Died in the 1930s (Ivy: 1934). "An old man" with "no other trade" in 1925. Vira says he went up in '95, the year before opening, to get the station ready. Strawn (who never names him) describes "their tester" in 1907: "keeps his book in his own hand, and his hand is better than most… works slow and clean and reads his own figure without being coaxed to it"; in 1916 "the tester complains that his patrons blame him for their figures. I told him that is the trade and he said he knew it." Nym (1972): his statement to the board (Item 8) is "One leaf, ruled, both sides, in the hand of Item 3 [the station test book], signed"; the test book is "One hand throughout, twenty-nine seasons of it" — though Nym's Item 3 heading, evidently transposed, calls the test book "kept by E. Brant" and his Item 4 heading calls the Brant farm weigh book "kept by A. Keddie" (see Conflict 38). Selby Vose (1926) never names him and never mentions his dismissal.
- **Orra Keddie** — Ansel's wife. Emmet Brant's sister. First cousin of "old Mrs. Vose" (Vira). After the 1919 circular she and her brother never spoke; she would walk down to Larrow Green rather than pass his lane. Outlived Ansel "a good while."
- **Duncan Keddie** — Ansel's son; taught school at Ashlin; Ivy's father.
- **Ivy Keddie** — Ansel's granddaughter; college at Farland; gave the 1959 talk "What the Crate Held." Nym calls her "Miss Ivy Keddie of the State Agricultural College at Farland"; she found the crate in February 1958 (Nym: "in the Association office at Ashlin" — Ivy herself: the station loft), took it to Farland, measured it (sheet of 9 March 1958 "typed, figures entered by hand, signed by Miss Ivy Keddie"), and gave it to the Rooms in 1959; borrowed the Brant weigh book from Roy Brant in 1958 and returned it; found and copied Strawn's 1923 volume at Farland in 1958; the Alder Corners figures were "copied in 1958." Vira (1979) recalls "a young woman came round here in nineteen fifty-eight from the college away east" who poked about the old station and sat at her kitchen table — "Hazel Brant's first cousin once removed, so she was of the family both sides in a manner of speaking." (This visitor is evidently Ivy.)

**The Brant family (Hessel Bottom)**
- **Emmet Brant** — biggest man on Hessel Bottom: most cows, most hay ground; hauled more milk to the station than anyone; the station's largest patron in every year of record (Effie). Orra Keddie's brother. The 1919 letter "cost him the most." Before 1919 the Brant and Keddie households ate Sunday dinners together; after it, not one word. Nym Item 4: the farm weigh book "Begun at Hessel Bottom on 1 January 1897 and kept there by one household throughout"; its leaf for 1910 reads *"Year 1910. Delivered to the station, two hundred thousand pounds even, by my own scale and theirs agreeing. Paid on a test of 3.42."* The book went "to Roy Brant in 1929" (implying Emmet died or gave it up in 1929 — not stated outright). "E. Brant" appears (transposed) as the keeper of the station test book in Nym's Item 3 heading.
- **Roy Brant** — Emmet's son; lent Ivy the Brant weigh book (kept at Hessel Bottom from 1897 onward), which he has kept up since his father died. Nym: the weigh book came to Roy in 1929; he lent it to Ivy in 1958 and it was returned; it passed to Hazel Brant in 1962.
- **Hazel Brant** — named by Vira as the 1958 visitor's relative ("first cousin once removed"). Nym: "Miss Hazel Brant" held the weigh book from 1962 and it was "received here in 1963." Her relation to Roy/Emmet is not stated.

**The Vose family (Larrow Green)**
- **Gideon Vose** — "old Gideon"; organized/started the Association; elected first president 1889, held office until 1911 (22 years, longest ever). Drafted the by-laws himself; not a lawyer. Died 1917 (Effie: Article VII "was written in 1892 by a man who died in 1917"). Selby Vose (1926): "My father, who was your first president and who wrote the by-law under which every patron in this Association is paid on the test of the station where he delivers, kept a herd of Jerseys at the home place, as some of you remember, and I kept them after him. In 1910 that herd tested 4.56."
- **Selby Vose** — manager 1913–1938 (Effie). Signed the 1919 "water in the cans" circular (Vira); in 1926 he says outright "the circular I sent to the patrons of that station in 1919… I wrote it because it was my duty to write it." **CONFLICT:** Effie calls him "son of the late president"; Vira calls him "old Gideon Vose's nephew"; **Selby himself (1926) says "My father, who was your first president."** Signed the annual station sheet every year (Teague). Teague "put it to Mr. Vose in 1916 and was told to let it alone." Strawn 1913: "New manager at the Association this year and he came out with me to the platform, which the last one never did" (consistent with Selby's 1913 start; the earlier manager is unnamed). Strawn 1919: "The manager of the Association has sent a circular round to the patrons of this station about water in the cans… I have never found any, and I told him so." Strawn, 12 June 1923: "told the manager to order new" glass. His 1926 address (Source 8): thirteenth year of management "since 1913"; his explanation of the whole affair is water in the cans that "stopped when the Association began to look"; he dates the condemnation "the twelfth of July, 1923" and gives the 1924 average as 3.90; kept his father's Jerseys after him ("they are gone now"); "I thank you for thirteen years of your patience and I ask for one more" (he in fact managed until 1938).
- **old Mrs. Vose** — Orra Keddie's first cousin (Vira: "everybody up here knew and nobody wrote down anywhere").
- **"the last one" (manager before 1913)** — unnamed; Strawn says he never came out to the platform.

**Association officers**
- **Effie Loomis** — secretary 1901–1940; kept the minute book in her own hand; 14 years old in 1889 (so born c. 1875); wrote "Notes toward a history of the Association" in 1941. Nym Item 7: the board's minute book is "in the hand of the Association's secretary."
- **Wilbur Teague** — bookkeeper 1905–1945 (40 years); kept the ledgers; invented the "station gain" line; wrote Chapter Nine of an unpublished memoir "The Books of the Association" at Ashlin, winter 1948.
- **Hobart Sill** — buttermaker at Larrow Green; kept the churn book and the cream receiving book (Nym Item 16: "Receiving and churn books of the Larrow Green creamery, water-damaged at the foot").
- **Alonzo Frick** — attorney of Ashlin; chaired the 1925 board of arbitration. Teague: "as fair a chairman as ever a hearing had." Selby: "an able and impartial man."
- **The 1908 investigating committee chairman** — unnamed; signed the fair copy of the 1908 report (Nym Item 11).

## PLACES
- **Larrow Valley** — the valley; **Larrow Green** — hamlet where the Association organized (1889) and the main creamery was built (1891) on **Larrow Creek**; "same building yet" (1941). Strawn inspected the Larrow Green creamery in 1913 (two days: churn room and receiving books), 1917, 1931, March 1936. Selby (1926): "The creamery at Larrow Green ran through the flush without a day lost, which is more than the creameries below us can say." Nym Item 16: its receiving and churn books show the Larrow Green average "a little under two tenths of a point better" than the station's 3.61 over the same years.
- **Ostrey Hollow** — seven miles up the valley from Larrow Green (Strawn: "up seven miles from Larrow Green, opened before my time"; Selby 1926: "The skimming station at Ostrey Hollow, seven miles up the valley, ran its season out and its cream came down to us daily and sweet"); the skimming station stood at the head of the Hollow, "at the very top of the Hollow where the road bends and then goes to nothing but the ridge" (Vira). Low whitewashed building, cold as a spring house even in August; smelled of milk, steam and hot iron; engine audible before the last turn. Intake room; loft over the intake room reached by a ladder (Strawn 1928: "full of odds and ends… a loft over a milk room is a place for nothing but air… it had been full since before the war"). Wash room (floor holding water at the drain, 1915); cold room (fair 1907; short of ice fall 1920); boiler; separator (bearing ran hot 1934). Shut by 1958 and used for storage (fence wire and salt). Strawn 1909: the station "takes from the farms of the wet bottom below it and from three places on the hill." Strawn 1912: creek out over the flat road; he went round by the ridge. Selby: "A station in daily use since 1896."
- **Hessel Bottom** — the wet meadow below the hamlet of Ostrey Hollow; best milk came off it; seven farms had rights on it (hay and pasture); largest was Emmet Brant's. The Brant weigh book was begun and kept there (Nym).
- **Ashlin** — town where the Association's office was (Teague: one room over a harness shop, cold at the north end, never cold enough at the south; ledgers on a shelf a taller predecessor built; sloped desk with a brass rail). Board of arbitration sat there 1925. Ashlin creamery lent Keddie glass in 1896 (per his letter). Ordell County Historical Society meets there. Nym: the station test book was in the Association's office at Ashlin 1925–1959; the crate (per Nym) "was found in the Association office at Ashlin, in February 1958." Vira's father's joke: "there would be no lawyers in Ashlin… There are still lawyers in Ashlin."
- **Ordell County** — the county (Historical Society, Ashlin). **Ordell County Historical Rooms** — Nym's institution (Ivy's "these Rooms"); holds the dairy collection. **The Ordell circuit** — Strawn's inspection circuit: "fifty-odd plants in four counties — creameries, cheese factories, skimming stations, one or two little places that made butter three days a week and called themselves a creamery." Selby: "There is not a business house in Ordell County that would have done it."
- **Vessey County / Redlow Ridge / Alder Corners** — Alder Corners Cheese Company over the ridge in the next county; its book never left Vessey County (Nym: "has never been in this county"; to the **Vessey County Historical Society** in 1970). (Strawn 1917 visited "a cheese factory in the next county," unnamed; 1936 "the two factories over the county line.")
- **Farland** — Ivy's college; Nym names it "the State Agricultural College at Farland"; library holds Strawn's notebooks (since 1947); where the pipettes were measured.
- **Tarnet** — home of Ammon & Sons Dairy Supply (stencil on the crate; invoice head).
- **The State Agricultural College** — issued Bulletin No. 214 (1911) and Bulletin No. 471 (October 1958); per Nym it is at Farland.
- **The state dairy office** — received Strawn's 28 books and his 1936 copy.
- **"the home place"** — the Vose family farm where Gideon and then Selby kept Jerseys (Selby 1926; location not stated — the Voses are "of Larrow Green").
- **"the creameries below us" / "the trade below us"** — Selby's phrases for the buyers and creameries down-valley who call the Larrow Valley "poor milk country."

## TIMELINE (consolidated)
- **1889** — Larrow Valley farmers meet at Larrow Green; Larrow Valley Cooperative Creamery Association organized; Gideon Vose first president.
- **1889–1892** — minute book of these years lost in the 1938 office move.
- **1891** — main creamery built at Larrow Green on Larrow Creek.
- **1892** — Article VII adopted at annual meeting (Effie). Nym: the printed by-laws carry no year, only "adopted at the annual meeting."
- **1895** — Ansel Keddie goes up to get the station ready (Vira).
- **4 May 1896** — station book begun: "Ostrey Hollow station. Begun this 4th day of May, 1896. Glass borrowed of Larrow Green until our own comes. A. Keddie." Average test 1896: 3.80 (on borrowed glass). Nym: the station's intake for its first season, 4 May to the end of 1896, "is not in the ledgers and is nowhere else."
- **1 January 1897** — Brant farm weigh book begun at Hessel Bottom (Nym; Ivy: "from 1897"). The station column in the Association's ledgers begins with 1897 (Nym).
- **14 April 1897** — Ammon & Sons invoice for the Ostrey Hollow outfit (Nym Item 2; total $77.93).
- **Spring 1897 / 1 May 1897** — station's own outfit arrives in a crate (six dozen pipettes, twelve dozen bottles, acid measure, hand tester; invoice $77.93 incl. freight); office file has it "in use at the station from 1 May 1897, Larrow Green having lent until then." Bulletin 471 dates the short-pipette era from **1 May 1897**. 1897 = "part year."
- **1898** — average test 3.61; first full delivery year. Teague's quoted "1898" station-gain entry: 57,760 lb credited / 60,800 lb received / gain 3,040 lb (Nym Item 6 quotes the same entry as "typical" of every year).
- **1898–1922** — 25 full delivery years: 40,000,000 lb milk (avg 1,600,000/yr); tests "3.61 or within three hundredths of it." Nym: station gain accumulated across those years 76,000 lb butterfat, worth $19,000.
- **1901** — Effie Loomis becomes secretary.
- **1902** — complaints begin: first said at Larrow Green that Ostrey Hollow milk "did not test with the rest."
- **1905** — Wilbur Teague becomes bookkeeper; begins (he says) the station-gain line.
- **1907** — Merle Strawn takes the Ordell circuit; first round: Ostrey Hollow scales true, cold room fair, watched the tester through two tests.
- **January–March 1908** — a committee samples the herds of Hessel Bottom (Nym: "January, February and March 1908 only"); reports the wet hay of the Bottom thinned the cream; remedy "lay with the land" (report quoted under Source 7, Item 11). (Ivy dates Strawn's inspectorship from 1908 — conflicts with Strawn's own 1907.)
- **1909 (fall)** — Strawn: scales true, boiler in order, safety tried; station takes from the wet bottom and three hill places; one man cautioned about a rusted seam.
- **1910** — Brant weigh book leaf: 200,000 lb delivered to the station, "paid on a test of 3.42." Vose Jerseys at the home place tested 4.56 (Selby).
- **1911** — Gideon Vose's presidency ends. **8 May 1911**: "Pipette broke this morning. Using a borrowed measure." Tests rise to 3.80. **June 1911**: Strawn visits Ostrey Hollow "on my way over to the other side of the county. Scales true. Watched a test through. Nothing here to note" (i.e., during the borrowed-measure weeks). **10 July 1911**: "Our own measure in use again from today. Tests are back where they were. ? — A.K." (nine weeks; the borrowed measure "came up from Larrow Green with the cream wagon"). Same year the State Agricultural College tests bog hay (Bulletin No. 214).
- **1912** — Strawn: scales true; lactometer, no added water; cream shipped down the valley the same day it is separated; creek out over the flat road.
- **1913** — Selby Vose becomes manager. Strawn at Larrow Green two days: "New manager at the Association this year and he came out with me to the platform, which the last one never did."
- **1915** — Strawn: wash-room floor holding water at the drain; ordered graded.
- **1916** — Teague raises the station account with Mr. Vose; told to let it alone. Strawn: scales true, no added water; "the tester complains that his patrons blame him for their figures."
- **1917** — Gideon Vose dies. Strawn: cheese factory in the next county, then Larrow Green; bad year for help, two testers doing the work of three.
- **1919** — printed circular from the Association over the manager's (Selby Vose's) signature to nine Ostrey Hollow families: "water in the cans." It rained that day (Vira). Brant/Keddie families stop speaking. Strawn at Ostrey Hollow: scales true, lactometer no added water; "I have tested for water at this station on every visit I have made to it and I have never found any, and I told him so" (told the manager). Selby (1926) defends the circular: "It warned the patrons of Ostrey Hollow, in temperate language, that added water would be looked for and would be dealt with. It named nobody. It accused nobody. It said that this Association pays for butterfat and cannot pay for creek water." Alder Corners test-book figures for "the season of 1919" copied in 1958 (Nym Item 13).
- **1920** — Strawn twice (spring and fall): scales true, no added water; cold room short of ice in the fall, seen to.
- **1921** — state begins issuing burettes to field men ("graduated, in a wooden case, for checking the delivery of measures and pipettes at the plants"); Strawn's came in the spring with a printed sheet of directions.
- **1922** — Keddie makes up the summary leaf of averages at the back of the book. Strawn: scales true, no water, boiler in order; "I did not run their glass against the burette this year, the day being short and the road home being what it was, and I have put that down because it is what happened."
- **12 June 1923** — Strawn runs the milk pipettes against the burette: "These measures will not deliver their mark. Condemned the lot, the whole crate of them, and told the manager to order new." (Effie: "the state's inspector" condemned the crate in June 1923; set aside in the loft; new ones ordered. Nym Item 5: "Condemned by the state dairy inspector on 12 June 1923." **Selby Vose (1926): "he condemned the station's testing apparatus on the twelfth of July, 1923, and told us to order new. We ordered new inside the month and had it in use before the flush was over."**) **28 June 1923** — the Association orders replacement pipettes and bottles (Nym Item 10). **2 July 1923** — Ammon & Sons letter acknowledging the order, promising shipment that month, "declining to say anything of the earlier lot." **July 1923** — new glass in use (Strawn, October 1923: "New glass in use since July. Ran the new pipettes against the burette. They deliver their mark."). Tests thereafter rise "all at once, one summer… and stayed up" (Vira).
- **1924** — station average 3.80 (Keddie's last season). Strawn: "tests improved. Their book gives the year at 3.80." **Selby (1926): "the tests at that station rose by twenty-one hundredths and the average for 1924 stood at 3.90."** "The rise of 1924 is the measure of it."
- **January 1925** — Ostrey Hollow patrons petition the Association.
- **17 March 1925** — board of arbitration opens at Ashlin under Alonzo Frick; Keddie's nine-sentence statement. **March 1925** — station test book "impounded by the board of arbitration" (Nym).
- **30 April 1925** — Keddie put out ("the last day of the month").
- **4 May 1925** — board's last day; award recorded by Effie: $3,800, "finds no cause established… without admission, to be charged to the station account."
- **Spring 1925** — Keddie's unsent letter.
- **Fall 1925** — Strawn: "New man at the tester's bench this year. He is quick, and quick is not the thing I look for."
- **1926** — state discontinues the burette program (instruments kept, none returned; Strawn's "has been used twice since"). **Selby Vose's thirteenth annual address** (printed in the annual report): reviews the 1925 season (wet into June), the petition, hearing and award; gives his water-in-the-cans explanation; defends the 1919 circular; cites his father's Jerseys (4.56 in 1910); "I ask for one more" year.
- **1928** — Strawn: station "quieter than it was"; loft over the intake room full of odds and ends, "full since before the war."

## SOURCE 1 — Vira Toland (recorded at Ostrey Hollow, 1979)
Tape made at her kitchen table by "a young man from the county who asked three questions in an hour and let her have the rest"; transcribed as spoken with her doublings-back left in.

- Born 1912, top end of Ostrey Hollow, house her father's father put up. Two years at Ashlin when first married ("would not count those two years anyhow").
- Father hauled milk to the station "the same as everybody up here did." **Nine families** hauling to that station; "in and out of each other's kitchens the whole year round." She could name them all but "you don't want names, you want the trouble, they always want the trouble."
- Station description (see PLACES). **Ansel Keddie ran it.** "He went up there in ninety-five, the year before they opened it, to get it ready, and he tested that first summer too, and he was still there when I was a girl big enough to ride on the wagon with my father."
- **The book**: everybody knew about it, no secret. Ruled with his own straightedge ruling, every line the same height, figures "like print." Wrote standing at a slope desk by the window. If a man asked what his milk had done, "he turned to the day and he read it off." "He tested a thousand cans a week and never a blot in that book." Father: if the rest of the world kept accounts like Ansel Keddie "there would be no lawyers in Ashlin."
- **1919**: "the year everything went sour"; she was seven; remembers it "the way you remember a smell." A letter from the Association, printed, over the manager's signature: "there was water in the cans coming down from Ostrey Hollow." Went to nine families; her father read it at the table with his hat still on (which he never did), read it twice; mother stood at the stove with her back to him and never turned. He put it in the drawer of the desk in the front room; still there when he died; her brother has it yet. "Some of them kept theirs till they died. One woman I knew had hers pinned inside the lid of her trunk." "They will still keep the paper forty years to prove what was said of them."
- Signer: **Selby Vose, down at Larrow Green, old Gideon Vose's nephew**; Gideon "started the whole Association back in the beginning"; "the family always thought the valley belonged to them a little." **Orra Keddie and old Mrs. Vose were first cousins** — so "it was somebody's cousin saying it."
- **Orra** was Ansel's wife and **Emmet Brant's sister**. Emmet: "the biggest man on the Bottom… the most cows and the most hay ground… hauled more milk to that station than anybody… the one that letter cost the most." "The man that tested the milk had married the sister of the man that got the worst of it." Before 1919: Sunday dinners together, "the two houses in one kitchen and the men out at the fence after." After: "not one word, not at the store, not at a funeral." Orra walked to Larrow Green rather than pass her brother's lane; "within sight of each other's chimney smoke."
- **Then the tests went up**: "All at once, one summer, the figures came up and stayed up, and nobody ever said why." Father "had not done anything different, none of them had." "The thing corrected itself and no man stood up and gave a reason for it."
- **Then the hearing at Ashlin** — "the winter and spring after." Lawyers, Association officers, "our men going down in the wagons… it went on and on." "At the end of it they put Ansel Keddie out." **"They paid the patrons thirty-five hundred dollars and called it settled. Thirty-five hundred dollars, split up nine ways over all those years — my father's share of it did not buy a hay rake."**
- **Her verdict**: "He was taking the cream. They caught him at it and they put him out, and that is why the numbers went right the summer after… A man stands in a room by himself with the separator running and the door shut and nobody in the valley can add, and after a while he helps himself… it is that nobody ever came up that road and looked." Father never said it out loud: "an honest man who had got into something."
- Keddie "died in the thirties and he was still under it"; "all those years of this valley looking at him a certain way in church"; Orra had it too and outlived him.
- **1958 visitor**: young woman "from the college away east," poked about the shut station (fence wire and salt kept in it), sat where the interviewer sits, "nice girl, careful, wrote everything down." "Hazel Brant's first cousin once removed… of the family both sides." Vira: "here it comes round again after all this time and there is nobody left alive that it can do any good to." Told her about the letter in the drawer; "I don't know what she made of it."
- Close: "It rained the day of that letter, I remember the wet on my father's shoulders when he came in, and I have never in sixty years been able to think about the one without the other."

## SOURCE 2 — Effie Loomis, "Notes toward a history of the Association" (1941)
Numbered sections for the Association's files, written the year after she laid down the minute book; from the minutes where they bear it, from recollection otherwise, "the two kept apart."

1. **Organizing**: 1889, Larrow Green. Gideon Vose first president, 1889–1911 (22 years). She was 14 in 1889; secretary from 1901; "for the first twelve years… only a reader of its papers… the papers of exactly those years are the ones we have lost." 1889–1892 minute book lost in the 1938 office move.
2. **Creamery**: built at Larrow Green 1891 on Larrow Creek; same building yet. Before it: no common test, no common price, "every man's arithmetic was his own." "A plain remedy for a plain grievance."
3. **By-laws / Article VII** (quoted entire): *"Article VII. Every patron shall be paid for the milk he delivers according to the number of pounds of butter fat contained in it, ascertained by test, and not according to the weight of the milk; and the test of the station where he delivers shall govern."* Adopted at the 1892 annual meeting. Effie's gloss: before, paid by can weight; after, paid on fat found by test at the place of delivery. "The last clause, which is the one nobody quotes… Not the test of the creamery, not the average of the valley, not a test made over again anywhere else. The station's test, and no appeal from it. That clause is the hinge of the whole Ostrey Hollow business."
4. **Officers**: Effie secretary 1901–1940; Wilbur Teague bookkeeper from 1905; Hobart Sill buttermaker (churn book, cream receiving book); Selby Vose, "son of the late president," manager 1913–1938. "Not one of us ever went up the valley and stood in the Ostrey Hollow station on a working morning to watch the work being done. I include myself first."
5. **Station**: seven miles up from Larrow Green at the head of the Hollow; took milk, separated it, sent cream down, gave skim back. Hessel Bottom = wet meadow below the hamlet; **seven farms** had rights; largest Emmet Brant's, the station's largest patron in every recorded year.
6. **Petition & board**: January 1925 petition — milk "had never tested as their neighbors' milk tested… paid accordingly for a very long time… asked to be made whole." Board at Ashlin under Alonzo Frick, attorney, **17 March to 4 May 1925**. "Three explanations were argued before it. None of the three was accepted." Award (quoted): *"The board having heard the patrons and the officers, and having had before it the books and appliances of the station, finds no cause established. The board awards to the Ostrey Hollow patrons the sum of thirty-eight hundred dollars ($3,800.00), without admission, to be charged to the station account."* Effie: "it was not my place to ask how both halves of a sentence could be true at once."
7. **Quantities** (Teague's, from annual sheets; she added the columns twice): over the 25 years of the claim, station received **40,000,000 lb** milk = avg **1,600,000 lb/yr**; butterfat price averaged **25 cents/lb**.
8. **What the record does not contain**: (a) the lost 1889–92 minute book — "carried out with the rest and it did not arrive"; (b) **the crate** — "never mentioned in the hearing at all… the crate of measures that the state's inspector condemned up at the station in June of 1923, which was set aside and new ones ordered in its place. Not a word of it was spoken in any of the three sessions." The award's "books and appliances of the station" is "a form of words the board used and not a list of anything." "The interesting part is generally what none of them thought worth saying."

## SOURCE 3 — Ansel Keddie: statement to the board (17 March 1925) and unsent letter (spring 1925)
Two papers; the second found among his papers, never sent.

**I. Statement** (nine sentences, standing): kept the test book in his own hand since the station opened; every figure his; columns date/patron/pounds/test/fat; errors lined through with "my letters against the line"; "never written a figure over a figure"; tests "in the same order and at the same hour of every working day of every season." Neither patrons nor officers "has come up that road on a working morning and stood at my elbow." "I am an old man and I have no other trade." **"I have tested as I was taught and as the state man saw me do it, thirty-one times a week for twenty-nine seasons, and I cannot tell you why the figures are what they are."** "I have never taken a pound of cream out of that station and there is not a man in this room who thinks I have when he is by himself."

**II. Letter**: "where a man is allowed to answer and not allowed to explain."
- Day began **half past five**. Wagons up the Hollow in the dark most of the year; intake room open, lamps lit, "the scale swinging free." Every can weighed; weight called and written before the milk went anywhere. Scale tested by the state's man, "and it was true"; never in 29 seasons a patron dispute a weight "and be right."
- **Sampling**: sample from each patron's milk as weighed into "his own jar, numbered to him and to nobody else"; jar made up over **ten days** before testing. Testing morning: jar set in warm water till blood-warm through, poured vessel to vessel "until the cream that had risen was carried down through the whole of it."
- **The charge**: "The milk was drawn up in the measure to the mark on the stem, and the tip wiped, and the charge blown out into the bottle that carried the patron's number. Then the acid, poured down the side, and the bottle turned until the milk was dark. Four bottles in the hand tester, weighted against each other, the cover down, and the crank turned at the speed I was taught… Then the fat is up in the neck of the bottle in a clear column, and it is read off in per cent against the rulings, with a pair of dividers." "Not a hard trade. It is only a trade that must be done the same way every time."
- **"The glass I began on in ninety-six was borrowed off the Ashlin creamery, and we used it until our own outfit came up the valley in a crate the spring following."**
- Book: five columns. "Twenty-nine seasons, from 1896 through 1924. I did not test a pound in the season of 1925."
- **First leaf** (quoted): *"Ostrey Hollow station. Begun this 4th day of May, 1896. Glass borrowed of Larrow Green until our own comes. A. Keddie."*
- **1922 summary leaf** (quoted): *"Average test of all patrons, this station: 1896, 3.80. 1897, part year. 1898, 3.61. And 3.61 or within three hundredths of it every year since, save the nine weeks of 1911 that are noted in their place, when it stood at 3.80."*
- **1911**: "Our measure broke on the eighth of May and I made shift with one borrowed, and from that morning the tests came up and stood at 3.80, and on the tenth of July our own was in use again and the tests were down again the same day." Entries quoted: *"May 8. Pipette broke this morning. Using a borrowed measure."* / *"July 10. Our own measure in use again from today. Tests are back where they were. ? — A.K."* "That question mark is mine and I have let it stand for fourteen years."
- The gap "did not come and go with the season… in July as in February, in the flush of the grass and in the dead of the winter on dry hay… the same in a wet year as in a dry one." **1924 average: 3.80**.
- History of complaints: **1902** first said at Larrow Green the milk did not test with the rest; **1908** committee sampled herds through the winter, reported wet hay of the Bottom thinned the cream, "the remedy lay with the land"; **1919** circular to nine families: water in their cans. "Nine kitchens in this Hollow have had that letter in a drawer ever since." He carries "no charge against any man who put his name to that paper."
- "My wife Orra is Emmet Brant's sister. Emmet brought more milk to my door than any other man on the Bottom… He and I have not spoken since the circular. That is the plainest fact in this letter."
- **"I was put out on the thirtieth of April. They found no cause established and they put me out on the last day of the month, and both of those things are true at once."**

## SOURCE 4 — Ivy Keddie, "What the Crate Held" (talk, Ordell County Historical Society, Ashlin, 1959)
Read from typescript with the crate on a table and the borrowed books laid open; papers read aloud from originals.

- Ansel Keddie = grandfather; Duncan Keddie (schoolteacher at Ashlin) = father. "Nothing I am going to show you tonight depends in the smallest degree on my opinion of my grandfather. It depends on nine pieces of glass and a burette."
- **Five sets of records**, never in one room before 1958: (1) the station test book — in the Association's office since 1925, came to the Society's Rooms in 1959; (2) the Brant weigh book, Hessel Bottom, from 1897, lent by Roy Brant (Emmet's son); (3) Alder Corners Cheese Company test figures (Redlow Ridge, Vessey County), copied by Rosalie Cudd (runs the factory; Jerome Cudd's niece) — Ivy never saw the book; (4) Merle Strawn's circuit notebooks (state dairy inspector from 1908), in Farland's library since 1947; (5) the Association's annual ledgers, present at the talk.
- **The crate**: February 1958, station loft over the intake room, up a ladder, under a tarpaulin at the back: wooden crate stenciled **AMMON & SONS DAIRY SUPPLY TARNET**; carried up June 1923. Inside, in straw, the condemned pipettes. **Six dozen** bought new in 1897; invoice **$77.93** for the whole outfit (pipettes, bottles, acid measure, hand tester, freight). **Nine unbroken.**
- **9 March 1958**, measured at Farland against a calibrated burette. Sheet (quoted): *"Nine pipettes from the Ostrey Hollow crate, stems blown 17.6 c.c. Delivered against burette, mean of five runs each: 16.70, 16.71, 16.72, 16.72, 16.72, 16.73, 16.73, 16.74, 16.71. Mean 16.72 c.c. Short 0.88 c.c. That is 95 hundredths of the mark, and the mark is what the neck of the bottle was ruled for."*
- Reasoning: bottle neck ruled to read percent for a fixed charge; short charge → proportionally short reading. 16.72 ÷ 17.6 = 0.95 exactly. "Every test made at that station with that glass read ninety-five percent of the truth."
- Quotes grandfather's 1922 summary leaf (identical wording to Source 3).
- 3.61 ÷ 0.95 = 3.80. Recorded 3.61, true 3.80, gap 0.19 = 5% of 3.80. "The station did not lose a set number of points. It lost a twentieth of whatever was there."
- **Arithmetic**: 25 full delivery years **1898–1922**; 40,000,000 lb milk, 1,600,000/yr. At 3.61%: **1,444,000 lb** fat credited. At 3.80%: **1,520,000 lb** delivered. Difference **76,000 lb** butterfat. At 25 cents/lb: **$19,000** unpaid. Board awarded $3,800 — "That is a fifth of it."
- **Two percentages**: 76,000 is **5%** of fat delivered (1,520,000) and **5¼%** of fat credited (1,444,000). "The Association's ledger line uses the second, because a bookkeeper naturally reckons a surplus against the figure he has already entered. Neither figure is an error. But five percent is the one that is also the glass."
- Proportional error hit the best milk hardest: a herd recorded 4.56 was short 0.24; one recorded 3.42 short 0.18. "His good cows cost him more than his poor ones." (Note: 3.42 = the Brant herd's 1910 test per the weigh book; 4.56 = the Vose Jerseys' 1910 test per Selby — see Conflict 48.)
- **1911**: "an experiment… and nobody knew it was one." Own measure broke 8 May; substitute until 10 July; "the measure they borrowed in 1911 came up from Larrow Green with the cream wagon." Everything else the same, "one object changed," average went to 3.80 and back to 3.61 the day the station's own measure returned. "This valley's twenty-six-year anomaly."
- **Wet-hay explanation** ("the kindest of the old explanations… offered in good faith"): State Agricultural College tested it in 1911, **Bulletin No. 214** (quoted): *"Bog hay fed as the whole of the roughage depressed the butter fat test by not more than five hundredths of one per cent in winter, and by nothing that could be measured on summer pasture."* "Four times too small and it is the wrong shape." Alder Corners tested the milk of **four Hessel Bottom men** in their summer flush; where the two books overlap, **fourteen seasons**, that milk ran about 0.19 higher at Alder Corners than at home.
- **Bulletin No. 471**, issued October 1958, last paragraph (Professor Vaught's wording and Ivy's, quoted): *"No milk was watered at Ostrey Hollow and no cream was stolen there. From the first of May, 1897, to the twelfth of June, 1923, the station's tests were low by one part in twenty because its pipettes were short by one part in twenty, and the difference — seventy-six thousand pounds of butter fat — stands credited in the Association's own ledgers, year by year, as station gain."*
- Close: glass condemned 1923, cause found 1958 — **35 years**; grandfather dismissed 1925 — **33 years**; died 1934, dead **24 years** when the sheet was read. "I have no remedy to propose. I have only the crate, and it is on the table, and you may look into it on your way out."

## SOURCE 5 — Wilbur Teague, "The Books of the Association," Chapter Nine (Ashlin, winter 1948)
Unpublished memoir, three years after giving up the desk. "I have set the figures down as they stood in the books."

- Kept the books **1905 to 1945**; "never carried a column that would not prove." "A bookkeeper who wants admiring has generally got something behind him."
- Office: one room over a harness shop at Ashlin (see PLACES). Station sheets arrived in March in an envelope, stiffer paper than the creamery used.
- Association = creamery at Larrow Green, office at Ashlin, skimming station seven miles up at Ostrey Hollow. Mr. Selby Vose manager over the whole; Miss Effie Loomis secretary; Teague at the ledger. "The station was the smallest of the three and it made the most noise." Explains station: weigh in, sample, test, pay on pounds × test; separate; cream down to Larrow Green; skim back for hogs and calves.
- **The station account**: one side = butterfat credited to patrons on the station's test; other side = butterfat actually received at Larrow Green in the station's cream, weighed and tested into the vats. "The two were never the same. The second was always the larger." Difference = "station's account to the good," carried to the Association's general account.
- **"The station-gain line was my own device; I began it when I came in 1905."** Not "some old dodge of the founders." Entry quoted complete: *"Ostrey Hollow: butter fat credited to patrons on station test, 57,760 lb. Butter fat received at Larrow Green in station cream, 60,800 lb. Station gain, 3,040 lb, five and a quarter per cent, as usual. Carried to the general account."* — "That is the entry for 1898, and there is one like it for every year after." "The gain ran at five and a quarter percent of the fat that was actually in the cream." "As usual" got into the entry and stayed. "A figure that comes in at five and a quarter every year is furniture."
- **Two large numbers**: station took in **forty-four million pounds** of milk over the years the account ran; average test of all Ostrey Hollow patrons over the whole period **3.16**. "Down at Larrow Green we did better than that, and there was not a season in twenty-five years when the Ostrey Hollow men were not sore about it."
- **1911**: "The station's own measure broke in the spring of 1911, and they made shift with a borrowed one for **six weeks** before their own came up from town. It went into my accounts as a small charge for replacement and nothing else." Later people asked about that year "as though a bookkeeper kept the weather."
- **His evidence to the board (1925), repeated**: "Cream is portable, and that station was in one pair of hands for twenty-nine seasons." One man weighed, drew the sample, tested, wrote the pay-slip figure, ran the separator; "no second man in that building from the spring to the fall." "A business which is arranged so that a single man can take a little and write down a little less is a business that will sooner or later have a little taken from it… The remedy is two men or a lock, and the Association had neither." He does not say what any person did.
- Patrons petitioned "in the winter, when a farmer has time to be angry"; board at Ashlin under Mr. Alonzo Frick; "found nothing established against anybody"; awarded **$3,800** without admission, charged to the station account; "I entered that too, and it balanced."
- **Mr. Vose knew**: "He signed the sheet every year. I laid the annual sheet before him each spring and he put his name at the foot of it." "I put it to Mr. Vose in 1916 and was told to let it alone. I let it alone. I was the Association's bookkeeper and not its conscience."
- Figures "in the ledgers at Ashlin, in ink, dated."

## SOURCE 6 — Merle Strawn, state dairy inspector (retired), "Extracts from twenty-eight circuit books," with a covering note (1936)
Preface (italic): *"Copied out this spring at the request of the state dairy office, from the pocket books I carried on the Ordell circuit. I have copied what is in them and put in nothing that is not."*

**Covering note.**
- "I took the Ordell circuit in nineteen hundred and seven and I have laid it down this year." (= 1936.) "It was fifty-odd plants in four counties — creameries, cheese factories, skimming stations, one or two little places that made butter three days a week and called themselves a creamery — and the state expected each of them seen at least once in the year and the doubtful ones oftener."
- Travel: "by train where there was a train and by livery where there was not, and in the spring by neither, the roads up the side valleys being what they are in April." "A man on this circuit learns the plants by their smells before he learns them by their names: the sour-sweet of a receiving platform in July, the lime and steam of a wash room, the flat cold nothing of a well-run cold room, which is the smell I liked best because it meant I would have no letter to write."
- **The four things, always in the same order** ("so that I would not have to remember which of them I had done"): **First**, the scales, "against the sealed weights I carried, both the intake scale and the cream scale, loaded light and loaded heavy." **Second**, "the lactometer on the day's milk, patron by patron where there was any question, for added water." **Third**, "the boiler, the water supply and the cold room — pressure, gauge, the cased well if there was one, and whether the room would hold what they put in it overnight." **Fourth**, "and the one most men grudged me, I stood at the tester's elbow and watched him make a test through from the sample to the reading, and I did not let him hurry it."
- "I put down what I found. I did not put down what I thought about it, and I have not begun now. The state pays a field man to weigh and to look, and every time in my life I have seen a field man paid for an opinion instead, the opinion has outlived the weighing and done harm after the man was dead."

Years WITH entries in the extracts: 1907, 1909, 1911, 1912, 1913, 1915, 1916, 1917, 1919, 1920, 1921, 1922, 1923 (two entries: June 12 and October), 1924, 1925, 1926, 1928, 1931, 1934, 1936 — twenty distinct years. Years with NO entry: 1908, 1910, 1914, 1918, 1927, 1929, 1930, 1932, 1933, 1935.

**Closing note.** "The books themselves — twenty-eight of them, one to the year, oilcloth covers, pocket size — go to the state dairy office with this copy, as the rules require of a man laying down a circuit. There is nothing in them but what a field man puts in a field book: what I weighed, what I tested, what I told the plant to mend, and whether it was mended when I came back. They are dull reading and they were meant to be. I have been asked once or twice, by men who ought to know better, to say in a general way how a certain plant stood in my estimation over the years, and I have always answered that I do not keep an estimation, I keep a book, and the book is open to anybody the state sends for it. I never had a theory about a plant and I do not recommend having one. A theory is a thing a man carries out to the platform with him and it gets in the way of the weights."

## SOURCE 8 — Selby Vose, manager, annual address to the Larrow Valley Cooperative Creamery Association (1926)
*The manager's address to the members at the annual meeting, printed in the annual report for the year.* Opens "Members of the Association:".

- "It is thirteen years this spring, since 1913, that you have had the management of this Association in my hands, and I have stood up before you thirteen times to give an account of it. I have never had a year I liked less to report on than the one behind us, and I have never had one I was gladder to have behind us." "Plain speech is the only thing that has ever done this valley any good… a manager who talks around a difficulty leaves the difficulty exactly where he found it, with interest."
- **Ordinary business (1925 season):** "opened wet and stayed wet into June, and the hauling was heavy on the horses and heavier on the men. The creamery at Larrow Green ran through the flush without a day lost, which is more than the creameries below us can say. Our butter has held its place with the trade that buys it, our churn room is clean, our ice went into the house early and lasted, and I have no complaint of any man on the payroll. The skimming station at Ostrey Hollow, seven miles up the valley, ran its season out and its cream came down to us daily and sweet."
- **The petition and board:** "in January of last year, 1925, the patrons of the Ostrey Hollow station laid a petition before this Association… a board of arbitration was constituted upon it… sat at Ashlin under Mr. Alonzo Frick, an able and impartial man… heard everybody who wished to be heard, at length, and at some cost in time to men who could ill spare it in the spring." "The board found no cause established. It found no cause established against the station, against the office, or against any officer or servant of this Association, and I ask you to sit with that sentence a moment, because it is the only sentence in this business that was arrived at by men under oath with the books in front of them."
- **The award:** "Having found no cause established, that board nevertheless awarded the Ostrey Hollow patrons thirty-eight hundred dollars, without admission, and charged it to the station account. I supported the payment then and I support it now. It was, I say to you frankly, a generous settlement of an unproved claim. This Association paid thirty-eight hundred dollars rather than let a body of its own members go on believing they had been used badly by their neighbors. There is not a business house in Ordell County that would have done it. We did it because we are not a business house; we are the farmers of this valley in company together, and a company that will not spend money to keep peace among its own members has not understood what it is for."
- **His cause:** "But I will not have peace bought a second time… **Water was going into cans at the head of that valley and it stopped when the Association began to look. The rise of 1924 is the measure of it.** For a generation the milk brought to that station tested below the milk brought to Larrow Green, and every year we were told it was the wet hay, or the tester, or the office, or the weather. Then the Association's attention came to rest on the station, and there was a petition, and a hearing, and men were required to answer questions in front of their neighbors. **And in the very next season the tests at that station rose by twenty-one hundredths and the average for 1924 stood at 3.90**, which is as good a figure as any station in this county will show you. Nothing was done to those cows. Nothing was done to that bottom land. Nobody changed their feed and nobody changed their pasture. The tests came up when the looking began, and any man who wants a different explanation of that is welcome to stand where I am standing and give us one."
- **On the state man:** "I am told, and I have been told it kindly and by men who mean well, that the state man's visit of that period explains the whole thing. It does not. He came through the county as he came through it every year or two, he tried our scales against his weights and found them true, and **he condemned the station's testing apparatus on the twelfth of July, 1923, and told us to order new. We ordered new inside the month and had it in use before the flush was over.** Apparatus wears. A station in daily use since 1896 will wear its fittings out twice over in that time, and it is the state man's business to say so before they fail him, and ours to replace them when he does. I decline to build a mystery on a piece of housekeeping that appears in our own order book with a date and a price against it."
- "I am aware that this is not a comfortable thing to say to a room with Ostrey Hollow men in it, and I say it without naming a name, because I do not know a name and I would not use it if I did. A little water in a can is not a felony in a man's own mind. It is a way of making a hard year come out even, and the man who does it tells himself he will stop when the price improves. I have no wish to see anybody prosecuted, and nobody has been or will be. I wish only to have it understood, once, in the hearing of the whole membership, why that station's figures were what they were and why they are what they are now."
- **The 1919 circular:** "It follows that I defend the circular I sent to the patrons of that station in 1919, which has been thrown at me a good deal in the last two years. I wrote it because it was my duty to write it. It warned the patrons of Ostrey Hollow, in temperate language, that added water would be looked for and would be dealt with. It named nobody. It accused nobody. It said that this Association pays for butterfat and cannot pay for creek water, and I would write every word of it again tomorrow. A manager who sees his figures going one way for twenty years and says nothing to the men who make them is not being kind to them; he is being idle at their expense."
- **His father's Jerseys:** "My father, who was your first president and who wrote the by-law under which every patron in this Association is paid on the test of the station where he delivers, kept a herd of Jerseys at the home place, as some of you remember, and I kept them after him. **In 1910 that herd tested 4.56.** I do not bring that forward to boast of the cows; they are gone now and the ones I have are no better than yours. I bring it forward because it settles a thing that gets said about this county in the trade below us — that the Larrow Valley is poor milk country, that our land is sour and our cattle are indifferent and our figures are what a man must expect off ground like ours. That is not true. There is no better milk in the state than the milk this valley can produce when it comes to the platform as it left the cow, and the proof of it stood in my own barn."
- **Close:** "That is what I want from every man in this room in the coming season. Bring it as it left the cow. Wash the cans, cool the milk, keep the dog out of the milk house, and bring it as it left the cow. Do that, and the Ostrey Hollow tests will stay where they went in 1924, and this business will be dead and buried where it belongs, and your manager can stand up here next spring and talk to you about butter prices, which is what he is paid for. I thank you for thirteen years of your patience and I ask for one more."
- Never mentions Ansel Keddie by name, his dismissal (30 April 1925), the new tester, Strawn's "no added water" findings, or the 1911 borrowed-measure episode.

## CONFLICTS AND ODDITIES (between and within sources)

1. **Award amount**: Vira — **$3,500** ("thirty-five hundred… split up nine ways"). Effie (recorded it herself), Teague (entered it), Ivy, Nym (Item 7), Selby (1926) — **$3,800**.
2. **Selby Vose's relation to Gideon**: Vira — "old Gideon Vose's **nephew**." Effie — "**son** of the late president." **Selby himself (1926) — "My father, who was your first president."** Two sources (including Selby) for son; Vira alone for nephew.
3. **Total milk**: Effie (copying Teague's sheets), Ivy, Nym (Item 6) — **40,000,000 lb** (1,600,000/yr) for 1898–1922. Teague himself (1948) — **44,000,000 lb** "over the years the account ran."
4. **Average test**: Keddie's leaf, Ivy, Nym — recorded **3.61** (true 3.80). Teague — **3.16** (looks like transposed digits).
5. **Duration of the 1911 borrowed measure**: Keddie (dated entries 8 May–10 July) and Ivy — **nine weeks**. Teague — **six weeks**. Teague also says their own "came up from town"; Keddie says "our own measure in use again" (the original, repaired/returned?) — Teague booked "a small charge for replacement."
6. **Where the 1896 borrowed glass came from**: Keddie's letter — "borrowed off the **Ashlin** creamery"; his own first leaf — "Glass borrowed of **Larrow Green**." (Internal contradiction within Source 3.) Nym's office file: "Larrow Green having lent until then." Ivy says the 1911 borrowed measure came "from Larrow Green with the cream wagon."
7. **Station-gain percentage base**: Teague says 5¼% "of the fat that was actually in the cream" — but 3,040/60,800 = 5.0%, while 3,040/57,760 = 5.26%. Ivy and Nym explain 5¼% is of fat *credited*, 5% of fat *delivered* ("each is right of its own denominator and of no other"). Teague's own description of his base is wrong.
8. **Teague's 1898 entry vs. 1905 start**: he says he invented the line "when I came in 1905," yet quotes "the entry for 1898," in his terms, with "as usual" already in it. Nym confirms "the station column runs from 1897; each year carries an entry in one form." Either Teague back-entered earlier years or the dating is confused.
9. **Dismissal date vs. board dates**: Keddie put out **30 April 1925**; Effie says the board sat until **4 May 1925** and she recorded the award on the last day. Keddie was dismissed before the award was given. Selby (1926) says the board found no cause "against any officer or servant of this Association" and never mentions the dismissal.
10. **Vira's chronology and causation**: she orders events as (a) tests went up one summer, (b) hearing the winter and spring after, (c) Keddie put out — then says his removal "is why the numbers went right the summer after." By the other sources the rise came in 1923 (crate condemned June 1923; 1924 average 3.80), before the hearing (1925) and dismissal. Her explanation ("he was taking the cream") is refuted by Bulletin 471 — and Strawn's 1912 entry notes cream was shipped down the valley the same day it was separated.
11. **Vira on Keddie's start**: "went up there in ninety-five, the year before they opened it… and he tested that first summer too" — the book begins 4 May 1896; "that first summer" is ambiguous (1895 or 1896).
12. **"Nine families" vs "seven farms" vs "seven + three"**: nine families hauled to the station / nine got the 1919 letter (Vira, Keddie); seven farms had rights on Hessel Bottom (Effie); Strawn (1909) says the station "takes from the farms of the wet bottom below it and from three places on the hill." Seven Bottom farms plus three hill places would be ten, not nine (unless not every farm with rights on the Bottom hauled milk). Not necessarily contradictory, but a reader may be asked. Brant's 200,000 lb (1910) is one-eighth of the station's ~1,600,000 lb/yr.
13. **"A thousand cans a week" (Vira) vs "thirty-one times a week" (Keddie)** — cans weighed vs. composite tests made; different things, but a reader may be asked.
14. **Span of the anomaly**: Ivy — "twenty-six-year anomaly" and Bulletin 471's 1 May 1897–12 June 1923; Effie — "twenty-five years over which the patrons laid their claim"; Ivy's arithmetic and Nym — 25 full delivery years 1898–1922; Keddie — 29 seasons 1896–1924; Selby — "for a generation," "twenty years."
15. **Office location**: Teague places the office at Ashlin; Effie mentions an office move in 1938 without saying from/to; Vira has Selby Vose "down at Larrow Green" and the hearing "in an office down there" at Ashlin; Nym: "the Association's office at Ashlin from 1925 to 1959."
16. **Who condemned the glass**: Effie — "the state's inspector" (June 1923); Bulletin 471 — 12 June 1923. CONFIRMED by Strawn's own entry dated "1923, June 12" (and Nym's photostat, Item 9): he ran the pipettes against the burette, "condemned the lot, the whole crate of them, and told the manager to order new." The "state man"/"state's inspector" = Merle Strawn. **Selby alone dates it "the twelfth of July, 1923"** (see 40).
17. **The 1919 letter's signature**: Vira — "over the manager's signature… Selby Vose"; Keddie — "a circular went out from the Association… any man who put his name to that paper"; Strawn — "the manager of the Association has sent a circular round… about water in the cans." Strawn told the manager in 1919 that he had never found added water at the station. **Selby (1926) confirms: "the circular I sent… I wrote it."**
18. **The three explanations argued in 1925** (Effie doesn't name them): from other sources they appear to be (i) wet hay of the Bottom (1908 committee), (ii) water in the cans (1919 circular), (iii) cream taken by the tester (Teague's "cream is portable"). None accepted. Inference, not stated. Selby's list of what "we were told it was": "the wet hay, or the tester, or the office, or the weather." Strawn's books (lactometer "no added water" at every visit) refute (ii); Bulletin 214 refutes (i); Bulletin 471 refutes (iii). Selby in 1926 nevertheless asserts (ii).
19. **Effie's award paradox**: "finds no cause established" yet awards $3,800 — noted by Effie, Keddie ("both of those things are true at once"), Teague, and Selby ("Having found no cause established, that board nevertheless awarded… a generous settlement of an unproved claim").
20. **Ivy's relation via Vira**: Vira calls the 1958 visitor "Hazel Brant's first cousin once removed… of the family both sides." Ivy is Ansel and Orra Keddie's granddaughter, hence Emmet Brant's grand-niece. "Hazel Brant" appears elsewhere only in Nym (held the weigh book 1962–63).
21. **Teague's "Mr. Vose" in 1916**: Selby (manager from 1913), not Gideon (president to 1911, died 1917) — not stated which. (Strawn's 1916 entry — the tester complaining that patrons blame him — is the same year.)
22. **Consistency check that holds**: 1,600,000 lb × 3.61% = 57,760 lb; × 3.80% = 60,800 lb — Teague's 1898 line matches Ivy's rates exactly. 1896's 3.80 average on borrowed glass matches the later "true" figure. Strawn's 1924 "Their book gives the year at 3.80" matches Keddie and Ivy. Nym's invoice arithmetic checks to $77.93. Brant's 3.42 ÷ 0.95 = 3.60 (short 0.18) and 4.56 ÷ 0.95 = 4.80 (short 0.24) match Ivy's two examples exactly.
23. **Effie's 1938 Selby Vose end / office move** both 1938 — coincidence noted, not explained.
24. **Vira says Keddie died "in the thirties"**; Ivy — 1934. (Strawn's 1934 entry: the book "not in the hand it used to be kept in" — the hand had already changed in 1925.)
25. **Strawn's start year**: Strawn — "took the Ordell circuit in nineteen hundred and seven"; first entry 1907. Ivy — "state dairy inspector for the county from 1908." Also Ivy says "for the county," Strawn says four counties.
26. **Strawn's book count**: "twenty-eight of them, one to the year" — but 1907 through 1936 inclusive is thirty years. (The extracts show entries for only twenty distinct years.) Oddity.
27. **Where Strawn's books went**: Strawn (1936) — to the state dairy office with his copy. Ivy — in the Farland college library since 1947 ("eleven years in which nobody put a question to them" = 1947–1958). Nym reconciles: state dairy office 1936 → library of the State Agricultural College at Farland 1947 → 1923 volume found and copied 1958. Note 1936→1947 is also eleven years.
28. **Strawn's June 1911 visit fell inside the borrowed-measure period (8 May–10 July 1911)**: he "watched a test through" and wrote "Nothing here to note." He saw a test made with the borrowed (true) measure and noticed nothing; likewise in 1907 he watched two tests with the short glass and noticed nothing — the shortfall was invisible to the eye and only the burette (1921 onward) could show it.
29. **The 1922 non-check**: Strawn had the burette from spring 1921 but did not run the Ostrey Hollow glass until 12 June 1923 ("I had carried the burette two seasons before this check"); he expressly records skipping it in 1922 "the day being short." Had he run it in 1922 the discovery would have come a year earlier.
30. **Gap between condemnation and new glass**: Strawn condemned the pipettes 12 June 1923; the Association ordered replacements 28 June (Nym Item 10); Ammon & Sons acknowledged 2 July, promising shipment that month; new glass "in use since July" (Strawn, October 1923). What the station tested with between 12 June and July 1923 is not stated. Bulletin 471 ends the short-pipette era at 12 June 1923. Selby: "ordered new inside the month and had it in use before the flush was over."
31. **The manager knew before the hearing**: Strawn told the manager in 1919 that no water was ever found, and in June 1923 that the measures "will not deliver their mark" and to order new; the Association's own order book carries the replacement "with a date and a price against it" (Selby) — yet Effie says the crate was "never mentioned in the hearing at all… Not a word of it was spoken in any of the three sessions" (March–May 1925). In 1926 Selby explicitly dismisses the condemnation as "a piece of housekeeping" and "apparatus wears." Strawn also says "I did not weigh what it has cost them" — he never computed the loss.
32. **The loft "full since before the war"** (Strawn 1928, quoting the station men) vs. Ivy: the crate was carried up in June 1923 and found "at the back under a tarpaulin" in 1958; Vira: fence wire and salt kept in the shut station by 1958. Not contradictory (the loft was already a lumber room before 1923), but note "before the war" presumably means before 1914–18. (See also 39 — Nym puts the crate's finding at the Ashlin office.)
33. **Strawn on the new tester (fall 1925)**: "New man at the tester's bench this year… quick, and quick is not the thing I look for" — contrasts with his 1907 note on Keddie ("works slow and clean"). Consistent with Keddie's "I did not test a pound in the season of 1925" and dismissal 30 April 1925. The new tester is unnamed in every source; Selby (1926) does not mention him.
34. **Strawn 1913 "new manager… the last one never did"**: consistent with Selby Vose becoming manager in 1913 (Effie; Selby: "thirteen years this spring, since 1913"), and implies a prior manager (pre-1913) whom no source names. Whether Gideon Vose (president to 1911) was also manager is not stated anywhere.
35. **Strawn's 1912 "cream shipped down the valley the same day it is separated"** bears on Teague's "cream is portable" theory; Strawn draws no conclusion. Selby 1926: "its cream came down to us daily and sweet."
36. **Station still running in 1936** (Strawn's last round: scales true at Ostrey Hollow) — vs. Vira's shut station by 1958 and Effie's 1938 end of Selby Vose's management; when the station closed is not stated by anyone.
37. **Noise caution (not a story conflict)**: the unrelated store ledger carries a recurring marginal note "The scale was checked against the county standard and found true" (many dates 1893–1957, including years such as 1919, 1923, 1925, 1929, 1933, 1934, 1936, 1938, 1940, 1941, 1943, 1944, 1946, 1947, 1951, 1952, 1953, 1954, 1956). It is a shopkeeper's scale, not the Ostrey Hollow intake scale; only Strawn's "Scales true" entries (1907–1936) and Selby's "tried our scales against his weights and found them true" bear on the station. Likewise the ledger's "Store closed at noon for a funeral," "A dispute over an old charge was settled by splitting the difference," "Settled an old account carried over from the spring," and "The clerk misfigured a bill" are store events, not the Keddie death, the 1925 award, or any Association bookkeeping. The Cold Spring Water-Cure's "liniment compounded on the premises" and the store's "bottles liniment" are likewise unrelated to anything in the story.
38. **NEW — Nym's transposed keepers**: Item 3 (station test book) is headed "kept by E. Brant"; Item 4 (farm weigh book) is headed "kept by A. Keddie." Every other source, and Nym's own details, have it the other way: the test book's first leaf is signed "A. Keddie," Item 8 (Keddie's statement) is "in the hand of Item 3," and the weigh book was "begun at Hessel Bottom… kept there by one household throughout; to Roy Brant in 1929." The headings are evidently swapped; a reader asked "who kept the test book per Nym's heading" must answer "E. Brant" while noting the contradiction.
39. **NEW — Where the crate was found (February 1958)**: Ivy (1959) — in the station loft over the intake room at Ostrey Hollow, up a ladder, under a tarpaulin at the back, "carried up June 1923" (supported by Effie's "set aside," Strawn's 1928 loft note, and Vira's account of the 1958 visitor poking about the shut station). Nym (1972, Item 5) — "found in the Association office at Ashlin, in February 1958, by Miss Ivy Keddie."
40. **NEW — Date of the condemnation**: Strawn's own dated entry, Bulletin 471, Nym — **12 June 1923**. Selby Vose (1926) — "**the twelfth of July, 1923**." Nym's Item 10 (order of 28 June 1923; supplier's letter 2 July) is impossible on Selby's date; Selby also says "we ordered new inside the month."
41. **NEW — The 1924 figure and the size of the rise**: Keddie (1924: 3.80), Strawn ("Their book gives the year at 3.80"), Ivy (3.61→3.80, gap 0.19) — vs. Selby: "rose by twenty-one hundredths and the average for 1924 stood at **3.90**." By every other source the rise came in summer 1923 (new glass) and the 1924 average was already 3.80 before the petition. He also says the tests "stopped when the Association began to look," though Strawn had been testing for water at every visit since 1907.
43. **NEW — Selby's explanation vs. the evidence**: Selby (1926): "Water was going into cans at the head of that valley"; Strawn's lactometer found "no added water" at every Ostrey Hollow visit (1912, 1916, 1919, 1920 ×2, 1922, 12 June 1923 "as always here," 1924, 1925, 1928, 1934) and he told the manager so in 1919; Bulletin 471: "No milk was watered at Ostrey Hollow." Selby knows of the condemnation and dismisses it as wear ("Apparatus wears… I decline to build a mystery on a piece of housekeeping"). Selby names no one ("I do not know a name and I would not use it if I did"); Vira's theory (cream taken by the tester) and Selby's (water added by patrons) are the two folk explanations, both refuted.
44. **NEW — Selby on the state man's frequency**: "he came through the county as he came through it every year or two"; Strawn's extracts show Ostrey Hollow visited in most years and twice in 1920.
45. **NEW — Test-book columns**: Keddie — five (date/patron/pounds/test/fat); Nym Item 3 — "columns for date, patron, pounds and test" (four).
46. **NEW — Alder Corners figures**: Ivy — fourteen overlapping seasons, the milk of four Hessel Bottom men in summer flush, copied by Rosalie Cudd (runs the factory; Jerome Cudd's niece); Nym Item 13 — figures "for the season of 1919" only, and the book "passed from its founder to his daughter in 1941 and to the Vessey County Historical Society in 1970." Niece vs daughter; fourteen seasons vs one; and 1919 is the circular year. Both agree the book never left Vessey County.
47. **NEW — Hazel Brant**: Vira — the 1958 visitor's "first cousin once removed"; Nym — "Miss Hazel Brant" held the Brant weigh book 1962–63 and it came to the Rooms from her. Her place in the Brant family (Roy's daughter? sister?) is never stated.
48. **NEW — Ivy's two example herds identified**: Ivy said "a herd recorded 4.56 was short 0.24; one recorded 3.42 short 0.18." Nym's weigh-book leaf gives the Brant herd's 1910 test as 3.42; Selby gives his father's Jerseys' 1910 test as 4.56. Oddity: Ivy's 4.56 herd is implicitly an Ostrey Hollow patron (tested with the short glass), but the Vose "home place" belongs to a Larrow Green family whose milk would presumably go to the Larrow Green creamery (true glass). Where the Vose herd's milk was tested is not stated; either it hauled to Ostrey Hollow or Ivy's 4.56 is a different herd.
49. **NEW — Article VII's date**: Effie — adopted at the 1892 annual meeting (and Article VII "was written in 1892 by a man who died in 1917"); Nym — the printed copies "carry no year on their face, only the words 'adopted at the annual meeting.'"
50. **NEW — The 1908 sampling**: Keddie — "sampled the herds through the winter"; Nym — "January, February and March 1908 only, as the report states." The report says "The milk is whole and the scales are honest" — i.e., the 1908 committee itself found no watering.
51. **NEW — Ivy's college**: Nym — "Miss Ivy Keddie of the State Agricultural College at Farland"; Vira — "the college away east"; Ivy — "Farland." So Ivy's college = the State Agricultural College that issued Bulletins 214 and 471 (Nym makes this explicit; Ivy never quite says so).
52. **NEW — The test book's custody**: Ivy — "in the Association's office since 1925"; Nym — "impounded by the board of arbitration in March 1925; in the Association's office at Ashlin from 1925 to 1959; received here in 1959." Consistent. Since the Rooms' book is "one hand throughout, twenty-nine seasons," the book Strawn saw "kept up… not in the hand it used to be kept in" (1934) must be a later volume (inference).
53. **NEW — Nym's pipettes "two chipped at the tip"** vs Ivy's "nine unbroken." Minor.
54. **NEW — Larrow Green's average**: Nym Item 16 — "a little under two tenths of a point better" than the station's 3.61 (≈ the 0.19 gap); Teague — "Down at Larrow Green we did better than that"; Selby — "tested below the milk brought to Larrow Green."
55. **NEW — 1896 intake**: Nym — the station's first-season intake (4 May–end of 1896) "is not in the ledgers and is nowhere else… there is none," though the 1896 average (3.80) is on the summary leaf.
56. **NEW — Selby's "thirteen years… I ask for one more"** (1926) vs Effie's 1913–1938 (he served twelve more).
57. **NEW — Selby says the board "heard everybody who wished to be heard"**; Keddie says no patron or officer ever came to the station on a working morning; Effie says none of the officers ever went up.
58. **NEW — Selby's "A station in daily use since 1896"** and "seven miles up the valley" agree with all other sources.
59. **NEW — Emmet Brant's death**: Ivy — Roy "has kept up [the weigh book] since his father died"; Nym — book "to Roy Brant in 1929." So Emmet Brant apparently died (or ceded the book) in 1929 — inference, not stated.
60. **NEW — Invoice detail**: Nym's invoice lists "12 doz. test bottles, 8 per cent neck" — the bottle necks were ruled to 8 per cent; Ivy said "the mark is what the neck of the bottle was ruled for." Ivy's summary of the invoice ("pipettes, bottles, acid measure, hand tester, freight") matches.
