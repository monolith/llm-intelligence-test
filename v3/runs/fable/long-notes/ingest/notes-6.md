# Retention notes — Segment 6 (Retellings 01–11; the Ostrey Hollow skimming-station affair)

Eleven retellings so far, all about the same events: the Larrow Valley Cooperative Creamery Association, its skimming station at Ostrey Hollow, tester Ansel Keddie, the long low butterfat tests there, the 1925 board of arbitration, and the 1958–59 discovery that the station's pipettes were short. Retellings 01–08 (Vira Toland 1979; Effie Loomis 1941; Ansel Keddie 1925; Ivy Keddie 1959; Wilbur Teague 1948; Merle Strawn 1936; Halbert Nym 1972; Selby Vose 1926) are carried forward unchanged. **THREE NEW retellings this segment:** **Retelling 09 — Roy Brant, farmer at Hessel Bottom, account for the county farm bureau's local-history collection, 1955, with postscript 1958** (Source 9); **Retelling 10 — Rosalie Cudd, cheesemaker at Alder Corners, letter to Ivy Keddie, 1958** (Source 10); **Retelling 11 — Leland Vaught, professor of dairy husbandry, introduction and method to Bulletin No. 471, State Agricultural College at Farland, October 1958** (Source 11). All three are set out in full below and merged into cast, places, timeline, conflicts and objects.

## CAST (consolidated across all eleven sources)

**The Keddie family**
- **Ansel Keddie** — tester at the Ostrey Hollow skimming station. Kept the test book in his own hand from the station's opening. Tested 29 seasons, 1896 through 1924; did not test in 1925. Dismissed ("put out") 30 April 1925. Died in the 1930s (Ivy: 1934). "An old man" with "no other trade" in 1925. Vira says he went up in '95 to get the station ready. Strawn (never names him), 1907: "keeps his book in his own hand, and his hand is better than most… works slow and clean and reads his own figure without being coaxed to it"; 1916: "the tester complains that his patrons blame him for their figures. I told him that is the trade and he said he knew it." Nym Item 8: his statement is "One leaf, ruled, both sides, in the hand of Item 3, signed"; the test book is "One hand throughout, twenty-nine seasons" — though Nym's Item 3 heading calls it "kept by E. Brant" and Item 4 (the Brant weigh book) "kept by A. Keddie" (transposed; Conflict 38). Selby Vose (1926) never names him nor mentions his dismissal. Roy Brant (1955): "Ansel Keddie, who kept the station's test book, was married into the family that had more to lose by that station than any other family on the meadow"; Emmet stopped speaking to him in 1919 "because my father held that the man who kept the test book ought to have stood up in front of that circular and did not." Vaught (1958): "a man who did not know what he was recording and set a question mark against it."
- **Orra Keddie** — Ansel's wife; Emmet Brant's sister — Roy: "full brother and sister, same mother and same father, whatever you may hear from people who like a story with a stepmother in it." First cousin of "old Mrs. Vose" (Vira). After 1919 she and her brother never spoke; she walked to Larrow Green rather than pass his lane. Outlived Ansel "a good while."
- **Duncan Keddie** — Ansel and Orra's son ("boy"); taught school at Ashlin; Ivy's father; Roy Brant's first cousin (Roy).
- **Ivy Keddie** — Ansel's granddaughter; college at Farland; gave the 1959 talk "What the Crate Held." Nym: "Miss Ivy Keddie of the State Agricultural College at Farland." Rosalie Cudd addresses her as "Miss Keddie," "a graduate student at Farland," "a young woman from the college who asks [a plain question] in four lines and apologizes for troubling me." Found the crate February 1958 (Nym: Association office at Ashlin; Ivy: station loft); measured nine pipettes at Farland 9 March 1958; gave crate to the Rooms 1959; borrowed the Brant weigh book from Roy Brant in 1958 (Roy's postscript: "Duncan's girl Ivy came out to the place this year and asked whether she might borrow the book for a piece of work she is doing at the college at Farland. I let her have it. She is careful with paper") and returned it; found and copied Strawn's 1923 volume at Farland 1958; wrote to Rosalie Cudd 1958 and received the Alder Corners figures. Vira's 1958 visitor "from the college away east… Hazel Brant's first cousin once removed" is Ivy; **Roy Brant says Hazel and Ivy are second cousins** (Conflict 20/47).

**The Brant family (Hessel Bottom)**
- **Emmet Brant** — **born 1854, died 1929** (Roy). Biggest man on Hessel Bottom: most cows, most hay ground; largest patron every year (Effie); Roy: "Ours was the largest of the six — sixty-odd acres of the meadow, and thirty-eight cows in a good year, which for this valley was a herd." Orra Keddie's full brother. Roy: "From the first of January, 1898, until the week before he died, he wrote down every can of milk that went out of this barn" (Nym/Ivy: from 1 January 1897 — Conflict 61). Wrote "at the kitchen table after the second milking, in the lamp, with his hat still on, and my mother would not have the table cleared till he was done." Among the first to say out loud (1902) that the milk tested low — "and they were laughed at, and one of them was my father." Read the 1908 report twice, put it in the drawer, "said that the land had been wet since the flood and the tests had not." Noticed the 1911 rise and fall and entered both; "could make nothing of it." "One who never forgave" the 1919 paper. 1910 leaf: 200,000 lb even, "Paid on a test of 3.42"; he wrote "even" and underlined it, "which for him was carrying on."
- **Roy Brant** — Emmet's son; **sixty-seven in winter 1955 (born c. 1888), "still milking"**; wrote Retelling 09 in longhand at Hessel Bottom for the county farm bureau, postscript 1958. Kept up the weigh book since his father died, "in the same book until it was filled and then in another, in the same way." Cut the meadow "with a scythe and with a mower behind two horses." Did not haul to Alder Corners ("We had the biggest herd on the meadow, and the biggest herd is the one that cannot afford to be talked about, and I have thought since that this was a poor reason and cost us money"). Duncan Keddie's first cousin. Lent the book to Ivy 1958. "It goes to Hazel when I am done with it." Nym: book to Roy 1929; to Hazel 1962.
- **Hazel Brant** — **Roy's daughter** (Roy: "My daughter Hazel and his daughter Ivy are second cousins… both of them have had to have it explained to them by me more than once"). Vira: the 1958 visitor's "first cousin once removed." Nym: "Miss Hazel Brant" held the weigh book from 1962; received at the Rooms 1963.

Roy (1955): "In 1919 the manager sent a paper round to the Hollow patrons warning us against putting water in the milk."

## PLACES
- **Larrow Valley**; **Larrow Green** — hamlet; Association organized 1889; main creamery built 1891 on Larrow Creek. Strawn inspected 1913, 1917, 1931, 1936. Nym Item 16: Larrow Green average "a little under two tenths of a point better" than the station's 3.61. Roy: the Hollow milk ran "seventeen hundredths of a point under what the same class of milk was making down at Larrow Green." Nyle Grigg was "of Larrow Green."
- **Ostrey Hollow** — seven miles up the valley; station at the head of the Hollow where the road bends to the ridge; low whitewashed building; intake room, loft over it (ladder), wash room, cold room, boiler, separator; shut by 1958 (fence wire and salt). Strawn 1909: takes from the wet bottom below and three hill places. Vaught: "one skimming station in Ordell County… below those of its own creamery seven miles down the same valley."
- **Hessel Bottom** — Roy: "a hundred and sixty acres of wet meadow lying under the hill at Ostrey Hollow. **Six farms** had rights on it and cut hay off it and pastured it" (Effie: seven farms). Brant's sixty-odd acres of it. "The water stands on the low end into May, and there is a soft place by the alders that has taken a horse to the hock in my time and my father's. The hay off it is coarse, and it cuts late, and a cow will eat it and do well enough… it never failed to grow something." Jerome Cudd: "Good milk off wet ground."
- **Ashlin** — Association office (over a harness shop); board of arbitration 1925; Duncan Keddie taught school there; Historical Rooms/Society; Nym: crate found there Feb 1958.
- **Ordell County / Ordell County Historical Rooms / the Ordell circuit** (four counties, fifty-odd plants).
- **Redlow Ridge** — Rosalie: "Four miles of switchback, cut for a lumber road and never much improved since. It shuts in November and it does not open before April, some years May… the drifts sit in the cut below the top." Cloud "sits on the top of the ridge till the sun is well up."
- **Alder Corners, Vessey County** — the Alder Corners Cheese Company, built 1913 (Rosalie) / opened 1914 (Roy), "on the flat below the church"; spring water on the lot; hard road out to the east; make room; "little room off the intake" (Jerome's test bench); platform with scale. Its book never left Vessey County (Nym); to the Vessey County Historical Society 1970.
- **Farland** — the State Agricultural College (Ivy's; Vaught's; Strawn's notebooks in its library from 1947; pipettes measured there). Vaught calls it "This station."
- **Tarnet** — Ammon & Sons.
- **"the home place"** — Vose Jerseys.
- **"the creameries below us" / "the trade below us"** — Selby's phrases.

## SOURCE 1 — Vira Toland (recorded at Ostrey Hollow, 1979)
Tape at her kitchen table by "a young man from the county who asked three questions in an hour"; transcribed as spoken.
- Born 1912, top end of Ostrey Hollow, house her father's father put up; two years at Ashlin when first married.
- Father hauled milk; **nine families**; "in and out of each other's kitchens."
- **Ansel Keddie ran it**; went up in '95; "tested that first summer too."
- The book: ruled with his own straightedge, figures "like print," slope desk by the window; read off any man's day; "a thousand cans a week and never a blot"; father: "there would be no lawyers in Ashlin."
- **1919**: "the year everything went sour"; she was seven; printed letter over the manager's signature: "water in the cans coming down from Ostrey Hollow"; nine families; father read it twice with his hat on; mother at the stove with her back turned; letter in the front-room desk drawer; brother has it; one woman pinned hers inside her trunk lid; "forty years to prove what was said of them." It rained that day.
- Signer **Selby Vose, old Gideon Vose's nephew**; "the family always thought the valley belonged to them a little." **Orra Keddie and old Mrs. Vose first cousins.**
- **Orra** = Ansel's wife, Emmet Brant's sister; Emmet the biggest man on the Bottom, "the one that letter cost the most"; Sunday dinners before; after, "not one word, not at the store, not at a funeral"; Orra walked to Larrow Green rather than pass his lane.
- "All at once, one summer, the figures came up and stayed up, and nobody ever said why."
- Hearing at Ashlin "the winter and spring after"; "they put Ansel Keddie out"; **"paid the patrons thirty-five hundred dollars… my father's share of it did not buy a hay rake."**
- Her verdict: "He was taking the cream… nobody ever came up that road and looked." Father: "an honest man who had got into something."
- Keddie died in the thirties "still under it"; Orra outlived him.
- 1958 visitor: young woman from the college away east; "Hazel Brant's first cousin once removed… of the family both sides"; told her of the letter.
- "I have never in sixty years been able to think about the one without the other."

## SOURCE 2 — Effie Loomis, "Notes toward a history of the Association" (1941)
1. Organized 1889; Gideon Vose president 1889–1911; she 14 in 1889; secretary 1901; 1889–92 book lost 1938.
2. Creamery 1891 on Larrow Creek; before it "every man's arithmetic was his own."
3. **Article VII** (quoted): *"Every patron shall be paid for the milk he delivers according to the number of pounds of butter fat contained in it, ascertained by test, and not according to the weight of the milk; and the test of the station where he delivers shall govern."* Adopted 1892. "That clause is the hinge of the whole Ostrey Hollow business."

## SOURCE 11 — Leland Vaught, introduction and method to Bulletin No. 471 (State Agricultural College at Farland, October 1958) — NEW
*"The opening and method sections of the bulletin, written for extension agents and county workers, closing with the bulletin's own concluding paragraph reproduced entire."* Signed "— L. VAUGHT."
- **§I Why written**: "This station has been asked, at intervals for fifty years, why the butterfat tests at one skimming station in Ordell County ran below those of its own creamery seven miles down the same valley… answered at various times by reference to the land and by reference to the men. It is answered here by reference to a piece of glass." "Nobody in that valley lacked the intelligence to find it. What they lacked was any occasion to suspect the one article in the plant that nobody in the trade is in the habit of suspecting." Bold: **"the milk pipette is the one instrument in the butterfat test which the plant cannot check against anything else, and it is the instrument on which every figure in the plant depends."**
- **§II The instrument**: four operations — measured charge into a test bottle; sulfuric acid; spun in a hand tester; fat read directly in percent in the graduated neck. "The operator weighs nothing." Standard charge **17.6 c.c.** Short pipette → reading "low in the exact proportion by which the charge was short." Three consequences: **proportional not additive** (rich milk loses more; ranking of herds undisturbed; "Only against the outside world is it wrong"); **precise** ("A pipette that delivers 16.27 c.c. where it is blown 17.6 delivers 16.27 c.c. every time, for a quarter of a century, in January and in July"); **invisible from inside the plant** (scales checked with weights, tester's speed counted, acid checked, bottle graduation checked with mercury, but "the pipette is the standard… It can be caught only by a burette brought in from outside, and burettes reached the state's field men very late in this history").
- **§III Material**: "Of the lot bought for the Ostrey Hollow station in 1897 — **five dozen** pipettes were purchased — **nine** were recovered from the station's condemned crate, unbroken and legible… stems are blown 17.6 c.c… five runs to each pipette, and the mean delivery of the nine is **16.27 c.c.**" "The station's own glass went into use on **the first of May, 1898**, and remained in use until the state inspector condemned the lot in June of 1923." Method: water at room temperature, tip wiped, delivered in working manner into a certified burette, five deliveries averaged; "occupies an afternoon"; "no reason why every station in this state should not be checked once in this way."
- **§IV 1911**: nine weeks; "One variable changed"; 3.61 → 3.80 and back "on the day the station's own measure came back into service"; recorded "by a man who did not know what he was recording and set a question mark against it." "an experiment… that ran itself… and lay unread for forty-seven years."
- **§V Magnitude**: 16.27 ÷ 17.6 = **0.9244**; 3.61 ÷ 0.9244 = **3.905**, shortfall **0.295**; on 40,000,000 lb over twenty-five years, **118,000 lb** "delivered and not credited." "It should be said that this figure is not readily reconciled with the station's own average of 3.80 in the year following the replacement of the glassware." Station gain "five and a quarter percent of the butterfat actually delivered."
- **§VI Feed**: Bulletin No. 214 quoted (identical); "Five hundredths in winter; nothing at all in summer. The discrepancy… is present in every month of the year and is some six times the largest effect the trial could produce."
- **§VII Conclusion (quoted)**: *"No milk was watered at Ostrey Hollow and no cream was stolen there. From the first of May, 1897, to the twelfth of June, 1923, the station's tests were low by one part in twenty because its pipettes were short by one part in twenty, and the difference — seventy-six thousand pounds of butter fat — stands credited in the Association's own ledgers, year by year, as station gain."*
