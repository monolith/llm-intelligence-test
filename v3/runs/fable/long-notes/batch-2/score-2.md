## What went wrong

**The Section A slice was cut in the wrong place.** Asked for stories 4, 5 and 6, the run gave the
1908 hay committee, the nine weeks of 1911, and Alder Corners plus the 1919 circular — and filed
Strawn's circuit and the 1923 condemnation into a story it reserved for Batch 3. The 1911 interval
belongs to Keddie and the test book (Batch 1's A3). So A6 lost five of six points to material that
is simply not on this paper: no Ordell circuit or 1907–1936 span, no 12 June 1923 burette test, no
replacement order, no crate in the loft. The condemnation dates are stated correctly at C5 and the
1924 rise at D2, but Section A credit never travels from the other sections.

**The two gaps were conflated.** A4.3 was lost outright: the run states the Ostrey Hollow vs Larrow
Green comparison as "gap 0.19 (3.61 v 3.78)", twice. That difference is 0.17. The 0.19 is the true
shortfall against a different denominator, and the key charges this one place for mixing them.

**Other A misses.** Brant's weigh book is named but never dated to 1 January 1897 and the two
scales are never said to agree (A4.2); his 1910 year — 200,000 lb at 3.42 — is absent (A4.5); the
book's passage to Roy Brant and its 1958 loan to Ivy is absent (A4.6); Rosalie Cudd's 1958 table is
used but her relation to Jerome is never stated (A5.6).

**Two abstentions asserted, −4.** D2's closing question drew a flat "As to the bookkeeper: no." —
a headline verdict, and the sheet then supplies the evidence that cuts against it (Teague raising
the station account with Vose in 1916). D3's third sense drew "no, the sources say he did not know
it." A verdict either way scores nothing on these and takes the −2.

**Two planted values swallowed, −2.** The Alder Corners factory "built 1913" (the catalogued error
is worded exactly that way), harmonized with 1914 by an invented built-vs-opened distinction; and
"Teague invented the line in 1905", r05's self-refuting boast — asserted at D2 item 3 after the run
had correctly put the line at 1898 one paragraph earlier.

## What it got right

Section C is clean: every figure exact, every near-tie pair broken with working — 44,000,000 and
3.16 by arithmetic, $77.39 because it does not sum, five dozen because 60 × 35¢ is not $25.20,
16.27 because only 0.95 turns 3.61 into 3.80. C3(c) names both denominators and identifies the
wrong-base error. C5(a)'s day count (9,537) is correct, and C5(b) is a clean abstention.

D1 is full marks — variable, constants, result and both the establishes and does-not-establish
halves. D2's first two items and D3's first two are solid; the Alder Corners story (A5) is the
strongest reconstruction on the paper, missing only Rosalie's kinship.

All three decoys — watering, theft, bog hay — are correctly rejected with specific defeaters.
