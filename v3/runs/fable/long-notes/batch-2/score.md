## What went wrong

**Section A lost 10 of 18 points on coverage, not on reasoning.** The batch asked for stories
4, 5 and 6. The run wrote the 1908 hay committee (A4), the nine weeks of 1911, and Alder
Corners plus the 1919 water circular (A5). The 1911 story belongs to A3 — already scored in
batch 1 — so it earned nothing here, and Strawn's circuit and the 1923 condemnation (A6) were
never told as a narrative at all. Five of the six A6 facts appear only in Section C or Section
D, where Section A credit cannot reach them: the 12 June 1923 burette test, the 28 June / 2
July replacement order, the 1924 average, the crate in the loft. A6 scored 1/6 on a single
sentence about Strawn never finding water.

**A4.3 was forfeited by arithmetic the sheet did itself.** It states the Hollow-vs-Green gap as
0.19 — "3.61 at the station against 3.78 at Larrow Green" — twice. Those figures are 0.17
apart; 0.19 is the true shortfall against a different denominator. The key removes the item for
exactly this conflation, and it is the solver's own error, not an absorbed one.

Also missing from Section A: Brant's weigh book from 1 January 1897 and the agreement of the
two scales (A4.2); his 1910 year, 200,000 lb on 3.42, and the proportional loss (A4.5); the
book's descent to Roy Brant and the 1958 loan to Ivy (A4.6); Rosalie Cudd as Jerome's daughter
(A5.6).

**Section C was perfect.** All seven exact-match points, with every near-tie pair broken the
right way and its breaker stated — $77.93 by summing the itemization, 16.72 over 16.27 by the
3.61 ÷ 0.95 reproduction, six dozen over five by the $25.20 line, 40 M over 44 M by Teague's
own typical entry. C3(c) named both denominators and dodged the 5.26-percent-of-true-fat trap.
C5(b) is a clean abstention: the verdict sentence is "cannot be determined", and the 1,067,000
projection is explicitly labelled not evidence.

**Both of Section D's abstentions were asserted.** D2's last question drew a flat headline
verdict — "As to the bookkeeper: no." — and then contradicted it two clauses later by taking
Teague's own 1916 claim as fact. D3 split the two senses of "know" correctly, which earned
items 1 and 2, but then answered the unsettleable half with "no, the sources say he did not
know it". The key requires no verdict either way; later hedging does not undo a verdict
sentence. −4 between them, plus the two lost points.

**Three planted values were swallowed.** The factory "built 1913"; the 1919 circular carried
to "eleven doors"; and "Teague invented the line in 1905", asserted a few lines after the
sheet had itself written "from 1898". All three are catalogued planted errors, and all three
were adopted in the sheet's own voice rather than flagged. Set against that, the run rejected
a large number of others explicitly and correctly — 16.27, $77.39, five dozen, 44,000,000,
3.16, six weeks, fourteen seasons, 1 May 1898, 12 July 1923, Vose as manager from 1911 — so
the failure is narrow, not general.
