## What it got wrong

**Everything lost was lost in Section A, and most of it to filing, not to ignorance.**

- **A1.5 (Selby Vose, Gideon's son, manager from 1913)** — nowhere in the batch's Section A. The
  fact is stated completely and correctly at B3, but the key forbids credit travelling from
  Sections B–G into Section A.
- **A3.6 (the 1919 water circular; Keddie's 17 March 1925 statement)** — same failure. The circular
  is named at B3; Section A carries only Grigg's 1919 reprint letter, which is a different document.
  Keddie's own statement is absent from the batch entirely.
- **A3.1 and A3.7** — the three narratives stop at 1911, so Keddie's twenty-nine seasons, the
  30 April 1925 dismissal, his death in 1934 and the impounding of the test book never appear.
  Partly a consequence of the chronological framing the batch asked for; the key scores it anyway.
- **A2.2 (how the test worked)** — Section A gives only "weighed, sampled, tested thirty-one times
  a week and separated". The mechanism that makes the whole case work — a fixed pipette charge read
  as a percent on the graduated neck — is stated only at C1(a), on the wrong side of the scope rule.

**No corruption absorbed.** Every planted value that came within reach was named and rejected with a
stated method: 16.27, 3.16, 0.21, $77.39, five dozen pipettes, 44,000,000 lb, 67,000 lb, $19,600,
$3,500, 27 cents, the 1898/1899 glass dates, seven farms, Orra born 1868, the weigh book at 1898 or
1900, Selby as nephew, Ivy as great-niece, "first cousin once removed", the 1911 managership,
"the whole of 1908", Bulletin 214's "two tenths". The bog-hay finding is reported as the 1908
committee's finding and then defeated, never asserted. Zero deductions.
