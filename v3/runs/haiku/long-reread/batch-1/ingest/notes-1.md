# Ingest Notes — Segment 1 (Batch 1)

## Three Story Lines Identified

### Story 1: The Ostrey Hollow Butterfat Testing Scandal (1895-1925+)

**Core narrative:** A milk testing station at Ostrey Hollow, run by Ansel Keddie from 1895, reports systematically low butterfat test results from nine patron families in the Larrow Valley. In 1919, an accusation letter is sent claiming "water in the cans." After a hearing in 1925, the patrons receive a settlement of undetermined amount; Ansel is removed from his position.

**Key figures:**
- **Ansel Keddie**: Tester at Ostrey Hollow, 1895–1924. Kept precise records in a handwritten book with 5 columns (date, patron, pounds, test, fat). Claims in his spring 1925 testimony that he never falsified records and tested properly every time. Died in 1930s, carrying the scandal with him.
- **Orra Keddie**: Ansel's wife. Sister of Emmet Brant (the largest patron). First cousin to old Mrs. Vose. After the 1919 letter, refused to speak to her brother for decades.
- **Emmet Brant**: Largest patron at Ostrey Hollow, from Hessel Bottom. Had most cows, most hay ground, most milk delivery. Relationship with Ansel ruptured after 1919 letter. According to Ansel's letter, this separation hurt Emmet most financially.
- **Selby Vose**: Son of late Gideon Vose (first president of Association). Manager from 1913–1938. Signed the 1919 letter accusing Ostrey Hollow patrons of water in cans.
- **Gideon Vose**: Founding president of Association (1889–1911, 22 years). Old Gideon. Dead before 1925 hearing.
- **Vira Toland**: Born 1912 at top of Ostrey Hollow. Daughter of one of nine affected patrons. Recorded in 1979, remembering events from childhood.
- **Effie Loomis**: Secretary of Association 1901–1940. Took minutes throughout the scandal. Source of official record.
- **Alonzo Frick**: Attorney who chaired the board of arbitration at Ashlin, March 17 – May 4, 1925.
- **Hazel Brant**: Related to Emmet Brant (possibly his descendant). In 1958, her first cousin once removed (a college girl from the east) came to Ostrey Hollow asking questions about the scandal.

**Timeline:**

1. **1895-96**: Ansel Keddie arrives at station; borrows glass testing equipment from Ashlin creamery; his official book begins May 4, 1896.
2. **1896-1898**: Early years establish baseline: average test 3.80 (1896), 3.61 (1898).
3. **1902**: First complaints emerge that Ostrey Hollow milk does not test as well as neighbors' milk.
4. **1908**: Association committee visits, samples herds through winter, concludes wet hay of the Bottom thins cream naturally.
5. **1911 (May 8–July 10)**: Critical anomaly. Ansel's pipette breaks May 8; borrowing replacement measure, tests jump to 3.80. When his own measure returns July 10, tests drop back to 3.61 same day. Ansel records question mark; cannot explain.
6. **1919**: Association sends circular letter to nine patron families saying there is water in their milk cans. Vira Toland remembers her father reading it at table with his hat on. Families keep the letter for decades.
7. **1920-1924**: Tests remain at elevated level (per Vira: "went up and stayed up, and nobody ever said why"). Ansel's record shows 1924 average at 3.80. Ansel doesn't test in 1925.
8. **1925 (January)**: Ostrey Hollow patrons petition the Association claiming they have been underpaid for very long time.
9. **1925 (March 17–May 4)**: Hearing at Ashlin under Alonzo Frick. Three explanations presented; none accepted by board. Board finds "no cause established" but awards patrons money "without admission."
10. **1925 (April 30)**: Ansel Keddie put out (fired).
11. **1958**: Young woman (Hazel Brant's first cousin once removed, from college back east) visits Ostrey Hollow asking about the scandal.

**Key documents and measurements:**
- **Ansel's average test record (from his handwritten note in back of book):**
  - 1896: 3.80
  - 1897: partial year
  - 1898–1910: ~3.61 (within three hundredths)
  - 1911 (nine weeks): 3.80 (anomaly)
  - 1912–1924: ~3.61 returns, except 1924 at 3.80
- **Botterfat statistics over 25 affected years (Effie Loomis from ledgers):**
  - Total milk: 40 million pounds
  - Average per year: 1.6 million pounds
  - Average butterfat price: 25 cents per pound
  - Implied total butterfat value: 10 million pounds × $0.25 = $2.5M (order of magnitude check needed)
- **Award amount (CONFLICT):**
  - Vira Toland says: $3,500 split nine ways over all affected years ("didn't buy a hay rake")
  - Effie Loomis (official secretary's record): $3,800
  - Effie's figure preferred (contemporary official record vs. memory after 60+ years)

**Conflicting interpretations:**

1. **What caused the low tests?** Three theories were argued at the hearing but none accepted:
   - Ansel was skimming cream (Vira believes this; Ansel denies)
   - Water was actually in the cans (1919 accusation; Ansel denies he saw it)
   - Local hay was inferior (1908 committee report; not revisited in 1925)

2. **The pipette break in 1911:** Ansel can offer no explanation for why tests jumped when borrowed measure came in, then dropped when his own returned. This is the single oddest fact in his record.

3. **The June 1923 condemned measures:** State inspector condemned a crate of measures in June 1923. New ones ordered. **Crucially, this is NOT mentioned anywhere in the 1925 hearing** (per Effie Loomis). This is a major omission that bears investigation.

4. **Who benefited?** Selby Vose and the Association (by paying less to Ostrey Hollow patrons than they deserved). Who was harmed? The nine patron families, especially Emmet Brant (largest shipper, worst loss). Who carried the shame? Ansel Keddie (put out despite "no cause established") and his wife Orra (cut off from her own brother).

**Narrative uncertainty:**
- Vira suspects Ansel was skimming but says "my father never would say it out loud."
- Ansel's own testimony says he never took cream and that "there is not a man in this room who thinks I have when he is by himself"—implying even his accusers doubted the skimming theory privately.
- Effie's observation is sharp: the board said "no cause established" yet awarded money anyway, which is logically incoherent unless the award was a payoff to silence the question.

**Hazel Brant connection (1958):**
A college girl from the east, Hazel Brant's first cousin once removed, visited Ostrey Hollow in 1958 asking about the scandal. Vira told her about her father's letter. This indicates the Brant family was investigating their own history. No outcome of this investigation is reported in the sources read so far.
