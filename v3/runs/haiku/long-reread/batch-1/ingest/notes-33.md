### Moral Conflict (1919 Rift as Core Story)

The water-contamination circular (June 1919) became a test of integrity. Ansel could have delegated delivery; instead, he chose personal moral responsibility. Emmet interpreted this choice as hypocrisy (why would Ansel deliver a warning he doesn't believe in?). Ansel's answer—"he carried it because he would not have another man carry it"—reframed the act: not as agreement with the warning, but as refusal to shift the burden of truth-telling. The rift persisted until Emmet's death 1929, unreconciled.

**Ansel's tragedy:** Public vindication in 1958 came 24 years after his death. Orra died in 1948, 10 years before her husband's name was cleared. At his 1934 funeral, no mourner publicly named the apparatus defect; mourners praised his carpentry. Ansel's statement captures the core: vindication requires someone to publicly name the wrong. In his lifetime, nobody did.
