## Story 1: The Ostrey Hollow Butterfat Testing Scandal — FULL VERIFICATION EXTENDED

### FOUR INDEPENDENT SOURCES (Segments 1–9, now including r10 and r11)

**Source 1: Merle Strawn, State Dairy Inspector (r06-long, Segment 7)**
- Official circuit books, 29 years on Ordell circuit (1907–1936)
- June 12, 1923: "These measures will not deliver their mark. Condemned the lot, the whole crate of them..."
- October 1923: "New glass in use since July. Ran the new pipettes against the burette. They deliver their mark."
- 1924: "Tests improved. Their book gives the year at 3.80%"

**Source 2: Ivy Keddie, Physical Evidence + Measurement (r07, Segment 8)**
- Condemnation-dated crate of pipettes (June 1923)
- Nine pipettes measured: mean delivery 16.27 cc vs 17.6 cc nominal
- True average calculation: 3.80%
- Ledgers from Teague confirming pre-scandal test records

**Source 3: Halbert Nym, Accession Notes r07 (Segment 7) & Curator of Bulletin 471**
- USDA Bulletin 471 (1958) closure statement: "From the first of May, 1897, to the twelfth of June, 1923, the station's tests were low by one part in twenty because its pipettes were short by one part in twenty... seventy-six thousand pounds of butter fat — stands credited in the Association's own ledgers, year by year, as station gain."
- Annual ledgers: 1898–1922 intake 40,000,000 lbs milk; station gain 76,000 lbs butterfat, worth $19,000 at average price (~25 cents/lb)

**Source 4: Roy Brant, Farmer (r09 Chapter 4, Segment 8)**
- Age 67 in 1955; kept dairy ledger from Jan 1, 1898 until 1929
- Hessel Bottom farm, 160 acres wet meadow, 38 cows in good year
- Two scales (Emmet's, station's) kept in perfect agreement barring pound or two on sloppy days
- Year 1910 entry: "Delivered to the station, two hundred thousand pounds even, by my own scale and theirs agreeing. Paid on a test of 3.42."
- The trouble: "Our milk at the Hollow ran seventeen hundredths of a point under what the same class of milk was making down at Larrow Green" (0.17% below Larrow Green, consistent year-round)
- 1911 summer (9 weeks): Station's own measure broken; state man left his measure. Through those 9 weeks: tests rose for ALL Bottom farms. Station's own measure returned: figures went down again to previous level.
- From 1919: Emmet and Ansel Keddie "did not speak — not from quarrel over water, but because my father held that the man who kept the test book ought to have stood up in front of that circular and did not"

**Source 5: Selby Vose, Manager's Address 1926 (r08, Segment 8)**
- Thirteen years manager (since 1913)
- January 1925: Ostrey Hollow patrons filed petition
- Board of arbitration constituted at Ashlin under Alonzo Frick
- Board finding: "no cause established" against station, office, or officers
- Despite finding no cause, awarded $3,800 "without admission" to Ostrey Hollow patrons
- Apparatus condemned on June 12, 1923 (NOT July; Vose's date is incorrect)
- Apparatus ordered inside month; new in use before flush was over
- "In the very next season the tests at that station rose by twenty-one hundredths and the average for 1924 stood at 3.90"

**Source 6: Rosalie Cudd, Cheesemaker at Alder Corners (r10, 1958 letter to Ivy Keddie)**
- Father Jerome Cudd built factory 1913, ran until death 1941; Rosalie took over 1941, 17 years running at 1958
- From 1915, four men off Hessel Bottom hauled milk over to her in summer flush (June–mid-September) for 16 seasons through 1930
- Ridge shuts in November, opens April/May—winter hauling impossible
- Jerome Cudd tested himself on his own bench with his own glassware; never compared figures with Ostrey Hollow
- Jerome's 1919 entry (copied exactly by Rosalie): "The four Hessel Bottom men, summer of 1919: 3.79, 3.81, 3.78, 3.82. Good milk off wet ground, whatever they say over the ridge."
- Jerome thought Ordell County's theory about wet meadow thinning cream was "nonsense" and wrote so in his private book
- Rosalie: "That milk was good milk. Season after season those four men came in at about three-eighty, and not one of them ever gave me an hour's trouble in the vat."
- Summer averages, 16 seasons (1915–1930): 3.79, 3.81, 3.78, 3.80, 3.82, 3.79, 3.78, 3.80 / 3.81, 3.80, 3.79, 3.82, 3.78, 3.80, 3.81, 3.79
- Overall range 3.78–3.82; one summer with another, four men together, at about 3.80
- On watered milk: "A cheesemaker knows watered milk inside a day... Cheese is a witness. In sixteen summers my father never made one complaint of those four men, never docked one of them a cent."

**Source 7: Leland Vaught, Professor of Dairy Husbandry (r11, Bulletin 471 introduction, October 1958)**
- Definitive technical analysis of pipette defect
- The error is PROPORTIONAL, not additive (removes a fixed fraction, not fixed points)
- The error is PRECISE (16.27 cc every time, for a quarter-century)
- The error is INVISIBLE from inside the plant (pipette is the standard; cannot check itself)
- Material examined: Of 60 pipettes purchased for Ostrey Hollow in 1897, nine recovered from condemned crate, unbroken and legible
- Stems blown 17.6 cc nominal; delivered 16.27 cc mean
- Ratio: 16.27 ÷ 17.6 = 0.9244 (94.99% of nominal)
- Percentage short: 5.01% (one part in twenty)
- Station's own glass in use May 1, 1898 through June 1923 condemnation
- Nine-week interval 1911: Station's measure broken, borrowed state inspector's measure used. All Bottom farms' tests rose from 3.61 to 3.80, held there 9 weeks, returned to 3.61 when station's measure back in service. "One variable changed" — documented contemporaneously by operator who didn't know what he was recording
- Recorded station average 1898–1923: 3.61%
- Corresponding true average: 3.61 ÷ 0.9244 = 3.905% (shortfall of 0.295 points)
- Applied to 40,000,000 lbs milk over 25 years: 118,000 lbs butterfat delivered and not credited
- NOTE: Bulletin conclusion states 76,000 lbs (smaller figure); Vaught calculation yields 118,000 lbs. Discrepancy unresolved in Bulletin 471 introduction itself. Vaught notes "this figure is not readily reconciled with the station's own average of 3.80 in the year following the replacement of the glassware."
- Association's own ledgers: annual "station gain" (fat received at creamery vs credited to patrons) = 5.25% of butterfat actually delivered
- 1911 feeding trial (Bulletin 214): Bog hay depressed butterfat test by not more than 5 hundredths (winter) and nothing measurable (summer)—far less than the 6× discrepancy in question
- CONCLUSION: "No milk was watered at Ostrey Hollow and no cream was stolen there. From the first of May, 1897, to the twelfth of June, 1923, the station's tests were low by one part in twenty because its pipettes were short by one part in twenty, and the difference — seventy-six thousand pounds of butter fat — stands credited in the Association's own ledgers, year by year, as station gain."

## CRITICAL MATH FACTS FOR SECTION C (EXTENDED)

**Glassware defect:**
- Nominal: 17.6 cc per pipette
- Delivered: 16.27 cc per pipette (mean of 9 recovered pipettes)
- Shortfall: 0.88 cc per pipette
- Percentage delivered: 16.27 ÷ 17.6 = 0.9244 (94.99%)
- Percentage short: 5.01% (one part in twenty)

**Test averages (DISCREPANCY NOTE):**
- Recorded average (1898–1923): 3.61%
- True average per Strawn (1924 observation) and Ivy Keddie (1958): 3.80%
- True average per Vaught calculation (3.61 ÷ 0.9244): 3.905%
- Difference: 0.19 percentage points (Strawn/Keddie) or 0.295 points (Vaught)
- NOTE: Bulletin 471 introduction acknowledges discrepancy between Vaught's calculated 3.905 and the post-1923 station average of 3.80 observed by Strawn

**Delivery history:**
- Glassware first affected: May 1, 1898 (per Vaught) — BUT notes-8 says May 4, 1896 borrowed Larrow Green measure; this DATE DISCREPANCY remains unresolved
- New glassware ordered: June 12, 1923 (condemnation date)
- New glassware in use: July 1923 (July 10 breakpoint confirmed by Ivy's measurements)
- Glassware ledger start: January 1, 1898 (Emmet Brant's book)

**1910 reference:**
- Emmet Brant's delivery: 200,000 pounds exactly (noted as "even" — unusual)
- Credited test: 3.42%
- Price: 24 cents per pound

**Butterfat shortage (CRITICAL DISCREPANCY):**
- Per Bulletin 471 conclusion: 76,000 lbs (1897/1898–June 1923)
- Per Vaught calculation in Bulletin 471 introduction: 118,000 lbs (1898–1922, 25 years, 40,000,000 lbs milk delivered)
- Vaught explicitly notes this discrepancy: "It should be said that this figure is not readily reconciled with the station's own average of 3.80 in the year following the replacement of the glassware."

**Value and recovery:**
- Value: $19,000 (at average 25 cents/lb; per notes-8, likely based on 76,000 lbs × $0.25)
- Recovery: $3,800 award (January 1925, arbitration at Ashlin, board under Alonzo Frick)
- Recovery percentage: 20% of $19,000 shortfall

**Nine-week 1911 experiment:**
- Timing: Summer 1911, 9 weeks
- Station's measure broken; state inspector's measure used in its place
- All Bottom farms' tests rose (no one measure fixed)
- Average rose from 3.61% to 3.80% across all Bottom patrons
- Tests fell back to 3.61% when station's measure returned
- Documentation: Recorded contemporaneously by operator (question mark in margin)
- Significance: Conclusively eliminates herd, land, and milk-quality explanations

## INDEPENDENT VERIFICATION: ALDER CORNERS VS. OSTREY HOLLOW

Two independent testing systems, two independent operators, same herds (Hessel Bottom), same season (summer flush 1915–1930):

- **Ostrey Hollow station** (defective pipette, 1898–1923): Recorded tests 3.61% average
- **Alder Corners cheese factory** (Jerome Cudd's glassware, 1915–1930): Recorded tests 3.80% average (range 3.78–3.82)

**Same four farmers at both locations (different seasons):**
- Summer: Hessel Bottom farmers hauled to Alder Corners (3.80% average)
- Winter/other: Same farmers sold to Ostrey Hollow station (recorded as ~3.61% average)
- Difference: NOT due to herds or milk quality (same cows, same families)
- Difference: DUE to Ostrey Hollow's defective pipette

Rosalie Cudd's testimony: "Good milk off wet ground, whatever they say over the ridge" — directly contradicts Ordell County's theory that wet meadow produced poor milk.
