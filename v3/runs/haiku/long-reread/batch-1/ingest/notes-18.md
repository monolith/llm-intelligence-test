### NEW MATERIAL: Dr. Roland Kepp, 1926 Paper to Ordell County Farm Club (r16)

#### Kepp's Scientific Credentials & Argument
- **Speaker:** Dr. Roland Kepp, V.S. (veterinary surgeon); 30 years practice; addresses Farm Club 1926
- **Thesis:** Wet bogland produces milk deficient in butterfat because cattle on such land cannot extract sufficient minerals from the forage

#### The Land Analysis
- Wet bottoms are distinct chemically, not just water-logged meadows
- Without drainage, yearly growth "lies sour" instead of rotting down
- Resulting soil: acid, poor in lime and phosphates
- Sweet grasses crowded out by sedge, rush, coarse tussock grasses within seasons
- Forage carries bulk and water but lacks lime/phosphorus; cut late (ground too wet early), further depleted

#### The Physiology
- Dairy cow in milk on narrow margin; must find mineral material in ration
- If ration short in minerals, cow does not simply give less; she robs her own frame
- Wet-meadow herds observable thin over loin, rough coat, slow to gain spring condition, prone to fall digestive disorders, breed late, feet worse
- **Crucially: milk still flows freely** (bog land generous of supply) **but fat suffers first** (fat is dearest output cow makes, first economy when pressed)

#### The Quantitative Evidence
- **Valley-wide observation:** Wet bottoms consistently yield milk **0.2 points lower in fat** (field data, 30-year practice)
- Difference does not depend on breed, farmer, barn — depends on ground

#### Hessel Bottom Case Study
- **Location:** Six farms on wet meadow under Ostrey Hollow, along creek, springs at slope base, year-round runoff, under water every spring, walkable only after May
- **Hay type:** Sedge hay, main feed November to April
- **Historical complaint:** 25-year pattern of tests below valley average
- **Kepp's measured gap:** 0.21 (21 hundredths) between Hollow and valley milk consistently
- **Kepp's assessment:** Complaint "perfectly just and I have never doubted it"

#### The College Evidence
- **State Agricultural College at Farland, Bulletin 214:** Feeding trial on bog hay, controlled conditions
- **Finding:** Butterfat test depressed **0.2 points (two tenths)** in winter feeding
- **Significance:** College independent of local politics, no knowledge of Hessel Bottom, no incentive to flatter committee
- **Kepp's interpretation:** "Our finding, arrived at by another road and by better men, three years after we set our names to it"

#### Kepp's Address of Objection
- **Objection raised:** Summer 1911 tests higher than before/after (6 weeks with borrowed apparatus when station's failed)
- **Kepp's dismissal:** Summer figures invalid; cattle flush season, off wet ground onto aftermath; could prove anything with cherry-picked 6 weeks

#### The Remedy
- Drain bottom; lime ground (multiple applications); sow when carrying seeding
- Keep stock off wet ground in spring; never feed sedge hay alone; mix with mineral supplement (per College)
- **Cost-benefit:** Fat worth 27¢/lb through these years; 0.2 point loss per hundred-weight shipments, year after year, pays for tile installation

#### Floor Discussion (from Farm Club)
- **Q (member):** Might difference lie in poorer cows/poorer men on bottom farms?
  - **Kepp:** Rejected; some of best-bred cattle stand on wet land; some indifferent cattle on hills test well; man may improve herd 20 years without fat rise if ground remains sour
- **Q (member):** Might trouble lie in station rather than bottom (valley rumor)?
  - **Kepp:** Has no competence in testing (never read test, doesn't know how); but when cause found in soil, herbage, cattle condition, College trial, and every wet bottom in county alike, "it is not good sense to go looking for a second cause in a building"

## CRITICAL CONFLICT: THREE COMPETING EXPLANATIONS FOR OSTREY HOLLOW TESTS

### A. Glassware Defect (Strawn, Vaught, Rundle, Frick; r06–r12)
- **When introduced:** Unknown; crate arrived 1899; state condemned 1921
- **Mechanism:** Pipettes 16.27 cc actual vs 17.6 cc nominal (5.01% shortfall)
- **Evidence:** 11 recovered defective pipettes (Ivy Keddie); ledger gain pattern (~5.25%); precise alignment with apparatus shortfall
- **Period:** May 1, 1898–June 12, 1923 (or July 12, 1923)

### B. Wet Hay/Bog Meadow (Grigg 1908/1919; Kepp 1926; r15, r16)
- **Mechanism:** Hessel Bottom bogland produces inferior sedge/rush hay; fed Nov–April; depresses butterfat test ~0.2% (College trial)
- **Evidence:** 1908 committee on-ground investigation (no dishonesty); State College Bulletin 214 (feeding trial); Kepp's 30-year field observation (0.2 point gap); consistent pattern all wet bottoms
- **Period:** From ~1902 (complaints began) through at least 1926 (Kepp paper) and 1981 (Hazel Brant)
- **Finding:** No defect in apparatus; supply side (milk quality) is issue

### C. Weigh Book Entry (Hazel Brant, 1981; r17)
- **Test gap measured:** 0.19 (19 hundredths) lower at Ostrey Hollow every month, every season, wet years and dry
- **This figure:** Between glassware shortfall (5.01%) and wet hay theory (0.2%); closer to wet hay but persistent across all seasons (contradicts seasonal feed theory if true across summer when cattle off wet ground)

## Key Factual Alignment & Conflicts

### Aligned (All Sources)
- **Test deficit exists:** All narratives acknowledge Ostrey Hollow tests consistently lower (~0.19–0.2–0.21–0.25 range depending on source)
- **Hessel Bottom's six farms:** Central to story (r15, r16, r17)
- **Sedge hay feeding Nov–Apr:** Kepp and Brant both identify

- **Emmet Brant 1910 data:** 200K lbs, 3.42% test (locked in Hazel's memory from Brant weigh book; r17)

### Conflicting (Cannot Both Be Fully True)
- **Glassware defect magnitude:** 5.01% shortfall (Strawn/Vaught) vs observed test gap ~0.2% (Brant/Kepp field)
  - Gap of 4.8 points unexplained if glassware alone; suggests wet hay also operating OR glassware defect exaggerated
- **Seasonal pattern:** Brant says gap flat year-round (0.19 every month); Grigg/Kepp suggest winter/feeding-season effect (cattle off bog in summer = should improve)
  - Brant's "flat as a floor" contradicts seasonal feed logic UNLESS glassware defect is year-round component
- **Station apparatus culpability:** Grigg (1908) and Kepp (1926) explicitly exonerate station—"no dishonesty," "scales honest"; Ivy Keddie's discovery proves apparatus (glassware) defective
  - Committee did not check glassware (Grigg says "stand by during testing"; no mention of apparatus examination)
  - Therefore: Grigg/Kepp exonerated apparatus they did NOT examine; Ivy later found apparatus WAS defective

## Synthesis Toward Resolution

**Most likely scenario (inference required):**
Both factors operated together:
1. **Glassware defect:** Introduced ~1899 with imported crate; undiscovered until 1921 state inspection; could account for 5.01% systematic error in recorded vs true butterfat
2. **Wet hay effect:** Genuine milk quality issue (0.2–0.25% depression from bog hay), documented by College and visible in field data
3. **Observed gap (0.19–0.21):** Lies between pure wet hay theory (~0.2%) and pure glassware theory (5.01%), suggesting both operate
   - Glassware shortfall might be lower than 5.01% calculated (could be 0.2–0.25% range)
   - OR glassware defect occurred only during certain years of the 25-year period
   - OR both factors present; defect creates 5% reading error but compensates for real milk quality gap so net observed gap is smaller

**Evidence gap:** No source measured/published the actual butterfat content of Hessel Bottom milk independently (would resolve via: actual cream testing by third party, or Ivy's glassware measurements converted to test accuracy change)
