## Story 1: Ostrey Hollow Glass Defect — REVISED WITH NEW PRECISION DATA

### Timeline Clarification (Critical Discrepancy)

**Station opening and equipment dates:**
- r21 (Cleve, 1977): Station "opened before my time" (before 1900); glass "went into use" unspecified
- r22 (Falke, 1959): "Station opened on the 4th of May, 1896"
- r23 (Kyle, 1953): "Ostrey Hollow...went into use on the first of May, 1898" (referring to testing glass from supply house); "for its first season or two it borrowed its testing glass from the creamery here"

**Reconciliation:** Station opened 1896 (per r22). Borrowed glass first 1–2 years. Equipment ordered from supply house arrived and went into use May 1, 1898 (per r23). This defective equipment persisted until condemned June 12, 1923 (per r22, citing inspector Strawn).

### Apparatus Deficit (Confirmed)

**Per r22 (Falke, 1959):** "The deficiency, as the bulletin now fixes it, was nineteen hundredths of a point, and it ran at the same nineteen hundredths in every month of the year, in the flush of June and in the depth of February alike."

**Test averages:**
- Ostrey Hollow: 3.61
- Larrow Green (reference): 3.78
- Difference: 0.19 points (consistent year-round)

### The 1911 Incident (Decisive Evidence)

**Per r22:** "On the 8th of May the station's own measuring glass was broken, and a measure borrowed from elsewhere was used in its place for nine weeks, until the 10th of July. Through those nine weeks the station's tests stood markedly higher. On the day the station resumed its own glass they fell back to their former level. The tester noted the fact in his book and set a question mark against it."

**Per r23 (Kyle):** "In twenty-two the state man came into the office. Strawn...had run their milk pipettes against his burette and he had condemned the whole lot of them."

**Date clarification:** r22 says 1911 glass was broken May 8 and replaced July 10. r23 says the state inspection condemning occurred in "twenty-two" (1922 or 1923?). Per r22, Inspector Strawn's visit was June 12, 1923, when he condemned the glass.

### Ansel Keddie (The Tester)

**Who:** Employed from before Cleve Oram's birth (1900) through 1925 dismissal. Per r23, employed for full tenure with Ansel's test book in his handwriting used as evidence at 1925 hearing.

**Dismissed:** April 30, 1925 (per r22; per r23, "That was in twenty-four" referring to award, so dismissal April 30, 1925 is consistent).

**Post-1925:** Kept small farm, did carpentry work. Died in 1930s. Funeral well-attended (per r21).

### Post-1923 Outcome

**Per r22 (Falke):** "On the 12th of June of that year [1923] the state dairy inspector...condemned the whole lot on the spot and directed the manager to order new. New glass was in use from the following month. From 1924 the station's average test stood at three eighty-five and remained there."

**Confirmation:** Once good glassware was in use (July 1923 onward), station tests immediately rose to 3.85, matching the expected level (Larrow Green 3.78 plus margin).

### The 1925 Hearing and Award

**Board settlement:**
- r21 (Cleve): $3,500 ("What they gave the patrons at the end of it was thirty-five hundred dollars. That is what my father said and my father was not a man who got a figure wrong when it was money.")
- r22 & r23 (Falke and Kyle): $3,800 ("awarded the Ostrey Hollow patrons three thousand eight hundred dollars, without admission")

**Resolution:** $3,800 appears in two independent late sources (Falke 1959 report, Kyle 1953 recollection of contemporaneous events). r21 is Cleve's father's memory, three-hand via Cleve. Cleve's father may have heard the patrons' portion rather than the full board award. **Use $3,800 as primary figure; note $3,500 alternative.**

**No admission of guilt by anyone. Ansel never formally exonerated in his lifetime (died ~1930s).**

### Ivy Keddie Discovery (1958)

**Per r21:** "Winter 1958: went up ladder herself, brought crate down herself...Result: 'great deal of writing about it'...'Sixty-seven thousand pounds of butterfat' over full run of years patrons never paid for."

**Per r22:** "In February of 1958 Miss Keddie found the condemned glass, still crated, in the loft over the station's intake room, where it had lain since the day it was condemned. On the 9th of March she measured the nine surviving pipettes against a calibrated burette at Farland."

**Butterfat discrepancy (UNRESOLVED):**

These may refer to different time periods or include/exclude different patron groups. r21 may be the college's total revised calculation; notes-25 may be a subset. **Carry both; flag sources clearly.**

### Ivy Keddie Genealogy (RESOLVED)

### Bog Hay Theory (REFUTED)

**Per r22:** In response to the 1908 committee report and 1926 veterinarian's claim that Hessel Bottom wet hay depressed cream, Falke obtained the State Agricultural College's Bulletin No. 214 (feeding trial on bog hay, conducted 1911) and found:

> *"Bog hay fed as the whole of the roughage depressed the butter fat test by not more than five hundredths of one per cent in winter, and by nothing that could be measured on summer pasture."*

**Falke's judgment:** "Five hundredths in winter and nothing in summer will not carry nineteen hundredths in July. I withdraw the concession made on page four." The bog hay was a real but limited factor; apparatus defect is the only sufficient explanation for 0.19-point deficit year-round.

### New Fact from r23

**"Station gain" accounting practice:**
- Every year, Ostrey Hollow account showed: "So many pounds of butterfat credited to the patrons on the station test. So many pounds of butterfat received at Larrow Green in the station cream. And then the difference of the two, entered as station gain, so many pounds — and after it, always, in the bookkeeper's own words, five and a quarter per cent, as usual, carried to the general account."

**Kyle's insight:** "I copied that line, or the like of it, more times than I can count, and I will tell the Society plainly that it never once crossed my mind to wonder at it...A figure with a name on it has been explained, and you rule under it and go on to the next."

This 5.25% "station gain" was accepted practice, recorded openly every year, read at annual meetings to 50 farmers. The apparatus defect was in plain sight but normalized by naming.

### Ansel Keddie's Behavior (1911 Key)

**Per r22:** "The second explanation was that the tester himself removed cream and wrote low tests to conceal the loss. This was urged at the hearing by an officer of the Association. It cannot be sustained either, and the nine weeks of 1911 dispose of it: the same man made the tests in that interval, under no different supervision, and the figures rose."

**Conclusion:** Ansel's testing was honest; the apparatus was the culprit.
