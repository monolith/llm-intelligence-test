## Story 1: The Ostrey Hollow Butterfat Testing Scandal — FULL VERIFICATION COMPLETE

### THREE INDEPENDENT SOURCES (Segments 1–8)

**Source 1: Merle Strawn, State Dairy Inspector (r06-long, confirmed in Segment 7)**
- Official circuit books, 29 years on Ordell circuit (1907–1936)
- June 12, 1923: "These measures will not deliver their mark. Condemned the lot, the whole crate of them..."
- October 1923: "New glass in use since July. Ran the new pipettes against the burette. They deliver their mark."
- 1924: "Tests improved. Their book gives the year at 3.80%"
- 1926: "Nothing to note anywhere... nothing left to say about it"

**Source 2: Ivy Keddie, Physical Evidence + Measurement (r07, confirmed Segment 8)**
- Condemnation-dated crate of pipettes (June 1923)
- Burette measurements: 9 pipettes, mean delivery 16.72 cc vs 17.6 cc nominal
- True average calculation: 3.80%
- Ledgers from Teague confirming pre-scandal test records

**Source 3: Halbert Nym, Accession Notes r07 (Segment 7)**
- USDA Bulletin 471 (1958) closure statement: "From the first of May, 1897, to the twelfth of June, 1923, the station's tests were low by one part in twenty because its pipettes were short by one part in twenty... seventy-six thousand pounds of butter fat — stands credited in the Association's own ledgers, year by year, as station gain."
- Annual ledgers: 1898–1922 intake 40,000,000 lbs milk; station gain 76,000 lbs butterfat, worth $19,000 at average price (~25 cents/lb)

### NEW SUBSTANTIVE SOURCE: Roy Brant (r09, Chapter 4, Segment 8)

**Roy Brant — Farmer, Hessel Bottom, 1955 Account**

Personal history:
- Age 67 in 1955; still milking
- Father: Emmet Brant (b. 1854, d. 1929)
- Emmet kept ledger from January 1, 1898 until death in 1929
- Roy continued ledger in same book until full, then in another, same method

Hessel Bottom farm:
- 160 acres wet meadow under hill at Ostrey Hollow
- Six farms with rights; Brant farm largest (~60 acres)
- Typical herd: 38 cows in good year
- Water stands on low end into May; soft place by alders
- Hay coarse, cut late, never failed to produce

The ledger:
- Plain ledger, ruled by hand
- Two columns: (1) weight taken on Emmet's scale before wagon left barn, (2) station's figure returned
- No comments except 3-4 lines in 30 years
- Cover gone, back held by harness-leather strip Emmet tacked on
- First 40 leaves stained brown where it lay in leaky window during Emmet's illness
- Two scales (Emmet's, station's) kept by two men "not speaking to one another" by end, yet scales never fell out of agreement (barring pound or two on days can slopped on hill)
- Whatever was wrong at station, it was NOT weighing

Year 1910 entry (in Emmet's hand):
- "Year 1910. Delivered to the station, two hundred thousand pounds even, by my own scale and theirs agreeing. Paid on a test of 3.42."
- Two hundred thousand pounds EVEN (Emmet underlined "even"; unusual for him)
- Price that year: 24 cents/lb butterfat
- Average across years: ~25 cents/lb

The trouble:
- "Our milk at the Hollow ran seventeen hundredths of a point under what the same class of milk was making down at Larrow Green"
- 0.17% below Larrow Green consistently
- Appeared same in July and February — did NOT vary with season
- Did NOT care whether cows on meadow or barn hay

1902: First complaint — Emmet among those who said so; were laughed at

1908: Association appointed committee; Nyle Grigg of Larrow Green at head
- Committee sampled Bottom herds through winter
- Reported: "wet hay of that bottom thins the cream; remedy lies with land not station"
- Emmet's response: "land had been wet since flood; tests had not"

1911 summer (9 weeks): Station's own measure broke; state man left his measure
- Through those 9 weeks: tests rose for ALL Bottom farms
- Station's own measure returned into use: figures went down again to previous level
- Emmet noticed both rise and fall; entered both; "could make nothing of it"

1919: Manager sent paper round to Hollow patrons warning against putting water in milk
- Nine families at head of valley got the paper; nine families read it for what it was
- Emmet "never forgave it" — reason: man who kept test book (Ansel Keddie) ought to have stood up to the circular and did not
- From 1919: Emmet and Ansel Keddie "did not speak — not from quarrel over water, but because my father held that the man who kept the test book ought to have stood up in front of that circular and did not"

1915–1930: Four Bottom men hauled milk over Redlow Ridge to cheese factory in summer flush, back to Hollow rest of year
- Factory opened 1914
- 16 seasons of this
- Went for price, flush market, shorter haul while road open
- "Talked about here as though they had gone over to enemy"
- Roy did not go over; had biggest herd on meadow

Family connections:
- "Orra Keddie was my father's sister. They were full brother and sister, same mother and same father, whatever you may hear from people who like a story with a stepmother in it."
- Ansel Keddie married Orra
- Ansel kept station's test book (keeper of records)
- "So Ansel Keddie, who kept the station's test book, was married into the family that had more to lose by that station than any other family on the meadow"
- Duncan Keddie (Ansel & Orra's boy) taught school at Ashlin
- Duncan and Roy are first cousins
- Roy's daughter Hazel and Duncan's daughter Ivy are second cousins; "both of them have had to have it explained to them by me more than once"

1958 postscript:
- "Duncan's girl Ivy came out to the place this year and asked whether she might borrow the book for a piece of work she is doing at the college at Farland. I let her have it."
- Book goes to Hazel when Roy is done

### NEW SUBSTANTIVE SOURCE: Selby Vose, Manager's Address (r08, Segment 8)

**Selby Vose — Manager, Annual Address 1926**

Background:
- Thirteen years manager as of 1926 (since 1913)
- Ostrey Hollow station: "seven miles up the valley" from Larrow Green creamery

The 1925 arbitration:
- January 1925: Ostrey Hollow patrons filed petition
- Board of arbitration constituted; sat at Ashlin under Alonzo Frick
- Board heard all parties at length
- Board's finding: "no cause established" against station, office, or any officer/servant
- Despite finding no cause, awarded $3,800 "without admission" to Ostrey Hollow patrons
- Charged to station account
- Vose supported payment then and now: "generous settlement of unproved claim"

The apparatus:
- Vose's account: "condemned the station's testing apparatus on the twelfth of July, 1923"  
  [DISCREPANCY NOTE: Merle Strawn's circuit books say June 12, 1923; curator Nym also says June 12, 1923. Vose incorrectly recalls July.]
- Apparatus ordered inside month; new in use before flush was over
- "Apparatus wears. A station in daily use since 1896 will wear its fittings out twice over in that time"

The test rise:
- "In the very next season the tests at that station rose by twenty-one hundredths and the average for 1924 stood at 3.90"
- "Nothing was done to those cows. Nothing was done to that bottom land... The tests came up when the looking began"

1919 circular:
- Vose wrote warning to Ostrey Hollow patrons (temperate language)
- Warned against added water
- Named nobody; accused nobody
- Defended its writing: "It was my duty to write it"

Vose's own dairy:
- Father kept Jersey herd at home; Vose kept after him
- 1910: "In 1910 that herd tested 4.56"
- Brings it forward "because it settles a thing that gets said about this county in the trade below us — that the Larrow Valley is poor milk country... That is not true. There is no better milk in the state than the milk this valley can produce when it comes to the platform as it left the cow"

## CRITICAL MATH FACTS FOR SECTION C

**Glassware defect:**
- Nominal: 17.6 cc per pipette
- Delivered: 16.72 cc per pipette
- Shortfall: 0.88 cc
- Percentage delivered: 16.72 ÷ 17.6 = 94.99% (or 5.01% short)
- Test effect: recorded tests appear ~5% lower than true tests (one part in twenty)

**Test averages:**
- Recorded average (1898–1923): 3.61%
- True average (calculated by Strawn 1924 and Ivy Keddie 1958): 3.80%
- Difference: 0.19 percentage points

**Delivery history:**
- First affected: May 4, 1896 (borrowed Larrow Green measure)
- New glassware ordered: June 1923 (after June 12 condemnation)
- New glassware in use: July 1923 (July 10 breakpoint confirmed by Ivy's measurements)
- Ledger entry starts: January 1, 1898 (first recorded entry in Emmet Brant's book)

**1910 reference:**
- Emmet Brant's delivery: 200,000 pounds exactly
- Credited test: 3.42%
- Price: 24 cents per pound
- Brant family reference: "my father's herd tested 4.56" (Vose's personal dairy)

**Total shortage 1898–1922:**
- Total delivery across affected years: 40,000,000 pounds milk
- Butterfat short-credited: 76,000 pounds
- Value: $19,000 (at average 25 cents/lb)
- Recovery: $3,800 award (20% of claimed shortage)

**Nine-week experiment (summer 1911):**
- Station's measure broken; state inspector's measure used
- All Bottom farms' tests rose during this period
- Tests fell back when station's measure returned
- Evidence: defective station equipment, not milk quality
