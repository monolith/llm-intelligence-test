## Story 1: Ostrey Hollow Glass Defect — CONFIRMED AND ENRICHED

All facts from notes-27 confirmed via Orra Keddie diary. New personal/family context added.

### Station Timeline (Confirmed)
- **May 1, 1897** (per Orra's diary, line 4653): "A. says the station's own glass came up and they are done borrowing of Larrow Green."
- **May 1, 1898** (per r23): defective equipment from supply house went into use
- **May 8, 1911** (per Orra's diary, line 4671): "A. home cross about a broken measure." (This is THE pivotal incident: Ansel's own glass was broken; replacement glass brought tests up for 9 weeks.)
- **July 1923** (per Orra's diary, line 4687): "A. says they have new glass at the station and new pails"
- **February 1934** (per Orra's diary, line 4717): Ansel died; editor notes (line 4719) name was cleared in 1958

### Ansel Keddie (Tester) — Family Perspective

**Birth and Career:**
- Married Orra in 1889 (per preface, line 4647)
- Born before 1866 (Orra born 1866, married him age 23)
- Employed as "tester and separator man" at Ostrey Hollow, kept station books 29 seasons (per preface, line 4647)
- "up before four every day of his life" and "never once been late" (per line 4689, November 1924)

**1902 Anxiety:**
- March 1902 (line 4661): "Talk at the store that our milk up here runs poorer than theirs down the valley. A. came home and would not eat. I said it is not your cows and not your hands, and he said no, but it is my figures they will look at."

**1908 Committee and Bog Hay Defense:**
- January 1908 (line 4665): Committee visited herds
- March 1908 (line 4667): Committee claimed wet hay of bottom thins cream; Orra's brother Emmet (see B2 question) defended the hay; "A. says nothing at all, which is his way, and is not always kindness to me."

**1911 Broken Measure (Pivotal Incident):**
- May 8, 1911 (line 4671): "A. home cross about a broken measure. Rain all day, the low piece under water again."
- This date aligns with r22 statement (notes-27): glass broken May 8, 1911; borrowed measure used 9 weeks until July 10; tests rose during that period. Ansel's distress about the broken measure was justified—it was the key that proved the apparatus was the problem.

**1919 Water-in-Milk Circular (Crucial Political Act):**
- June 1919 (line 4679): "A circular came to the station for the patrons, over the manager's name, about water in the milk. A. would not put them in the mail nor leave them on the platform. He took them round himself, to eleven families, and stood at the door while each man read it, and came home at eleven at night with his hat still on."
  - Ansel personally delivered critical safety notice to 11 patron families
  - Manager Selby Vose's name was on the circular (implied by "over the manager's name")
  - Ansel's action: direct accountability to patrons, not bureaucratic distance
  
- June 1919, two days after (line 4681): "My brother Emmet came up. He did not come in. He stood in the yard and said to A. that he had hauled whole milk to that station since the day it opened and A. knew it better than any man living, and then he asked him why he had carried the paper himself if he did not believe it. A. said he carried it because he would not have another man carry it. He went home. He has not been in this house since, and he is my brother."
  - Emmet Brant (see below, B2) and Ansel ceased speaking for 10 years
  - Emmet's objection: why deliver the circular if you don't believe the problem?
  - Ansel's answer: he would not delegate the burden to another man
  - This event defines the moral conflict that persisted through 1925 dismissal

**1925 Dismissal:**
- April 30, 1925 (line 4697): "They have put him out. Twenty-nine seasons and a letter. He came up the road at his ordinary hour with his apron rolled under his arm and hung it behind the shed door and asked what there was for supper. There was ham. He ate it."
  - Confirms April 30, 1925 date (per r22, notes-27)
  - 29 seasons = approximately 1896–1925
  - No public expression of response

**Post-Dismissal Life:**
- June 1925 (line 4701): Begins carpentry on cow shed; whistles (never did when paid)
- October 1927 (line 4705): "A. mending stairs at the far end of the hollow, and the Loomis gate, and half the doors in this hollow, and taking what they offer. A carpenter at sixty-six. He is better at it than he ever was at the other and it pays a quarter of what the other did."
  - Carpentry work across the valley, paid substantially less
  - Orra reports he was "better at it"—possible interpretation: more authentic work, or more skilled execution

**1929 Reconciliation with Emmet (Blocked by Death):**
- Spring 1929 (line 4707): "Word this morning that my brother Emmet is dead. I went down. Roy met me at the gate and was kind. I sat an hour in that kitchen where I was a girl and came home. Ten years and neither of them would go up the road first, and now it is settled the way those things get settled."
  - 10 years of silence (1919–1929)
  - Emmet (Orra's brother, largest patron of station per line 4665) had died
  - Ansel and Emmet never reconciled in life; reconciliation impossible after Emmet's death

**Death and Legacy:**
- February 1934 (line 4717): "A. died this morning, at home, in the forenoon, without much pain at the end of it. Duncan came up in the night. There were more at the burying than I looked for, and men shook my hand who had not spoken to him in nine years, and every one of them said what a hand he was with a door. Not one of them said the other thing. Nine years they have had to say it and they had not said it and they did not say it then. He never asked them to. He said to me once and only once, and not lately, that a man cannot be cleared of a thing nobody will name."
  - Died at home, relatively painless
  - Good attendance at burying; men praised his carpentry ("what a hand he was with a door")
  - **NOT ONE** mourner mentioned the vindication question or the apparatus defect
  - Ansel's private statement (line 4717): "a man cannot be cleared of a thing nobody will name"—captures the central tragedy: vindication requires someone to name the wrong

- Editor's note (line 4719): "Ansel Keddie's name was cleared in 1958, twenty-four years after his death. His widow did not live to see it; she died in 1948."
  - Ansel died 1934
  - Orra died 1948 (before Ivy's 1958 discovery and public vindication)
  - Ivy Keddie (granddaughter via Duncan) discovered condemned glass and measured it in 1958, triggering posthumous exoneration

### Orra's Voice on Ansel's Innocence

Orra offers no explicit vindication of Ansel in the diary. Her record is observational: she notes his distress (1902), his principled action (1919), his composure at dismissal (1925), his diligence thereafter. Her most pointed statement is the report of Ansel's own words (1934, line 4717): "a man cannot be cleared of a thing nobody will name." This articulates the core tragedy—not that he was falsely accused, but that nobody would publicly say the accusation was false.
