## Story 1: Ostrey Hollow Investigation — NEW PRIMARY SOURCE (R18 — Duncan Keddie)

### Duncan Keddie's Document — Comprehensive Retelling with Family Genealogy

**Narrator:** Duncan Keddie
- Born 1894 (makes him 74 in 1968 when he writes)
- Son of Ansel Keddie and Orra Brant
- Daughter: Ivy Keddie (born 1931)
- Profession: Retired schoolteacher at Ashlin, taught arithmetic
- Writes 1968 with a 1970 addition; states "I have kept to what I know. Where I do not know a thing I have said so."

### Family Structure — Documented Genealogy

**Nuclear Family of Ansel Keddie:**
- **Ansel Keddie:** Born 1861, died 1934. Tester and separator man at Ostrey Hollow skimming station of Larrow Valley Cooperative Creamery Association "from the year it opened" (context: earlier sources indicate ~1895–1896 for start)
  - Described as "careful man in small things to a degree that other people found trying"
  - "The making of the record he left and, I have sometimes thought, the unmaking of him"
- **Orra Brant (wife):** Born 1868, married Ansel 1889
  - Younger sister of Emmet Brant (largest farmer on Hessel Bottom, hauled most milk to the station)
  - Fact that "the patron he was said to have shorted worst was his own wife's brother, who was at our table on a Sunday and had been for thirty-six years" (implies regular family relation)
  - Outlived Ansel by 14 years; died 1948 at age 82 (but says "born eighteen sixty-eight"—arithmetic: would be 80 at 1948; possible transcription typo or memory error; another source says she lived 82 years, making birth ~1866)

**Relationships — Primary Cousins:**
- Duncan (Ansel's son, born 1894) and Roy Brant (Emmet's son, Orra's nephew) are **first cousins** (children of brother and sister)
- Orra is Emmet's sister
- Roy became Emmet's successor on Hessel Bottom and inherited/maintained Emmet's weigh book

**Relationships — Secondary Cousins:**
- Hazel Brant (Roy's daughter) and Ivy Keddie (Duncan's daughter) are **second cousins** (children of first cousins), NOT "cousins once removed" (different generation marker) and NOT "aunt and niece"
- Duncan explicitly states these relationships as often misreported

### The Ostrey Hollow Investigation Crisis — Ansel's Dismissal

**Date and Sequence:**
- **April 30, 1925:** Ansel dismissed
- **May 4, 1925:** Board of arbitration handed down its "finding"
- **Critical timing:** Dismissal came 4 days BEFORE the board's finding, meaning "the finding found nothing and the dismissal said everything"

**Ansel's Lifespan After Dismissal:**
- Lived 9 years after dismissal; died 1934
- "Died with the imputation on him, in this county, among men he had weighed milk for since before their fathers died"
- Orra outlived him by 14 years (1948)

### Ansel's Suspicion About Glass — The Private Knowledge

**Summer 1911 — Ansel's Realization:**
- Came home saying "it was something in the glass"
- Duncan was 17; remembers the kitchen, remembers father said it twice: "at supper and again by the door, which was not his way"
- Ansel "never dared put it in the book"
- What he wrote in the book: a question mark

**Ansel's Internal Conflict:**
- "A man who would not put down what he could not prove"
- Duncan reflects: "this was the most expensive virtue anybody in that valley ever had"
- Faced: suspicion about a piece of glass vs. outfit he was taught to trust vs. practice of every station in the county
- **Decision:** He "kept on. He kept on for another fourteen years, and wrote down every figure the glass gave him, and the figures were what convicted him."

### Documentary Evidence — Books and Their Provenance

**Station Test Book (Ansel's handwritten record):**
- Kept by Ansel in his own hand from 1896 until he left (1925)
- March 1925: Impounded by board of arbitration
- After hearing: deposited in Cooperative office at Ashlin, 1925–1959
- 1959: Transferred to Ordell County Historical Rooms, where it resides at time of Duncan's writing

**Brant Weigh Book (Emmet's weight-and-test record):**
- Started January 1, 1897 by Emmet; kept until Emmet's death 1929
- 1929: Went to Roy Brant (Emmet's son)
- 1958: Roy **lent** it to Ivy (Duncan's daughter); Duncan emphasizes "lent, and I want the word"
- After Roy's death (1962): Went to Hazel Brant (Roy's daughter)
- 1963: Hazel gave it to Historical Rooms at Ordell County

**Merle Strawn's Circuit Notebooks (state dairy inspector):**
- Strawn kept one notebook per year for every year on the Ordell circuit
- "Merle Strawn, the state's dairy inspector on this circuit, was kin to nobody in the valley whatever — not to us, not to the Brants, not to the Voses, not to anybody on the creek. That is worth writing down, because his notebooks turned out to matter, and it matters that the man who kept them had no interest in any of us."
- When Strawn retired 1936: notebooks went to state dairy office
- 1947: Transferred to library of State Agricultural College at Farland
- 1958 (11 years later, nobody had asked for them): Ivy found the volume for 1923 at Farland

**Alder Corners Test Book (Jerome Cudd's cheese factory):**
- Jerome Cudd kept it from company's founding; passed to his daughter Rosalie Cudd in 1941
- Rosalie took over the factory and in 1958 had figures copied out and sent to Ivy at the college
- Book itself "has never been out of Vessey County"

### Family Connections Explicitly Denied

Duncan addresses widespread rumor directly:
- "There was no connection whatever between us and the Voses."
- Two women in town have claimed his mother (Orra) and Mr. Gideon Vose's wife were cousins (some say first cousins)
- Duncan firmly refutes: "It is talk, and it has been talk since before I could walk. My mother would have told me."
- Orra was "the sort of woman who could not pass a headstone without giving you the connection, and she never once put a Vose in it."

### Name Corrections (Misreported Family Relations)

Duncan provides clarifications on commonly confused identities:
- **Selby Vose:** Son of Gideon Vose (who organized the Association and wrote its by-laws), NOT nephew as "I have heard it given"
  - Selby managed the Association
  - Gideon was first president
- **Rosalie Cudd:** Jerome Cudd's daughter, NOT misidentified relation
- **Ivy Keddie:** Duncan's daughter (born 1931), making her Ansel's granddaughter
  - Duncan states this plainly "because I have already twice seen it printed otherwise and once heard it said aloud in this town by a man who ought to have known"

### Orra's Letters and Legacy

- Orra kept every letter related to the business and left them to Duncan
- Contents: "nothing in them that anybody would want, only the weather and the price of things and her brother's health"
- Duncan is "keeping them anyway"

## Critical Conflicts & Unresolved Questions

1. **Emmet's weigh book start date:** Notes 18 states "January 1, 1900"; Duncan says "January 1, 1897"
   - Resolution needed before final reconstruction

2. **Orra Brant's birth year:** Duncan says "eighteen sixty-eight"; arithmetic with death year (1948) and stated lifespan (14 years after Ansel's 1934 death, or 82 years old) implies birth ~1866
   - Possible transcription error in Duncan's document or in notes transcription

3. **Ansel's tenure start:** Hazel says 1895; Duncan says test book "from 1896 until he left"; earlier notes suggest "year it opened" (likely 1895–1896)

4. **Nature of Ansel's accusation:** All sources describe him as suspected of "shorting the patrons," but Duncan's narrative suggests Ansel himself suspected the GLASS, not that he was committing fraud. The accusation against Ansel may have been misplaced blame for the apparatus defect.

5. **Why did Ansel not expose the glass in 1911?** Duncan provides insight: Ansel had no proof, couldn't "put down what he could not prove," feared the authority of the installed equipment and the practice of all county stations. Fear + lack of evidence = silence for 14 more years.
