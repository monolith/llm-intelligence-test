## Story 1: The Ostrey Hollow Butterfat Testing Scandal (DEEPENED)

### New Material from Segment 2

#### Ivy Keddie's 1959 Testimony (r04)
**Speaker:** Ivy Keddie, granddaughter of Ansel Keddie, speaking to Ordell County Historical Society, Ashlin, 1959.

**Family lineage:** Ansel Keddie (tester) → Duncan Keddie (Ansel's son, teacher at Ashlin) → Ivy Keddie (granddaughter).

**Physical Evidence - The Crate:**
- Located in loft of station building in February (previous year, 1958)
- Crate stenciled: "AMMON & SONS DAIRY SUPPLY TARNET"
- Carried up June 1923; had been there since
- Contained condemned milk-test pipettes
- Original purchase: six dozen (1897), cost $77.93 for entire outfit

**Pipette Measurements (March 9, burette calibration):**
- Nine pipettes measured at Farland
- Ran five times each
- Delivered: 16.72 cc
- Should have delivered: 17.6 cc
- Shortfall: 0.88 cc = 95 hundredths of the mark
- **Key finding:** Tests read 95% of truth, or equivalently, 5% SHORT

**Mathematical Proof:**
- Credited average test 1898–1922: 3.61 percent
- True average: 3.80 percent (from 1896 data and nine-week 1911 anomaly)
- Gap: 0.19 percentage points = 5% of 3.80
- Matches pipette shortfall exactly

**Butterfat Loss (25-year period, 1898–1922):**
- Total milk: 40 million pounds
- Average per year: 1.6 million pounds
- Credited butterfat: 1,444,000 pounds (at 3.61%)
- True butterfat: 1,520,000 pounds (at 3.80%)
- **Owed but not credited: 76,000 pounds**
- Average price: 25 cents per pound
- **Dollar value owed: $19,000**
- Board awarded 1925: $3,800 = one-fifth of true loss
- Percentage of credited amount: 5.25%
- Percentage of actual amount delivered: 5% (Ivy's wording)

**The 1911 Anomaly Explained:**
- May 8, 1911: Ansel's pipette breaks
- Borrowed replacement measure used May 8–July 10
- Borrowed measure: tests jump to 3.80
- July 10: station's own measure returns
- Same day (July 10): tests drop back to 3.61
- This proves the equipment was the issue, not the milk or procedure

**The June 1923 Condemned Measures:**
- State inspector condemned a crate of measures
- New ones ordered
- NOT discussed in 1925 hearing (per Wilbur Teague's observation)
- Ivy establishes this was the actual faulty equipment used throughout

**Ansel Cleared (1934 bulletin):**
- Bulletin No. 471, October (implicitly 1958), co-authored by Professor Vaught
- Final paragraph (Ivy's reading): "No milk was watered at Ostrey Hollow and no cream was stolen there. From the first of May, 1897, to the twelfth of June, 1923, the station's tests were low by one part in twenty because its pipettes were short by one part in twenty, and the difference — seventy-six thousand pounds of butter fat — stands credited in the Association's own ledgers, year by year, as station gain."

**Timeline of Vindication vs. Condemnation:**
- Pipettes condemned: June 1923 (35 years before proof)
- Ansel dismissed: April 30, 1925 (33 years before proof)
- Ansel died: 1934 (24 years before proof)
- Cause found: 1958 (carried to proof 1959)

**Ivy's closing statement:** "I have no remedy to propose. I have only the crate, and it is on the table, and you may look into it on your way out."

#### Wilbur Teague's 1948 Memoir (r05)
**Speaker:** Wilbur Teague, retired bookkeeper for the Association, Chapter 9, written winter 1948 (3 years after retirement). Kept books 1905–1945 (40 years).

**Office Setup:**
- One room over harness shop at Ashlin
- Cold north end, warm south end
- Ledgers on shelf built by previous bookkeeper
- Sloped desk with brass rail

**Station Account Structure:**
- Three entities on books: Larrow Green creamery, Ashlin office, Ostrey Hollow station
- Manager: Selby Vose
- Secretary: Effie Loomis (kept minutes)
- Bookkeeper: Teague

**The Station Gain Line (Teague's Innovation, 1905):**
- Not an old dodge—invented by Teague when he arrived 1905
- Recorded difference between milk tested at station vs. cream received at Larrow Green
- Example (1898): Milk credited to patrons on station test: 57,760 lb; Butterfat received at Larrow Green: 60,800 lb; Station gain: 3,040 lb (5.25% of credited; 5% of actual)
- Pattern consistent year after year: "as usual" became standard language
- Gain ran evenly enough to become "furniture" in the books—pattern stopped scrutiny

**Total Statistics:**
- Total milk over station account lifespan: 44 million pounds (vs. Ivy's 40 million for affected years)
- Average test of all Ostrey Hollow patrons over whole period: 3.16
- Larrow Green consistently tested higher
- Nine patron families chronically sore about lower tests

**1911 Measure Break:**
- Station's own measure broke spring 1911
- Borrowed one for six weeks before replacement arrived
- Recorded as small charge for replacement; no other notation
- Later inquiries about this year suggest people connected it to the scandal

**Teague's 1925 Board Testimony:**
- Testified to board of arbitration under Alonzo Frick
- Board found nothing established against anybody
- Board awarded $3,800 without admission
- Teague charged it to station account and it balanced

**Teague's Diagnosis (Bookkeeping Level):**
- Cream is portable; station in one pair of hands 29 seasons
- One man weighed milk, drew sample, tested, recorded pay figures, ran separator
- No second man in building spring–fall
- "A business which is arranged so that a single man can take a little and write down a little less is a business that will sooner or later have a little taken from it"
- Remedy: two men or a lock; Association had neither

**Teague's Inaction:**
- Raised concern with Selby Vose in 1916
- Told to let it alone
- Never raised it again: "I was the Association's bookkeeper and not its conscience"
- "I did not sit silent for forty years, whatever has since been said. I put it to Mr. Vose in 1916 and was told to let it alone."

**Vose's Knowledge:**
- Vose signed annual sheet every year
- "A man who signs a sheet has had the sheet in front of him"
- What Vose made of the station gain, never told Teague

**Teague's Final Statement:**
- "That is the whole of the station account as I kept it. The figures are in the ledgers at Ashlin, in ink, dated, and anybody who wishes to quarrel with this chapter is at liberty to go and read them, which is more than most of the quarrelers have ever done."

#### Ivy-Teague Consistency Check
- Teague's 1905–1945 books show 44 million pounds total
- Ivy's 25-year affected period (1898–1922) shows 40 million pounds
- Difference: 4 million pounds over ~10 additional years (probably 1923–1932) at ~400K/year
- Consistent: Teague covered wider period; Ivy focused on fully-affected years
- Station gain 5–5.25%: identical pattern in both sources
- Neither source refers to the other but both cite same underlying records
