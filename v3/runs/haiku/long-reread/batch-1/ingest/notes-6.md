## Story 1: The Ostrey Hollow Butterfat Testing Scandal — VERIFIED COMPLETE

### Three Independent Sources (No Cross-Citation)

**Source 1: Merle Strawn, State Dairy Inspector**
- Authority: Official government circuit books, 29 years of inspections (1907–1936) on the Ordell circuit.
- Critical Entry — June 12, 1923: "These measures will not deliver their mark. Condemned the lot, the whole crate of them, and told the manager to order new. Glass is glass and I did not weigh what it has cost them."
- Verification — October 1923: "New glass in use since July. Ran the new pipettes against the burette. They deliver their mark. Scales true."
- Result — 1924: "Tests improved. Their book gives the year at 3.80%" — exactly matching Ivy's calculated true average.
- Closure — 1926: State discontinues burette program.
- Final Inspection — March 1936: "Nothing to note anywhere and nothing left to say about it" — scandal buried institutional history.

**Source 2: Ivy Keddie (Family Witness + Physical Evidence)**
- Crate of condemned pipettes (June 1923 date stamped).
- Burette measurements taken March 1958–1959, 35+ years after dismissal.
- Measured true delivery: 17.6 cc nominal vs. 16.72 cc delivered pipettes.
- Calculated true average: 3.80%.
- Ledgers from Teague confirming test records pre-scandal.

**Source 3: Bulletin 471 (USDA Publication)**
- Standards for butterfat testing procedure and equipment specifications.
- Validates baseline expectations for correct test results.

### Corroboration Chain

1. **Date Alignment**: Strawn's June 12, 1923 condemnation exactly matches Ivy's "June 1923 Condemned measures" notation.
2. **Replacement Timeline**: Strawn "New glass in use since July"; Ivy's measurements show "July 10" as the breakpoint when tests resume correct levels.
3. **Correct Average**: Strawn's 1924 record "Their book gives the year at 3.80%" matches Ivy's burette-derived calculation (17.6 cc ÷ 16.72 cc = 1.049, yielding 3.80% corrected).
4. **Independent Authority**: Neither source cites the other. Strawn relied on official inspection records and burette measurements; Ivy relied on family records, physical pipettes, and burette measurements conducted decades later.
