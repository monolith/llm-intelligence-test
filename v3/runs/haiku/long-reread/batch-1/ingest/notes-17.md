### NEW MATERIAL: Nyle Grigg's Narrative (r15-long, Retelling 15)

#### Covering Letter, 1919
**Speaker:** Nyle Grigg, farmer of standing, chairman of 1908 investigating committee

**Timeline & Complaint:**
- From ~1902: Ostrey Hollow milk tested consistently below rest of valley
- Not one farmer, not one season — widespread, persistent phenomenon
- Entered annual meeting talk and crossroads store gossip
- Theory: "somebody must be doing it on purpose"
- By winter 1908: board could not ignore it longer

**Payment Context:**
- Payment by test system instituted 1893
- Prior to 1893, test differences were "opinion"; after 1893, became "money"

**The 1908 Investigation:**
- Three-man committee appointed January 1908
- Grigg was chosen because he was NOT a patron of Ostrey Hollow (hauled to Larrow Green instead)
- Sampled herds through **entire calendar year 1908** (every month, barn and pasture, night and morning milk)
- Did not announce sampling dates in advance
- Weighed samples, watched test made twice, tried scales against known weights
- Checked patrons' scales vs station scales, inspected water supply
- General observation: Hessel Bottom herds thin at end of winter, slow to gain flesh in spring, rough coats

#### The 1908 Committee Report (Reprinted 1919)

**Finding on Hessel Bottom:**
- Wet meadow with six farms
- Springy year-round, under water in spring
- Sedge, rush, coarse sour grasses
- Hay cut late, heavy, fed as main feed November to April

**Investigation Details:**
- Visited each of 6 farms three to four times
- Drew and sealed samples themselves
- Observed herds milked, cans filled, hauled to station
- Stood by during testing
- Tested station scales, tried patrons' scales against station's
- Inquired into water supply at each barn

**Critical Finding:**
- **"We found no dishonesty of any kind, in any quarter"** (to be printed in same type as rest)
- Milk was whole, scales honest
- Sampled specifically January, February, March 1908
- **Conclusion: wet hay of Hessel Bottom thins the cream, remedy lies with LAND not with STATION**

**Proposed Remedy:**
- Drain the bottom
- Sweeten it
- Keep stock off in spring's wet
- Feed coarse hay with mixed ration, not alone
- Otherwise "will be grumbling in twenty years' time as they were grumbling when we began"

#### 1919 Corroboration — Grigg's Addendum

**Scientific Support:**
- State Agricultural College at Farland published Bulletin No. 214 (timing: post-1908, before 1919)
- Feeding trial on bog hay under College's eye, weighed rations, no interest in outcome
- **Finding: bog hay depresses butterfat test by two tenths (0.2%) in winter feeding**
- College had never heard of Hessel Bottom, no reason to flatter committee
- Grigg sees this as independent verification: "our finding, arrived at by another road and by better men, three years after we set our names to it"

**Committee Bias Check:**
- Grigg claims abused by "men on both sides" of finding, takes as sign it was honest
- No personal financial interest (not a Hollow patron)
- Appointed specifically for neutrality

**1919 Context:**
- Question reopened by "men who were boys when we began" (1908)
- Committee chose to reprint 1908 report because "same complaint has been raised again this season"
- Grigg adds page because "a report is a dry thing and does not say why it was written"

#### Report Reception
- Report accepted and question closed for eleven years (1908–1919)
- Upon 1919 reopening, "men who have reopened it have brought nothing new before us but their impatience"

## CRITICAL DISCREPANCY: Two Competing Explanations for Ostrey Hollow

### Explanation A: Glassware Defect (from Segment 16, r06–r12)
- **Source:** Strawn, Vaught, Rundle ledger analysis, Frick arbitration brief
- **Mechanism:** Defective pipettes (16.27 cc vs 17.6 cc nominal = 5.01% shortfall)
- **Cause:** Unknown when introduced or discovered
- **Evidence:** 11 recovered defective pipettes (Ivy Keddie, 1959), ledger systematic gain pattern (~5.25%), precise alignment of discrepancy with glassware shortfall
- **Period:** May 1, 1898–June 12, 1923 (or July 12, 1923)
- **Total Loss:** 76K–118K lbs butterfat, $19K–$19.6K

### Explanation B: Wet Hay / Bog Meadow (from r15, Grigg 1908/1919)
- **Source:** Nyle Grigg, 1908 committee chair; 1919 covering letter; 1908 committee report
- **Mechanism:** Hessel Bottom bog meadow (six farms, wet sedge/rush/coarse grass) produces inferior hay, which depresses butterfat test by ~0.2% (per State Agricultural College Bulletin 214)
- **Location:** Hessel Bottom, under/near Ostrey Hollow
- **Period:** From ~1902 (when complaints began) through at least 1919
- **Remedy:** Land drainage, not station culpability
- **Finding:** "No dishonesty of any kind" (1908 report)

### Status of Discrepancy
- **Temporal Overlap:** Both explanations reference same period (c. 1902–1923), same station, same general magnitude of shortfall (~0.2–5% range)
- **Causal Conflict:** Grigg exonerates station operation (weighing, testing, honesty) entirely; attributes shortfall to milk quality input (boggy hay). Glassware defect theory attributes shortfall to measurement instrument failure (Keddie's pipettes).
- **Evidence Split:**
  - Grigg: direct investigation 1908, College confirmation 1919, no dishonesty finding
  - Glassware theory: ledger pattern (Rundle), recovered defects (Keddie), precise calculation match (Vaught/Strawn)
- **Reconciliation Unknown:** Whether boggy hay + glassware defect are both operating factors, or whether one explanation is correct and the other is misattribution, cannot be determined from these sources
