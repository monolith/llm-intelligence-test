## Story 1: The Ostrey Hollow Butterfat Testing Scandal (INDEPENDENT CORROBORATION ADDED)

### New Material from Segment 3

#### Merle Strawn's Circuit Books (r06-long.md, lines 3514–3572)
**Speaker:** Merle Strawn, retired state dairy inspector, "Extracts from twenty-eight circuit books." Covering note dated 1936, reflecting 29 years of inspections (1907–1936) on the Ordell circuit.

**Significance:** Strawn's circuit books provide an independent, official government record that confirms the equipment failure story WITHOUT citing Ivy Keddie's research or any other source. His entries are chronological observations from unannounced inspections.

**Critical Entries:**

- **1907 (First round):** Ostrey Hollow station "opened before my time." Scales true, cold room fair, tester "works slow and clean and reads his own figure without being coaxed."

- **1911–1922 (Regular inspections):** Scales consistently "true." Lactometer shows "no added water" (rules out the 1919 accusation letter's claim). Tests appear normal.

- **1921 (New equipment):** State begins issuing graduated burettes to field men for glass calibration. Strawn receives his in spring 1921.

- **1922 (Skipped check):** Notably, Strawn "did not run their glass against the burette this year, the day being short and the road home being what it was" — i.e., he admits he skipped the crucial test this year and has "put that down because it is what happened."

- **June 12, 1923 (The Smoking Gun):** Strawn runs burettes at Ostrey Hollow:
  > "These measures will not deliver their mark. Condemned the lot, the whole crate of them, and told the manager to order new. Glass is glass and I did not weigh what it has cost them."

- **October 1923 (Verification):** "New glass in use since July. Ran the new pipettes against the burette. They deliver their mark. Scales true."

- **1924 (Proof):** "Tests improved. Their book gives the year at 3.80" — the correct average from Ivy's March 1959 burette measurements (17.6 cc true, 16.72 cc delivered).

- **1925–1934 (Aftermath):** Tests remain improved; "new man at the tester's bench" in 1925 (Ansel had been dismissed April 30, 1925, per Segment 2).

- **1926:** State discontinues burette program: "The burette program is discontinued from this year. The instruments are to be kept by the men who have them but no more are to be issued."

- **Closing note:** Strawn emphasizes his books are factual records only: "I did not put down what I thought about it, and I have not begun now... I never had a theory about a plant and I do not recommend having one. A theory is a thing a man carries out to the platform with him and it gets in the way of the weights."

### Strawn-Ivey Consistency

**Same narrative, different angles:**
1. Both identify the exact same date: June 1923 (Ivy: "June 1923 Condemned measures"; Strawn: "June 12, 1923 Condemned the lot").
2. Both report the same replacement timeline: July 1923 new glass arrives (Strawn: "New glass in use since July"; Ivy: "July 10" measures drop back).
3. Both report 3.80% as the correct average: Strawn's 1924 entry "Their book gives the year at 3.80" matches Ivy's calculated true average from the 1911 anomaly.
4. Neither source cites the other; both rely on independent records (Strawn: pocket books he carried; Ivy: burette measurements, Teague's ledgers, crate of pipettes).
5. Strawn's authority (state inspector, official capacity) adds bureaucratic weight; Ivy's authority (family witness, direct measurement) adds evidentiary weight.

### The 1925 Dismissal and Delayed Vindication

- Ansel dismissed April 30, 1925 (Segment 2).
- Strawn's 1926 entry notes the burette program is discontinued — meaning no further detailed evidence-gathering via that method.
- Ansel died 1934 (Segment 2) — never vindicated in his lifetime.
- Proof arrived via Ivy's measurements in 1958–1959, 24 years after his death, 33 years after his dismissal.
- Strawn's final inspection in March 1936, 11 years before Ivy's proof, records only that "nothing to note anywhere and nothing left to say about it" — the scandal was buried institutional history by then.
