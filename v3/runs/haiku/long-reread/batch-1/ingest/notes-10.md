## Story 1: The Ostrey Hollow Butterfat Testing Scandal — PRIMARY NARRATIVE ADDED

### NEW EIGHTH SOURCE (Retelling 12)

**Source 8: Dell Rundle, schoolteacher, "The Larrow Valley Creamery: Fifty Years" (1939, reprinted 1959)**

- **Frame:** Jubilee pamphlet compiled from recollections and "two winters of asking questions at the store," printed at Larrow Green for Association's 50th year, reissued 1959 with one footnote and author's closing note unaltered
- **Authority:** Rundle has taught school at Larrow Green "a good many years" and listened to valley history "at funerals and in the back of the store"

**Part I. The Men of 1889:**
- 1889: Farmers met at Larrow Green schoolhouse, organized Larrow Valley Cooperative Creamery Association
- Motive: Tired of hauling butter to Ashlin; wanted to own the buyer
- Gideon Vose elected first president: "heavy, quiet, obstinate man who had once thought of the law and thought better of it"
- Vose "wrote the by-laws himself, at his own kitchen table, in a hand that the Association's clerks were still complaining about thirty years later"
- Selby Vose (Gideon's nephew, later manager) said uncle "wrote by-laws the way other men build stone wall — slowly, and to last"
- 1891: Creamery built at Larrow Green on creek below mill; churn set up; valley began to be "paid in cash once a month, which had never happened to it before"

**Part II. Article VII (The Rule That Made the Test the Master):**
- From Gideon Vose's by-laws, **Article VII is central:**
> "Every patron shall be paid for the milk he delivers according to the number of pounds of butter fat contained in it, ascertained by test, and not according to the weight of the milk; and the test of the station where he delivers shall govern."
- Article VII dates from 1891, "the year the churn was set up"
- "Thought a hard rule at the time and there were men who left the Association over it"
- Rundle notes: "It is a just rule." The rule distinguishes good herds from poor ones
- **CRITICAL INSIGHT:** "But mark the last clause, because the valley has had fifty years to learn what it means. *The test of the station where he delivers shall govern.* Not the test of the creamery. Not any test but the one made at his own door. Gideon Vose put that in for convenience and it was convenient for thirty years, and it is the sentence on which the whole trouble at the head of the valley afterward turned."

**Part III. The Station at Ostrey Hollow:**
- The Hollow is seven miles up from Larrow Green
- In the 1890s, the pull down was three hours in mud, impossible in March
- Association built skimming station in 1896: milk weighed, tested, separated on-site; cream went down to Green by wagon; skim went home same morning
- "It was a great convenience and the Hollow was glad of it"
- Building: "plain one, boarded and battened, with the intake room at the road end and a loft over it that I believe has never been cleared out since it was put up; and behind it the creek, which in a wet April comes up to the sill and has twice come over it"

**Part IV. The Trouble at the Head of the Valley:**
- "I would rather not write this chapter and it cannot be left out"
- **From 1902** (NOTE: differs from earlier 1908 date in notes-9): Men of the Hollow complained their milk tested lower at their own station than "the same class of milk tested at the Green. They were not believed."
- **1908:** Association sent committee under Nyle Grigg; committee sampled Hessel Bottom herds through winter and reported "the wet hay of that bottom thinned the cream, and that the remedy lay with the land. The Hollow did not accept it."
- **1919:** Manager circulated warning to Hollow patrons "against adding water to their milk, and after that the two ends of this valley did not speak civilly to one another for six years"
- Rundle witnessed this: "I have had the children of both ends in one schoolroom through those years and I can tell you that it came down to them entire, without a word of it being explained, the way a debt does"
- **VALLEY THEORY:** "It is the settled opinion of the valley that cream left that station by the back door, and the figures went right when the man who kept it did not"
- Rundle's note on credibility: "I set that down as I have heard it set down a hundred times, by men who were there and men who were not, and I have never heard it contradicted at the store"
- **July 12, 1923** (CORRECTS Vose's July date): State inspector condemned station glassware "for reasons of his own that had nothing to do with the quarrel"; new glass put in
- **January 1925:** Hollow patrons petitioned Association
- **Milk intake:** "The station took in, over the years complained of, forty-four million pounds of milk"
- **Loss claim:** "The patrons' loss upon it was nineteen thousand six hundred dollars, and their petition set the figure out to the dollar"
- **Arbitration:** "A board of arbitration sat at Ashlin that spring under Alonzo Frick, attorney, of that town. It heard everybody. It found nothing established, it made an award to the patrons without admitting anything, and it sent this valley home to live with itself, which the valley has done, after a fashion, ever since."

**Part V. Over the Ridge:**
- 1914: Alder Corners Cheese Company opened over Redlow Ridge in Vessey County by Jerome Cudd
- "In the summers a few of our Hessel Bottom men hauled over the mountain to him rather than down to their own station. That was resented here"
- "It is Jerome Cudd's daughter, Rosalie Cudd, who makes the cheese at Alder Corners now" (as of 1939)
- **1959 Footnote by Rundle:** "The Hollow matter has since been taken up again at the college at Farland by Miss Ivy Keddie, who is second cousin to Hazel Brant of Hessel Bottom. Eleven pipettes out of the station's old condemned crate were found still to be in existence. I am too old to rewrite a pamphlet and I refer the reader to the college."
