## Carried Forward — Story 1: Ostrey Hollow Investigation (REVISED WITH CRITICAL DISCREPANCIES)

### Retelling 21 (Cleve Oram, age 77, recorded 1977)

**Who:** Cleve Oram, cream hauler's son; his father drove cream wagon from Ostrey Hollow station for 20+ years; Cleve rode with him from age 9 (~1909 onward).

**The Ostrey Hollow Station Physical Plant:**
- Located on flat by creek where road bends, 7 miles up the valley
- Board building, two floors, shingle roof (needing repair throughout period)
- Covered loading platform (waist-high, for rolling cans level off wagon)
- Intake room: scale, vat, separator inside; smell of warm milk, steam, machine oil
- Skim milk returned on platform for farmers' hogs
- Cream went into cans
- Loft above intake room accessed by ladder with rope-tied trap door

**The Condemned Glassware Crate:**
- Crate in loft under tarpaulin for 35 years
- Original outfit: 5 dozen (60) glass measures for test
- **At discovery (winter 1958): 9 measures remaining** (51 broken over 35 years)
- Cleve had seen it 100 times, put hand on it, knew contents by seeing hay fork retrieval

**The Hearing (1925):**
- Cleve was 25 years old at time (born 1900)
- Three sittings total: first in Ashlin at Cooperative office, two held at station in weigh room (intake room)
- Cleve stood outside on platform in rain with dozen others during afternoon sitting
- Crate was in loft 20 feet above the hearing room (above heads of board members entire time)

**Board Settlement:**
- **Patrons awarded $3,500** (Cleve's father said it, Cleve states father was not wrong on money figures)
- No admission of anything by anybody
- Farmers took it as expected in that era

**Ansel Keddie (the Tester):**
- Employed from before Cleve was born (born 1900) through dismissal in 1925
- Long, dry, quiet man; wore canvas apron with strap over one shoulder
- Had way of holding bottle up to window with arm straight out (memorable to Cleve)
- Not frightening, though most boys were afraid of him
- **Dismissed after 1925 hearing**
- Kept 4–5 cows on small piece of ground
- Did good carpentry work (hung barn door for Cleve's family, hung true for 30 years)
- **Died in the 1930s**
- Funeral well attended; nobody said much

**Cleve's Father's Testimony:**
- Father hauled cream, saw both ends of process daily (station platform + delivery)
- Always said less cream came off platform than went on it
- Said it to Cleve on wagon seat many times
- Said it to men in village (now dead) during Cleve's childhood
- Never said it to Keddie's face
- Never said it under oath (was not asked)
- Believed it and believed it to the end of his life

**Post-1925 Outcome:**
- Keddie went home after 1925, stayed home
- Maintained small farm with inadequate acreage
- Did carpentry work (example: barn door hung 1925 onward, still true 30 years later)
- Died in 1930s
- Funeral attendance suggests community respect despite official judgment against him

**Ivy Keddie & Discovery (1958):**
- Described as granddaughter (came back to area)
- Schooled at Farland
- **Winter 1958: went up ladder herself, brought crate down herself**
- Result: "great deal of writing about it"
- **"Sixty-seven thousand pounds of butterfat" over full run of years patrons never paid for**
- Cleve: "You cannot picture it. I have hauled cream all my young life and I cannot picture it"

**Ivy Keddie Family Connections:**
- "Ivy Keddie was Hazel Brant's first cousin once removed"
- The two "not much alike to look at"
- Ivy was Hazel Brant's people too
- **"The Keddies and the Voses were cousins of some sort"** (per Cleve's mother's mother, never heard contradicted at time, but Cleve cannot draw the line on paper)

**Post-1946 Station History:**
- Station closed in 1946 (no work left; milk went down valley in truck by then)
- Building used for hay tools after 1946 (mowers, side rake, out of weather)
- When Cleve last passed: platform gone, roof going, ladder still on wall (Cleve looked)

## CRITICAL DISCREPANCIES WITH PRIOR SOURCES

### Butterfat Shortfall: 67,000 pounds vs 19,600 pounds

**r21 (Cleve Oram, 1977):** "Sixty-seven thousand pounds of butterfat was what they made it, over the whole run of years, that the Hollow men were never paid for."

**notes-25 (from r19, Ashlin Chronicle 1958 + State Bulletin):** "Total butterfat not credited to patrons (26 years): 19,600 pounds"

**Confidence Assessment:** r21 quotes the public outcome ("what they made it") — the college/bulletin conclusion. notes-25 appears to have underreported by a factor of ~3.4x, or r21 figure includes a different time span, different calculation method, or different patron pool. **No clear winner; both are primary-source-adjacent, but r21 is an eyewitness memory of what "they" (the college/bulletin) calculated, while notes-25 claims direct access to r19 original.**

### Total Patron Settlement: $3,500 vs $3,800

**r21 (Cleve Oram, 1977):** "What they gave the patrons at the end of it was thirty-five hundred dollars. That is what my father said and my father was not a man who got a figure wrong when it was money."

**notes-25 (from notes-24, Duncan Keddie 1968):** "Board awards Ansel $3,800 with no admission of guilt"

**Confidence Assessment:** r21 is Cleve's father's memory of patrons' award (third-hand via Cleve). notes-25 references Duncan Keddie's 1968 account but does not specify whether the $3,800 was Ansel's personal share, patrons' collective award, or total combined settlement. The discrepancy of $300 is within memory error range, but the source ambiguity in notes-25 is problematic.

### Vose-Keddie Family Connection: Denied vs Confirmed

**notes-24 (Duncan Keddie, 1968):** "no family connection" between Keddies and Voses, explicitly stated

**r21 (Cleve Oram, 1977):** "The Keddies and the Voses were cousins of some sort... My mother had it from her mother and I never heard it contradicted at the time, though I could not draw you the line on paper."

**Confidence Assessment:** r21 is third-hand hearsay (mother's mother → mother → Cleve), not witnessed genealogy. notes-24 is also second-hand (Duncan's account). Neither source provides a documented line. Cleve's caveat ("I could not draw you the line") acknowledges uncertainty. **The contradiction stands unresolved; Cleve's weaker confidence hedge favors notes-24's explicit denial, but both are suspect.**

### Ivy-Ansel Relationship: Granddaughter vs Great-Niece

**notes-24 & notes-25 genealogy:** Ivy = Duncan's daughter (Duncan = Ansel's son) → Ivy is Ansel's **granddaughter**

**r19 (Ashlin Chronicle 1958, per notes-25 line 102):** Ivy is Ansel's **great-niece**

**r21 (Cleve Oram, 1977):** Refers to Ivy only as "the granddaughter" (Ansel's), **granddaughter**

**Confidence Assessment:** r21 (Cleve, 1977) confirms notes-24's genealogy (granddaughter). r19 (1958) contradicts it (great-niece). The 1958 article may have made an error, or notes-24's lineage is incomplete/misidentified. **Favor granddaughter (r21 + notes-24 consensus); flag r19 as unreliable.**

## Story 1: Reconstruction-Ready Outline

**Title:** "The Ostrey Hollow Glass Defect" or "The Tester's Vindication"

**When:** 1897–1923 (apparatus in use); 1925 (hearing); 1958 (discovery/measurement); 1959+ (historical record)

**Who:** Ansel Keddie (tester), patrons (~20 farmers), board members, State inspector (Strawn), Ivy Keddie (granddaughter), Cleve Oram (delivery-side witness)

**What Happened:**
- 1897: Larrow Valley Co-op ordered defective glassware from Ammon & Sons (72 pipettes, 17.6 cc nominal capacity marked)
- 1897–1923: Ansel Keddie used apparatus to test cream samples; all readings underestimated butterfat due to apparatus delivering ~1.33 cc short per test (~7.55% deficit per sample)
- 1911: Ansel suspected glass defect ("something in the glass") but could not prove it; made no formal record (policy: only record what provable)
- 1923: State inspector condemned glassware; crate moved to station loft
- 1925: Formal hearing; board awarded $3,500 to patrons, dismissed Ansel with no admission of guilt. Ansel never formally exonerated.
- 1925–1930s: Ansel returned to small farm, did carpentry work (well-regarded), died without official vindication
- 1958: Ivy Keddie discovered crate, measured 11 remaining pipettes (of original 60) at State Agricultural College
- 1958–1959: Bulletin published confirming apparatus defect; Ivy's name credited; Ansel posthumously exonerated (but had died ~24 years prior)

**Quantities:**
- Apparatus deficit: ~7.55% short per test (1.33 cc of 17.6 cc)
- Butterfat unpaid: 67,000 pounds (r21) OR 19,600 pounds (notes-25) — **source discrepancy**
- Years affected: 26 years (1897–1923)
- Original glassware: 5 dozen (60) pipettes
- Surviving at 1958 discovery: 9 pipettes

**Documents:**
1. Ammon & Sons invoice, 1897-04-14 (original equipment cost: $77.93)
2. Ansel's test book (impounded 1925; location 1959+: Ordell County Historical Rooms)
3. Strawn circuit notebooks (state dairy inspector; 1923 volume located ~1958)
4. Brant weigh book (Emmet → Roy → Hazel → Ordell County Historical Rooms, 1963)
5. Cudd test book (Jerome Cudd, Alder Corners; copies made 1958; original to Vessey County Historical Society 1970)
6. Condemned glassware (measured March 1958; location 1959+: Ordell County Historical Rooms)
7. State Agricultural College Bulletin No. 471 (published 1958-10, Vaught & I. Keddie, authors)
8. Ashlin Chronicle article (1958-10-03, announcing findings)
