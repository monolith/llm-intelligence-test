## Grader's notes on ambiguity in the key

Three items were close calls where the key is not decisive. All three were resolved in the
solver's favour and are flagged here so the call can be reversed if wanted; reversing all three
would put the total at **60**.

1. **A1.5** — The item bolds three elements (Selby Vose · Gideon's son · 1913) but carries no
   `REQUIRED:` line, and it is not among the six the key names as bundled. The sheet's Section A
   has "Selby Vose (Association manager, 1913 onward)" — correct, and it avoids the 1911 trap —
   but the paternity appears only in Section B, which cannot travel into A. Credited under the
   key's fallback that a list item without a REQUIRED line "wants its core fact". Against it:
   the item's sole cited fact, F009, *is* the paternity.
2. **A2.1** — Same shape. "Glassware borrowed from Larrow Green" is bolded and absent from
   Section A; the station, 4 May 1896 and the seven miles are all present. Credited on the core
   fact.
3. **A3.3** — Credited on the key's explicit escape clause: "a solver that gives the two figures
   anywhere in Section A has earned it." Both 3.61 and 3.80 appear repeatedly in Section A. But
   the summary leaf itself is never mentioned, and the step the item is about — 1896 at 3.80 on
   borrowed glass against 3.61 from 1898 on the Tarnet crate — is absent; the sheet uses the two
   figures only as recorded-vs-true averages.

One further reading affects Section E: **E-a was treated as closed to the key's sixteen tabled
rows.** The key says explicitly that E-b is open to all twenty-three near-tie pairs but gives
E-a and E-c fixed tables, which reads as an enumeration. On an open reading of E-a, the sheet's
resolution of Hazel Brant's 1921 condemnation date (documentary notebook over a 1981 memory)
would add 1 point, putting the total at 64.

## Profile

The signature is the key's own "fluent-model" pattern: a high A (45/50) beside a low E (3/8) and
six asserted abstentions. The sheet reconstructs the eight histories fluently and does the
interlocking arithmetic almost perfectly (C 13/14, all four money and fat chains correct), but it
arbitrates by weighing narrators impressionistically rather than by document or derivation, and
it does not stop talking where the sources stop. Its two best moves — breaking the 16.27/16.72
and $3,500/$3,800 pairs by naming both carriers and the breaker — show it can do the work; it
simply did not do it sixteen more times. Section A losses cluster in A2 and A4, both stories where
the missing facts turn out to exist in the sheet but filed in C, E or F, outside Section A's scope.
