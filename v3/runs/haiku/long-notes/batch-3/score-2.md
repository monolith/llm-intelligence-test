## What went wrong

**It swallowed 16.27.** The single most expensive error in the corpus. Every downstream number
follows it off the cliff: ratio 0.9244 instead of 0.95, true average 3.905 instead of 3.80,
shortfall 0.295 instead of 0.19, "the same fixed fraction (5.76%)", and 94.24 percent of marked
capacity carried all the way into the 150-word summary. That costs A8.4, A8.5, F1, G1.3 and a
corruption deduction — and it does so while the same sheet elsewhere prints 3.80, 0.19, 40 million
lb and 76,000 lb correctly, without ever noticing that its own headline ratio cannot produce them.

**Section A is thin because the content is filed in F.** Frick, the exhibit list, the three
explanations, the February 1958 find, the 40-million-pound arithmetic — all of it is in Section F,
where Section A cannot draw on it. The reconstructions themselves are mostly atmosphere: a
carpentry narrative for Story 7 and a discovery scene for Story 8, with the load-bearing facts
elsewhere. Story 7 also contradicts itself on Keddie's age within four paragraphs (fifty-four, then
sixty-six; he was sixty-four).

**It found conflicts but never found a pair.** Six conflicts resolved correctly with methods
stated — enough to cap E-a at 3 — but the run's own near-tie section concludes there are none,
after dismissing its single candidate as "not independent narrators." That is exactly backwards:
the corpus holds twenty-three genuine pairs, and the run resolved four of them (12 June, $3,800,
nine pipettes, 1 May 1897) without ever noticing that each wrong value had two carriers. E-b = 0.
It also explicitly reports no narrator self-refutation, which zeroes E-d.

**It answered all four abstention questions it touched.** A07 gets a flat "No." A05 gets "Ansel
Keddie suspected the glass." A04 is asserted twice as a reground mold at Tarnet. A06 is asserted in
passing — "a borrowed measure from another station." A10 is never raised at all. Four definite
assertions where the sources decide nothing: −8, the largest single line on the sheet.

**Three planted values asserted as fact:** 16.27 cc (NT-1), five dozen pipettes (NT-15, in Story 8,
while Section F says six dozen), and Strawn's lactometer "from 1908" (NT-23, canon 1907).

**What it got right.** The three decoys are all named, attributed and defeated with specific
evidence — F2, F3 and F4 are clean, and the 1911 nine-week interval is used correctly against the

and got the arithmetic that proves it wrong by one transposed digit, then defended the wrong number
everywhere it mattered.
