# Retention Notes: Segment 1

## Document Overview
Three retellings of a historical dispute (1889-1925) over milk testing practices in a rural valley, with traces extending to 1979. The conflict centered on Ansel Keddie, tester at Ostrey Hollow station, whose milk-test records showed consistently lower butterfat percentages than neighboring patrons' milk. The case resulted in an arbitration award with no admission of wrongdoing, a lasting family rift, and profound stigma affecting two people for decades.

## Cast of Characters and Relationships

**Ansel Keddie** (Tester, Ostrey Hollow station, 1896–1924)
- Ran the skimming station 7 miles up valley from Larrow Green
- Kept meticulous test books in his own hand, handwriting "like print," no blots across 29 seasons
- Married to Orra Keddie
- Denies taking cream: "I have never taken a pound of cream out of that station and there is not a man in this room who thinks I have when he is by himself" (March 1925 statement, revealing his awareness that people suspected him)
- Put out (fired) April 30, 1925, after arbitration award
- Died in the 1930s, still under stigma from the 1919 letter
- His question mark in the test book about the 1911 anomaly stood unanswered for 14 years

**Orra Keddie** (Ansel's wife)
- First cousin of old Mrs. Vose (wife of Association founder)
- Sister of Emmet Brant (largest milk patron, from Hessel Bottom)
- After 1919 letter, rift with her brother Emmet: "they did not speak. Not one word, not at the store, not at a funeral"
- Would walk to Larrow Green rather than pass her brother's lane
- Outlived Ansel and "had it the whole time" — the stigma — for years after his death
- The rift between Ansel and Emmet lasted "years and years" while they lived "within sight of each other's chimney smoke"

**Emmet Brant** (Farmer, Hessel Bottom)
- Largest of seven farmers with rights to Hessel Bottom (wet meadow below hamlet)
- Largest milk patron to Ostrey Hollow station "in every year of which we have a record"
- Brother of Orra Keddie; brother-in-law of Ansel
- "The letter cost the most" to him, since he brought the most milk
- Stopped speaking to Ansel Keddie after 1919: "they did not speak. Not one word"

**Gideon Vose** (Association founder and president)
- Started the Larrow Valley Cooperative Creamery Association in 1889
- First president, held office 22 years until 1911
- Drafted the by-laws (not a lawyer, "read as a farmer wrote them")
- Died in 1917 (could not be asked about Article VII later)
- "Family always thought the valley belonged to them a little"

**Selby Vose** (Association manager, 1913–1938)
- Son of Gideon Vose
- Became manager in 1913
- Signed the 1919 circular letter that triggered the crisis
- Nephew: "old Gideon Vose's nephew"

**Old Mrs. Vose**
- Wife of Gideon Vose
- First cousin of Orra Keddie
- Created family overlap: manager Selby (old Gideon's son) was linked to both the Vose family and the accused tester via the Brant/Keddie connection

**Effie Loomis** (Secretary of Association, 1901–1940)
- Became secretary in 1901 (was 14 in 1889, so only read earlier records)
- Kept minute books in her own hand for 39 years
- Records official award amount: $3,800.00 (not Vira's figure of $3,500)
- Left her position in 1940, wrote her account in 1941
- Never visited Ostrey Hollow station during her tenure: "not one of us ever went up the valley and stood in the Ostrey Hollow station on a working morning to watch the work being done"

**Wilbur Teague** (Bookkeeper, 1905+)
- Kept the ledgers with annual sheets
- Provided figures for 25-year claim period: 40 million pounds of milk, average 1.6 million per year, at 25 cents per pound

**Hobart Sill** (Buttermaker, Larrow Green)
- Had the churn book and cream receiving book
- Never visited Ostrey Hollow station

**Vira Toland** (Narrator, born 1912)
- Daughter of a milk-hauling patron
- Lived her entire life in Ostrey Hollow except two years at Ashlin after marriage
- Recorded account in 1979 by a young man from the county (tape made at her kitchen table, transcribed with her "doublings back" preserved)
- Remembers: "I was seven years old and I remember it the way you remember a smell" (referring to 1919)
- Observed: "It rained the day of that letter, I remember the wet on my father's shoulders when he came in, and I have never in sixty years been able to think about the one without the other"
- "Both of them long in the ground now, and for what" — final remark on the rift between Ansel and Emmet

**Young woman from college (1958)**
- Visited Ostrey Hollow in 1958
- "Nice girl, careful, wrote everything down"
- Hazel Brant's first cousin once removed (thus connected to family both sides)
- Poked around the old (by then abandoned) station building
- Sat where Vira sat, heard the story about the letter in Vira's father's drawer
- Vira wonders: "I don't know what she made of it"

**Hazel Brant**
- Mentioned as relative of the 1958 college visitor
- First cousin (once removed) relationship to that visitor

## Dates and Timeline

**Association founding and creamery construction:**
- 1889: Larrow Valley Cooperative Creamery Association organized at Larrow Green; Gideon Vose elected first president
- 1891: Main creamery built at Larrow Green on Larrow Creek (building still standing at time of Effie's writing, 1941)
- 1892: Article VII adopted at annual meeting (the critical rule about butterfat payment)
- 1896: Ostrey Hollow skimming station opened (May 4: "Ostrey Hollow station. Begun this 4th day of May, 1896. Glass borrowed of Larrow Green until our own comes. A. Keddie")
- Ansel Keddie began keeping test book; station received glass borrowed from Ashlin creamery

**The testing anomaly emerges:**
- 1902: Complaints begin: milk from Ostrey Hollow "did not test with the rest" (first mentioned at Larrow Green)
- 1908: Committee from Association visited, sampled herds through winter, reported that "wet hay of the Bottom thinned the cream, and that the remedy lay with the land"
- Average test over 1896–1924: 3.61 percent butterfat (or within three hundredths of it) every year except 1911

**Critical 1911 incident (measure-related):**
- May 8, 1911: Pipette broke at station; Ansel began using borrowed measure
- Immediately after borrowed measure started: tests jumped to 3.80 percent
- July 10, 1911: Station's own new measure arrived and was put into use; tests dropped back to 3.61 same day
- Ansel recorded both: "Pipette broke this morning. Using a borrowed measure." and "Our own measure in use again from today. Tests are back where they were. ? — A.K."
- The question mark remained unanswered for 14 years (at least until 1925 when he wrote the letter)
- Duration: "nine weeks of 1911" at 3.80 percent

**1919: The crisis letter**
- 1919: Circular letter from Association (signed by manager Selby Vose, Gideon's son) went to nine families in Ostrey Hollow
- Message: "There was water in the cans coming down from Ostrey Hollow"
- Timing: Nineteen nineteen was "the year everything went sour up here" (Vira's assessment)
- Impact: Letter kept in drawers for decades as proof; one woman pinned hers inside her trunk lid
- Effect on Ansel and Emmet: "Before nineteen nineteen those two families ate their Sunday dinners together... After nineteen nineteen they did not speak. Not one word, not at the store, not at a funeral"
- Orra (Emmet's sister, Ansel's wife) would walk to Larrow Green rather than pass her brother's lane
- "Years and years those two lived within sight of each other's chimney smoke and never said good morning"

**The mysterious improvement:**
- One summer after 1919 (no date given): "All at once, one summer, the figures came up and stayed up, and nobody ever said why"
- Father of Vira: "came in from hauling and said the milk was testing better and he did not know what he had done different, and he had not done anything different, none of them had"
- Vira's observation: "That was the queerest part of the whole business to me even as a child, that the thing corrected itself and no man stood up and gave a reason for it"

**The arbitration case (1925):**
- 1923 June: State's inspector condemned crate of measures at Ostrey Hollow station; new ones ordered in place
  - Note: This event was NOT mentioned in the 1925 hearing despite being directly relevant
- January 1925: Patrons of Ostrey Hollow filed petition with Association
  - Complaint: "Their milk had never tested as their neighbors' milk tested and that they had been paid accordingly for a very long time, and they asked to be made whole"
- March 17 – May 4, 1925: Board of arbitration sat at Ashlin under Alonzo Frick, attorney
  - "Three explanations were argued before it. None of the three was accepted"
- Ansel's statement to board, March 17, 1925: Nine sentences, standing
  - Core claim: "I have kept the test book of this station in my own hand since the station opened, and there is not a figure in it that I did not put there... I am an old man and I have no other trade. I have tested as I was taught and as the state man saw me do it, thirty-one times a week for twenty-nine seasons, and I cannot tell you why the figures are what they are. I have never taken a pound of cream out of that station and there is not a man in this room who thinks I have when he is by himself."
- Award (May 4, 1925): "The board having heard the patrons and the officers, and having had before it the books and appliances of the station, finds no cause established. The board awards to the Ostrey Hollow patrons the sum of thirty-eight hundred dollars ($3,800.00), without admission, to be charged to the station account."
  - CONFLICT: Vira says $3,500 split 9 ways; Effie's official records say $3,800.00
  - PARADOX noted by Effie: "Finds no cause established" and then an award of $3,800 in the same sentence
  - Effie: "I have written many things into that book with more pleasure... I took it down as it was given to me and it was not my place to ask how both halves of a sentence could be true at once"

**After the award:**
- April 30, 1925: Ansel Keddie "put out" (fired)
- Spring 1925: Ansel writes unsent letter (never sent, found among his papers)
- Quote from letter: "They found no cause established and they put me out on the last day of the month, and both of those things are true at once, and I have given up trying to hold them in my head together"
- 1925 (unspecified): Did not test a pound that year
- 1930s: Ansel Keddie died, "still under it" — still under the stigma

**Later history:**
- 1938: Office moved; the 1889–1892 minute book was lost in the move ("It was carried out with the rest and it did not arrive")
- 1941: Effie Loomis wrote her "Notes toward a history of the Association" one year after laying down the minute book
- 1958: Young woman from college (Hazel Brant's first cousin once removed) visited Ostrey Hollow
- 1979: Vira Toland's account tape-recorded at her kitchen table; she was then 67 years old

## Conflicts and Oddities

1. **Award amount discrepancy:** Vira says patrons received "$3,500... split up nine ways" (father's share "did not buy a hay rake"). Effie's official records state the award was "$3,800.00" — a $300 difference.

2. **"Finds no cause established" but awards $3,800:** The board simultaneously found no wrongdoing yet awarded substantial compensation. Effie notes this contradiction explicitly and questions how "both halves of a sentence could be true at once."

3. **The 1911 measure incident:** When Ansel used a borrowed measure, tests jumped to 3.80 percent (matching the "normal" level). When his own new measure arrived, tests dropped back to 3.61. This suggests the measure itself, not cream-taking, caused the discrepancy — yet it was never resolved.

4. **Condemned measures not mentioned in hearing:** June 1923 state inspection condemned a crate of measures at Ostrey Hollow station. This was directly relevant to the case but "was never mentioned in the hearing at all... by the patrons, by the officers, or by the board." The board's phrase "books and appliances of the station" is noted by Effie as "a form of words the board used and not a list of anything."

5. **Ansel's language about cream-taking:** His statement "I have never taken a pound of cream out of that station and there is not a man in this room who thinks I have when he is by himself" acknowledges what people say while denying the act — a hedge that undermines his defense.

6. **No explanation for improvement:** Tests suddenly improved one summer after 1919 with no explanation offered by anyone (station, Association, or otherwise). No patron changed practice; conditions remained the same in wet and dry years.

7. **Unanswered question mark:** Ansel's question mark in his 1911 entry about why tests returned to normal when the new measure arrived stood for 14 years unanswered. In 1925 he still could not explain it.

8. **Lost early records:** The 1889–1892 minute book, covering the Association's founding years, was lost in a 1938 office move. This means no one can verify claims about what was resolved in those early years.

9. **Lasting rift despite "no cause established":** Even though the board found "no cause," the stigma stuck. Ansel died under it in the 1930s. Orra outlived him and suffered it for years after. Their family rift with Emmet lasted decades.

10. **The letter as permanent record:** Patrons kept the 1919 letter for decades—one woman pinned it inside her trunk—as tangible proof of the accusation against them, turning a business matter into a permanent shame marker.

## Locations Mentioned

- **Ostrey Hollow:** Valley setting; Ansel's station stood at the "very top of the Hollow where the road bends and then goes to nothing but the ridge"
- **Larrow Green:** Location of Association creamery and main office, 7 miles down valley from Ostrey Hollow
- **Hessel Bottom:** Wet meadow below Ostrey Hollow hamlet; seven farms held rights to pasture and hay; Emmet Brant's farm was the largest
- **Ashlin:** Town where the arbitration hearing was held; also where lawyers lived ("no lawyers in Ashlin" was Vira's father's dry joke)
- **Larrow Valley:** Official name of the Association

## Physical Objects and Records

- **Test book:** Ansel's handwritten record kept from 1896–1924, ruled with straight-edge, five columns (date, patron, pounds, test, fat), no blots, figures "like print"
  - First entry: "Ostrey Hollow station. Begun this 4th day of May, 1896. Glass borrowed of Larrow Green until our own comes. A. Keddie."
  - Had 1922 leaf added with averages: 3.61 (or within three hundredths) for most years except 1911 at 3.80
  - Entry for May 8, 1911: "Pipette broke this morning. Using a borrowed measure."
  - Entry for July 10, 1911: "Our own measure in use again from today. Tests are back where they were. ? — A.K."
- **The 1919 letter:** Printed circular from Association (signed by manager Selby Vose) announcing water in the cans; kept by patrons for decades as proof of accusation
- **Minute books:** Kept by Effie Loomis; 1889–1892 volume lost in 1938 office move
- **Ledgers:** Kept by Wilbur Teague (bookkeeper); contained annual sheets with price and volume data
- **Crate of measures:** Condemned by state inspector June 1923; new measures ordered; this evidence never appeared in 1925 hearing
- **Glass measure/pipette:** Borrowed from Ashlin creamery in 1896, used until Ostrey Hollow station's own arrived spring 1897; broke May 8, 1911

## Summary

This is a case study in blame without proof, stigma without certainty, and a family rift that outlasted the protagonists. The test discrepancy (Ostrey Hollow milk showing consistently lower butterfat) may have stemmed from equipment (the 1911 borrowed-measure incident suggests this), local conditions (wet hay theory), or actual cream-taking. The board never determined which. Yet the letter of 1919 broke a family bond (Ansel and Emmet never spoke again), destroyed reputations (Ansel and Orra were stigmatized for life), and awarded money "without admission" of fault. The 1911 measure incident, which might have explained everything, was condemned in 1923 and forgotten by 1925. Ansel took the secret to his grave in the 1930s, leaving behind a question mark he placed in his own record and never answered.
