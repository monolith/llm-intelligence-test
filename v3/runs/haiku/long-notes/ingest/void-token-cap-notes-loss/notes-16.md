## Segment 16 New Material: THREE CRITICAL RETELLINGS

### Retelling 13 — Alonzo Frick (1946 Arbitration Recollections)

**Source:** Article for yearbook of Ordell County Bar Association, 1946

**Key Points:**
- Arbitration board chairman, lawyer, 40-year trial practice (titles, boundaries, two homicides)
- Ostrey Hollow matter "stayed with me as nothing else ever argued in courthouse"
- Not litigation but arbitration under by-laws; no appeal, no reported decision
- **The complaint**: Larrow Valley Creamery Association pays for butterfat by test (not weight); station where man delivers governs test; leaves dissatisfied man with no remedy except quarrel with figure
- Ostrey Hollow station 7 miles up valley; patrons paid average 3.16 test vs rest of valley better; same cows, 25-year steady difference
- **Petition and board**: January 1925 (three-person board); first session March 17, 1925 at Ashlin office; second and third sessions at Ostrey Hollow itself in station's weigh room (Frick's decision to see physical plant)
- **Board saw**: Scales tried; sampling and test done same way twice; no hands hiding
- **Three explanations** (no finding against Keddie from any):
  1. **Water** (Selby Vose, manager): Hollow's milk thinned, water in cans oldest trick; station cannot test what not there; stated without heat or proof
  2. **Cream theft** (Wilbur Teague, bookkeeper): Tester taking cream, writing low tests to cover; put accountant-style (answered about how account would look if man helping himself, then said made no charge) — no cross-examination reaches such witness
  3. **Hay** (1908 committee report read into record): Bog meadow at Hessel Bottom, wet ground, coarse hay off it, cows eating it winter; old valley opinion, blamed nobody in room
- **Ansel Keddie statement** (March 17): Tested since station opened 1896, kept book own hand every season; tested as taught and state man saw, 31 times week for 29 seasons; could not tell board why figures what they were; never took pound cream
- **Frick's belief**: Keddie accurate, denial did him no good; man who cannot account for figure presumed by everyone to be figure's author
- **Private corridor comment** (Frick records): Keddie told him believed patrons at fault but would not say in room; lived among them 30 years, would not accuse neighbors; went to grave accusing nobody, suspected nobody (NOT)
- **Dismissal and award**: April 30, 1925 (four days before award), Association dismissed Keddie; no finding against him from board; board had not concluded he done anything; simply let go while board still sitting — "I have never been able to think well of Association for it"
- **May 4, 1925 award**: "No cause established"; awarded Ostrey Hollow patrons $3,800 without admission; patrons' own reckoning 67,000 pounds butter fat lost over years complained of; nothing brought forward to disturb that figure; "We were not able to say who owed it or why, and we said so. An award without a finding is unsatisfactory thing"
- **Exhibits**: (1) Keddie's test book 4 May 1896–31 December 1924 in own hand; (2) receiving book; (3) Emmet Brant's private weigh book begun 1 January 1897; (4) state dairy inspector circuit notebook year 1923
- **Reflection**: Board can only decide between accounts put to it; three accounts urged by standing men; each would have been believed by half valley; not one proved; answer may have been in room but not in any three stories; lawyer understanding chemistry might have found it (Frick did not understand chemistry)

### Retelling 14 — Hobart Sill (1934 Retirement Remarks)

**Source:** Remarks recorded at Larrow Green creamery day he left, 1934

**Key Points:**
- Buttermaker 41 years (1893–1934); never sent tub of butter would not eat himself; office cannot say same about anything it sent out
- **Cream quality**: Hollow cream ran heavy (more fat in cans than papers said bought); every year ran heavy; weighed it myself most years; platform man weighed, I weighed over again when came into cold room; never took another man's figure; cans off, on scale, into ice, book ruled signed before coat off
- **Receiving book**: "You may go and look at receiving book if they will let you at it, and you will find my hand on every page from ninety-three, and you will find Hollow's cream coming in heavy on page after page after page, in my own writing, for as long as I have been in this room"
- **Cream properties**: Thick thing, clings, separates, any man tells you he can put figure on cream to pound and hold it never had arm in can to elbow; ran heavy, everybody's does; never made anything of it; nobody ever asked me to
- **Local milk test**: Larrow Green own milk 3.78 taking one year with another; knew because made butter; buttermaker knows cream worth before handed paper
- **Hollow milk quality**: Really 3.85 and no better; wet ground, good cows, never word against cows; if figures they were paid on came under 3.85, somebody up hill road was helping cans along; said so at time, say it now
- **Water theory**: You cannot tell me nine families all got honest in one summer; water in those cans; one summer along 1920s figures came up and stayed up; nothing on earth changed but season; men do not suddenly pour better milk from same cows same barns; something got stopped, something got said to somebody (never knew what, never asked)
- **Complaint history**: From about 1902 grumbling from Ostrey Hollow; not one man, not one season; went on and grew; talk at annual meeting, crossroads stores; took shape such things take (somebody must be doing it on purpose); winter they were named board could not let it alone; manager put out paper 1919 circular warning Ostrey Hollow patrons about water; "thought well of him for it. It was only plain thing that office ever did, and of course they hated him for it up the Hollow, and of course it changed nothing, because a paper never emptied a can"
- **Office critique**: Papers; Association paper for everything and butter for nothing; men come collar tell him churn running slow when could not tell which end cow gets up first; made butter took premiums three states; not one man office ever came see how done; every one could tell half cent what fat worth (27 cents pound)
- **First season at Hollow**: May 1896 Ostrey Hollow opened; no outfit own; ordered not come; ready start milk, talk holding off season; said no, can have mine; took own bench this room made outfit from own glass own (used and knew); went valley wagon; opened on it; own came spring after, sent back one piece chipped never said word
- **First season volume**: Nine hundred thousand pounds May end year (not whole one); good deal milk hill station starting nothing; biggest thing happened Ostrey Hollow; glad had hand in it
- **Evaluation**: Sixty-four years old, thought about butter; never went up there but twice life; took cream off platform, made into butter, butter good—good any, better most; never season not good; whatever Hollow doing cans, fat came down hill; went out room good butter; forty-one years; would like go home

### Retelling 15 — Nyle Grigg (1919 Covering Letter to 1908 Committee Report)

**Source:** Farmer's covering letter of 1919 to reprinted report of investigating committee of 1908

**1919 Covering Letter:**
- Asked to let 1908 committee report printed again; Ostrey Hollow question reopened this season by men boys when report made
- Consents willingly; nothing in report to take back; ask leave add page (report dry thing does not say why written)
- Eleven years brought light from quarter had no light give in 1908
- **State of things (1902 onward)**: Grumbling from Ostrey Hollow that milk station tested under rest of valley; not one man, not one season; went on grew; got into talk annual meeting, crossroads stores; took shape (somebody must be doing on purpose)
- Winter they were named board could not let alone without seeming have interest in letting alone; three were named investigate; "never in my life been given duty I liked less"
- Payment by test 1893 meeting; from that year difference stopped being opinion, became money; known Hollow men since young, bought hay off two of them
- **Committee work (1908)**: Went as plain farmers; sampled herds through whole 1908—every month; barn pasture; night's milk morning's; did not let man know which day coming; weighed; watched test made watched again; put what found in report below; will not repeat
- **Frick's fairness statement**: "had no interest whatever in answer. I am not patron that station. I haul to Larrow Green and always have, and whatever Hollow was paid or not paid never put cent into my pocket nor took one out of it. I was chosen for work on that account and for no other, and I have been abused for finding by men both sides of it, which I take to be fair sign that finding was honest one."
- **College evidence**: "When we wrote, had nothing go on but our own eyes and general opinion practical men, and I knew committee of farmers saying wet hay thins cream would be told farmers will say anything own land. But State Agricultural College at Farland has since published its Bulletin No. 214, which was feeding trial upon bog hay carried out under College's own eye, with weighed rations and no interest in outcome. That trial found bog hay to depress butter fat test by two tenths in winter feeding. Two tenths, gentlemen, out of College had never heard of Hessel Bottom and had no reason earth to flatter us. There is our finding, arrived at by another road and by better men, three years after we set our names to it."
- **Remedy and conclusion**: "I am content to let matter rest where College and this committee have jointly put it. The remedy lies with land and not with station. Drain that bottom, sweeten it, keep stock off it in wet of spring, and feed coarse hay off it with mixed ration and not by itself, and Hollow will test with rest of us. Go on as they are going and abuse man who reads their figures for them, and they will be grumbling in twenty years' time as they were grumbling when we began, and no better off."
- Signed respectfully, NYLE GRIGG, Larrow Green

**1908 COMMITTEE REPORT:**
> REPORT OF THE COMMITTEE APPOINTED TO INQUIRE INTO THE COMPLAINTS OF THE PATRONS OF THE OSTREY HOLLOW STATION
> 
> To the President and Directors of the Larrow Valley Cooperative Creamery Association.
> 
> Your committee, appointed in January last, respectfully reports as follows. The complaint before us is of some years' standing and is in substance this: that the milk delivered at the Ostrey Hollow skimming station is returned by the test at a figure below that of milk delivered elsewhere in this valley, and that the patrons of that station are in consequence paid less for the same labor and the same cows than their neighbors are paid. Since payment by test came in among us and the pound of butter fat took the place of the pound of milk as the measure of a man's month, a difference of this kind is no longer a matter of opinion. It is a matter of money, and it comes out of the same pocket every month, and your committee has approached it in that light and not as a quarrel.
> 
> Hessel Bottom, where six farms cut hay and turn out stock, is a wet meadow lying along the creek under Ostrey Hollow. Its ground is springy the year round and is under water in part every spring. Its herbage is sedge, rush, and the coarse sour grasses that come in on such land, and its hay is cut late and heavy and is fed as the main dependence of those farms from November to April. There is no better winter keep to be had there. We say this without reproach to any man on that bottom; the land is what it is, and they took it as their fathers left it to them.
> 
> Your committee visited each of the six farms, some of them three and four times. We drew our samples ourselves and we sealed them ourselves. We saw the herds milked, we saw the cans filled, and we saw them hauled to the station and weighed in at the intake, and we stood by while the test was made and saw nothing whatever in the doing of it to remark upon. We tried the station scales against a known weight and we tried the patrons' own scales against the station's. We inquired into the water supply at each barn and satisfied ourselves upon it.
> 
> Your committee further observed the general condition of the stock upon that bottom, as men who have kept cattle all their lives are entitled to observe it. The herds are thin in the flank at the end of the winter feeding and slow to come to their flesh in the spring. Their coats are rough later in the season than the coats of hill herds. They do well enough in flow, for the wet land is generous of milk, but it is our opinion, formed on the ground and not out of a book, that what such land gives in quantity it withholds in quality, and that a cow cannot put into her pail what her keep has never put into her.
> 
> We report that we found no dishonesty of any kind, in any quarter, and we wish that finding to be printed in the same type as the rest.
> 
> We have sampled the herds of Hessel Bottom through January, February and March. The milk is whole and the scales are honest. It is our opinion that the wet hay of that bottom is what thins the cream, and that the remedy lies with the land and not with the station.
> 
> NYLE GRIGG, chairman, and the committee.

Report received and accepted, question closed for eleven years — until this season, men who reopened it brought nothing new but their impatience.
