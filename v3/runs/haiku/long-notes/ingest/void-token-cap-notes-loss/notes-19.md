### Complete Cast and Relationships

**Ansel Keddie** (Tester, Ostrey Hollow station, 1896–1934): Ran skimming station 7 miles up valley from Larrow Green; kept meticulous test books in handwriting "like print"; married to Orra Keddie; denied taking cream (March 1925 statement); **fired April 30, 1925**; **died 1934** (9 years after dismissal, having lived under the imputation); **exonerated 1958** (33 years after dismissal, 24 years after death). Born 1861. Went to the station "from the year it opened" (1889 or start of Association). Summer 1911: suspected "something in the glass" but did not dare put it in book, wrote question mark instead; would not accuse it outright despite 14 years of suspicion. Man who would not put down what he could not prove; "most expensive virtue anybody in that valley ever had."

**Ivy Keddie** (Granddaughter, historian, State Agricultural College at Farland): Ansel's granddaughter; father Duncan Keddie (teacher at Ashlin); presented findings to Ordell County Historical Society 1959; **conducted scientific forensic analysis of pipettes**: measured nine pipettes delivering 16.72 cc instead of 17.6 cc (95% accuracy); published findings Bulletin No. 471 with Professor Vaught (October 1958); created measurement worksheet March 9, 1958; wrote to Rosalie Cudd requesting test figures 1958; second cousin to Hazel Brant; borrowed Emmet Brant's weigh book from Roy Brant 1958; found defective glassware crate in loft of Ostrey Hollow weigh room (under tarpaulin, chaff) February 1958; took glass back east and measured it (winter of 1958).

**Duncan Keddie** (1894–present 1968, son of Ansel; teacher at Ashlin; born 1894): Wrote "Notes for the family" 1968 with addition 1970 to set down for daughter (Ivy) and grandchildren who everyone was and where every book went; kept to what he knew, not made anybody better than he was; witnessed father's 1911 suspicion (summer, at supper and by door—uncharacteristic for father); **witnessed April 30, 1925 dismissal** while father still alive; noted father dismissed four days before board finding (dismissal May 4, 1925 while board sat); **father died 1934 with imputation on him**, in county among men he weighed milk for since before their fathers died. Teacher of arithmetic, set out family arithmetic "in the way I should have wanted it set out for me." First cousin to Roy Brant (children of Orra and Emmet, brother/sister).

**Wilbur Teague** (Bookkeeper, 1905–1945): Kept Association books 40 years; **invented "station-gain" account 1905**: line ran **5.25% of actual cream yearly** (critical figure matching later evidence); warned Selby Vose 1916, told to "let it alone"; testified 1925 arbitration hearing; wrote memoir "The Books of the Association" 1948; recorded board award "finds no cause established" yet $3,800 awarded.

**Orra Keddie** (Ansel's wife, 1868–1948): First cousin of old Mrs. Vose (NO—see below for correction); full sister of Emmet Brant; married Ansel 1889; rift with brother after 1919 letter; would not speak; outlived Ansel by 14 years; died 1948 at 82; kept every letter from Ansel; never spoke of business except once yearly in April.

**Emmet Brant** (Farmer, Hessel Bottom, 1854–1929): Largest milk patron; brother of Orra Keddie; stopped speaking to Ansel 1905 (14+ year silence); **kept meticulous weigh book beginning January 1, 1897 (or 1900?)** until death 1929 (32 years); **scale and station figures agreeing**; largest piece of Hessel Bottom; spring at upper end ran cold enough to ache wrist in July; cans stood there. Largest patron/largest hauler of milk into station.

**Roy Brant** (Son of Emmet, 1888–1962): Continued father's weigh book after Emmet died 1929 (exact year unclear); aged 67 in 1955; loaned father's book to Ivy Keddie 1958; mother read water-warning letter twice and burned it in stove; attended 1926 hearing, never returned to Association meetings thereafter; found records took him a long while to reconcile; at death 1962 passed all records to daughter Hazel Brant. First cousin to Duncan Keddie.

**Hazel Brant** (Roy's daughter, Ivy Keddie's second cousin, 1981): Last keeper of Emmet's weigh book; custodian records; now housed at historical rooms in Ashlin; poor with years but precise with quantities; notes poor memory but can cite 1910 figure with certainty: 200,000 pounds even delivered to station, paid on 3.42% test; knew 0.19 point gap (nineteen hundredths) every month, every season, flat as floor; knew Ansel Keddie went up to station 1895, stayed until they turned him out; Orra half-sister to Emmet (by father's second wife); Duncan Keddie (Ansel's son) and Roy first cousins; Ivy second cousin Hazel; glassware crate from Tarnet came 1899, sat in loft over weigh room; state inspector condemned glass 1921 (measured with instrument, said would not deliver marked amount); hearing 1926 (Roy drove down, board heard everybody, found nothing, let Ansel go); Ansel did not live to see exoneration.

**Gideon Vose** (Association founder, president 1889–1911): Started Larrow Valley Cooperative Creamery Association 1889; held office 22 years until 1911; died 1917; kept Jerseys testing 4.56% butterfat 1910; wrote by-laws at kitchen table in "heavy, obstinate hand"; authored Article VII on butterfat payment and testing.

**Selby Vose** (Association manager, 1913–1938): Son of Gideon Vose; became manager 1913; signed 1919 circular warning against water addition; signed all annual sheets; delivered 1926 annual address defending settlement; **theory: water added at Ostrey Hollow**; tests rose when scrutinized (1924 average 3.90 vs long-term 3.61); 13 years as manager by 1926; sent nine warning letters about water (February 1919) to nine families on that bottom; Roy's mother read hers twice and put it in the stove; **nine families, six farms**; answered by none, which in that valley is a shout; only plain thing office ever did; of course they hated him for it up the Hollow; of course it changed nothing because paper never emptied can.

**Effie Loomis** (Secretary, 1901–1940): Became secretary 1901.

**Rosalie Cudd** (Alder Corners Cheese Company, 1958): Received letter from Ivy Keddie requesting test figures; provided correspondence; daughter of Jerome Cudd (founder of factory); took on factory 1941; sent Ivy copied test figures 1958.

**Leland Vaught** (Professor): Co-published Bulletin No. 471 with Ivy Keddie, October 1958.

**Alonzo Frick** (Arbitration board chairman, lawyer): 1946 memoir; sat three times (March 17, March 18-19, Ostrey Hollow sessions 1925); described three explanations presented by board: water (Vose), cream theft (Teague), hay (1908 committee); no finding against Keddie; dismissed April 30, 1925 while board sitting; award May 4, 1925 $3,800 without cause established; exhibits: Keddie's test book (4 May 1896–31 December 1924), receiving book, Emmet Brant's private weigh book (begun 1 January 1897), state dairy inspector circuit notebook (1923); Keddie accurate, denial did him no good; man who cannot account for figure presumed by everyone to be figure's author; Keddie told him believed patrons at fault but would not say in room; lived among them 30 years, would not accuse neighbors; went to grave accusing nobody, suspected nobody; private corridor comment (Frick records).

**Nyle Grigg** (Farmer, Larrow Green, committee chairman 1908): 1919 covering letter to reprinted 1908 report; not patron of Hollow (hauled to Larrow Green); committee had "no interest whatever in the answer"; abused on both sides for finding, sign of honest work; State Agricultural College Bulletin No. 214 (feeding trial on bog hay) proved 0.2% butterfat depression; committee found no dishonesty but opinions on wet hay and remedy at land; charges applied to land management, not station; went as plain farmers 1908, sampled herds through whole year—every month, barn pasture, night's milk morning's; did not let man know which day coming; weighed, watched test made, watched again; bog meadow hay thins cream 0.2%; remedy land-based, not station-based; knew hollow men since young, bought hay off two of them; one summer along 1920s figures came up and stayed up; nothing on earth changed but season; men do not suddenly pour better milk from same cows same barns; something got stopped, something got said to somebody.

**Hobart Sill** (Buttermaker, ret., 1934): 1934 remarks on retirement after 41 years at creamery (1893–1934); cream from Hollow ran heavy (more fat in cans than papers), every year; weighed it myself most years; platform man weighed, I weighed over again when came into cold room; never took another man's figure; cans off, on scale, into ice, book ruled signed before coat off; receiving book shows hand on every page from 1893; Hollow cream coming in heavy page after page after page; ran heavy, everybody's does; never made anything of it; nobody ever asked me to; local milk tested 3.78 taking one year with another (knew because made butter, buttermaker knows cream worth before handed paper); Hollow milk really 3.85 and no better (wet ground, good cows, never word against cows); figures they were paid on came under 3.85, somebody up hill road was helping cans along; said so at time, say it now; six weeks in June/July flush of season when every herd in county at best and stock off wet ground on aftermath (when testing broken off 1911 for six weeks); first season 900,000 pounds May-end of year; forty-one years, thought about butter; made into butter went out room good butter; forty-one years; would like go home.

**Dr. Roland Kepp** (V.S., veterinarian, Ashlin, thirty years practice, 1926): Paper read before Ordell County Farm Club 1926, "The bog meadows and the milk"; wet meadow = distinct chemistry, not just water; drainage wanting, year's growth does not rot down, lies sour, soil acid, poor lime and phosphates; sweet grasses crowded out by sedge/rush/coarse tussock; that herbage carries bulk and water, **not lime or phosphorus in quantity**; cut late because ground cannot be got onto early, late cutting takes out what little was there; cow in milk = manufactory on narrow margin, obliged to find milk material somewhere; if ration short minerals, robs own frame; consequences visible in wet-meadow herd: stock thin over loin, rough coat, slow condition in spring, ready subjects every digestion disorder fall, more often called to wet land than any other, breed later, feet worse; will milk—wet land generous of flow—but fat is dearest thing cow makes, first economy when pressed; kept eyes open thirty years, **wet bottoms yield milk poorer in fat by fifth of point** (0.20+, approximately); hill herds vs bottom herds show difference farm for farm **not on breed, man, or barn—on ground**; **Hessel Bottom completest example wet meadow in county**, six farms cut hay/pasture stock, lies along creek, springs out at foot of slope run year round, part under water every spring, not fit walk on until May, hay sedge hay, winter dependence very nearly whole of it; **Ostrey Hollow patrons complaint perfectly just**, quarter century (25 years), **figure of gap 0.21** (twenty-one hundredths point) between that station and rest of valley; **committee practical men 1908 found herds sound, milk whole, scales honest, laid cause to hay of that bottom**; **matter before committees since, before boards, not settled, will not be settled by men shouting**; **settled already, in literature**; **State Agricultural College Bulletin No. 214: College fed bog hay under control, measured it, found butter fat test depressed by two tenths** (0.2) in winter feeding; **whole case**: two tenths from College, fifth of point in field, twenty-one hundredths station complaining, three agree so nearly — reasonable man requires more?; **objection: summer 1911 testing broken off six weeks (their own apparatus failed, borrowed one used, figures stood higher before/after)** — six weeks June/July flush season every herd at best, stock off wet ground, aftermath; astonished anybody found argument on six weeks summer figures; could produce six weeks any year prove anything; **remedy not mystery, not cheap**: drain bottom, open old ditches cut new, lime ground again, sow down when carry seeding, keep stock off spring wet (hoof does more harm day than season repair), never feed hay alone, feed mixed ration mineral supplement as College directs; **fat twenty-seven cents pound through years**, fifth point off every hundredweight farm ships yearly in and out, pay for great deal tile; **discussion from floor** asked whether difference might not be cows rather than land, bottom farms poorer men poorer stock; Kepp replied considered rejected it, some best-bred cattle county stand on wet land, some indifferent cattle hills test well, man improve herd twenty years never get fat up while ground sour; another member asked whether trouble might lie in station rather than bottom, said in valley years; Kepp replied no interest that quarrel, no competence, never read test, would not know how, when cause found in soil, herbage, condition stock, College trial, every wet bottom county alike, not good sense looking for second cause in building; chairman thanked Kepp, paper printed for members.

### Critical Archival Evidence: Dell Rundle's Ledger Note

**Source:** Dell Rundle, jubilee pamphlet (1939, reprinted 1959) — Archival Addition While in Press.

While Rundle's manuscript was being typeset, he examined Association's old ledgers and revised Chapter IV:

> "Ostrey Hollow: butter fat credited to patrons on station test, **57,760 lb**. Butter fat received at Larrow Green in station cream, **60,800 lb**. Station gain, **3,040 lb, five and a quarter per cent**, as usual. Carried to the general account."

**Rundle's explicit revision:** "I must go back on Chapter IV. Cream stolen at the Hollow would have reached the Green light. It reached the Green heavy, every year, and I cannot make my paragraph stand. I leave it as written and correct it here." — Dated D. RUNDLE, Larrow Green.

**Significance:** Systematic overweight receipts at central facility **every year**; contradicts single-tester theft theory; **supports pipette defect explanation confirmed by Ivy Keddie's 1958 analysis**: 16.72 cc vs 17.6 cc (exactly 95% accuracy = 5% underfilling, matching 5.25% station gain).
