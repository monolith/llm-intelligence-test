# Circuit Court in Session: A Full Docket at Vandermeer — The Vandermeer Weekly Sentinel (1953)

Judge Thurlow Vane opened the fall term of the Elkhorn County Circuit Court on Monday last, the 14th of September, with what the clerk's office describes as the fullest docket in six years, forty-one cases entered for hearing over the two weeks the term is expected to run. Our readers who could not attend in person will find below a summary of the more notable matters disposed of through Thursday.

**State versus Norval Steeg, disorderly conduct.** Steeg, of the Millrace neighborhood, was fined $15.00 and costs of $4.20 for an altercation at the feed store on the 2nd of August in which, witnesses agreed, he threw a sack of chicken feed at one Ambrose Duckett over a disputed weight on the store's scale. Duckett was not charged, the court finding that whatever provocation existed did not excuse the response but noting also that Duckett's own language, as reported by two witnesses, had not been temperate. Steeg paid the fine in cash at the clerk's window before leaving the courthouse.

**Greer Milling Company versus Osgood Farrand, debt.** Judgment for the plaintiff in the sum of $186.40, representing feed and seed sold to the defendant on credit between March and June of this year, plus interest from the date of the last item at 6 per cent, plus costs. The defendant did not contest the amount owed but pleaded inability to pay in full at once; the court granted an order permitting payment in four monthly installments of not less than $50.00 each, the first due the 1st of October, execution to issue against his property should any installment be missed by more than ten days.

**In re the estate of Ambrose Duckett's late father** (a probate matter placed on the circuit docket owing to a contested claim exceeding the probate court's ordinary jurisdiction in this county): the claim of one Verlin Hasch for $310.00, said to be due for carpentry work on the decedent's barn in the year before his death, was denied by the jury after a trial of one day, the jury apparently crediting the family's evidence that the work had been paid for in full at the time, in cash, though no receipt was produced by either side. Costs taxed against the claimant, $22.00.

**State versus Delia Fought, petty theft.** The defendant, charged with taking a length of dress goods from the dry goods counter of the Vandermeer Mercantile without payment, valued by the store at $2.40, was found guilty on her own admission and fined $5.00, the court remarking from the bench that it took into account both the small value involved and the defendant's clean record, and that a harsher penalty was not warranted on a first offense of this character, though the court would view a second offense differently.

**Fought versus Fought, divorce.** The bill of the wife, Delia Fought (the same defendant as above, the two matters heard on successive days, a coincidence the court noted without comment), against her husband, Marlin Fought, on grounds of abandonment and failure to provide, was granted, the court finding that the husband had been absent from the county, by his own admission through counsel, for upward of fourteen months without communication or support. Custody of the two children, ages six and nine, was awarded to the wife, with no order for support entered, the husband's whereabouts and means being unknown to the court at this time.

**State versus three defendants, obstruction of a public road.** Norval Steeg (see above), together with his brother Delmore Steeg and one Ashbel Corum, were each fined $8.00 and costs for having placed a fallen tree across the Millrace road on the night of the 19th of July, apparently as a prank connected with a wedding celebration, the tree having blocked the road for some six hours the following morning until removed by the road overseer. The court accepted the defendants' plea that no malice was intended but noted that a milk wagon and the rural mail carrier were both delayed by the obstruction, the latter delay being reported to the postal authorities though no federal charge was brought.

**Bank of Vandermeer versus the estate of Perceval Nash, foreclosure.** Decree of foreclosure entered on the mortgage covering 80 acres in Elkhorn Township, the debt found to be $1,240.00 principal and $87.00 accrued interest, the property to be sold at the courthouse door on the first Monday in November unless redeemed before that date by payment in full, the widow Nash having appeared and stated through counsel that she did not contest the debt but hoped to arrange refinancing before the sale date.

**State versus Homer Quist, assault.** A charge arising from a dispute at a threshing crew over the division of the day's pay, in which Quist was said to have struck a fellow hand, one Garrit Voss, with his fist, breaking no bones but raising a considerable bruise. The jury acquitted after a trial of half a day, apparently accepting Quist's defense that Voss had struck the first blow, a claim two of the eleven other hands present supported and two others denied, the remaining seven professing not to have seen the start of the affray clearly enough to say.

**Voss versus Quist, civil action for damages.** Arising from the same threshing-crew affray described above, Garrit Voss brought a separate civil suit against Homer Quist seeking $150.00 for a physician's bill and lost work time, notwithstanding Quist's acquittal on the criminal charge, the court noting for the benefit of readers unfamiliar with the distinction that an acquittal on a criminal charge does not bar a civil suit arising from the same facts, the two proceedings being held to different standards of proof. The jury in the civil matter, a different jury from the one that heard the criminal charge, found for the plaintiff in the reduced sum of $60.00, apparently splitting the difference between the parties' conflicting accounts of who struck first, a result neither side appeared entirely satisfied with, though neither has as yet noted an appeal.

**Miscellaneous matters.** The court also disposed of nine uncontested divorce matters, six default judgments on small debts ranging from $12.00 to $95.00, and three guardianship appointments for minor children, none of these presenting facts the editor judges of sufficient general interest to detail here. The grand jury, sitting the first three days of the term under the charge of the court, returned four true bills and no-billed two matters presented to it, the true bills to be tried in the criminal session set to open the second week of the term.

The court is expected to complete its docket by Friday of next week, the 25th, barring the usual delays from witnesses failing to appear, of which the clerk reports two already this term, both in minor debt matters continued to the spring docket at the plaintiffs' cost.

Reported for the Sentinel by our courthouse correspondent, who reminds readers that the winter term of the Circuit Court, dealing chiefly with matters arising after the fall settling of accounts, is expected to open the second Monday in January, 1954.
