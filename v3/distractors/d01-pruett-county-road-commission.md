# Annual Report of the Pruett County Road Commission — Pruett County Road Commission (1966)

To the Board of Supervisors of Pruett County, greeting: the undersigned Road Commissioners submit this report of labor performed and money expended on the public roads for the year ending the 30th of November, 1966, as required by the act of the General Assembly governing road districts of the second class.

**District No. 1, Cammack Forge.** Overseer Hiram Betts reports 14 days of team labor and 22 days of hand labor spent dragging and crowning the River Road between the forge and the toll gate, at $2.75 the team day and $1.60 the hand day, total $73.70. The plank approach to the county bridge was relaid with 640 feet of 2-inch oak plank furnished by Ira Cobbett at $19.00 the thousand feet, cost $12.16, and 60 pounds of spikes at 4 cents the pound, $2.40. Betts further reports that the culvert at the foot of Meeting House Hill washed out twice in the spring freshets and was each time repacked with fieldstone at no cash cost beyond two days' hand labor, $3.20.

**District No. 2, Skiff Crossing.** Overseer Amasa Deering reports the graveling of 1.4 miles of the Millstone Pike from the crossing east to the township line. Gravel was hauled from the Coyle pit at 38 cents the cubic yard delivered and spread, 210 cubic yards, cost $79.80. The commission notes that the price is 6 cents below the 1965 figure of 44 cents, owing to the shorter haul. Forty rods of 12-inch tile were laid at three low places in the pike at 38 cents the rod, $15.20, purchased of the Cammack Forge Tile Works. Deering also reports 9 days spent by hand gangs cutting brush and briar along the fence lines, at $1.60 the day, $14.40.

**District No. 3, the Millstone settlement.** Overseer Josiah Fenner reports the rebuilding of the bridge over Sable Creek at Coburn's crossing, which the June flood carried thirty feet downstream on the 11th of June. The old stringers were recovered and sixty per cent were sound enough to reuse. New stringers, four of them, 28 feet each, were furnished by the Cobbett mill at $1.10 the running foot, $123.20. Iron drift bolts, 16 in number at 22 cents each, $3.52. Labor of raising and decking, 11 days team and 30 days hand, $30.25 and $48.00 respectively. Total cost of the Sable Creek bridge, $205.97, which the commission asks the Board to allow as an extraordinary item outside the regular road fund, the bridge having been carried away by no fault of the district.

**Petition and dispute.** A petition signed by nineteen freeholders of the Skiff Crossing neighborhood, presented at the March meeting, prayed that the River Road be relocated forty rods to the north around the orchard of the Widow Aldous Crum, on the ground that the present road cuts her dooryard within eleven feet of the kitchen step. The commission viewed the ground on the 2nd of April in company with the petitioners and with Mrs. Crum, who opposed relocation on the ground that the new line would take her spring house. The commission finds, first, that relocation would cost not less than $140.00 in new grading and a new culvert; second, that the present road has run on its present line since before any living resident's memory and no deed of record shows a formal dedication; and third, that the matter is one for the Circuit Court to settle as a question of prescriptive right rather than for this commission to decide as a question of grade. The commission therefore declines to relocate the road and refers the petitioners to their remedy at law. Two commissioners, Hatch and Sturgis, so voted; Commissioner Deering dissented, being of the opinion that eleven feet from a kitchen step is a public nuisance regardless of the state of the title, and asked that his dissent be entered on the minutes, which the clerk has done.

**Poll tax and commutation.** Under the statute, every able-bodied male resident between the ages of twenty-one and fifty is liable to two days' labor on the roads or the commutation of $1.00 the day. This year 216 residents commuted in full, yielding $432.00 to the road fund; 84 worked out their full obligation in labor, valued at $134.40 at the statutory rate; and 11 worked one day and commuted the second, yielding a mixed credit of $28.60.

The large balance is explained by the fact that the county's contract with the Cammack Forge Tile Works for 400 rods of tile drainage on the Millstone Pike, let at the January meeting at 36 cents the rod, has not yet been performed, the works having been unable to deliver before the ground froze; the commission expects this contract, amounting to $144.00, to be carried out in the spring of 1967 and charged against the present balance.

**Equipment.** The commission purchased one split-log drag, iron-shod, of the pattern recommended by the state road bureau, at $14.50, for use on District No. 2, and repaired the two drags already owned by the county at a cost of $3.10 in new bolts and one new tongue. The scraper formerly used in District No. 3, badly sprung in the June flood, was sold for junk at $1.25 and a new one purchased at $11.00. The commission recommends that each district be furnished a drag of its own before the spring thaw of 1967, rather than the present practice of passing one drag among three overseers, which Overseer Rourke reports costs him the better part of two days each spring merely fetching the implement from Cammack Forge.

**Complaint of the adjoining county.** The road supervisor of the adjoining county wrote on the 14th of September to complain that the county line bridge over the branch of Sable Creek at the southeast corner of District No. 3 had been left unrepaired by this county for two seasons running, each county supposing the other bore the maintenance. On investigation the commission finds that the bridge lies eleven feet inside the neighboring county by the original survey of 1871, though it has always been called the county line bridge by local usage, and recommends that the two counties exchange a small parcel by agreement to settle the question of maintenance once for all, rather than continue the yearly correspondence.

**Certification.** We, the undersigned, being the duly elected Road Commissioners of Pruett County, certify the foregoing to be a true and correct statement of the labor performed and money received and expended on the county roads for the year 1966, and recommend to the Board of Supervisors a levy of six mills on the dollar for the year 1967, being one mill less than the levy for 1966, the reduction being warranted by the balance now on hand.

Orville Hatch, Chairman
Levi Sturgis, Commissioner
