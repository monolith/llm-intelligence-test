# Minute Book of the Board, Millbrook School District No. 4 — Sowerby Township, Renfrow County (1927)

**Meeting of 3 January 1927.** The board met at the schoolhouse, all three directors present: Hollis Bream, chairman; Warden Sackett; and Delphia Yount. Ada Purviance, clerk, recorded the minutes. The chairman reported that the coal bin was down to less than a ton and moved that ten tons of nut coal be ordered of the Renfrow Fuel & Feed Company at $7.40 the ton delivered, total $74.00. Carried unanimously. The clerk was directed to draw a warrant on the district treasury for the same when delivered. Miss Sabra Lund, the teacher, reported an enrollment of thirty-one pupils, of whom twenty-eight were in regular attendance, and asked that the board consider a second stove for the cloak room, the present arrangement leaving wet coats to freeze on cold mornings. Tabled for want of funds.

**Meeting of 7 February 1927.** All directors present. The clerk reported the coal delivered, 10 tons and 140 pounds by the company's own weight ticket, and the warrant drawn for $74.35 to reflect the overage. Director Sackett raised the matter of the outhouse, reporting that the boys' side had been tipped over twice since November, the second time on the night of the 30th of January, and moved that the district offer a reward of $2.00 for information leading to the offender's identification. Carried, two in favor, Yount opposed on the ground that $2.00 would only encourage boys to tip it themselves and then inform for the reward. The board resolved to set the new one, when built, in fresh footings of stone rather than posts, which Sackett contracted to do himself at cost of materials only, he being a mason by trade, the district to furnish $6.50 for stone and mortar.

**Meeting of 4 April 1927.** All present. Miss Lund reported that the McGuffey readers for the third and fourth grades were too few, several pupils sharing one book among three, and the board ordered twelve new readers of the county depository at 34 cents each, $4.08, and eight arithmetics at 41 cents, $3.28, total $7.36, to be paid from the district's textbook fund, which the treasurer reported stood at $19.60 before the order. The board also voted to whitewash the interior of the schoolhouse before the fall term, letting the work to Orin Tibbetts at $9.00 for labor, the district to furnish the lime.

**Meeting of 6 June 1927.** Directors Bream and Sackett present; Yount absent, excused, being confined with a broken wrist. The board took up the matter of Miss Lund's contract for the coming year. On motion of Sackett, seconded by Bream, the board renewed her contract at $68.00 the month for an eight-month term, an increase of $4.00 the month over the year just closing, in consideration of her having completed the summer normal course at the county seat. The clerk was directed to have the contract drawn and signed before the 1st of July. The board further discussed, without action, a proposal from the Sowerby Township trustees to consolidate Districts No. 4 and No. 6 and haul the No. 6 pupils to Millbrook by wagon, the No. 6 building being in poor repair; the board took no position, pending a meeting of patrons of both districts to be called for the fall.

**Meeting of 15 August 1927.** All three directors present, the board having met specially at the request of six patrons who presented a petition objecting to the proposed consolidation with District No. 6, on the ground that the wagon road between the two districts is impassable in the spring and that No. 6's building, though old, is sound enough with $40.00 of repair. The board heard the petitioners at length and voted two to one, Bream and Yount in favor, Sackett opposed, to recommend against consolidation to the township trustees, while noting that the final decision lies with the trustees and not with this board. The board also fixed the opening of the fall term for the 6th of September and voted to purchase a new dictionary, unabridged, for $6.75, and a globe at $4.25, both from the proceeds of the district library fund, which stood at $22.00.

**Meeting of 5 September 1927.** All present. The fall term opened as scheduled with an enrollment of thirty-four, three more than the spring closing, owing, Miss Lund reported, to a family newly moved into the district from the county seat. The board approved payment of the September coal bill, six tons at $7.60 the ton delivered before the price rose for the season, $45.60, and approved Miss Lund's first month's warrant, $68.00.

**Meeting of 3 October 1927.** All present. Director Yount reported a complaint from the parent of one Delbert Cranch, aged eleven, that Miss Lund had kept the boy after school three days running for whispering and had made him copy a page of the reader each time as punishment. The board heard Miss Lund, who explained that the boy had been warned twice before resorting to the copying exercise, and that his work had in fact improved since. The board voted unanimously that the punishment was within the teacher's proper discretion under the district's rules as adopted in 1918, and declined to take further action, though the chairman was directed to speak informally with the boy's father to smooth over hard feeling.

**Meeting of 7 November 1927.** All present. The board reviewed the district's financial statement for the first four months of the school year: receipts, state apportionment $210.00, township levy $340.00, balance from prior year $61.15, total $611.15; expenditures, teacher's salary four months $272.00, coal $119.60, textbooks and supplies $11.09, repairs $15.50, insurance premium on the building, three-year policy renewed with the Girard Mutual Fire Insurance Company, $18.00, total $436.19, leaving a balance of $174.96. The board voted to carry $100.00 of the balance forward as a reserve against winter coal and repair bills, the remainder to be available for the spring term's needs.

**Meeting of 5 December 1927.** All present. The board voted to close school for the two weeks of the Christmas holiday, from the 19th of December through the 2nd of January, and to pay Miss Lund her full December warrant of $68.00 notwithstanding the shortened month, as her contract calls for a fixed monthly sum rather than a per-diem rate. The board adjourned to meet again on the first Monday of January, 1928.

**Meeting of 2 January 1928.** All three directors present, the board's first meeting of the new year. The clerk read the December statement: expenditures for the month, teacher's salary $68.00, coal $22.80 for three tons, total $90.80, leaving the reserve fund, after the winter's coal, at $84.16. Director Sackett reported the new outhouse footings had held through the fall rains without settling and that the carpentry, let separately to Orin Tibbetts's brother Amzi at $11.00, was complete. The board voted to thank both Tibbetts brothers by entry in the minutes for work done at or below cost. The chairman raised, for discussion only, the question of a spring examination day to be held jointly with District No. 6 regardless of the consolidation question, so that pupils of both districts might be tested by a common examiner from the county superintendent's office; the board asked the clerk to write the superintendent and inquire what the county would charge for such a service, if anything, and to report at the February meeting.

Ada Purviance, Clerk
Hollis Bream, Chairman
Warden Sackett, Director
Delphia Yount, Director
