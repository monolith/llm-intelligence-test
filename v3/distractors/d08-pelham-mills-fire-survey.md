# Survey of the Risk at Pelham Mills — Girard Mutual Fire Insurance Company, Marsden County (1935)

Report of survey made this 11th day of September, 1935, by the undersigned surveyor for the Girard Mutual Fire Insurance Company, upon the application of Ottis Marchbanks, proprietor, for insurance in the sum of $6,500.00 upon the grist and feed mill known as Pelham Mills, situated on the Sable Fork three miles west of Dunfries, Marsden County, together with its contents and the frame warehouse adjoining.

**Description of the risk.** The main mill building is of frame construction, three stories and a basement, 40 feet by 60 feet, built in the year 1909 upon the foundation of an earlier mill destroyed by fire in that same year, the present building therefore being of comparatively recent construction though the water-power works beneath it, the wheel pit and the head-race, are of much older date, said by the proprietor to go back well before living memory, the exact year not being known to him. The building is roofed in wood shingle, which the surveyor notes as a hazard of the first order and recommends be replaced with standing-seam metal at the earliest opportunity, at an estimated cost of $340.00 for the main roof alone.

**Motive power.** The mill is driven by a water wheel of the overshot pattern, 18 feet in diameter, supplemented since 1928 by a gasoline engine of 12 horsepower kept in a separate small frame house 30 feet from the main building, used only when the water is low in late summer. The surveyor finds this separation of the engine house from the mill proper to be good practice and a material mitigation of the risk, gasoline engines being a frequent cause of loss where housed within the mill itself.

**Contents.** The mill contains one set of burr stones for corn, one roller mill for wheat flour installed in 1918 at a cost the proprietor states was $1,800.00 though the surveyor was unable to verify this from any bill of sale, one corn sheller, one feed grinder, and the usual bolting and sifting machinery. Grain on hand at the date of survey: corn, about 3,000 bushels; wheat, about 800 bushels; both stored in bins on the second and third floors. The warehouse adjoining, of frame construction, 24 feet by 50 feet, connected to the mill by a covered passage 8 feet wide, holds sacked feed and flour ready for market, estimated at the date of survey at 400 sacks, value about $480.00 at the mill's own reckoning of 60 cents the sack net.

**Heating and lighting.** The mill office, a small room partitioned off in the northeast corner of the ground floor, is heated by a small coal stove, the pipe of which passes through the ceiling and the floor above in a tin thimble the surveyor found in fair condition but recommends be inspected yearly, coal-stove pipes through wood partitions being a common source of fire from creosote accumulation. Lighting throughout the mill is by kerosene lantern, no electric service being available at this location, and the surveyor notes eleven lanterns hung on nails at various points, none of them provided with any form of guard or shield, which he recommends be remedied by the installation of wall brackets with wire cages, at a cost he estimates at not more than $25.00 for the whole building.

**Housekeeping.** The surveyor found the mill reasonably free of the loose grain dust that is the chief hazard of this class of risk, the proprietor evidently sweeping the floors and the tops of the bins with some regularity, though the boot of the elevator leg on the second floor was found choked with old dust and cobweb to a degree the surveyor thinks should be cleaned out before cold weather, when the stove is likely to be run harder. No smoking is permitted within the mill by the proprietor's own rule, posted on a card by the door, and the surveyor's inquiry of two employees found the rule to be observed.

**Exposure.** The mill stands alone, the nearest building being the proprietor's dwelling house, 140 feet distant across the mill yard, and a small tenant house, 200 feet distant, occupied by the mill's one regular hand, Amasa Tewksbury, and his family. Neither exposure is considered material. The nearest source of water beyond the mill's own race is the Sable Fork itself, which runs the year round though at low stage in August and September, and there is no organized fire company nearer than Dunfries, six miles distant by the county road, response time in a genuine emergency not to be counted upon.

**Prior loss history.** The proprietor states, and the surveyor has no means to verify beyond the statement, that the mill has had no fire loss since the rebuilding of 1909, though he recalls a small fire in the engine house in 1927, extinguished before material damage was done, caused by a backfire from the gasoline engine, after which the engine's exhaust was rerouted away from a nearby stack of empty sacks, the arrangement the surveyor found satisfactory on this date.

**Comparison with a prior risk in this territory.** The surveyor notes for the company's file that a similar mill risk eleven miles east of this one, the Coburn roller mill, was declined by this company in 1933 on account of an open elevator boot found choked with dust to a depth the surveyor at that time considered dangerous, combined with unguarded lanterns of the same kind found here, and that risk subsequently suffered a total loss by fire in the spring of 1935 while insured elsewhere, a fact the present surveyor considers relevant to his recommendation that the lantern guards at Pelham Mills be treated as a firm condition of acceptance rather than a mere suggestion.

**Boiler and steam apparatus.** None. The mill uses no boiler of any kind, all power being water or gasoline as described above, which the surveyor notes as a further mitigation, boiler risks in mill properties of this class commonly commanding a higher rate on account of the additional hazard of an unattended firebox.

**Recommendation.** The surveyor recommends the risk be accepted at the rate customarily applied to grist mills of frame construction in this territory, namely $1.40 the hundred dollars of insurance for a three-year term, subject to the roof being reshingled or replaced within eighteen months and the lantern guards installed within six months, failing either of which the company reserves the right to reinspect and adjust the rate or cancel the policy on notice. Total premium at the rate stated, for $6,500.00 for three years, $91.00, payable $30.34 the first year and the balance in equal annual installments, per the company's usual practice on mill risks of this size.

Survey made and reported by Thaddeus Rook, Surveyor for the Company
Countersigned, Ottis Marchbanks, Applicant, acknowledging the recommendations above and undertaking to comply within the periods stated.

Filed with the district agency at Dunfries, 14 September 1935. Policy No. 4471 issued 20 September 1935, to run three years from that date.
