# Surgical Waiting Area C, Between Nine and Eleven — recorded and transcribed by Ineke Ravensdale (2017)

*Vinyl chairs on three sides, a television at low volume showing a cooking program, a volunteer
at a podium desk with a clipboard and a pager rack. Two speakers hold most of the recording. A
volunteer speaks four times. A pager goes off twice.*

---

**EVELYN:** Is that seat somebody's?

**RUSS:** It's got my coat on it and nothing else.

**EVELYN:** I'll take the one down.

**RUSS:** Take either one. Take the coat one, I'll move the coat.

**EVELYN:** No, no. I like the corner, I can see the door.

*(pause; chair; a bag set down)*

**EVELYN:** Who're you here for?

**RUSS:** Wife. Knee.

**EVELYN:** Which one.

**RUSS:** Right. She had the left done four years ago at the other place, the one out on the
bypass, and she said never again, and here we are.

**EVELYN:** Never again lasts about four years.

**RUSS:** About that.

**EVELYN:** My husband said never again about a hernia and then he said it again about the other
side.

**RUSS:** They come in pairs.

**EVELYN:** Everything comes in pairs.

*(television; a timer sound from the program)*

**RUSS:** Did you park in the garage?

**EVELYN:** I parked in the garage.

**RUSS:** Get your ticket stamped at the desk. She'll do it. Otherwise it's eighteen dollars
after four hours and it's eleven if you get it stamped, and if you're here past six it's
eighteen anyway and the stamp does nothing.

**EVELYN:** Eighteen dollars.

**RUSS:** For a garage attached to the building you're already paying.

**EVELYN:** Well, the garage is a separate company.

**RUSS:** That's what they say.

**EVELYN:** No, it genuinely is. My nephew does the insurance for parking structures and he says
they're almost never the hospital, they're a company that leases the deck and the hospital gets
a cut and that's why nobody can fix anything about it.

**RUSS:** That's the first explanation of that I've ever gotten.

**EVELYN:** It doesn't help you.

**RUSS:** No, but I like knowing.

*(pager; a family of three rises and goes out left)*

**VOLUNTEER:** If anybody needs their parking validated I can do it here at the desk, you don't
have to wait until you leave.

**EVELYN:** I'll come do it now.

**VOLUNTEER:** Take your time, I'm here till two.

*(footsteps; the stamp, twice; footsteps back)*

**EVELYN:** She stamped it twice.

**RUSS:** It doesn't stack.

**EVELYN:** I didn't think it did.

*(pause)*

**EVELYN:** Have you eaten?

**RUSS:** I had a coffee at six.

**EVELYN:** They've got a cafeteria down on one but it's the kind where the soup is the thing and
everything else is a sandwich under plastic.

**RUSS:** What's the soup.

**EVELYN:** There's two. There's always a chicken and rice and then there's a second one that
changes and today the sign said corn chowder, but I looked at it and I don't know that I'd call
it that.

**RUSS:** What would you call it.

**EVELYN:** Corn.

*(laughter)*

**RUSS:** How long's yours?

**EVELYN:** My daughter. It's a scope, so they said an hour, hour and a half, and then two hours
after that before I can take her, and she can't drive for a day, and she's already told me she's
going to try to drive.

**RUSS:** She'll try.

**EVELYN:** She'll try and I'll have the keys in my pocket and she'll be furious in the car and
by tomorrow she'll have forgotten she was ever furious. That's how she's been since she was
four.

**RUSS:** My grandson's the opposite. He remembers everything, and he's fifteen.

**EVELYN:** That's a hard age to remember everything at.

**RUSS:** He's got his permit test in three weeks and he's been studying a booklet for two
months. Two months for a test on stopping distances.

**EVELYN:** Better than not.

**RUSS:** Oh, better than not, sure, but I keep telling him the test isn't the part. The part is
the four-way where nobody knows who goes and you have to just — go.

**EVELYN:** You can't put that in a booklet.

**RUSS:** You cannot.

*(long pause; the television; a cart passes in the corridor)*

**EVELYN:** Is that clock right?

**RUSS:** It's nine minutes slow. I checked it against my phone when I got here. It's been nine
minutes slow the whole time.

**EVELYN:** In a hospital.

**RUSS:** In a hospital.

**EVELYN:** You'd think of all the places.

**RUSS:** You'd think.

*(pager; nobody rises; VOLUNTEER checks the rack)*

**VOLUNTEER:** That's forty-one, is anybody forty-one?

*(no response)*

**VOLUNTEER:** All right, I'll hold it.

*(pause, approximately ninety seconds, television only)*

**EVELYN:** They ought to give you the number of what number they're on. Like a bakery.

**RUSS:** Like a bakery.

**EVELYN:** So you'd know if it was moving.

**RUSS:** That's the whole thing right there, isn't it. Nobody minds waiting. Everybody minds not
knowing whether the waiting's doing anything.

**EVELYN:** That's true of most of it.

**RUSS:** Most of what?

**EVELYN:** Most of it.

*(pause)*

**RUSS:** Four years ago at the other place they had a board. A screen on the wall, and your
person was a code, and the code moved from *in prep* to *in procedure* to *closing* to
*recovery*, and you sat there and watched your code move and it was the best four hours I ever
spent in a chair.

**EVELYN:** They don't have that here.

**RUSS:** They have a pager.

**EVELYN:** The pager only tells you it's over.

**RUSS:** The pager only tells you to come to the desk. Then somebody at the desk tells you it's
over, or tells you it's going to be another while, and either way you've stood up for nothing
twice.

*(pause)*

**EVELYN:** What's her name? Your wife.

**RUSS:** Dorrit.

**EVELYN:** That's a name you don't hear.

**RUSS:** Her mother's mother. There's been one in every generation and then there's my daughter,
who is Anne, because my daughter put her foot down at eleven years old and said she was Anne and
that was the end of it.

**EVELYN:** At eleven.

**RUSS:** At eleven.

*(recording continues thirty-one minutes; at approximately 10:52 a pager sounds and RUSS goes to
the desk; the recording ends before he returns.)*
