# Inventory and Appraisement of the Estate of Ezra Pardee, Deceased — Office of the Probate Judge, Colby County (1933)

State of record, Colby County Probate Court. In the matter of the estate of Ezra Pardee, late of Druitt's Landing, deceased intestate the 6th of February, 1933, aged seventy-one years. Letters of administration having been granted to his son, Nathaniel Pardee, on the 20th of February, the undersigned appraisers, Silas Rourke, Adin Wexford, and Barnabas Coyle, being first duly sworn according to law, did on the 14th and 15th days of March, 1933, view and appraise the goods, chattels, and credits of said deceased, as follows.

**Grain, hay, and provisions on hand.**

Corn in the crib, estimated 340 bushels at 28 cents, $95.20. Hay in the barn, estimated 9 tons at $6.00, $54.00. Wheat threshed and in sack, 40 bushels at 62 cents, $24.80. Potatoes in the cellar, 30 bushels at 40 cents, $12.00. Two barrels of salted pork, $14.00. Subtotal, $200.00.

**Notes and accounts receivable.**

Note of hand, Absalom Greer, dated 1 May 1928, principal $150.00, interest at 6 per cent from date, unpaid, appraised at face and accrued interest, $193.88. Note of hand, the estate of the late Orrin Teale, principal $40.00, appraised as doubtful at one-half face, $20.00. Open account, T. J. Fessenden's mill, for corn delivered and not yet settled, $31.40. Subtotal, notes and accounts, $245.28.

**Real estate.** (Appraised separately for the record, though not included in the personalty total below.) The home place, 118 acres more or less, in Section 14, Druitt's Landing township, with dwelling, barn, corn crib, and hog house, appraised at $38.00 the acre, $4,484.00. A wood lot of 12 acres in Section 23, unimproved, appraised at $9.00 the acre, $108.00.

**Debts of the deceased, as reported to the appraisers though not part of the inventory proper.** Account owing to Norcross's store, $18.60. Doctor's bill, Dr. Amasa Kittredge, for the last illness, $32.00. Funeral expenses, Coyle & Son, undertakers, $85.00. Note held by the Druitt's Landing Farmers Bank, principal $200.00, dated 3 November 1927, with five years' interest at 7 per cent accrued, $70.00, total $270.00.

**Widow's allowance and exempt property.** The deceased left surviving him his widow, Permelia Pardee, aged sixty-six, and four adult children, of whom Nathaniel is the eldest and the administrator. Under the exemption statute the appraisers set apart to the widow, in lieu of her distributive share pending final settlement, the following: one milk cow of her choosing (she chose the roan), the corn-husk bedstead and bedding, the cook stove, the sewing machine, and provisions sufficient for herself for one year, estimated at $60.00 in corn, wheat, and pork drawn from the stores above listed. The appraisers fix the widow's year's allowance, over and above the exempt articles named, at $150.00, to be paid from the first monies coming into the administrator's hands, this being the statutory allowance for a widow of a decedent leaving real estate of the value found above.

**Claims filed against the estate, in addition to those reported by the appraisers.** Norcross's store claim of $18.60, noted above, was duly filed and allowed by the court on the 2nd of April, 1933. Dr. Kittredge's claim of $32.00 was allowed the same day. The Druitt's Landing Farmers Bank filed its note and interest, $270.00, and asked in addition for attorney's fees of $15.00 under the note's own provision, which the administrator resisted; the court allowed the principal and interest but disallowed the attorney's fee, there having been no default requiring suit before the claim was filed. A claim by one Orville Stackpole, alleging $40.00 due for pasturing the deceased's cattle in the summer of 1932, was contested by the administrator on the ground that the pasturing was in payment of a debt the deceased owed Stackpole for seed corn, and the two claims were allowed to offset one another, leaving nothing due either way.

**Disagreement among the heirs.** Nathaniel Pardee's youngest brother, Willis Pardee, of the next county, filed written objection to the appraisal of the home place, contending that land of like quality nearby had sold the previous year at $45.00 the acre and that $38.00 undervalues the place by some $826.00 in the aggregate. The appraisers, recalled on the 22nd of March to hear the objection, adhered to their original figure, noting that the comparison sale cited by Willis Pardee included a dwelling in far better repair and lay nearer the gravel pike, whereas the home place fronts on an unimproved township road passable by wagon only in dry weather. The court confirmed the appraisal as made and overruled the objection, without prejudice to Willis Pardee's right to raise the question again at final distribution should the land in fact be sold for a higher figure.

**Total appraised value of personal property:** household goods $59.10, livestock $276.80, implements $115.25, grain and provisions $200.00, notes and accounts $245.28. **Grand total, personalty, $896.43.**

The appraisers further report that among the effects was found a tin box containing $34.60 in currency and coin, which the administrator has taken into his possession and which is included above only as to the $34.60 itself, no separate item having been made for it beyond this note. They further report that the deceased's gold watch, willed by no instrument since none was found, descends as personalty and is appraised at $22.00, included in the household goods figure above under a general heading not separately itemized in the table, the appraisers having omitted it from the table by oversight and correcting the omission in this paragraph, so that the true household total, corrected, is $81.10 rather than $59.10 as tabled, a discrepancy the administrator is directed to note in his final accounting.

Sworn to and subscribed before me this 16th day of March, 1933.

Silas Rourke
Adin Wexford

Filed for record 18 March 1933, Book 11, Page 206, Colby County Probate Records. Nathaniel Pardee, administrator, to render his final account not later than the March term next ensuing, or show cause why letters should not be revoked.
