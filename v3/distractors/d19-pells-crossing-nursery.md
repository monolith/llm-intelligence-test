# Spring Catalogue and Price List — Pell's Crossing Nurseries, Jasper Latimer, Prop. (1933)

**A letter to our customers, old and new:**

*This makes my twenty-second season sending out trees from this ground, and I will say plainly that it has not been an easy year to fix prices for. Cash is scarcer with most folks than it was in my father's time here, and scarcer than it was even a few seasons back, so I have cut my figures below what I charged three years since wherever the stock allows it, and I would rather sell a good tree at a fair price to a man who will plant it right than hold out for the old figure and ship nothing at all. I guarantee every tree true to name and alive on arrival, or replacement free the following season, which has been my rule since I took the business over from my father and will remain so as long as I run it.*

*Orders under five dollars, please add twenty-five cents toward packing and cartage to the depot at Pell's Crossing. Orders of five dollars or more, packing free. I ship the first two weeks of April as a rule, weather allowing, and hold no order later than the third week of April for spring planting, it being too late by then in this climate to do the young stock justice.*

*— Jasper Latimer*

**Testimonial, printed with permission:** *"I set forty of Mr. Latimer's Winter Colquitt apples in the fall of 1928 and have had three good crops running, the trees never having lost a one to winter kill though we stand higher than most orchards hereabout." — signed, a customer at Dunlap Notch, name withheld at his own request, being a modest man about such things.*

**A further word on the Fenwick Sweet.** This is not a tree found in the trade catalogues generally, and I raise it in small quantity, perhaps four hundred trees a season, from grafts taken off the original tree, which still stands, or did as of my last visit in the fall, on the old Fenwick place near Prosper Mills. It is a sweet apple, good for cider and for feeding, not a keeper past Christmas, and I do not recommend it to a man wanting a commercial orchard, only to the man who remembers the taste of it from his boyhood and wants one or two trees of his own. I have sold perhaps sixty of these a year for a dozen years now and never had a complaint of the fruit, only twice of a tree that failed to live, both replaced free per my guarantee above.

**PLANTING NOTES, for those new to setting an orchard:** Dig the hole wider than seems needed, a full two feet across for a two-year apple tree, and no deeper than the tree stood in the nursery row, which I mark on each tree with a strip of cloth at the old soil line before digging it here. Puddle the roots in a thin mud before setting if the ground is at all dry when the box arrives, and do not manure directly against the roots the first year, well-rotted compost being safer for young stock than fresh barn manure, which I have seen burn more roots in twenty years of this business than winter cold ever did.

**A note on this year's stock.** The late frost of this past May cut my strawberry plant supply by perhaps a third, and I have accordingly limited orders for the Senator Dunlap variety to two hundred plants per customer this season, first come served first, so that no one family takes the whole of a short crop. The apple and pear stock was not touched by the frost, having bloomed later, and I have my full usual supply of both.

**Grange discount.** Any member of a chartered Grange presenting his membership card, or ordering through his Grange's cooperative buying committee where one exists, may deduct five per cent from the prices above on any single order of three dollars or more, this being my father's custom before me and one I am glad to continue, the Grange having been a good customer of this nursery for as long as I can remember. Prosper Grange and Marsh Fork Grange have both ordered through their committees in past years and are welcome to do so again.

**Terms:** Cash with order preferred; C.O.D. shipment available at the buyer's added expense of the C.O.D. fee, presently twenty cents on orders under ten dollars; no order held on credit this season, a rule I regret but cannot avoid in times like these, several nurseries larger than mine having failed last year on that very account.
