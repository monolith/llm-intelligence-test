# Field Notes of a Boundary Survey, Thistlewood Township — Marius Fallow, County Surveyor, Wexley County (1965)

Field notes of a survey made at the request of Orval Pettengill and Bascom Threlkeld, adjoining landowners, to establish the disputed line between their two farms in Section 9, Thistlewood Township, Wexley County, the line in question not having been run by any surveyor of record since the original government survey, according to the county recorder's office, which could produce no field notes earlier than the plat itself.

**Tuesday, 4 May 1965.** Began at the northwest corner of Section 9, marked by a stone monument found in place, 14 inches by 9 inches, set flush with the ground and marked with a chiseled cross on its upper face, agreeing with the original government description so far as that description can be read in the recorder's book, the book itself being a copy of a copy and none too legible on this point. Chained east along the section line 40.00 chains to a point, set an iron pipe 30 inches long, 1 inch in diameter, as a witness monument, there being no natural object at this point suitable for a permanent mark. Weather clear, warm, no difficulty from brush along this stretch, the ground being in pasture.

From the iron pipe, ran south 1° 15′ west (all bearings magnetic, this date, declination not separately determined) along the claimed division line for 26.40 chains to a large white oak, 32 inches in diameter, blazed on two sides and marked with the initials "O.P." on the north-facing blaze, which Pettengill states was cut by his father in a year he believes to have been in the 1950's, though he could not give the surveyor a precise year and no other record was found to confirm it. Threlkeld disputes that this blaze marks any boundary at all, contending it was cut merely to mark a good tree for future timber and not as a line tree, a dispute the surveyor notes here without attempting to resolve, resolution of title questions being beyond a surveyor's office.

Continued the same bearing 18.20 chains to a small creek, here about 6 feet wide, running generally east, forded at a point where the bank is low on both sides. Set a stake with a stone mound at the ford, the creek itself being an unreliable monument on account of its tendency to shift course in flood, as both landowners agreed has happened at least twice within their joint memory, once about 1960 and once more recently, they believe about 1964, moving the channel as much as 30 feet at one bend some 200 feet downstream of this point, though not materially affecting the ford itself.

**Wednesday, 5 May 1965.** Resumed at the stake of the previous day. Continued south 1° 10′ west (a slight change of bearing noted and accepted by both parties as within the ordinary variation to be expected over such a run) for 31.60 chains to the section line forming the south boundary of Section 9, marked by a stone found badly disturbed, apparently by cattle rubbing against it over many years, and reset by the surveyor in what he judges, from the remaining evidence of the original hole and from the distance called for in the government plat, to be its correct original position, within perhaps 6 inches either way.

Total length of the disputed line, from the section-line point of beginning to the south section line, 76.20 chains (26.40 plus 18.20 plus 31.60, less an allowance the surveyor made for a small correction of 0.20 chain arising from a discrepancy between the forward and back readings taken at the creek stake, discovered on checking the notes overnight and resolved by splitting the discrepancy equally between the two courses on either side of it, a common and accepted practice where the discrepancy is too small to warrant re-chaining the whole line).

**Areas affected.** The disputed strip, being the difference between the line as Pettengill has long claimed it (following the old fence, which runs on average about 1.4 chains east of the line as now surveyed) and the line as Threlkeld contends it should run (following the white oak as a true line tree, which would put the line about 0.6 chains west of the line as now surveyed), amounts, by the surveyor's calculation, to approximately 15.2 acres under Pettengill's claim and approximately 6.5 acres under Threlkeld's, the surveyor's own run, based strictly on the section-line points of beginning and closing and the recorded distances, falling between the two claims and closer to Threlkeld's, though the surveyor expresses no opinion on which party's claim is the better one in law, that being a question for the parties' attorneys and, failing agreement, for the circuit court.

**Instruments and method.** Survey made with a transit reading to single minutes, checked against a known line of record two days before the survey and found to read true within the surveyor's satisfaction. Chaining done with a standard 66-foot (4-rod) surveyor's chain, checked against the surveyor's own standard steel tape before the survey began and found to be within 0.02 foot of standard length over its full run, a correction of that order being applied to the total distances reported above though not to each individual course separately, the error being too small to affect any single course by more than a fraction of a link.

**Witnesses present.** Orval Pettengill, Bascom Threlkeld, and one hired chainman, Garret Oshinsky, who assisted the surveyor on both days and whose notes, taken independently as a check on the surveyor's own, agreed with the figures reported above to within the ordinary tolerance for chaining of this kind, no discrepancy exceeding one link on any single course.

**Surveyor's recommendation.** The surveyor recommends that, whatever line the parties or the court ultimately fix upon, permanent iron monuments be set at not fewer than three points along its length, the present stake-and-stone marks at the creek and elsewhere being subject to disturbance by both cattle and flood, as this survey itself has demonstrated with respect to the original section corner monuments.

**Fee and division of cost.** The survey was undertaken at the joint request of both parties, who agreed in advance, in a written memorandum the surveyor kept with his own papers, to divide the cost equally regardless of the survey's outcome. Total charge: two days' field work at $28.00 the day, $56.00; one day's office work computing courses and distances and preparing this record, $18.00; chainman's wage for two days, paid by the surveyor and included in his charge rather than billed separately, $16.00; total $90.00, of which $45.00 was collected from each party at the time these notes were delivered, receipted below.

**Note added 12 May 1965.** Threlkeld's attorney has since written the surveyor requesting a certified copy of these notes for use in a suit he expects to file if the parties cannot agree upon a line within thirty days, which the surveyor has furnished at the standard copying charge of $3.00, this being a separate matter from the survey fee proper and not included in the $90.00 above.

Field notes recorded and certified a true record of the survey as made.

Marius Fallow, County Surveyor, Wexley County
Garret Oshinsky, Chainman
5 May 1965, filed with the county recorder 10 May 1965, Survey Book 6, Page 91.
