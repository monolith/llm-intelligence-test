# Premium List and Judges' Remarks — Silas Whitfield, Secretary, Bexley County Agricultural Society (1932)

*Fair held the 17th through the 20th of September, 1932, on the society's own grounds at Oakhurst Center. Premiums paid from the gate receipts and the county appropriation of $200 voted by the commissioners in August.*

**DEPARTMENT A — LIVESTOCK, CATTLE**

*Jersey cattle, judged by Homer Colquitt, of Hartshorn, an experienced breeder engaged for the day at a fee of $5.00 and his dinner.*

Best cow, three years or over: 1st, Enos Delacroix, **$4.00**; 2nd, Ambrose Ridgway, **$2.50**. *Judge's remark: "Delacroix's cow shows better in the udder and carries more flesh without going coarse in the bone; Ridgway's animal is younger in appearance than her card states and may yet come up to this one in another season."*

Best cow, under three years: 1st, Micah Arceneaux, **$3.00**; 2nd, none entered worthy of a second premium, the judge so noting on his card.

Best bull, any age: 1st, Hosea Comeaux, **$4.00**. *Judge's remark: "A well-set animal, a little straight in the shoulder, but the only bull entered and would have placed regardless."*

**DEPARTMENT B — LIVESTOCK, SWINE AND SHEEP**

Best pen of three market hogs: 1st, Barnabas Loftus, **$3.50**; 2nd, Cyrus Underhill, **$2.00**. Judged by weight and finish, Loftus's pen averaging 220 pounds against Underhill's 205, both pens uniform within themselves, which the judge remarked counts for as much as the raw weight in market hogs sold as a lot.

Best pen of lambs: 1st, Ira Stoddard, **$3.00**.

**DEPARTMENT B (continued) — POULTRY**

*Judged by Wilfred Chalmers.*

Best pen, Plymouth Rock, six birds: 1st, Sarah LeBlanc, **$2.00**. Best pen, Rhode Island Red: 1st, Ira Stoddard, **$2.00**; 2nd, Norah Garrahan, **$1.25**, this being Mrs. Garrahan's first entry in poultry after some years showing only in the domestic departments, which the judge remarked upon favorably, calling her birds "close-feathered and well-fleshed for a first year's effort at breeding rather than merely keeping." Best dozen eggs, brown: 1st, Content Averill, **$0.75**.

**DEPARTMENT C — FIELD CROPS AND VEGETABLES**

*Judged by Ambrose Ridgway (excused from judging cattle above on account of his own entry) and Jasper Latimer, the nurseryman from Pell's Crossing, engaged jointly for this department.*

Best ten ears field corn: 1st, Levi Marchbanks, **$1.50**; 2nd, Wilfred Chalmers, **$1.00**.

Best half bushel potatoes: 1st, Content Averill, **$1.50**. *Remark, in Latimer's hand: "Mrs. Averill's entry shows remarkably even sizing, scarcely a culled potato in the lot she brought, which speaks to careful grading before the entry was ever made rather than to the crop alone."*

Best display of squash and pumpkins, not less than five varieties: 1st, Jethro Overstreet, **$2.00**.

Best three heads cabbage: 1st, Delia Marchetti, **$0.75**; 2nd, Winifred Colquitt, **$0.50**.

**DEPARTMENT D — DOMESTIC, BAKED GOODS**

*Judged by Mrs. Norah Garrahan, of Wrenfield.*

Best loaf white bread: 1st, Almira Sherwin, **$1.00**. *Remark: "A close, even crumb and a good crust color, no sourness detected, which is more than can be said of half the entries this year, the weather having been damp for the rising."*

Best layer cake, any kind: 1st, Prudence Fenwick's granddaughter, entered under the family name, **$1.50**; 2nd, Content Thibodeaux, **$1.00**.

Best dozen doughnuts: 1st, Electa Boudreaux, **$0.75**.

Best jar of preserves, any fruit: 1st, Vashti Pruitt, peach, **$0.50**; 2nd, Sarah LeBlanc, plum, **$0.35**.

**DEPARTMENT E — NEEDLEWORK AND FANCY WORK**

*Judged by Ellen Brennan, of Millbrace.*

Best quilt, pieced: 1st, Almira Sherwin (her second premium this fair, a fact the secretary notes for the record though the rules place no limit on it), **$2.00**. *Judge's remark: "A well-matched Star pattern, the piecing exact and the quilting stitch fine and even throughout; I would have liked to see it entered in the exhibition class as well as the competition, being nearly good enough for both."*

Best embroidered pillowcases, pair: 1st, Miriam Colquitt, **$1.00**.

Best knitted afghan: 1st, Winifred Colquitt, **$1.50**.

**DEPARTMENT E (continued) — FLORAL HALL**

Best dozen dahlias, cut: 1st, Miriam Colquitt, **$0.75**. Best potted geranium: 1st, Prudence Fenwick's granddaughter, **$0.50**. Best floral arrangement, table centerpiece: 1st, Ellen Brennan (the milliner, entering outside her own judged department, which the rules permit so long as a judge does not enter a class she herself is judging), **$1.00**.

**A protest noted and settled.** Cyrus Underhill protested the swine judging, contending that his pen of hogs, placed second, weighed within five pounds of the winning pen and should have been judged more on uniformity of finish than on the judge's stated weight difference. The department committee heard the protest the evening of the 18th, reviewed the judge's card, and let the original award stand, finding that the judge had in fact weighed uniformity into his decision as claimed and that the weight difference cited was not the sole ground of his placing. Mr. Underhill accepted the ruling without further complaint and was thanked by the committee for raising the question through the proper channel rather than airing it about the grounds, as such protests too often are.

**DEPARTMENT F — SPECIAL AND SWEEPSTAKES**

Sweepstakes, best single exhibit in the fair, any department, chosen by the four department judges in conference: awarded to Almira Sherwin's quilt, by unanimous agreement of the judges present, **$5.00** additional to her department premium, and the privilege of having the quilt displayed in the society's own case through the winter meeting season.

**DEPARTMENT G — HORSES, DRAFT**

*Judged by Homer Colquitt, engaged for a second fee of $3.00 beyond his cattle judging noted above.*

Best pair, matched draft horses: 1st, Jethro Overstreet, **$3.00**. Best single draft horse: 1st, Levi Marchbanks, **$2.00**; 2nd, Barnabas Loftus, **$1.25**. *Judge's remark: "Overstreet's pair is the better matched I have seen at this fair in some years, both animals near enough alike in height and color that a stranger would take them for litter mates, though I am told they were bought separately three years apart."*

**Secretary's summary.** Total premiums paid this fair, across all departments, **$58.35**. Gate receipts, **$186.00**; county appropriation, **$200.00**, per the note above; total income, **$386.00**. Deducting the premiums and a further **$58.00** for tents, judging fees, and printing this list, the society's treasury shows a surplus for the fair of **$269.65**. The society's directors will meet the second week of October to settle the final accounts and to consider whether the corn and cabbage classes, poorly filled again this year, should be combined into a single "field vegetables" class for 1933, a change three of the five directors have already indicated they favor.

**A note on the weather:** rain on the second day, the 18th, held attendance down by perhaps a third against the previous year's fair, by the gatekeeper's own estimate, though it cleared by evening and the third and fourth days were fair throughout.
