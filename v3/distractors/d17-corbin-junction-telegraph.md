# Copy-Book of Messages, Corbin Junction Office — Operator Ada Halvorsen (1887)

*Kept in duplicate per company rule, one leaf to the sender, one bound in this book. Tolls per the posted schedule: ten words or under, twenty-five cents within the division; each word beyond ten, two cents; night rate after 9 p.m., half the day rate where the wire is not needed for train orders.*

**No. 214. Filed 8:10 a.m., March 3, 1887. From Bramlett Mercantile, Corbin Junction. To Sowerby & Petrie, Corliss City.**
"SEND TWENTY BBLS FLOUR SOONEST PRICE ADVANCING HERE WIRE CONFIRMATION" — 10 words, **$0.25**. Confirmation received back at 9:40 a.m., filed as No. 215, forwarded to Bramlett by messenger boy, no charge for delivery within the village.

**No. 216. Filed 9:55 a.m. From Dr. N. Pemberton, Dunlap Notch, per messenger, to Corliss City Supply House.**
"RUSH DIPHTHERIA SERUM CHILD DANGEROUS MARK URGENT" — 7 words, marked "urgent" by the sender at an added charge of ten cents per company rule, **$0.35** total. I sent this at once ahead of two commercial messages waiting, per the company's standing instruction that a physician's urgent message takes precedence over ordinary commerce. Note: this office has no serum on hand; the message was relayed to Corliss City for shipment by the afternoon train, not for wire delivery of the goods themselves, a point on which Dr. Pemberton's man seemed confused when he brought the message in.

**No. 217. Filed 11:20 a.m. From Colquitt Bros. Mill, to a grain buyer at Farview Junction (a connecting office, relayed by wire through the county seat).**
"WHEAT SEVENTY TWO CENTS TODAY CAN SHIP CAR LOT THURSDAY" — 10 words, **$0.25**, plus relay charge of the connecting office, **$0.10**, total **$0.35** collected of the sender.

**No. 218. Filed 1:05 p.m. From Increase Trumbo, Bellcourt Landing (filed by wire from that office, received here for delivery), to Levi Marchbanks, Marsh Fork.**
"BOAT DELAYED SANDBAR ARRIVE THURSDAY INSTEAD WEDNESDAY MEET ME THEN" — 11 words, one word over ten, **$0.27**, charge already collected at Bellcourt Landing, this office to deliver only. Delivered by messenger to the Marsh Fork stage driver, who agreed to carry it out on his afternoon run, there being no wire to Marsh Fork itself.

**No. 219. Filed 2:40 p.m. From Perley Dunmore, Corbin Junction, to his brother at a distant address outside this division.**
"FATHER VERY LOW COME AT ONCE" — 6 words, **$0.25** minimum charge, plus the toll of the connecting company beyond our division, **$0.60**, total **$0.85**, which Mr. Dunmore paid without question, being in evident distress. I held the wire open a full four minutes waiting for the connecting office to answer, there being some trouble on their line that afternoon, and told Mr. Dunmore so, that he should not think the delay was ours.

**No. 220. Filed 4:15 p.m. From this office, on the company's own account, to the Division Superintendent.**
"POLE NO 44 LEANING BADLY NORTH OF DEPOT NEEDS ATTENTION BEFORE STORM SEASON" — 12 words, no charge, company business. I have raised this twice before, in November and again in January, without a repair crew being sent; I enter it a third time so that the book will show the office did its part.

**No. 221. Filed 5:30 p.m. From Almira Sherwin, Corbin Junction, to a cousin at Reed's Ferry.**
"ARRIVING SATURDAY NOON BRING THE WAGON" — 6 words, **$0.25**.

**No. 222. Filed 7:50 p.m. (night rate). From Barnabas Loftus, on behalf of the Marsh Fork Grange, to the State Grange office.**
"PROSPER GRANGE DELEGATE CALLOWAY CONFIRMED ARRIVING FRIDAY" — 7 words, night rate, **$0.13** (half the day rate of $0.25, the ten-word minimum still applying at half). Note: I queried whether the sender meant this office's own Corbin Junction connection to Prosper Grange or a message strictly for Marsh Fork's own delegate business, as the wording confused the two Granges; Mr. Loftus confirmed it concerned Prosper Grange's man Calloway and I sent it as written, not being my place to correct another man's message once he has approved the count and paid for it.

**Garbled message, received garbled from the Corliss City office, filed for the record though not delivered as sense could not be made of it:** "...ILL SEND...LOUR AT ONCE...ICE FIRM..." — apparently a repeat or continuation of No. 214's business, the line having crossed with a storm west of here. I wired back requesting a repeat; none had come by the close of the office at 8 o'clock, and I have left word with the night operator, when the office is open for train orders only, to take it down if it comes over after hours and hold it for morning.

**No. 223. Filed 8:15 p.m. (night rate). From Vashti Pruitt, Corbin Junction, to a dressmaker at the county seat.**
"SEND THE BLUE TRIM PATTERN SATURDAY STAGE PAYMENT ENCLOSED SEPARATELY" — 10 words, night rate, **$0.13**. Miss Pruitt asked that I hold this until after 9 o'clock so as to get the cheaper rate, the message being in no hurry, and I obliged, entering it at the hour actually filed with her rather than the hour she first brought it in, per the company's rule that the sender may wait for night rate if the wire is not otherwise wanted.

**No. 224. Filed 8:50 p.m. (night rate). From this office, relaying for the stationmaster, to the Division Superintendent.**
"CATTLE CAR NO 60 AXLE HOT HELD AT CORBIN JUNCTION SEND INSPECTOR" — 11 words, company business, no charge to any private party, entered here as it occupied the wire for some minutes and delayed the Pruitt message above by that much, which is why I have noted the actual filing time for hers rather than the hour she came to the window.

**Weekly toll summary appended.** For the week ending March 3, 1887, this office collected in tolls a total of **$14.85**, of which **$9.20** was remitted to the division office by the Saturday mail and the balance held against the following week's supply requisition for battery renewals and forms, per the standing arrangement allowed a single-operator office of this size.

**Day's summation, March 3, 1887.** Messages filed outward, nine, of which one company message no charge. Messages received for local delivery, three. Total tolls collected, **$2.32**. Cash on hand reconciled against the toll book, no discrepancy. Batteries examined and found in fair order, one cell renewed at a cost to the company of **$0.40**, entered separately on the supply requisition.

*A. Halvorsen, Operator, Corbin Junction.*

**Memorandum, following morning, March 4th:** The garbled message of yesterday afternoon came through clean overnight, read complete as "COLQUITT MILL WILL SEND FLOUR AT ONCE PRICE FIRM," evidently a second and separate matter from Bramlett's order of the morning and not a repeat of it as I had supposed. Delivered to Bramlett Mercantile by messenger this morning with my apology for the day's delay, no charge assessed for the redelivery, this office bearing the cost of its own error in judgment as to what the garbled fragments meant.
