# Order Book, Ellen Brennan's Millinery — Ellen Brennan, Proprietress, Millbrace (1909)

*Kept for customer orders, materials charged against each, and the wholesale account with the supply house at the county seat.*

**March 1.** Mrs. Almira Sherwin, ordered one spring bonnet, straw braid, trimmed with silk ribbon (rose) and a bunch of artificial violets. Materials: braid, $0.60; ribbon, two yards at $0.20, $0.40; violets, $0.35; buckram frame, $0.15. Labor charged, $1.50. Total to customer, **$3.00**. Ordered for delivery before Easter, which falls the 11th of April this year; delivered the 8th, paid in full on delivery.

**March 3.** Miss Vashti Pruitt, ordered one hat remade from last season's frame, new trim only: black velvet band and a single ostrich tip, dyed to match. Materials: velvet, $0.30; ostrich tip, $0.65; dye charge for the tip, $0.10. Labor for remaking, $0.75. Total, **$1.80**. Delivered March 20th, paid $1.00 down, balance of $0.80 still owing as of this writing.

**March 9.** Mrs. Delia Marchetti, mourning order, her mother having died the week before. One hat, plain black straw, crepe veil to the shoulder, no trim beyond a black grosgrain band. Materials: straw, $0.55; crepe, one and a half yards at $0.40, $0.60; grosgrain, $0.20. Labor, $1.25, reduced from the usual $1.50 as is my custom for mourning orders, no ornament being wanted. Total, **$2.60**. Delivered within two days as is proper for such an order, the 11th, paid in full at delivery by her husband.

**March 15.** Wholesale order sent to Halloran Bros., Millinery Supply, Corliss City: 3 doz. assorted ribbon, mixed colors and widths, $4.50; 2 doz. artificial flower sprays, assorted, $3.60; 1 doz. ostrich tips, natural and dyed assorted, $6.00; 6 buckram frames, wide brim, $0.90; 1 bolt black crepe, mourning weight, $2.40. Total order, **$17.40**, terms 2/10 net 30 per the house's standing terms, which I take advantage of when the month's receipts allow, as they do this month.

**March 22.** Mrs. Content Averill, Easter order, one hat entirely new: leghorn straw, wide brim, trimmed with a wreath of small silk roses and two yards of pale green ribbon for streamers. This being her first new hat since her husband's estate settled, she asked for something "not somber," her words, and I obliged with the green rather than the black or gray I might otherwise have suggested for a widow only lately out of black. Materials: leghorn, $0.90 (the better grade, imported through the supply house rather than the domestic braid I use for plainer orders); roses, $0.50; ribbon, $0.40; frame, $0.20. Labor, $2.00, the wreath work being fine and slow. Total, **$4.00**. Delivered April 9th, two days ahead of Easter, paid in full.

**March 25.** Miss Electa Boudreaux, schoolteacher, ordered a plain everyday hat for spring wear, felt remaining from a winter piece cut down and re-blocked, new band of brown grosgrain only. Materials: grosgrain, $0.15. Labor for reblocking, $0.60. Total, **$0.75**, the lowest priced order of the season, teachers being generally my most economical customers and my most regular, Miss Boudreaux having ordered from me each spring and fall these three years.

**April 2.** A rush order from Mrs. Winifred Colquitt, her daughter's confirmation being the 6th and the child having outgrown last year's hat entirely. Small hat, child's size, white straw, single white silk rosette, no other trim as is proper for the occasion. Materials: straw, $0.30; rosette, $0.15; frame, $0.10. Labor, $0.75, done in one evening at her particular asking, for which I added no rush charge, this being a small order and Mrs. Colquitt a steady customer of some years. Total, **$1.30**. Delivered the 5th, paid in full.

**April 3.** A wedding order, Miss Sarah LeBlanc, marrying the second Tuesday of May. One traveling hat for the wedding journey, not the veil, which her mother is making at home: navy straw, small brim, trimmed with a single wide grosgrain bow and a small silver buckle brought by the bride herself from a shop at the county seat, which I set into the bow at no charge beyond the ribbon, this being a courtesy I extend to wedding orders generally. Materials: straw, $0.70; ribbon, $0.35. Labor, $1.75, wedding work being worth the extra care and priced accordingly. Total, **$2.80**, buckle not counted as it was the customer's own. Delivered April 30th, well ahead of the date, at Miss LeBlanc's particular request that she have time to see it against the traveling suit before the day itself.

**April 4.** Note to self: the Easter rush this year brought in eleven orders in the fortnight before, against nine the year previous, the increase I put down to the new families come to Millbrace with the mill's expansion rather than to any falling off of Halloran's competing shop at Corliss City, whose prices I understand remain a few cents under mine on the plainer grades of straw, though not, I am told, on the trimmed work, where my hand is better known.

**April 8.** Two hats ordered together by the Misses Underhill, sisters, for their mother's fiftieth birthday supper: matching hats, cream straw, trimmed alike with pink rosebuds, differing only in the ribbon, one rose and one pale blue at the sisters' own choosing so as not to appear too exactly alike. Materials for the pair: straw, $1.10; rosebuds, $0.60; ribbon, both colors, $0.50; frames, $0.30. Labor for the pair, $2.50. Total for both, **$5.00**, or $2.50 each, which I entered as a single order at the sisters' request though I have shown the materials divided as near as may be for my own accounting.

**April 20.** Season's summary through Easter. Orders filled, seventeen. Total charged to customers, **$41.85**. Collected in cash at delivery, **$34.30**. Outstanding on account, **$7.55**, of which Miss Pruitt's balance of $0.80 noted above is the oldest, now five weeks unpaid, and one further balance of $2.00 owed by a Mrs. Guilbeau of Wrenfield, ordered through her sister here, not otherwise entered above as the order itself was taken and filled before this book's present page was begun.

**Materials on hand, counted this date:** 4 buckram frames remaining of the 6 ordered in March; ribbon stock low, chiefly odd short lengths under a yard, to be used up in trimming rather than reordered until the fall stock comes in; 2 ostrich tips remaining, both the natural color, the dyed having all been used; crepe, about half a bolt remaining, sufficient for perhaps three more mourning orders before another must be laid in.

**A memorandum on prices for the fall season:** I mean to raise the plain everyday hat from $0.75 to $0.85 this fall, materials having gone up somewhat at Halloran's own prices to me, but will hold the trimmed and mourning work at present figures, those being priced more on the labor than the material and my labor having gone up not at all.
