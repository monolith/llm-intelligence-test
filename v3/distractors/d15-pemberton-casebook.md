# Case-Book of a Country Practice — Nathaniel Pemberton, M.D., Dunlap Notch, Kestrel County (1906)

*Kept daily, in order of call, with fee and settlement noted at the right margin.*

**Jan. 3.** Called before daylight to the Averill place on the ridge road, three miles, for Mrs. Content Averill, confinement, her fourth. Labor had been going twelve hours before I was sent for. Delivered a son, living, at 7:15 in the morning, after a hard second stage; used forceps, the head having come to a stand. Mother did well after. Fee for confinement and mileage, **$8.00**. Paid in full, cash, the 20th of January.

**Jan. 5.** Homer Kilbride, age 9, croup, bad. Sat with the family until midnight, steam kettle and the usual expectorant. Improved by morning; called again the 6th, found him mending; discharged the 8th. Two visits and medicine, **$3.50**. Entered on the book, unpaid as yet.

**Jan. 11.** Called to the Sherwin place for old Mr. Zebulon Sherwin, a fall from the hay mow, right leg broken below the knee. Set the leg with splints, the boy Barnabas holding the lamp. Directed complete rest six weeks. Visit and setting, **$5.00**; two follow-up visits at fifty cents mileage each already included. Paid **$3.00** on account the 15th, balance carried.

**Jan. 19.** Vashti Pruitt, sore throat, examined, ordinary quinsy, no membrane present, painted the throat and left a gargle. Fee, **$1.00**. Paid at the office.

**Jan. 22.** A second case in the Kilbride family, the daughter Ada, age 6, this one with membrane on the tonsils and a temperature of 103. I told the mother plainly this looked like diphtheria and not croup, and had the house marked and the two other children kept from school. Sent to Corliss City by the afternoon train for antitoxin, which did not arrive until the 24th on account of the agent's error in the telegram. Child improved from the 25th. I attended six times between the 22nd and the 30th. Fee for the antitoxin case, including the six visits and the serum itself at my own cost forwarded, **$14.00**. The family being poor, I have entered **$6.00** as paid and marked the rest "to be forgiven if not collected by spring," per my usual practice with this family.

**Jan. 26.** Warned the school trustees of the diphtheria in the Kilbride house; school closed for one week by order of Trustee Osgood, reopened Feb. 2 with no further case appearing.

**Feb. 2.** Called to Wren's Bend, six miles, an unusual distance for me, for Mr. Ezra Dabney, the bridge-tender there, complaining of pain in the chest after lifting. Examined, found no evidence of anything more than a strained muscle of the wall, prescribed rest and liniment. Mileage alone came to **$1.50** of the **$3.00** fee, the distance being what it was.

**Feb. 9.** Miss Electa Pemberton (no relation), teacher at the Birch Center school, complaining of exhaustion and a cough of some weeks. Examined the chest, found nothing at either apex, attributed it to the closeness of the schoolroom stove and advised she have the room ventilated at recess regardless of weather. Fee, **$1.00**.

**Feb. 14.** Called in haste to the Loftus place, a child, age 3, having swallowed a button. Watched the child two hours, no distress developing, advised the parents to watch the stool and sent no medicine. No fee charged, per my rule in such cases where nothing is done.

**Feb. 20.** Delia Marchetti, age 41, complaint of the joints, worse in damp weather, of some years' standing. I have seen her for this twice before, in the autumn and again now. Prescribed the usual salicylate mixture and advised flannel next the skin through the winter. Fee, **$1.00**, paid.

**Feb. 27.** Called to the Overstreet farm for Mr. J. Overstreet himself, a wound of the hand from the mowing-machine crate he was uncrating, a deep cut across the palm requiring four stitches. Dressed and stitched, advised return in four days. Fee, **$2.50**.

**Mar. 3.** Overstreet returned, wound healing clean, stitches removed, no further charge as agreed.

**Mar. 6.** Death recorded: old Mrs. Prudence Fenwick, age 84, of Dunlap Notch village, whom I had attended off and on for eleven years for a weak heart. Died in her sleep, no attendance at the last, certificate given as heart failure of old age. No fee, having none to collect of the estate and none asked.

**Mar. 12.** Called again to the Sherwin place; old Mr. Sherwin's leg, six weeks out from the break, examined and found well knit, splints removed, advised a cane for a further month. Balance of the January account, **$2.00**, settled in full, paid by Barnabas Sherwin on his father's behalf.

**Mar. 19.** A second confinement this quarter, for Mrs. Winifred Colquitt, her first child, an easy labor of five hours, delivered a daughter living at 4 in the afternoon. Fee, **$6.00**, this family's first confinement being reckoned less on account of the shorter attendance. Paid **$4.00** at the time, balance on the spring settling.

**Mar. 25.** Examined Thaddeus Osgood for a hernia of some years, discussed the truss maker at Corliss City with him, made no charge beyond the consultation, **$1.00**.

**Mar. 28.** Called to the Cathcart place, a case of measles in three of the four children, mild in each, no complications. Advised the usual isolation and darkened room for the eyes. Attended twice over the week, fee for both visits **$2.00**, entered on the book, this family generally slow to pay but reliable by year's end.

**Mar. 30.** My own memorandum on instruments: the forceps used in the Averill confinement of the 3rd of January were the long pair bought new from the supply house at Corliss City two years since, at a cost to me of **$9.00**, and they have now done service in four confinements without needing to be sent back for repair. I note this because I had occasion this month to advise young Dr. Cathcart, newly settled at Marsh Fork, on the purchase of his own set, and told him not to economize on the point, a false saving being dear in the one case where the instrument is wanted and found short.

**Note on fees generally.** My schedule, posted in the office and unchanged these six years, reads: ordinary office call, **fifty cents**; house call within two miles, **one dollar**; house call beyond two miles, one dollar plus twenty-five cents the mile beyond the second; confinement, ordinary, **six dollars**; confinement with instruments or unusual attendance, **eight to ten dollars**; setting a simple fracture, **five dollars**; consultation only, no treatment, **one dollar**. I have departed from this schedule twice this quarter, both times downward, in the Kilbride and Sherwin accounts, for reasons of the families' circumstances rather than of the medicine required.

**Quarterly summary, close of March, 1906.** Total calls this quarter, forty-one, of which two confinements, one fracture, one case of diphtheria, and the remainder minor illness and injury. Total fees charged, **$61.50**. Collected in cash or on account during the quarter, **$39.50**. Carried forward unpaid, principally the Kilbride diphtheria account and the Colquitt confinement balance, **$22.00**, of which I expect to collect perhaps half by the autumn settling, the Kilbride portion being, as noted above, largely written off in advance.

**Memorandum for my own use:** the antitoxin delay of five days in the Kilbride case was the telegraph agent's doing and not the railway's; I have written to the express company at Corliss City asking that such matters be marked urgent hereafter regardless of the sender's instructions, since a child's life turned on it this time and came out well only because the membrane was slow to spread in her case.
