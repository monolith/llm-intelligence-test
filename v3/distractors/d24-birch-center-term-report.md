# Term Report, Birch Center School, District No. 4 — Cora Ledbetter, Teacher (1943)

*Submitted to the County Superintendent of Schools at the close of the winter term, this being my fourth term in this district and my sixth year of teaching in all.*

**Term dates:** opened the 4th of January, closed the 19th of March, 1943, being eleven weeks, four days lost to a January blizzard that closed the roads, one day lost to my own illness (a heavy cold), making seventy-four days of actual school held against the seventy-nine scheduled.

Percentage of attendance for the term, 91.4 per cent, an improvement over the fall term's 87 per cent, which I attribute chiefly to the fewer cases of grippe this winter and to two families who moved into the district in November whose children have been regular since.

**Pupils completing the eighth grade this term, eligible for the county examination in May:** Miriam Colquitt and Content Fenwick, both of whom I certify as having satisfactorily completed the prescribed course in arithmetic, grammar, geography, United States history, physiology, and spelling, per the county course of study. I have coached both an extra half hour after school twice weekly since February in preparation for the May examination, at no charge to their families, this being my custom with graduating pupils.

**Course of study followed.** With eight grades and one teacher, I have combined recitations where the county course allows: 1st and 2nd together for reading and numbers, 3rd and 4th together for geography, and 5th through 8th together for United States history, each grade nonetheless held to its own separate standard in arithmetic and grammar, recited in turn while the others work seat exercises. I have found this arrangement, which I inherited from my predecessor here, Miss Boudreaux, workable so long as the seat work is planned the evening before in some detail, a discipline I have kept without exception this term.

**The district spelling match**, held the last Friday of February against Marsh Fork's District No. 6, was won by Birch Center, seventeen words to twelve, our champion being Miriam Colquitt, who has not misspelled a word given from the county list all year. The two schools have held this match each winter for as long as I have taught here and, I am told, several winters before that.

**A visit from the board.** Two of the three district board members, Mr. Overstreet and Mr. Delacroix, visited the school on the 10th of February, as the district's by-laws require at least once a term, and found the school, in their written note left with me, "in good order and the children attentive," a copy of which I enclose with this report per the superintendent's standing request that board visitation notes accompany the term report where available.

**Textbooks and supplies.** The district owns, per the inventory taken at the opening of term and again at closing: 29 readers of various grade levels, 3 lost or badly worn beyond use since the fall inventory and replaced from the district's supply fund at a cost of **$2.70**; 14 arithmetic texts, sufficient with doubling up in the lower grades; 6 grammar texts, insufficient for the 5th through 8th grades combined, two pupils sharing throughout the term, which I have noted to the board before and note again here. Paper, pencils, and chalk supplied by the district came to **$4.15** for the term, within the **$5.00** allowed.

**Building and grounds.** The stove drew poorly through January until Mr. Overstreet, the district's hired man for such repairs, cleared the pipe on the 9th at a charge of **$1.00** to the district. Wood supply, six cords laid in before the term by the board's contract with Mr. Delacroix at **$4.00** the cord, **$24.00** in all, proved sufficient with a half cord carried over to the spring term. The outhouse door, blown off in the January storm, was rehung by Mr. Marchbanks the following week at a charge of **$0.75**. The well remains sound; water tested by the county health nurse on her visit of February the 3rd and found satisfactory.

**Health.** The county nurse's visit noted above also included the vaccination clinic, at which twenty-two of the twenty-nine pupils were vaccinated or showed proof of prior vaccination, the remaining seven excused on their parents' written objection, which the nurse recorded without comment, this being within the parents' right under the present county rule. Two pupils were sent home in February with what the nurse later confirmed as chicken pox; both returned within two weeks with no further cases developing in the school.

**Discipline.** One matter of note: on the 22nd of February I kept two of the older boys, Wilfred Fenwick and Enos Marchbanks, after school for throwing snowballs at the youngest pupils during the noon recess, contrary to my standing rule, and required each to write a note of apology to the two smaller children struck, which they did, and I consider the matter closed, there having been no repetition since.

**War-time notes, per the superintendent's request that these be included this term:** the district has continued its collection of scrap paper and tin, sent to the county collection point monthly by whichever family's wagon is going that direction; the pupils raised **$3.85** toward defense stamps through a penny-a-week collection I have kept since September, all of which has gone to stamps held in the school's own book rather than distributed to individual pupils, per the plan recommended by the superintendent's office. Sugar for the pupils' hot lunch program, where held, has been reduced per the ration allowance, which has meant plainer fare on baking days than in years past, though no pupil has gone without a hot dish at noon on the days the program operates, twice weekly through the coldest months.

**My own circumstances.** I board with the Overstreet family a half mile from the schoolhouse, walking in all but the worst weather, for which they charge the district's customary **$3.00** the week, deducted from my salary of **$65.00** the month before the district pays me the balance directly. I have signed for a fifth term here beginning in the fall, the board having offered the same salary, which I have accepted, this being a fair wage for the district though less, I am told, than districts nearer the county seat now pay for want of teachers, several having left the profession for war work this past year.

*Respectfully submitted, Cora Ledbetter, Teacher, District No. 4, this 22nd day of March, 1943.*
