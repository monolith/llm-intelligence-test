# Register and Rules, Mrs. Garrahan's Boarding House — Norah Garrahan, Proprietress, Wrenfield (1918)

*Norah Garrahan, Proprietress, Elm Street, Wrenfield. Established in this building since her husband's death in the influenza of two winters back; eleven rooms let, two reserved for the family.*

**RULES OF THE HOUSE, posted in the front hall and given to each new boarder to sign below:**

1. Board and room, **$8.00** the week for a single room, **$14.00** for two persons sharing, payable in advance each Saturday evening without exception.
2. Breakfast at 7, dinner at noon, supper at 6. A boarder missing a meal through his own fault, no reduction; missing through sickness of more than two days, one-half the meal charge refunded on the following Saturday.
3. No spirits brought into the house. Any boarder found intoxicated on the premises will be asked to leave within the day, no refund of the week's board.
4. Doors locked at half past ten. A boarder wanting a later key may have one for **twenty-five cents** the month, deposit **$1.00** returnable.
5. Gentlemen callers for the lady boarders received in the front parlor only, and not after 9 in the evening.
6. Laundry done by the house at **fifteen cents** the dozen pieces, collected Monday, returned Thursday. Boarders doing their own laundry may not hang it in the yard before 6 a.m. or after dark.
7. No cooking of any kind in the rooms. A boarder found with a spirit lamp or the like will forfeit the deposit named in Rule 4 whether or not he holds a key.
8. One week's notice required before leaving, or forfeiture of the deposit and the last week's board due regardless.
9. The bathroom on the second floor is for the use of all boarders on that floor in rotation, a schedule posted separately by Mrs. Garrahan and changed the first of each month.

*Signed and understood, this register being the record of that signature for each boarder named below.*

**Ledger notes, kept separately by Mrs. Garrahan in the same book:**

Mr. Underhill has been prompt every Saturday since January without exception, twenty-three weeks paid to date at $8.00, **$184.00** in all, and takes his supper out perhaps one evening in four, for which no deduction is made per Rule 2.

Miss Boudreaux paid regularly through her stay of twenty-three weeks less two, having gone home to her people for a fortnight in April on account of her mother's illness, for which the two weeks were not charged at Mrs. Garrahan's own discretion, this not being a matter of the rules but of long acquaintance between the families. Total paid, twenty-one weeks at $8.00, **$168.00**.

Mr. Chalmers left owing **$4.00**, a part-week, on account of a dispute over Rule 7, having been found with a small spirit lamp for heating shaving water, which he held was not "cooking" within the meaning of the rule. Mrs. Garrahan held otherwise, kept his deposit of $1.00 against the debt, and the balance of $3.00 remains uncollected, entered here as a loss.

The Guilbeaus, at the double rate, have paid every week without fail, twenty-two weeks to date, **$308.00**.

Mr. Stoddard, the traveling man, paid for two full weeks and left owing three days at the daily rate of a little over a dollar, having been called away suddenly by his firm; he mailed **$3.50** from the county seat within the month to settle it, which Mrs. Garrahan credits to his honesty, traveling men being generally the hardest to collect of.

**A note appended by Mrs. Garrahan for whoever keeps this book after her:** the bathroom schedule (Rule 9) causes more trouble among the boarders than all the other rules together, and I have found it best to post it the evening before the month turns rather than the morning of, so that anyone with a grievance may air it to me privately before breakfast rather than at the table before the others. The Guilbeaus, being the only married couple, are given the first turn of each week by long custom, not written into the rule but understood by all who have boarded here more than a month.

**On the matter of Sunday dinner.** Mrs. Garrahan notes that Sunday dinner is served at 1 o'clock rather than the weekday noon, and is a heavier meal by custom, roast rather than the weekday boiled dinner, at no extra charge to the boarders, this being covered within the regular board and not a separate item, though she remarks that a boarder taking Sunday dinner elsewhere and none of the weekday meals through illness or absence should not expect the same half-refund named in Rule 2, Sunday dinner being reckoned as part of the week's board taken as a whole rather than a single meal.

**An incident of April.** Mr. Comeaux and Miss Thibodeaux were both later than the half-past-ten hour named in Rule 4 on the night of April the 9th, having been detained, so they said, by a broken wagon wheel on the Marsh Fork road returning from a supper at a neighboring farm. Mrs. Garrahan let them in without charging the key deposit of Rule 4, this being the first such occurrence for either, but noted in this book that a second time would not be excused so easily, and read both Rules 4 and 5 aloud to them the following morning at breakfast, to the visible discomfort of the table generally.

**The second family room**, ordinarily reserved and not let, was given for the month of May to Mrs. Garrahan's own niece, Miss Sarah LeBlanc, entered above at the boarder's rate of $8.00 the week though family, Mrs. Garrahan holding that a rule bent for kin without payment is a rule broken for all, and that Miss LeBlanc herself insisted upon paying rather than take charity from an aunt so lately widowed.

**Vacancies as of the close of June, 1918:** Room 6 will be open from the 12th, Miss Boudreaux having taken a position at a school in another township for the autumn term; inquiries already received from two parties, to be decided by the 1st of July.
