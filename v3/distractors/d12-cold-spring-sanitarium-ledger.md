# Admission Ledger of the Cold Spring Water-Cure — Dr. Josiah Vantine, Superintendent, near Mercer's Gap (1884)

Being the register of patients received at the Cold Spring Water-Cure Establishment, near Mercer's Gap, for the season beginning the 1st of May and closing the 30th of September, 1884, kept by the superintendent for the proprietors' accounting and for the guidance of the attending physicians as to each patient's terms of admission, diet, and course of treatment prescribed.

**No. 1. Received 3 May 1884.** Mrs. Cordelia Ashgrove, aged 52, of the county seat, admitted for what she describes as a nervous prostration of some two years' standing, attended by loss of appetite and disturbed sleep. Prescribed the standard course: cold sitz bath morning and evening, wet-sheet pack three times weekly, graduated walking exercise, and a plain diet excluding coffee, tea, and pastry. Terms: $9.00 the week for board and treatment, private room. Remained 6 weeks, discharged 14 June, reporting herself much improved, having gained 8 pounds during her stay. Total charge, $54.00, paid in full at departure.

**No. 2. Received 3 May 1884.** Mr. Ansgar Krell, aged 38, farmer, admitted for rheumatism of the joints, chiefly the knees and left shoulder, of long standing and aggravated, he states, by exposure in a wet spring some years back. Prescribed hot and cold alternate baths, sulfur-water soaking of the affected joints from the spring itself, and a course of the establishment's own liniment, compounded on the premises. Terms: $7.00 the week, shared room. Remained 4 weeks, discharged 31 May, reporting the shoulder much eased though the knees little changed, and expressing his intention to return the following season for a longer course. Total charge, $28.00, paid $20.00 in cash and the balance, $8.00, in a note due 1 September 1884, since paid.

**No. 3. Received 10 May 1884.** Miss Prudence Alderking, aged 24, admitted for a general debility following a severe attack of the ague the previous winter. Prescribed a mild course, warm baths only at first, advancing to cool as strength permitted, tonic diet including beef tea and eggs, and rest, the superintendent noting in the margin that too vigorous a course is inadvisable in cases of this kind until the patient's strength has begun to return. Remained 9 weeks, discharged 12 July, much recovered, though the superintendent notes she left somewhat sooner than he would have advised, her family having need of her at home for the harvest season. Terms $8.00 the week, total $72.00, paid by her father, Osric Alderking, in two installments of $36.00 each.

**No. 4. Received 17 May 1884.** Mr. Barnabas Quire, aged 61, retired merchant, admitted for dyspepsia of many years' standing and a general disorder the superintendent attributes to a sedentary life and rich diet. Prescribed the establishment's plainest diet, graham bread, fruit, and vegetables chiefly, with meat but once a day, cold baths, and two hours' walking daily in the establishment's own grounds, which the patient at first found irksome but came to enjoy, by his own account, before his stay was out. Remained 8 weeks, discharged 12 July, reporting his digestion much improved and his general spirits better than in some years. Terms $10.00 the week, best room with private bath, total $80.00, paid in full on arrival, an arrangement the superintendent notes is uncommon but was the patient's own preference.

**No. 5. Received 24 May 1884.** Mrs. Salathiel Purbeck, aged 45, admitted for what her own physician at home diagnosed as a liver complaint, with jaundice apparent on examination. The superintendent, doubting the water-cure alone sufficient to so serious a case, recommended she also continue certain medicines prescribed by her own physician, which she had brought with her, a departure from the establishment's usual practice of relying on water treatment and diet alone, but one the superintendent judged prudent in this instance. Prescribed warm baths only, no cold treatment, and a very plain diet. Remained 5 weeks, discharged 28 June, the jaundice much diminished though not entirely gone, and advised to continue a plain diet at home and to return the following season if the complaint persists. Terms $9.00 the week, total $45.00, paid in full at departure.

**No. 6. Received 7 June 1884.** Mr. Zebulon Dabney, aged 29, admitted for what he describes as an affliction of the nerves following a severe fright, having been thrown from a runaway wagon the previous autumn without serious physical injury but with a lasting nervous shock the local physicians could not relieve. Prescribed graduated cold baths, vigorous exercise including a daily ride on the establishment's own horses, and a full diet, the superintendent's theory in cases of this kind being that a return of physical vigor is the surest remedy for the nervous trouble that follows a shock. Remained 10 weeks, discharged 16 August, reporting himself entirely restored and stating his intention to recommend the establishment to others of his acquaintance. Terms $8.00 the week, total $80.00, paid $50.00 in cash and $30.00 by a draft on his bank at home, honored without difficulty.

**No. 7. Received 21 June 1884.** Two patients, sisters, the Misses Araminta and Theodosia Fennimore, aged 19 and 17, admitted together for a general run-down condition following a winter of illness in the family that had fallen chiefly on them as the elder daughters to nurse the rest. Prescribed a mild course for both, warm baths, tonic diet, and rest, sharing one room at the reduced rate the establishment allows for two members of one family taking the same course together. Remained 6 weeks, discharged 2 August, both much improved. Terms $14.00 the week for the two together (rather than $16.00 at the individual rate), total $84.00, paid by their father in full on their departure.

**No. 8. Received 5 July 1884.** Mr. Wendell Corcoran, aged 44, a schoolteacher from a neighboring county, admitted for a chronic catarrh of the head and throat that had troubled him through two winters and, he feared, was beginning to affect his voice, a serious matter to a man of his profession. Prescribed cold douche to the head and throat daily, steam inhalation of the establishment's own preparation twice daily, and a diet excluding all dairy, which the superintendent holds to aggravate catarrhal conditions though he allows the point is disputed among physicians of his acquaintance. Remained 7 weeks, discharged 23 August, reporting his voice clearer than it had been in two years, though the catarrh itself not entirely cured, and advised to repeat the treatment the following summer as a preventive measure rather than wait for a full return of the complaint. Terms $8.00 the week, total $56.00, paid in full at departure, with an additional $2.00 for a bottle of the steam-inhalation preparation to take home, at his own request and not part of the establishment's ordinary terms.

**Season summary.** Eight admissions of record for the period 1 May through 30 September 1884 as detailed above, together with three further admissions of brief duration, one week or less, not separately itemized here as their charges were nominal and their conditions unremarkable, contributing a further $21.00 to the season's receipts. Total receipts for the season, all admissions, $522.00. The superintendent notes that this is a somewhat lighter season than the year before, which he attributes to an unusually wet June discouraging travel, but expresses confidence that the establishment's reputation, now some nine years built, will bring a fuller house in 1885.

Josiah Vantine, M.D., Superintendent
Cold Spring Water-Cure, near Mercer's Gap
30 September 1884
