# Muster Roll and Expense Account of the Colfax County Home Guard — Capt. Ezekiel Marsh, Winder's Depot (1862)

Muster roll of the company of Home Guard raised at Winder's Depot, Colfax County, under the call of the county court dated the 3rd of August, 1862, for the defense of the county against irregular incursion, the company to serve at such times as called out by the captain and to furnish its own arms where possible, the county to furnish ammunition and to pay a per diem when actually mustered for duty.

**Roll of the company, as first mustered 9 August 1862.**

Ezekiel Marsh, Captain, own rifle and horse.
Perceval Doane, First Lieutenant, own shotgun.
Ambrose Kittle, Second Lieutenant, own rifle.
Barzillai Coombs, Orderly Sergeant, own rifle.
Privates: Elnathan Mowbray, Japheth Kelso, Enos Rafferty, Return Talcott, Jethro Pinckney, Loammi Sharrow, Manasseh Groat, Obed Cranmer, Philetus Ward, Zenas Askey, Amariah Grubb, Tilghman Rowe, Uriah Bassett, Whipple Doty — fourteen privates, together with the four officers named above, eighteen men in all as first mustered.

The company was recruited chiefly from the neighborhood of Winder's Depot and the adjoining Sharon settlement, most of the men being past the age of the regular enrollment or excused from it by reason of dependents, the county court having judged a home guard of such men preferable to leaving the county entirely without armed force while the regular companies were away.

**Musters and days of actual duty.**

9 August 1862: full muster and organization, one day, no pay claimed, the men understanding the first day to be a matter of organization only.

23–25 August 1862: called out for three days upon a report, since found false, of a raiding party crossing from the county to the south; the company patrolled the river road and the two fords nightly but encountered nothing beyond a peddler's wagon, which was stopped and examined and allowed to proceed. Pay claimed, 18 men, 3 days each, 54 man-days at 75 cents the day, $40.50.

14 September 1862: called out for one day to assist the sheriff in the pursuit of two deserters believed to be hiding in the Sable bottoms; the deserters were not found this day, though see below. Pay claimed, 16 men (two absent, excused), 1 day each, $12.00.

15–16 September 1862: eight men, at the captain's selection rather than a full muster, continued the search into the second day and apprehended one of the two deserters, a man not of this county, near the old mill dam, and turned him over to the sheriff. Pay claimed, 8 men, 2 days each, 16 man-days, $12.00.

2 October 1862: full muster, one day, for target practice and inspection of arms, at the captain's order, following complaints that some of the men's pieces were in poor condition. Pay claimed, 18 men, 1 day each, $13.50.

11–13 November 1862: called out for three days on report of a small band of irregulars said to be taking horses in the county to the north; the company rode to the county line and conferred with a similar company from that county, but no engagement occurred, the band having apparently moved off before either company arrived. Pay claimed, 17 men (one, Uriah Bassett, laid up with a fever), 3 days each, 51 man-days, $38.25.

**Disposition of the apprehended deserter.** The man taken near the old mill dam on the 16th of September gave his name as Corbett and stated he belonged to a regiment from a distant state, having left it, he said, on account of illness in his family rather than from cowardice, a claim the sheriff was in no position to verify and did not attempt to. He was held two nights in the county jail at the county seat and then turned over to a provost detachment passing through on the 19th, the company's part in the matter ending with the turnover. The second deserter reported in the same neighborhood was never found, and the captain records his private opinion, for whatever it may be worth to the court, that the man had likely already crossed out of the county before the company was ever called out, the report reaching Winder's Depot some four days after the fact by the ordinary slowness of the neighborhood grapevine.

**Complaint against a private.** Private Whipple Doty was reported by Lieutenant Doane to have appeared at the 2nd of October muster without his piece, having lent it the week before to a neighbor for hog-killing and not yet recovered it, a lapse the captain considered serious enough to note formally though not serious enough to bring before the full company for discipline. Doty recovered the piece and produced it in serviceable order at the November muster, and the captain considers the matter closed, recording it here only so that the county court may see that the company's own officers do not let such lapses pass unremarked.

**Summary of pay claimed for the year 1862.** Total man-days of actual mustered duty: 146. Total pay claimed at 75 cents the day: $109.50, together with the captain's additional allowance of $1.00 the day for each day of actual command in the field beyond the ordinary private's pay, 10 days, $10.00, and the two lieutenants' additional allowance of 25 cents the day each for the same days, $5.00 in all. **Total pay account, $124.50.**

**Expense account, supplies and equipment.**

Powder, 20 pounds, purchased of Gantry's store at Winder's Depot, at 32 cents the pound, $6.40.
Lead, 30 pounds, at 11 cents the pound, $3.30.
Caps, 8 boxes, at 20 cents the box, $1.60.
Repair of two rifles found defective at the October inspection, by the county's own gunsmith, Jethro Pinckney (also a private in the company, paid separately for this work as a tradesman rather than as a soldier), $4.50.
Forage for horses used on the mounted patrols, hay and corn, an estimated $8.20 over the several musters, furnished partly by the men themselves without charge and partly purchased, the purchased portion being the sum stated.
One drum, purchased for the company's musters and alarms, from a peddler passing through in September, $3.00.

**Total expense account, supplies and equipment, $27.00.**

**Grand total, pay and expense, $151.50,** submitted to the county court for allowance and payment from the county's contingent fund, per the order of 3 August 1862 authorizing this company and pledging county funds for its support within reasonable limits, the court having previously indicated that it did not expect the annual cost of the Home Guard to exceed $200.00, a limit this account, together with such further musters as may occur before the year's end, is not expected to exceed.

The captain further reports that the company's arms remain, on the whole, in serviceable condition after the October repair, that morale is good, and that no man has asked to be relieved of his enrollment though the service is unpaid save for actual days mustered and takes the men from their farms at inconvenient seasons. The captain recommends that the county court consider furnishing at least ten stand of arms from the state, there being at present four men in the company, Askey, Grubb, Rowe, and Doty, who carry only such old fowling pieces as their households happen to own, of doubtful service in a genuine engagement.

Submitted this 30th day of November, 1862.

Ezekiel Marsh, Captain, Colfax County Home Guard
Barzillai Coombs, Orderly Sergeant, attesting the roll and the days of duty as above stated

Approved for payment by the Colfax County Court, session of December 1862, warrant drawn on the county treasurer for $151.50 in favor of the captain, to be distributed by him to the men according to the days severally served.
