# Toll Book, Wren's Bend Bridge over the Kettle River — Ezra Dabney, Tender (1899)

*The bridge, a covered wooden structure of one hundred and ten feet, the property of the Wren's Bend Bridge Company under its charter of some years' standing, toll rates posted at both ends per the company's schedule below.*

*The present bridge was raised in the year the old one washed out in the spring ice-break, the company's charter dating from some years before that and renewed by the county for a further term of twenty years at the time of rebuilding. I have kept this book myself, in my own hand, since I took the tender's place from my predecessor, one Overholt, who held it eleven years before me and left it, by his own account, in good order.*

**Toll schedule, as posted:** foot passenger, **1 cent**; man and horse, **3 cents**; horse and buggy, **5 cents**; horse and wagon, **8 cents**; two-horse team and wagon, **12 cents**; head of loose cattle or sheep driven across, **1 cent** each; hogs driven across, **1 cent** each; funeral processions, free; ministers of the gospel going to or from a service, free on showing of the company's pass card; mail carrier, free by contract with the company; residents of Wren's Bend village proper holding an annual pass, **$2.00** the year, unlimited crossings for themselves and one horse.

**Week of June 5–11, 1899, toll collected by day:**

Monday, the 5th: foot passengers, 14, $0.14; horse and buggy, 6, $0.30; wagons, one-horse, 9, $0.72; two-horse teams, 4, $0.48; cattle driven, 22 head, $0.22. Day's total, **$1.86**.

Tuesday, the 6th: foot, 11, $0.11; buggy, 4, $0.20; one-horse wagon, 12, $0.96; two-horse, 3, $0.36; cattle, none driven. Day's total, **$1.63**.

Wednesday, the 7th, being court day at the county seat, traffic heavier than usual: foot, 19, $0.19; man and horse, 8, $0.24; buggy, 11, $0.55; one-horse wagon, 15, $1.20; two-horse, 7, $0.84; hogs driven, 40, $0.40. Day's total, **$3.42**.

Thursday, the 8th: foot, 9, $0.09; buggy, 3, $0.15; one-horse wagon, 8, $0.64; two-horse, 2, $0.24. Day's total, **$1.12**.

Friday, the 9th: foot, 12, $0.12; buggy, 5, $0.25; one-horse wagon, 10, $0.80; two-horse, 5, $0.60; cattle, 15 head, $0.15. Day's total, **$1.92**.

Saturday, the 10th, market day: foot, 24, $0.24; man and horse, 6, $0.18; buggy, 9, $0.45; one-horse wagon, 21, $1.68; two-horse, 8, $0.96; cattle, 30 head, $0.30; hogs, 12, $0.12. Day's total, **$3.93**.

Sunday, the 11th: two funeral processions crossed, no toll; one minister crossed on pass card, no toll; foot passengers to and from church, 31, no toll collected on Sunday per the company's own rule of long standing, entered here for the record though it earns nothing. Day's total, **$0.00**.

**Week's total, June 5–11, 1899: $13.88.**

**Passes issued or renewed this week:** the Reverend Hosea Comeaux, minister at the Wren's Bend chapel, pass renewed, no charge; three annual resident passes renewed, Jethro Overstreet, Content Averill, and Barnabas Loftus, at $2.00 each, $6.00 received and entered separately from the daily tolls above, this sum going direct to the company's annual account rather than the toll-keeper's weekly settlement.

**Repairs and incidents, noted in the tender's own hand:**

*Tuesday the 6th.* A plank on the third span, west side, found split under a two-horse load about ten in the morning. I stopped traffic on that side for near an hour while I laid a spare plank over it, the company keeping three spares always under the toll house for this purpose. No charge to the company beyond my own time, already covered by my wages.

*Wednesday the 7th.* A dispute with a stranger driving a hired rig, who held that a man and horse pulling an empty cart should pay the horse-and-buggy rate of five cents rather than the horse-and-wagon rate of eight, there being no load. I told him the schedule reads by the vehicle and not by the load, a cart being a cart whether loaded or empty, and collected the eight cents, which he paid though grumbling, and drove on. I mention it as this is the second such dispute this year and I would ask the company to consider whether the posted schedule might be made plainer on this point.

*Saturday the 10th.* The Hessian fly reported bad in the wheat two townships over, according to talk among the market-day travelers, though I have no way to confirm it myself, not being a farming man, and set it down only as it was the chief talk crossing my bridge this week.

**On high water.** The company's rule, posted alongside the toll schedule, is that when the river stands within two feet of the bridge floor, as read on the gauge post at the east abutment, foot and light-vehicle traffic may still cross at the tender's discretion but heavy teams are to be turned back and directed to the ford a mile upstream, passable only in low water and without charge, the ford being a public road and not the company's property. This happened twice already this spring, once in March for the better part of a week during the thaw, and once for a single day in April after a heavy rain, on neither occasion any toll being lost worth recording beyond the ordinary Sunday abatement, most travelers simply waiting a day rather than risking the ford.

**Correspondence with the company.** I wrote to the company's secretary, Mr. Ambrose Ridgway, following the Wednesday dispute noted above, asking that the next printing of the toll schedule state plainly that the horse-and-wagon rate applies "whether loaded or empty, the rate being for the vehicle and team and not for its contents." Mr. Ridgway replied by the 12th that the board would take up the wording at its next quarterly meeting, and thanked me for bringing a matter of some standing confusion to a head at last, this apparently not being the first tender to raise it, though the first to put it in writing.

**Monthly settlement, May 1899, for comparison:** total tolls for the four full weeks and two odd days of May, **$54.20**, of which **$8.00** was in annual passes and **$46.20** in daily tolls, against **$51.90** total for April, the increase in May being put down by the company's treasurer to the start of the hay hauling season rather than to any change in the toll rate, which has stood unaltered since the bridge was rebuilt after the ice went out heavy some years back and took the old structure with it.

**My wages,** paid by the company at **$18.00** the month, plus the toll house itself to live in rent free, my wife keeping a small trade in eggs and garden truck from the same building to travelers waiting to cross when the water is high enough to make them wait, which happens perhaps four or five times in an ordinary spring.

*E. Dabney, Toll-keeper, Wren's Bend Bridge.*
