# Manifest and Way-Bill, Steamer Marigold — Increase Trumbo, Purser, Bellcourt Landing (1874)

*Voyage down, Bellcourt Landing to Corliss City and return, departing Tuesday, the 9th of June, 1874, Capt. Harlan Dietz commanding, Increase Trumbo, Purser.*

**Cargo received at Bellcourt Landing, for way points and Corliss City:**

| Shipper | Consignee | Goods | Marks | Weight/Count | Freight |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Sowerby & Petrie | J. Massingale, Corliss City | 40 bbls. flour | S&P | 8,000 lb | $12.00 |
| Ezra Dabney | self, Wren's Bend | 6 hhds. tobacco | E.D. | 3,300 lb | $6.60 |
| Bramlett Mercantile | Colquitt Bros., Reed's Ferry | 18 crates dry goods | B.M. No. 1–18 | 1,900 lb | $9.50 |
| Content Averill | consigned to order, Corliss City | 2 crates household goods | C.A. | 640 lb | $2.00 |
| Thaddeus Osgood | Corliss City Bank | 1 strongbox, sealed | T.O. | — | $1.00 |
| Whitfield Bros. | Renshaw & Co. | 30 coops live poultry | W.B. | — | $4.50 |
| J. Overstreet | Wren's Bend depot | 1 mowing machine, crated | J.O. | 640 lb | $3.00 |
| Perley Dunmore | self return trip | 12 bbls. lime | P.D. | 2,400 lb | $3.60 |

Total freight this voyage down, **$42.20**, entered against the boat's account and to be settled with the agent at Bellcourt Landing on the up trip, per custom.

**Passengers taken aboard at Bellcourt Landing:**

Mrs. Almira Sherwin and infant, cabin, to Reed's Ferry, **$1.25**; Mr. Silas Cathcart, deck, to Corliss City, **$0.75**; the Reverend Otis Marchetti, cabin, to Corliss City, clergy rate, **$0.60**; Miss Vashti Pruitt, cabin, to Wren's Bend, **$0.90**; two drovers, names Kilbride and Loftus, deck, with eleven head of stock, to Reed's Ferry, **$0.50** each plus **$0.10** a head for the stock, **$2.10** in all; Mr. Homer Applewhite, cabin, through to Corliss City, **$1.25**, paid in advance at the Bellcourt office and so noted on the way-bill.

**Wood and stores taken on:** 4 cords wood at Reed's Ferry landing, **$1.00** the cord, **$4.00**; 1 quarter of beef and sundries for the boat's table from the steward's own purse, **$6.40**, entered to the boat's expense account, not to freight.

**Draft of water reported** at the Sand Bar below Wren's Bend, three feet two inches at the gauge, the Marigold drawing two feet ten when loaded as above; passed without lightering.

---

**Purser's note, entered this same voyage, in the hand of Increase Trumbo:**

*I set down here what is not proper to the printed form, that the captain may have it before him at Corliss City.*

*The strongbox shipped by Thaddeus Osgood for the Corliss City Bank came aboard at half past six, after the manifest was already made up in ink, and I have entered it above by inserting the line, there being room. It was received into my own keeping, put under the counter of the clerk's office, and I sat by it myself until we made Reed's Ferry at eleven, when Captain Dietz relieved me so that I might sleep two hours. It went ashore into the hands of the Corliss City Bank's own messenger, one Mr. Colquitt, who signed my book, and I have his signature to show for it. I mention this only because the office at Bellcourt Landing did not weigh it and I could not tell the captain its weight when he asked, there being no scale free at that hour of the evening.*

*Second, the coops of poultry shipped by Whitfield Bros. arrived at the landing short by four coops of the number named in their own bill of lading, which called for thirty-four. I have taken on and receipted for thirty only, and have written across their shipping ticket to that effect, over my own initials, I.T. Whoever settles with Whitfield Bros. at Corliss City should charge freight on thirty coops and no more, whatever the bill of lading says, as I did not see the other four and will not carry paper for goods I never took aboard.*

*Third, the mowing machine shipped by J. Overstreet came aboard with one crate stove in at the corner, apparently from rough handling at the Bellcourt wharf before ever it reached the boat, and I noted this on the freight book and had the mate witness it, one Barnabas Loftus, deck hand, so that Overstreet cannot lay the damage to the boat. The crate is not open and I do not know what is broken inside it, if anything.*

*Fourth, Miss Vashti Pruitt paid her fare in advance at Bellcourt but asked leave to ride through to Corliss City instead of setting off at Wren's Bend as ticketed, on account of high water making the Wren's Bend road impassable to her people's wagon. I allowed it and have charged her the additional thirty-five cents, being the difference in fare, which she paid without complaint, and I have entered it separately below the passenger list rather than alter the printed figure already set down.*

*Fifth and last, the boat burned eleven cords of wood between Bellcourt Landing and Corliss City this trip against a usual nine, on account of the low water and the extra steam wanted to work off the Sand Bar without lightering. Captain Dietz remarked on it and I set it down so that the owners, seeing the wood bill high this month, will have the reason before them and not lay it to waste in the fire room.*

*Sixth, I should note that the mail pouch for Wren's Bend and Corliss City was twenty minutes late to the wharf at Bellcourt, brought down by a boy on horseback after the gangplank was already in, and Captain Dietz held the boat rather than leave it, there being no other boat down until Thursday. I make no complaint of this, the captain having the right of it, but the up-trip schedule posted at Bellcourt should perhaps allow the extra twenty minutes as a rule and not an exception, this being the fourth time since April that the pouch has come down late.*

*Signed, Increase Trumbo, Purser, Steamer Marigold, this 9th day of June, 1874, at Reed's Ferry, entered up further at Corliss City the following morning.*

**Endorsement, Bellcourt Landing agent, on receipt of this book back into the office:** Read and noted. The four short coops of Whitfield Bros. were taken up with their drayman, one Cathcart, who allows the shortage and will settle with the shipper direct, the boat's freight bill to stand at thirty coops as the purser has it. The strongbox matter requires nothing further, the bank's receipt being sufficient. Agent's initials, O.P., 12th June, 1874.

**Return cargo, taken on at Corliss City for Bellcourt Landing:** 60 bales cotton, consigned Sowerby & Petrie, **$18.00** freight; 4 bbls. molasses, consigned Bramlett Mercantile, **$3.20**; empty crates and coops returned, no charge. Return passengers, seven, fares **$5.15** in all. Voyage closed and books balanced at Bellcourt Landing the evening of the 11th of June, 1874.
