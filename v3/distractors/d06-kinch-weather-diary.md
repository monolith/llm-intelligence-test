# Weather Diary Kept at Sutcliffe's Notch — Obadiah Kinch, Garnett Township (1943)

Being a daily record of weather and farm work kept by the undersigned at his place at Sutcliffe's Notch for his own use and, as his father did before him for forty years, entered each evening by lamp with the thermometer read at sunrise, at noon, and again at sunset, hung on the north side of the porch out of direct sun.

**April 1943.**

1st. Sunrise 34, noon 52, sunset 44. Clear, light west wind. Ground still frozen an inch down in the shaded fence corners. Turned the horses out to the near pasture for the first time this year.

2nd. Sunrise 31, noon 49, sunset 40. Clear. Frost heavy on the low ground, killed what little the strawberry bed had put up. 

3rd. Sunrise 38, noon 58, sunset 47. Overcast by afternoon, rain beginning about eight in the evening, light. Began harrowing the east forty.

4th. Rain all day, 0.9 inch by the gauge, sunrise 44, noon 46, sunset 45, no real change in temperature the whole day. No work possible in the fields.

5th. Sunrise 43, clearing by ten, noon 61, sunset 50. Finished harrowing the east forty, 38 acres in all, three days' work with the two teams.

6th. Sunrise 39, noon 63, sunset 52. Clear and fine. Sowed oats on 14 acres of the east forty, the rest to go to corn later in the month.

7th through 10th. Weather held fine and settled, mornings between 37 and 43, noons between 60 and 66, no rain. Finished sowing oats on the 8th, 22 acres in all this spring. Began plowing the bottom ground for corn on the 9th.

11th. Sunrise 46, noon 68, sunset 58. A warm day for the season. Wind shifted south in the afternoon and by evening there was a smell of rain.

12th. Rain from before daylight until near noon, 1.4 inches by the gauge, the heaviest fall so far this spring. Sunrise 52, noon 54, sunset 49. Creek up over the notch road at the ford by mid-morning, impassable until the following afternoon.

13th. Sunrise 48, noon 57, sunset 46. Overcast, no further rain, ground too wet to work.

14th. Sunrise 41, noon 59, sunset 48. Clear. Resumed plowing the bottom ground.

15th. Sunrise 37, noon 55, sunset 44. Frost again in the low spots, the second killing frost this month, the strawberries hurt worse than the first time and the early peas nipped as well, though they may come again from the root.

16th through 20th. Settled fair weather, mornings 40 to 47, noons 62 to 70. Finished plowing the bottom ground the 17th, 26 acres. Planted the first corn the 19th and 20th, 18 acres of the earliest ground.

21st. Sunrise 50, noon 72, sunset 61. The warmest day of the year so far. Set out the tomato plants from the cold frame, a week earlier than my father would ever have risked it, but the ground was warm to the hand at four inches down.

22nd. Sunrise 53, noon 71, sunset 59, overcast building through the day. Thunderstorm about six in the evening, sharp but short, 0.6 inch, some hail mixed in the first ten minutes of it, no damage that I could find the next morning beyond a few bent corn shoots that straightened within the day.

23rd. Sunrise 49, noon 66, sunset 55. Clear after the storm. 

24th and 25th. Fair, mornings near 45, noons near 68. Planted the remainder of the corn ground, 22 acres, finishing on the 25th, 40 acres of corn in all for the year, four more than last year on account of having broken the old sedge field on the ridge.

26th. Sunrise 44, noon 60, sunset 48. Overcast, threatening, no rain fell.

27th. Rain beginning in the night, continuing through the morning, clearing by two in the afternoon, 0.7 inch. Sunrise 47, noon 51, sunset 50.

28th. Sunrise 45, noon 64, sunset 53. Clear and fine, a good drying day.

29th. Sunrise 41, noon 67, sunset 55. Clear. Began cutting the first hay off the timothy meadow, earlier than usual on account of the warm middle third of the month.

30th. Sunrise 46, noon 69, sunset 57. Clear. Finished the first hay cutting, 19 acres, put up dry with no rain interference, a stroke of luck I do not remember matching in any April of the last dozen years I have kept this book.

**Summary for the month.** Total rainfall by the gauge, 3.6 inches, which is a little under the average of the last ten Aprils by my reckoning, that average standing at about 4.1 inches. Two killing frosts, on the 2nd and the 15th, the second the more damaging. Mean of the noon readings for the month, 61.3 degrees, which I make out to be some two degrees above the average of the years I have kept close figures, though I did not begin taking a noon reading regularly until 1937 and cannot speak with confidence further back than that. Corn planted, 40 acres; oats, 22 acres; first hay cutting begun and finished within the month for the first time in my memory, always before a matter of early May at the soonest. My wife notes in the margin here, in her own hand, that the lilacs by the kitchen door bloomed on the 24th, six days ahead of last year, which she keeps her own separate record of and which I include here only because she asked me to leave the page for her note.

**Note on the keeping of this book.** My father, Amos Kinch, began a weather book at this place in the year he took up the land, and kept sunrise and sunset readings only, no noon figure, until his eyes failed him in his last years and the entries grew sparse and then stopped. I have kept this book since taking over the place, adding the noon reading from 1937 as noted above, on the advice of the county agent, who said a single midday figure would let a man compare one season to another better than the two extremes alone. I have also, since 1942, kept a small rain gauge of tin set on a post clear of the eaves and any tree, replacing my father's practice of guessing depth from how the barnyard puddled, which he always maintained was accurate enough for a farmer's purposes though I have never found it so.

**First days of May, added here for completeness though the month proper will have its own page.** 1st. Sunrise 43, noon 65, sunset 54, clear, a good day. 2nd. Sunrise 47, noon 70, sunset 59, clear, began the second cutting of the timothy on the low piece by the creek, which had come on faster than the upper meadow on account of the wetter ground. 3rd. Wind rose in the night out of the northwest, blew hard until near dawn, took six shingles off the corn crib roof and laid over a section of the garden fence, no other damage found on a walk round the place at first light; sunrise 41, noon 58, sunset 46, the wind having brought colder air down behind it as it generally does this time of year.

Obadiah Kinch
Sutcliffe's Notch, Garnett Township
30 April 1943, with the note on the book and the first days of May added the 4th of May
