# Inquest upon the Body of Obadiah Chalmers — Coroner Increase Marchbanks, Hartshorn, Dray County (1927)

*Held at the town hall, Hartshorn, this 14th day of January, 1927, upon view of the body of Obadiah Chalmers, carpenter, aged about forty-one years, found dead the morning of the 13th in the mill pond behind the Colquitt Bros. mill.*

**Jury sworn:** Ambrose Ridgway (foreman), Enos Delacroix, Micah Arceneaux, Cyrus Underhill, Hosea Comeaux, and Ira Stoddard, six good men of the township, none related to the deceased so far as examined and none objected to by any party present.

**Testimony of Levi Marchbanks, who found the body:**

"I was crossing the mill dam about half past six in the morning, on my way to the mill to see about a wagon wheel needing the smith there, when I saw what I took for a coat floating near the sluice gate on the pond side. I went down the bank and saw it was a man, face down, and I did not touch him but went straight to the mill and had them send for the coroner and for Dr. Pemberton, who came out from Dunlap Notch by half past eight, the roads being icy that morning."

**Testimony of Amasa Guilbeau, mill bookkeeper:**

"Chalmers had been doing carpenter work about the mill for the Colquitt Bros. these three weeks past, repairing the flume boards above the wheel. I saw him last on the evening of the 12th, about five o'clock, when he told me he meant to go back after supper and finish caulking one board that was still leaking, the mill being wanted to run again the next morning for a grinding the Sherwin place had ordered. I did not see him again."

**Testimony of Norah Garrahan, at whose boarding house the deceased had taken a room for the duration of the work:**

"Mr. Chalmers did not come in to supper on the evening of the 12th, which was not unusual of him when working late, and I kept his plate warm as I generally did. When he had not come in by eleven I supposed he had gone to visit kin at Wren's Bend, as he sometimes did of a Wednesday, and thought no more of it until word came the next day of what was found."

**Testimony of Dr. Nathaniel Pemberton, physician, who examined the body:**

"I examined the body at the mill about nine o'clock on the morning of the 13th. Death had occurred, in my judgment, some ten to fourteen hours before, which would place it between seven and eleven the evening previous. There was water in the lungs consistent with drowning, and no wound upon the body save a bruise upon the left temple, of a kind that could have been made by a fall against the flume timbers, or against a stone at the bottom of the pond, or indeed by very little at all, the flesh there being thin over the bone. I found no sign that the man had been struck by another hand, no defensive marks, and nothing to suggest a struggle. In my opinion he went into the water living, struck his head in the fall or shortly after, and drowned before he could recover himself, the water at that point being some four and a half feet, by the mill's own gauge, more than enough to drown a stunned man though not enough, I should think, to drown one in full possession of his senses."

**Testimony of Barnabas Loftus, who worked with the deceased on occasion:**

"I told Chalmers more than once that board he was caulking wanted a plank laid across for a man to stand on rather than balancing on the flume edge itself, the footing there being narrow and often wet from the spray. He always meant to get to it and never did, there being, as he said, always some other thing more pressing that week."

**Testimony of Homer Colquitt, one of the proprietors of the mill:**

"We had let the caulking work to Chalmers on the strength of work he had done for us before, at a price of thirty-five cents the running foot, the flume needing perhaps sixty feet gone over in all. He had done about forty feet of it by the evening of the 12th, by my own measure taken since, and would have finished within two or three more evenings had this not happened. I do not recall ever telling him to use the flume edge itself for footing rather than a plank; if it was understood between us that he would, it was not by my instruction, and I would have furnished a plank on the asking, several lying idle in the mill yard at all times."

**Testimony of the deceased's brother, Wilfred Chalmers, as to his general habits:**

"My brother was not a drinking man to my knowledge, and had not been since he came out of the war, whatever he may have been before it. He wore spectacles for close work but could see well enough at a distance, and I know of no fits or fainting spells troubling him. He was, I will say, careless of his own safety in small ways, the sort of man who would step out on a beam rather than walk around, and this had worried me before, though never enough that I thought to write him about it."

**The coroner examined the flume edge himself, in company with two of the jury, and reports to the jury that the plank Loftus describes was indeed never laid, and that the flume edge at the point nearest the leaking board is some ten inches wide, wet, and without any rail.**

**Verdict of the jury,** returned after deliberation of some forty minutes:

*"We find that Obadiah Chalmers came to his death on the night of the 12th of January, 1927, by drowning in the mill pond of Colquitt Bros., Hartshorn, the same having resulted from an accidental fall from the flume edge while at his work, without evidence of the hand of any other person, and we recommend to the owners of said mill that a rail or footing plank be provided at that point hereafter, that no like accident befall another man there."*

**Expenses of the inquest, certified to the county for payment:**

Coroner's fee, **$5.00**; six jurors at **$1.00** each, **$6.00**; witness fees, four at **$0.75** each, **$3.00**; physician's fee for examination and testimony, **$4.00**; hall rent, **$1.00**; total, **$19.75**, submitted to the Dray County commissioners for allowance at their next regular session.

**Disposition of the body:** released to the deceased's brother, one Wilfred Chalmers, of the county seat, who arrived the afternoon of the 14th and arranged burial there, the undertaker's charges being no part of the county's expense.

**Coroner's note, appended for the county record:** I have written separately to Colquitt Bros. asking that the rail recommended by the jury be installed within thirty days, and have asked the township clerk to note the matter on the agenda of the next meeting of the board, that it not be forgotten once the immediate matter has passed from mind, as such recommendations too often are.

*Increase Marchbanks, Coroner, Dray County, this 15th day of January, 1927.*
