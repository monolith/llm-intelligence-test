# Subscription List for the Repair of Mount Ebal Church — Rev. Increase Wardlaw, Dunmore's Crossing, Thorncastle County (1953)

In the fall of the year 1952 it pleased Providence to send a wind out of the west on the night of the 9th of November such as the oldest members of this congregation could not recall the like of, and it took the greater part of the roof from the north side of Mount Ebal Church, carried the steeple bell-frame down into the churchyard where it broke two headstones in its fall, though doing no harm to any person, the building being empty at the hour. The session met on the following Sabbath and appointed the undersigned, together with the elders Josephus Kane and Reuben Stark, to view the damage and estimate what repair would cost, and to circulate a subscription among the members and friends of the congregation for that purpose, the church having no fund of its own beyond $40.00 kept against ordinary repairs, which sum was plainly insufficient to the work now required.

The committee obtained the estimate of a builder from the county seat, one Hosea Trumbull, who examined the roof, the bell-frame, and the north wall, which had also been strained by the fall of the frame upon it, and set the cost of full repair at $412.00, namely $260.00 for new roof timbers, shingling, and the rehanging of the bell in a rebuilt frame; $95.00 for repointing and bracing the north wall; and $57.00 for glass, paint, and incidental carpentry. The committee, believing this figure to be a fair one and having no reason to doubt Trumbull's word, being a man of good repute known to several members through his work on the new bridge at Dunmore's Crossing two years before, recommended that the congregation raise the sum by subscription rather than by borrowing, and the session so ordered on the 20th of November.

The subscription was opened the following Sabbath, the 27th of November, 1952, and stood open through the winter, being closed and the accounts settled on the 2nd of April, 1953, in time for the repair to be finished before the summer camp meeting. The subscribers, and the manner of their giving, cash or in kind, follow below, together with the date each subscription was paid in full, where it has been.

Deacon Josephus Kane, $25.00, paid 30 November 1952.
Reuben Stark, elder, $20.00, paid 5 December 1952.
Widow Salome Purnell, $8.00, paid in two installments, $4.00 on 3 December and $4.00 on 14 January 1953, she having asked the committee's indulgence on account of her circumstances, which was freely given.
Nehemiah Millhouse, $15.00, paid 12 December 1952.
The Trundle family, jointly, father and two grown sons, $30.00, paid 18 December 1952.
Constable Titus Loveall, $5.00, paid 20 December 1952.
Hezekiah Whately, $10.00, paid 2 January 1953.
Mrs. Ophelia Kane, separately from her husband's subscription above, out of her own egg money as she stipulated, $3.00, paid 10 January 1953.
Cornelius Whitlatch, $18.00, paid 15 January 1953.
Ezekiel and Almira Sperry, household, $12.00, paid 1 February 1953.
Roxana Ondrus, $6.00, paid 1 February 1953.

Sundry subscribers of $1.00 and $2.00 each, eleven persons, $16.00 in the aggregate, all paid by 1 March 1953, their names entered in the church's own book but not repeated here for brevity.

**Total cash subscribed and paid:** $177.00.

**Subscriptions in kind.**

Hosea Trumbull himself, the builder, subscribed 20 days of his own labor toward the roof timbering, valued by the committee at $1.75 the day, being his ordinary wage, $35.00, in addition to being paid in cash for the balance of the contract; this the committee records with gratitude as going beyond the letter of his estimate.
Reuben Stark furnished oak timber from his own wood lot sufficient for the principal rafters, sawed at his own expense at the Whitlatch mill, valued at $22.00.
Josephus Kane furnished the lime for repointing the north wall, twelve barrels at $0.90, $10.80.
Six members of the congregation, whose names are entered in the church book, gave a total of 34 days' common labor toward hauling stone, mixing mortar, and general assistance to the builder, valued at $1.25 the day for unskilled work, $42.50.
The Ladies' Aid Society of the church held a supper on the 3rd of February, 1953, and turned over its proceeds of $19.60 to the building fund, this being the whole of the society's earnings for the winter's work.

**Total value of subscriptions in kind:** $130.30 (rounded in the committee's own summary to $130.00, the odd thirty cents being absorbed into the general fund without separate accounting).

**Grand total raised, cash and kind together:** $307.30, against the estimate of $412.00, leaving a shortfall of $104.70 at the closing of the subscription on the 2nd of April. The committee reports that this shortfall was met by a loan of $100.00 from Cornelius Whitlatch personally, without interest, to be repaid from the ordinary collection over the two years following at not less than $50.00 the year, and by $4.70 remaining in the church's ordinary repair fund after the immediate needs of worship were provided for. The committee further reports that the work itself, under Trumbull's hand, began the 10th of April and was substantially complete by the 30th of May, 1953, the bell rehung and rung for the first time in its new frame on Whitsunday, to the satisfaction of the whole congregation, several of whom remarked that it rang truer than before, though the committee ventures no opinion on whether a bell can in fact be improved by a fall.

**A note on the bell itself.** The committee records, for the satisfaction of future readers of this book, that the bell was cast in the year 1867 by a foundry since gone out of business, and bears no maker's name that any member has been able to make out through the weathering of the metal, only a scrollwork band and the numerals of the casting year. It weighs, by the estimate of Hosea Trumbull, who lifted one end of it himself in the course of the repair, upward of 200 pounds, though no scale in the neighborhood was equal to weighing it exactly, and the committee did not think the question worth the trouble of hauling it to the county seat for the purpose. The frame that broke in the November storm was itself a replacement of an earlier frame, said to have been built when the church was new, of which no member now living has any first-hand memory, the present building being the second on this ground, the first having burned in a year none of the older members could agree upon when the committee inquired, some saying it was before the war and some after, a question the committee does not undertake to settle here, it having no bearing on the present accounting.

**Disposition of the two broken headstones.** The stones broken by the fall of the bell-frame marked the graves of members of the Purnell and Kane families, both long deceased, and the committee arranged with a stonecutter from the county seat to repair them as part of the general work, at a cost of $6.00 for the two, charged against the general fund rather than the building subscription proper, since the churchyard is technically a separate trust from the building itself under the terms of the original deed of 1867, a distinction the committee notes here only so that no later reader of this book mistakes the $6.00 for part of the $412.00 estimate above, the two sums having no connection to one another beyond having been paid out of the same strongbox in practice.

Submitted to the session and ordered entered in the church record this 2nd day of April, 1953.

Increase Wardlaw, Pastor
Josephus Kane, Elder
Reuben Stark, Elder
