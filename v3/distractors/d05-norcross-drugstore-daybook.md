# Day-Book of Norcross's Drug Store — Elias Norcross, Proprietor, Bellhaven, Sedge County (1932)

Being the sales entered day by day for the month of March, 1932, in the store kept by the undersigned on the public square at Bellhaven, entered each evening after closing from the counter slips, cash and charge alike, charge sales marked "chg." against the customer's name in the ledger proper and not repeated here beyond the day-book note.

**Tuesday, 1 March.** Castor oil, 4 oz., Mrs. Pettibone, 15 cents, cash. Sen-sen and a cigar, Constable Fry, 10 cents, cash. One box headache powders, Rev. Doak, 25 cents, chg. Postage stamps sold, $1.20, cash, commission 2 cents the dollar retained. Total cash $1.45, charges $0.25.

**Wednesday, 2 March.** Quinine capsules, one dozen, for the Amsden baby's fever, 30 cents, chg. to Orrin Amsden. Peppermint drops, 2 cents, to three different children, 6 cents cash total. Epsom salts, 1 lb., 12 cents, cash. Total cash $0.18, charges $0.30.

**Thursday, 3 March.** Liniment, Norcross's own compounding, 4 oz. bottle, 20 cents, to Osric Bellamy for a strained shoulder, cash. Sticking plaster, one roll, 15 cents, cash. Talcum powder, one tin, 20 cents, Mrs. Fry, chg. Total cash $0.35, charges $0.20.

**Friday, 4 March.** A busy day, being the day the mill hands are paid. Cigars, 14 sold at 5 cents, 70 cents cash. Tobacco, plug and loose, $1.10 cash. Soda water at the fountain, 23 glasses at 5 cents, $1.15 cash. Ice cream, 9 dishes at 10 cents, 90 cents cash. Total cash $3.85, no charges entered.

**Saturday, 5 March.** The heaviest day of the week, as usual. Fountain trade, sodas and ice cream together, $4.60 cash. Patent medicines: one bottle bitters, 75 cents, cash; two boxes liver pills, 50 cents, cash. Toilet soap, six cakes sold at 8 cents, 48 cents, mixed cash and chg. Sundries, combs, hairpins, shoe polish, $1.30 cash. Prescription compounded for Dr. Sedgwick, cough syrup for the widow Crane, 40 cents, chg. to the doctor's account as is his custom. Total cash $6.98, charges $0.40.

**Monday, 7 March.** Store closed in the morning for the funeral of old Mr. Sanger; opened at one o'clock. Afternoon trade light. Stationery, one box, 25 cents, cash. Aspirin, two tins, 20 cents, cash. Total cash $0.45.

**Tuesday, 8 March through Saturday, 12 March.** (Entered together as in prior months when trade is ordinary and no item calls for separate note.) Cash sales for the five days, fountain and sundries together, $14.60. Charge sales for the five days: Mrs. Pettibone, cold cream and face powder, 45 cents; Rev. Doak, one more box headache powders, 25 cents; Osric Bellamy, razor blades, 20 cents; the Bellamy household account otherwise nil this week. Prescriptions compounded for Dr. Sedgwick, three, at an average of 55 cents each, $1.65, all chg. to the doctor. Total cash $14.60, charges $2.55.

**Monday, 14 March.** The traveling man from the wholesale house, Purcell & Weare of the county seat, called and took the month's order: quinine, 3 lb. at $1.10 the pound; Epsom salts, 25 lb. at 6 cents; castor oil, 2 gal. at $1.60 the gallon; assorted patent medicines per the printed list, $28.40; sundries and toilet goods, $19.75; total order $59.65, on the usual sixty days' credit. Store trade this day, cash $2.10, charges $0.30.

**Tuesday, 15 March, through Saturday, 19 March.** Cash sales, $16.20. Charges: Constable Fry, tobacco on account for the first time this month, 40 cents; Mrs. Pettibone, castor oil and a fever thermometer, 65 cents; Orrin Amsden, more quinine for the baby, still poorly, 30 cents; the mill company's account, for first aid supplies kept at the mill office under standing arrangement, iodine and bandage, $1.10. Total charges for the week, $2.45.

**Sunday, 20 March.** Store closed, as every Sunday, excepting only that the proprietor answered the door at nine in the evening to sell one bottle of paregoric to Mrs. Fry for a child with the colic, entered Monday as an accommodation sale, 20 cents, cash, no profit taken on Sunday trade as a matter of the proprietor's own rule though the statute does not forbid it in this county.

**Monday, 21 March, through Saturday, 26 March.** Cash sales, $19.85, fountain trade being heavier with the first warm days. Charges: Rev. Doak's account now stands at $1.10 unpaid since the 1st, and the proprietor notes in the margin that it is the parson's custom to settle quarterly rather than monthly, which has always been honored before. Dr. Sedgwick's account for prescriptions this stretch, five compounded, $2.90. Osric Bellamy, paint and turpentine, not strictly drugstore goods but carried as a side line, $1.40.

**Thursday, 24 March.** A dispute arose with Mrs. Pettibone over her account, she contending that a bottle of tonic charged to her on the 9th at 60 cents had in fact been returned unopened on the 10th and never credited. The proprietor examined the day-book and found no entry of a return on the 10th, but on inquiry of his clerk, young Fenn, learned that the boy had indeed taken back such a bottle and set it on the shelf without telling anyone or entering it, being new to the work at the time. The proprietor credited Mrs. Pettibone's account the 60 cents and spoke to Fenn about the importance of entering every transaction, return or sale, on the day it occurs, no matter how small.

**Friday, 25 March through Wednesday, 30 March.** Trade continued at the ordinary pace. Notable sales: a case of the grippe going through the schoolhouse brought a run on quinine and camphor, six households buying in the same three days, cash sales for quinine alone in this stretch reaching $1.90 where the ordinary week sees perhaps 40 cents. The traveling photographer who stopped in Bellhaven the 28th and 29th bought postcards and a bottle of hair oil, 35 cents, cash, and remarked that the store was the neatest kept he had seen between here and the county seat, which the proprietor records here for his own satisfaction rather than for any bearing on the accounts.

**Thursday, 31 March.** Month's end. Cash drawer counted and reconciled against the day-book entries above, found to agree within 4 cents, which discrepancy the proprietor attributes to a miscounted nickel in making change on the 26th and does not propose to chase further. Charge accounts posted to the ledger proper: Dr. Sedgwick, Rev. Doak, Mrs. Pettibone, Osric Bellamy, Constable Fry, Orrin Amsden, and the mill company, seven accounts current, none more than forty-five days old excepting Rev. Doak's, which stands on its usual quarterly footing and is not considered overdue.

Wholesale order placed with Purcell & Weare, $59.65, due 13 May 1932. Rent of the store building, paid to the landlord, Thaddeus Prue, $18.00 for the month. Clerk's wages, one part-time boy, Tobias Fenn, three afternoons a week after school, $3.00 for the month. Net for the proprietor's own reckoning, after these and sundry small expenses not separately itemized, approximately $71.00 for March, which he notes is $4.00 better than February and about even with March of the year before.

Elias Norcross
Bellhaven, Sedge County
31 March 1932
