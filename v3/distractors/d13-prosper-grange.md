# Minutes of Prosper Grange, No. 9, Patrons of Husbandry — Secretary Editha Osgood, Prosper Mills, Coble County (1900)

*Kept by the Secretary, Prosper Mills, in the township hall over Denniston's store.*

**Regular meeting, second Saturday of February, 1900. Called to order 7:40 in the evening by Worthy Master Amos Pryor. Twenty-six members present, four visitors from Marsh Fork Grange admitted on card.**

The Chaplain, Sister Delia Wickett, opened with a reading from the Psalms and a prayer for the season's plowing. The Secretary read the minutes of the January meeting, which stood approved as read, one correction made to the spelling of Brother Zebulon Sherwin's name.

**Roll of officers present:** Worthy Master, Amos Pryor; Overseer, Rufus Calloway; Lecturer, Sister Prudence Marchetti; Steward, Thaddeus Osgood; Chaplain, Sister Delia Wickett; Treasurer, Orville Petrie; Secretary, Editha Osgood; Gate Keeper, Perley Dunmore; Ceres, Sister Almira Sherwin; Pomona, Sister Louisa Trask; Flora, Sister Winifred Colquitt; Lady Assistant Steward, Sister Clarissa Boucher.

**Treasurer's report.** Brother Petrie reported a balance on hand at the close of January of **$114.60**. Dues collected during the month from nineteen members at **fifty cents** each amounted to **$9.50**. Two initiation fees were received, from Brother Homer Kilbride and Brother Eli Renshaw, at **two dollars** each, being **$4.00**. Total receipts for the month, **$13.50**. Disbursements: hall rent to Denniston's estate, **$3.00**; lamp oil and wicks, **$0.85**; postage for the Lecturer's correspondence, **$0.30**; total disbursements **$4.15**. Balance carried forward, **$123.95**. Report accepted.

**Old business.** The committee appointed in December to inquire into the joint purchase of a corn sheller reported that the Whitcomb make could be had of the implement dealer at Corbin Junction for **$38.00**, freight paid to Prosper Mills, or for **$41.50** delivered with a spare set of teeth. On motion of Brother Calloway, seconded by Sister Marchetti, it was voted to purchase the machine at **$38.00** and to assess the fifteen members who signed the sheller list **$2.55** apiece, the odd nickel to be absorbed by the Grange treasury. Brother Osgood was appointed to take delivery and to keep the machine at his barn, open to signers on two days' notice.

The Lecturer reported that the essay contest announced in November had drawn three entries: "Diversified Farming Against the Single Crop," by Sister Marchetti; "The Care of Fence Line Timber," by Brother Sherwin; and "What the Grange Owes Its Daughters," by Sister Boucher. The Grange voted, twenty-two to four, to award the small prize, a subscription to the county paper for one year valued at **$1.25**, to Sister Marchetti's essay, which was read in full and ordered spread upon the record book kept separately for such matters.

**New business.** Brother Dunmore moved that the Grange petition the township supervisors for a covered shed at the Prosper Mills depot platform, the present arrangement leaving milk cans standing in the weather from five in the morning until the nine o'clock train. The motion carried without dissent, and Brothers Pryor and Petrie were named a committee of two to draw the petition and to present it at the supervisors' next session.

A communication was read from the State Grange office announcing the annual session to be held in the fall, and inviting each subordinate Grange to send delegates in the proportion of one to every twenty-five members in good standing. Prosper Grange, having forty-one members in good standing as of this meeting, is entitled to one delegate. On ballot, Brother Calloway was chosen, with Sister Wickett as alternate.

The question of admitting hired farm hands to membership at the reduced rate allowed by the by-laws was raised by Brother Renshaw and referred to the executive committee for report at the March meeting, no vote being taken tonight.

**Applications for membership** were received from Miss Content Averill, of the Dunlap Notch road, and from Mr. Seneca Marchetti, brother to Sister Prudence Marchetti, both proposed by Brother Pryor and referred to the usual committee of investigation, to report in two weeks.

**Sickness and distress.** The Chaplain reported that Sister Boucher's mother had been laid up since Christmas with a broken hip and moved that the Grange send a card of sympathy, which was so ordered, and that the sum of **$3.00** be voted from the relief fund toward a nurse's wages for one week. Carried.

**The lecture hour** was given over to a discussion, opened by Brother Osgood, of the price then being paid for wool at Corbin Junction, which he reported at **twenty-two cents** the pound for unwashed fleece, against **twenty-six cents** the year previous, and the meeting generally agreed that the falling price owed more to the tariff talk in the newspapers than to the quality of the local clip. Sister Trask read a set of household hints clipped from a farm journal, concerning the keeping of eggs in waterglass over winter, which several sisters asked to have copied into the Grange scrapbook.

**Refreshments** were served by the committee of Sisters Colquitt, Trask, and Boucher: coffee, doughnuts, and a cake contributed by Sister Wickett, the whole enjoyed by all present. The Gate Keeper reported the hall swept and the stove banked before the members' departure.

**Dues in arrears.** The Secretary read the standing list of members behind on dues: Brother Ozias Fenwick, three quarters, **$1.50**; Brother Barnabas Loftus, two quarters, **$1.00**; Sister Electa Pemberton, one quarter, **$0.50**. On motion, the Secretary was instructed to send each a card of notice, and to report to the executive committee any member two full years in arrears for possible suspension under Article IV of the by-laws.

**Committee on the Harvest Home Supper.** Though the season is early, Brother Pryor asked leave to name a committee now so that the hall might be engaged ahead of the Odd Fellows, who hold their own supper the same week most years. Sisters Colquitt, Wickett, and Trask were named, with authority to spend up to **$15.00** from the social fund toward hire of dishes, a fiddler, and provisions beyond what members bring in themselves. The date was tentatively set for the last Thursday in August, to be confirmed at the June meeting.

**Communication.** A letter was read from the corresponding secretary of Marsh Fork Grange, thanking Prosper Grange for the loan of its degree regalia at their December installation, and returning it by the bearer, Brother Renshaw, in good order save one soiled sash, for which Marsh Fork enclosed **$0.40** toward cleaning. The sum was ordered turned over to the Steward, who has charge of the regalia chest.

Brother Sherwin raised, informally and without motion, the matter of a joint order for binder twine ahead of haying, observing that the Corbin Junction dealer had quoted **nine and one-half cents** the pound in lots of five hundred pounds or better against **eleven cents** for smaller orders. The chair asked members wishing to join such an order to leave their names with Brother Osgood before the March meeting, when the matter would come up as regular business.

**Attendance for the roll of the year** now stands: meetings held since April of the previous year, ten; average attendance, twenty-one; largest attendance, thirty-four, at the December meeting held jointly with Marsh Fork Grange; smallest, fourteen, in August during haying.

Voted to adjourn at twenty minutes past nine. Closed in due form.

*Next regular meeting: second Saturday of March, 1900, same hour. Degree work on two candidates to be conferred at the April meeting, the Master to appoint the degree team not later than the first of April.*

Respectfully submitted and entered in the permanent book this 14th day of February, 1900.

*[Secretary's initials: E.O.]*
