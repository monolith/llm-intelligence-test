# Retention notes — retellings 1–8 (Ostrey Hollow / Larrow Valley dairy cycle)

## Master cast and relationships
- **Gideon Vose** — founded/organized the Larrow Valley Cooperative Creamery Association in 1889 at Larrow Green; first president 1889–1911 (22 years, longest-serving); drafted the by-laws himself (not a lawyer); died 1917. Father of **Selby Vose**.
- **Selby Vose** — son (NOT nephew — r01 wrongly calls him Gideon's "nephew"; r08, in Selby's own words, confirms he is the son of "your first president") — Association manager 1913–1938 (r02) though his own 1926 address says "thirteen years this spring, since 1913." Signed/sent the 1919 circular alleging water in the Ostrey Hollow cans. Kept his own Jersey herd at the home place; it tested 4.56 in 1910. Gave the 1926 annual address (r08) blaming patrons for watering milk, not the tester.
- **Orra Keddie** (née Brant) — wife of Ansel Keddie; sister of Emmet Brant. Old Mrs. Vose's first cousin (per r01) — i.e., some Vose-family tie to the Keddie household existed independent of Selby. After 1919 she and her brother Emmet never spoke again; she'd walk to Larrow Green rather than pass his lane.
- **Ansel Keddie** — tester at the Ostrey Hollow skimming station from its start (went up in 1895 to prepare it, station opened/began testing 4 May 1896) through 1924 (29 seasons); dismissed 30 April 1925 after the arbitration; died in the 1930s (r01: "died in the thirties"; r04 specifies 1934). Kept the test book in five columns (date, patron, pounds, test, fat) in his own hand; meticulous, never wrote a figure over another. Married to Orra Brant.
- **Emmet Brant** — Orra's brother; the station's largest patron every year on record; had the most cows/hay ground on Hessel Bottom; the man the 1919 circular cost the most. Father of **Roy Brant**, grandfather (via Roy or another line) of **Hazel Brant**.
- **Duncan Keddie** — Ansel's son; taught school at Ashlin; father of **Ivy Keddie**.
- **Ivy Keddie** — Ansel's granddaughter (via Duncan); affiliated with the State Agricultural College at Farland; in February 1958 found the crate of condemned pipettes; measured them 9 March 1958; gave the 1959 talk "What the Crate Held" to the Ordell County Historical Society at Ashlin (r04), proving the pipettes — not theft or watering — caused the low test readings.
- **Effie Loomis** — Association secretary 1901–1940; wrote "Notes toward a history of the Association" (1941) (r02). Was 14 in 1889, so has no firsthand memory of the founding.
- **Wilbur Teague** — Association bookkeeper 1905–1945 (per his own account, r05) — though r02 (Effie) says he "came in as bookkeeper in 1905," consistent on the start date. Invented the "station-gain" ledger line himself starting 1905 (but see conflict below re: 1898 entry predating his tenure). Testified to the 1925 board that the station's one-man setup (weigh/sample/test/write/separate all done by Keddie alone) invited theft — suspicious of Keddie. Wrote his memoir chapter ("The Books of the Association," ch. 9) in winter 1948, i.e. before Ivy's 1958 exoneration.
- **Hobart Sill** — buttermaker at Larrow Green; kept the churn book and cream receiving book (r02).
- **Alonzo Frick** — attorney of Ashlin; chaired the 1925 board of arbitration, described by multiple sources (Teague, Vose) as fair/impartial.
- **Merle Strawn** — state dairy inspector for the Ordell circuit, 1907–1936 (took circuit 1907, retired/laid it down 1936); kept 28 pocket circuit-books (oilcloth covers), one per year; his covering note and extracts written 1936 (r06). His notebooks went to the state dairy office (1936), then to the Farland college library (1947), sat unused 11 years until Ivy consulted the 1923 volume in 1958.
- **Rosalie Cudd** — runs the Alder Corners Cheese Company (Vessey County, over Redlow Ridge); Jerome Cudd's niece; copied out (in 1958) the factory's 1919-season test figures for Ivy; has never seen the actual test book (it never left Vessey County).
- **Jerome Cudd** — presumably the Alder Corners factory's founder (implied); per catalog Item 13, the test book passed from "its founder to his daughter" in 1941, then to the Vessey County Historical Society in 1970.
- **Halbert Nym** — curator, Ordell County Historical Rooms; wrote the 1972 accession notes for items 1–16 (r07), the most precise/authoritative documentary source.
- **Vira Toland** — narrator of r01; born 1912 at the top of Ostrey Hollow; daughter of a Hessel Bottom milk-hauling patron; recorded on tape in 1979 at Ostrey Hollow by a young man who asked three questions in an hour.
- Unnamed young woman researcher from "the college away east" who visited Vira in 1958, was "Hazel Brant's first cousin once removed" — likely Ivy Keddie herself (timing matches Ivy's 1958 research trip), though r01 never gives her name.
- Nine families hauled milk to the Ostrey Hollow station (per Vira); seven farms had rights on Hessel Bottom itself (per r02), the largest being Emmet Brant's.

## Places / institutions
- **Ostrey Hollow** — valley with milk-hauling families; the skimming station sits at its head/top, seven miles up from Larrow Green, where the road bends toward the ridge.
- **Hessel Bottom** — wet meadow below the Hollow hamlet; the best milk-producing land; seven farms had rights there.
- **Larrow Green** — site of the main creamery, built 1891 on Larrow Creek (same building still standing per r02); Association's home base for the cooperative.
- **Ashlin** — county seat area; site of the Association office (one room over a harness shop), the 1925 board of arbitration hearings, Duncan Keddie's school, and (later) the Ordell County Historical Rooms/Society.
- **Larrow Valley Cooperative Creamery Association** — organized 1889 at Larrow Green.
- **Vessey County / Alder Corners** — over Redlow Ridge; site of the Alder Corners Cheese Company, used as an independent cross-check on milk-fat test results.
- **Farland** — site of the State Agricultural College; holds Strawn's circuit notebooks (since 1947) and where Ivy Keddie measured the pipettes (9 March 1958).
- **Ammon & Sons Dairy Supply, Tarnet** — the equipment supplier; sold the station's original 1897 testing outfit and the 1923 replacement.
- **Ordell County Historical Society / Rooms** — hosted Ivy's 1959 talk; later (1972) the site of Halbert Nym's cataloguing.

## The central object: the milk-fat testing apparatus
- Original outfit invoice (Ammon & Sons, 14 April 1897, per catalog Item 2 — quoted verbatim):
  > *Sold to the Larrow Valley Co-op. Creamery Assn., for the Ostrey Hollow station: 6 doz. milk test pipettes, 17.6 c.c., at .35 — 25.20; 12 doz. test bottles, 8 per cent neck, at .22 — 31.68; 1 acid measure — .90; 1 hand tester, 4 bottle — 18.00; Freight — 2.15; Total — 77.93*
- In use at the station from 1 May 1897 (Larrow Green had lent equipment before then; r03 confirms Keddie began on borrowed Ashlin/Larrow Green glass in 1896, own outfit arrived "the spring following," i.e. 1897).
- Six dozen (72) pipettes bought; 9 unbroken pipettes survived in the crate found in 1958.
- Pipettes were blown/marked for 17.6 c.c. but measured (9 March 1958, against a calibrated burette, mean of 5 runs each) at: 16.70, 16.71, 16.72, 16.72, 16.72, 16.73, 16.73, 16.74, 16.71 — mean 16.72 c.c. — short 0.88 c.c., i.e. delivering 95% (19/20) of the marked charge.
- Because the test-bottle neck is ruled to read percent for exactly the marked charge, a short charge reads short in the same proportion: every test at that station read 95% of true.
- The pipettes were condemned by state inspector Merle Strawn on **12 June 1923** ("Ran their milk pipettes against the burette. These measures will not deliver their mark. Condemned the lot, the whole crate of them, and told the manager to order new. Glass is glass and I did not weigh what it has cost them." — r06/catalog Item 9, verbatim). New glass ordered 28 June 1923 (Ammon & Sons letter of 2 July 1923 acknowledged the order, promised shipment, "declining to say anything of the earlier lot" — catalog Item 10); new pipettes in use from July 1923; Strawn confirmed in October 1923 the new pipettes "deliver their mark."
- CONFLICT: Selby Vose's 1926 address (r08) gives the condemnation date as "the twelfth of July, 1923" — one month off from Strawn's own notebook and the catalog's "12 June 1923."
- The condemned crate: wooden, stenciled "AMMON & SONS DAIRY SUPPLY TARNET," carried up in June 1923. CONFLICT on where it was later found: r04 (Ivy's 1959 talk) says she found it herself in February 1958 in the old station building's loft at Ostrey Hollow, under a tarpaulin, having been carried there in June 1923; catalog Item 5 (Nym, 1972) instead says the crate "was found in the Association office at Ashlin, in February 1958, by Miss Ivy Keddie." Same person, same month/year, different location.
- The crate/pipettes never came up at all in the 1925 arbitration hearing — explicitly noted by Effie Loomis (r02): "Not a word of it was spoken in any of the three sessions."

## The test-result numbers and the arithmetic of exoneration
- Ansel Keddie's own summary leaf (station test book), made up 1922, quoted identically in r03, r04, and catalog Item 3:
  > *Average test of all patrons, this station: 1896, 3.80. 1897, part year. 1898, 3.61. And 3.61 or within three hundredths of it every year since, save the nine weeks of 1911 that are noted in their place, when it stood at 3.80.*
- Ivy's 1958 arithmetic (r04): recorded/credited average 3.61; true average = 3.61 ÷ 0.95 = 3.80; gap = 0.19 points = 5% of the true 3.80 (and 5.25% of the recorded/credited 3.61 — "two percentages... not interchangeable," each correct of its own denominator, per catalog Item 6's curatorial note).
- Quantities (25 full years, 1898–1922): station took in **40,000,000 lb** of milk (confirmed independently by r02/Effie AND catalog Item 6 — CONFLICTS with Wilbur Teague's r05 memoir figure of **44,000,000 lb** "over the years the account ran," and Teague's stated overall average test of **3.16**, both of which disagree with the rest of the record and appear to be Teague's own errors/imprecision — average ~1.6 million lb/year per Effie).
- At the recorded 3.61%: patrons were credited with 1,444,000 lb of fat. At the true 3.80%: they had actually delivered 1,520,000 lb. Difference = **76,000 lb of butterfat**, worth **$19,000** at the period's average price of $0.25/lb for butterfat.
- The error, being proportional (a fixed 95% ratio, not a fixed point deduction), hit richer milk hardest: a herd recorded at 4.56 was short 0.24 points; one recorded at 3.42 was short 0.18 points. (Note: Selby Vose's own 1910 Jersey herd tested exactly 4.56 — possibly the same herd used as r04's illustrative example, though not stated explicitly.)
- The 1925 board of arbitration awarded the Ostrey Hollow patrons **$3,800** — about a fifth of the true $19,000 shortfall. CONFLICT: Vira Toland (r01) misremembers/states the settlement as "**thirty-five hundred dollars**" ($3,500); every documentary source (Effie's minutes quote, Teague's memoir, Vose's address, and the arbitration minute book itself per catalog Item 7) gives **$3,800**.
- The board's award language (quoted identically by r02, r05[paraphrased], r08[paraphrased], and catalog Item 7 verbatim):
  > *The board having heard the patrons and the officers, and having had before it the books and appliances of the station, finds no cause established. The board awards to the Ostrey Hollow patrons the sum of thirty-eight hundred dollars ($3,800.00), without admission, to be charged to the station account.*
- 1911 anomaly: the station's own measure/pipette broke 8 May 1911; a borrowed measure (from Larrow Green, via the cream wagon) was used until 10 July 1911; during those nine weeks the recorded average jumped to the "true" 3.80 and dropped back to 3.61 the day the station's own (short) measure returned. Ansel Keddie's own ledger entries (quoted in r03):
  > *May 8. Pipette broke this morning. Using a borrowed measure.*
  > *July 10. Our own measure in use again from today. Tests are back where they were. ? — A.K.*
  Keddie's own question mark stood unanswered for over 30 years. Strawn's circuit-book entry for June 1911 (mid-anomaly) merely says "Watched a test through. Nothing here to note" — he did not flag or apparently even notice the swapped measure.
- Post-fix confirmation: 1924 station average (with new, accurate pipettes in use) = **3.80** per Keddie's own book (r03) and Strawn's 1924 circuit entry ("tests improved... Their book gives the year at 3.80" — r06) — matching Ivy's calculated "true" historical average exactly. CONFLICT: Selby Vose's 1926 address instead claims the 1924 average was "**3.90**," a rise of "twenty-one hundredths" (0.21) from 3.61 — both figures (3.90, and a 0.21 rise) are internally inconsistent with each other (3.61+0.21=3.82, not 3.90) and disagree with every other source's 3.80.
- Larrow Green's own creamery books (catalog Item 16, water-damaged), covering the same years the station recorded 3.61, run "a little under two tenths of a point better" — i.e. roughly consistent with (corroborates) the ~3.80 true value via a second, independent institution.
- Alder Corners Cheese Company (Vessey County) test figures for the same Hessel Bottom farmers' milk, in their summer flush, ran about 0.19 points higher than at Ostrey Hollow, across 14 overlapping seasons (r04) — a third independent corroboration; catalog Item 13 specifies the copied data was for the 1919 season specifically.

## The competing theories/explanations, in chronological order of when each was raised
1. **1902** — first complaints down at Larrow Green that Ostrey Hollow milk didn't test with the rest (r03).
2. **1908** — investigating committee sampled Hessel Bottom herds through January–March 1908 only; concluded "the milk is whole and the scales are honest" but blamed wet hay for thinning the cream, remedy "lies with the land and not with the station" (catalog Item 11, quoted verbatim; matches r03's paraphrase).
3. **1911** (State Agricultural College Bulletin No. 214, published 1911, tested the wet-hay theory): "Bog hay fed as the whole of the roughage depressed the butter fat test by not more than five hundredths of one per cent in winter, and by nothing that could be measured on summer pasture." Ivy's 1958 analysis: this effect is four times too small (need 0.19, bulletin gives max 0.05) and "the wrong shape" (station's gap was flat year-round, not winter-only).
4. **1916** — Wilbur Teague privately raised his suspicions about the station's single-man vulnerability to Selby Vose; told to let it alone; did not pursue it further ("I was the Association's bookkeeper and not its conscience").
5. **1919** — Selby Vose's printed circular to nine Ostrey Hollow families alleging "water in the cans," over the manager's signature (r01). Strawn's own 1919 circuit-book entry states he tested for added water at every visit to that station and never found any, and told the manager (Vose) so directly (r06) — directly refuting the circular's premise at the time it was issued.
6. **1919 reaction** — the letter devastated the nine families; kept for decades (one in a desk drawer, another pinned inside a trunk lid); caused the permanent breach between Orra Keddie and her brother Emmet Brant.
7. **1925 (Jan–May)** — formal petition (January 1925) and board of arbitration (sat at Ashlin, 17 March–4 May 1925, under Alonzo Frick). Teague testified suspecting Keddie of skimming cream (one-man control of the whole process). Ansel Keddie's own 17 March 1925 statement to the board (quoted in full in r03) flatly denies ever taking cream. The board found "no cause established" yet awarded $3,800. Keddie was dismissed 30 April 1925.
8. **Popular/oral version (per Vira Toland, r01, recorded 1979)** — the folk memory settled on "he was taking the cream; they caught him at it," crediting the corrected 1924 figures to Keddie's removal — this is the version most people in the valley believed and is the one r01 repeats as fact.
9. **1926 — Selby Vose's own theory (r08)**: patrons were adding water to the cans, and stopped once the Association "began to look" (scrutiny effect); dismisses the pipette-condemnation as ordinary equipment wear ("Apparatus wears... I decline to build a mystery on a piece of housekeeping"). He explicitly defends his own 1919 circular as necessary and fair. Does not mention Keddie's dismissal at all.
10. **1958 — Ivy Keddie's forensic proof (r04)**: using five independent record sets (station test book; Brant farm weigh book; Alder Corners figures; Strawn's circuit notebooks; Association ledgers) plus her own calibration of the surviving 9 pipettes, demonstrates the entire 26-year gap (1897–1923) was caused by systematically short pipettes (95% delivery), not theft, not watering, not wet hay. Published as State Agricultural College Bulletin No. 471 (October 1958), closing paragraph quoted verbatim by both r04 and catalog Item 15:
    > *No milk was watered at Ostrey Hollow and no cream was stolen there. From the first of May, 1897, to the twelfth of June, 1923, the station's tests were low by one part in twenty because its pipettes were short by one part in twenty, and the difference — seventy-six thousand pounds of butter fat — stands credited in the Association's own ledgers, year by year, as station gain.*
    Timeline given by Ivy: glass condemned 1923, cause found 1958 (35 years); grandfather dismissed 1925 (33 years before exoneration); he died 1934, had been dead 24 years when the exonerating bulletin was issued.

## The "station-gain" ledger mechanism (Wilbur Teague's device, r05; also catalog Item 6)
- Teague, as bookkeeper, tracked two figures for the station account: (a) butterfat credited to patrons on the station's own (short) test, and (b) butterfat actually received/weighed at Larrow Green in the station's cream. The second was always larger; the difference was entered as "station gain" and credited to the Association's general account.
- Example ledger entry (quoted identically, word-for-word, by r05 and catalog Item 6 as "typical"):
  > *Ostrey Hollow: butter fat credited to patrons on station test, 57,760 lb. Butter fat received at Larrow Green in station cream, 60,800 lb. Station gain, 3,040 lb, five and a quarter per cent, as usual. Carried to the general account.*
  (3,040/57,760 = 5.26%, matching Ivy's later-derived 5.25%-of-credited figure exactly — the ledger mechanism and Ivy's 1958 forensic math independently agree.)
- Teague says he "began" this ledger line himself in 1905 — but then presents "the entry for 1898" (seven years before he started) as an example "in the same terms and in the same hand for as long as the hand was mine," which is an internal inconsistency within r05 (he couldn't have written an 1898 entry if he started in 1905; possibly he means the *practice*, inherited or reconstructed, extended back before his tenure, but the text as given is self-contradictory).
- Selby Vose signed the annual station-account sheet every year (per Teague); Teague raised his suspicions to Vose in 1916 and was told to drop it.
- Total accumulated "station gain" across 1898–1922: 76,000 lb butterfat, $19,000 — same figures as Ivy's independent 1958 calculation (confirms the ledger mechanism and forensic math describe the same underlying shortfall).

## Summary of all identified conflicts/oddities between and within sources
1. Selby Vose's relationship to Gideon Vose: "nephew" (r01, Vira Toland) vs. "son" (r02 Effie; confirmed directly by Selby himself in r08).
2. Settlement/award amount: "$3,500" (r01, Vira's memory) vs. "$3,800" (r02, r05, r08, and the arbitration minute book itself, catalog Item 7) — the documentary record is unanimous at $3,800; only the oral/folk account gives $3,500.
3. Total station milk intake 1898–1922: "40,000,000 lb" (r02 Effie; catalog Item 6) vs. "44,000,000 lb" (r05 Teague) — plus Teague's stated overall average test of "3.16," which is far off from the recorded 3.61 given everywhere else (his own memoir's numbers appear unreliable/inconsistent with the documentary record).
4. Cause of the low test: (a) folk belief — Keddie stole/skimmed cream (r01); (b) Teague's institutional suspicion — same theory, based on one-man opportunity, given formally to the 1925 board (r05); (c) Selby Vose's official theory — patrons were adding water to cans, and stopped once watched (r08); (d) the 1908 committee's theory — wet hay thinned the cream (catalog Item 11); (e) the eventually-proven truth — systematically short/miscalibrated pipettes, no wrongdoing by anyone (r04, r06, catalog Items 5/9/14/15). Ansel Keddie himself (r03) denies theft and can offer no explanation, flagging the 1911 measure-swap anomaly as the only concrete clue he had.
5. Date the pipettes were condemned: "12 June 1923" (Strawn's own notebook, r06; catalog Items 5 and 9) vs. "the twelfth of July, 1923" (Selby Vose's 1926 address, r08) — one month apart.
6. 1924 station test average: "3.80" (Ansel Keddie's own book, r03; Strawn's 1924 circuit entry, r06; matches Ivy's independently calculated "true" value) vs. "3.90" with a stated "twenty-one hundredths" rise from 3.61 (Selby Vose, r08) — Vose's own numbers are internally inconsistent (3.61+0.21=3.82≠3.90) as well as inconsistent with every other source.
7. Where the crate was found in February 1958: the old Ostrey Hollow station building's loft, under a tarpaulin (r04, Ivy's own first-person account) vs. "the Association office at Ashlin" (catalog Item 5, Nym's 1972 accession note) — same finder, same month/year, two different locations given.
8. Catalog Items 3 and 4 attribution swap: Item 3 (the station test book, clearly Ansel Keddie's, signed "A. Keddie") is headed "kept by E. Brant"; Item 4 (the farm weigh book, clearly a farmer's own delivery record, matching r04's description of "the Brant weigh book") is headed "kept by A. Keddie." The two attributions appear to be reversed.
9. Wilbur Teague's internal inconsistency: claims he personally invented/began the "station-gain" ledger line in 1905, but then presents "the entry for 1898" (predating his tenure) as an example of that same line "in the same terms and in the same hand for as long as the hand was mine."
10. Timing looseness in r01 (Vira Toland's oral account): she frames the Ashlin hearing as occurring "the winter and spring after" the 1919 circular, but all documentary sources place the petition/hearing in 1925 — six years later, not the following season. This is consistent with an elderly oral narrator compressing/telescoping a long timeline in memory.
11. Strawn's own June 1911 circuit-book entry — made during the exact nine-week window when the borrowed (accurate) measure was in use and the true 3.80 figure was showing — records nothing unusual ("Watched a test through. Nothing here to note"), a missed opportunity/blind spot that, with hindsight, was the one moment the truth was directly visible and unremarked.
12. The 1919 circular's tone/content is remembered differently by its recipients versus its author: Vira Toland (r01) recalls it as a direct accusation ("there was water in the cans coming down from Ostrey Hollow," naming the Hollow specifically) while Selby Vose (r08) insists it "named nobody" and "accused nobody," calling it a general, temperate warning.
13. Possible minor tension in catalog Item 13's provenance: Rosalie Cudd (Jerome Cudd's niece) is said to run the Alder Corners factory and to have personally copied its 1919 test figures for Ivy in 1958, yet the test book itself is said to have passed from its founder to his daughter in 1941 (i.e., held by the daughter's line, not explicitly Rosalie's, at the time of copying) — not a hard contradiction but an unresolved gap in who actually had custody of the book in 1958.
