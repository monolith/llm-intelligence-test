## What it got wrong

**The four abstention items it answered anyway (−8, and 4 points lost).** This is where the whole
loss sits. The sheet declines A01, A02, A06, A08 and A10 cleanly, then answers four of the
remaining five with a flat verdict:

- **A09** (D2): "Did the bookkeeper ever question the entries that refute him? **No.**"
- **A03** (D3): "What he did not know, or would not let himself learn, was that the low tests were
  the station's own error … the sources support incuriosity, not concealment."
- **A07** (F5): "Did the board examine the condemned glassware? **No.**"
- **A05** (F6): "On balance: he suspected the glass from 1911."

Each is a headline verdict sentence with no declining qualifier attached, so later hedging does not
rescue it. F6 is the sharpest case: the sheet says "not settled" at E44 and then settles it in
Section F, where the key scores the item.

**One corruption absorbed (−1).** D2 states as fact that "Teague invented the line (1905)". The
station-gain line was carried from 1898; 1905 is a planted error (X19). The sheet actually gets
this right twice elsewhere — Story 6 calls it "unresolved", E40 "cannot be settled" — which makes
the D2 sentence a slip rather than a swallowed error, but it is asserted flatly.

  for "I have never taken a pound of cream", not for "I cannot tell you why the figures are what
  they are". That quote, too, sits in Section F.
