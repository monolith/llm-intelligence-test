## 0. The whole affair in one paragraph (synthesis across r01–r08)

The Larrow Valley Cooperative Creamery Association (organized 1889 at Larrow Green; creamery built 1891 on Larrow Creek) ran a skimming station at Ostrey Hollow, seven miles up the valley at the head of the Hollow, taking milk from the farms of Hessel Bottom (the wet meadow below the hamlet) and three places on the hill. Ansel Keddie tested there alone from 4 May 1896 through the 1924 season (29 seasons). For the first season he used glass borrowed from Larrow Green (or Ashlin — sources disagree) and the station average was 3.80; from 1 May 1897 the station used its own outfit bought from Ammon & Sons, Tarnet (invoice 14 April 1897, $77.93, six dozen pipettes blown at 17.6 c.c.), and from 1898 the average sat at 3.61 (within three hundredths) every year — except nine weeks in 1911 (8 May–10 July) when the station's pipette broke, a borrowed measure was used, and the average jumped to 3.80 then fell back the day the station's own measure returned. Complaints that Ostrey Hollow milk "did not test with the rest" began 1902; a 1908 committee blamed the wet hay of the Bottom; in 1919 manager Selby Vose sent a printed circular to nine families saying there was water in their cans. The Association's ledgers carried every year a "station gain" line: fat received at Larrow Green in the station's cream exceeded fat credited on the station's test by 5¼% of credited (= 5% of delivered), "as usual" (typical entry 57,760 lb credited / 60,800 lb received / gain 3,040 lb). State dairy inspector Merle Strawn ran the station's pipettes against a burette on 12 June 1923, found they "will not deliver their mark," and condemned "the whole crate of them"; new glass was ordered 28 June and in use from July 1923; the 1924 average rose to 3.80. The patrons petitioned in January 1925; a board of arbitration under attorney Alonzo Frick sat at Ashlin 17 March–4 May 1925, found "no cause established," and awarded the patrons $3,800 "without admission," charged to the station account; Keddie was put out on 30 April 1925 and died in 1934. In February 1958 his granddaughter Ivy Keddie (State Agricultural College, Farland) found the crate of condemned pipettes; on 9 March 1958 she measured the nine unbroken ones at a mean delivery of 16.72 c.c. against the 17.6 mark — 95 hundredths — proving every test read 5% low; over the 25 full delivery years 1898–1922 (40,000,000 lb milk) that was 76,000 lb of butterfat worth $19,000 at the 25¢/lb average, five times the award. Bulletin No. 471 (October 1958, Professor Vaught) cleared Keddie: "No milk was watered at Ostrey Hollow and no cream was stolen there." Ivy read her talk "What the Crate Held" to the Ordell County Historical Society at Ashlin in 1959; curator Halbert Nym catalogued the collection in 1972.

## 4. Key quantities (with arithmetic checks)

- Station opened 4 May 1896; Keddie 29 seasons (1896–1924); "thirty-one times a week for twenty-nine seasons" (Keddie); "a thousand cans a week" (Vira).
- Pipettes: blown 17.6 c.c.; nine survivors deliver 16.70, 16.71, 16.72, 16.72, 16.72, 16.73, 16.73, 16.74, 16.71 (mean of five runs each); mean 16.72 (sum 150.48 ÷ 9 = 16.72 ✓); short 0.88 c.c. (17.6 − 16.72 ✓); 16.72 ÷ 17.6 = 0.95 exactly ✓.
- Invoice 14 April 1897: 6 doz. pipettes @ .35 = 25.20 (72 × .35 ✓); 12 doz. test bottles, 8 per cent neck, @ .22 = 31.68 (144 × .22 ✓); acid measure .90; hand tester, 4 bottle, 18.00; freight 2.15; total 77.93 ✓.
- Averages: 1896 3.80; 1897 part year; 1898 3.61; 3.61 ± 0.03 every year after save nine weeks of 1911 at 3.80; 1924 3.80. Gap 0.19 = 5% of 3.80 (3.61 ÷ 0.95 = 3.80 ✓). Teague's whole-period average 3.16 (conflict). Vose's 1924: 3.90, up "twenty-one hundredths" (conflict; 3.61 + 0.21 = 3.82, not 3.90).
- Proportional shortfall: herd recorded 4.56 short 0.24 (4.56 ÷ 0.95 = 4.80 ✓); herd recorded 3.42 short 0.18 (3.42 ÷ 0.95 = 3.60 ✓).
- Milk 1898–1922 (25 full delivery years): 40,000,000 lb; 1,600,000 lb/yr (Effie, Ivy, Nym). Teague: 44,000,000 lb (conflict).

- 1919 circular: nine families. Hessel Bottom: seven farms with rights (r02) + three hill places (r06).

## 6. Documents quoted (verbatim)

**Article VII** (r02 and r07 Item 1, identical):
> Article VII. Every patron shall be paid for the milk he delivers according to the number of pounds of butter fat contained in it, ascertained by test, and not according to the weight of the milk; and the test of the station where he delivers shall govern.

**Award of the board of arbitration, 4 May 1925** (r02, r07 Item 7, identical):
> The board having heard the patrons and the officers, and having had before it the books and appliances of the station, finds no cause established. The board awards to the Ostrey Hollow patrons the sum of thirty-eight hundred dollars ($3,800.00), without admission, to be charged to the station account.

**Keddie's statement to the board, 17 March 1925** (r03, nine sentences):
> I have kept the test book of this station in my own hand since the station opened, and there is not a figure in it that I did not put there. I have set down the date, the patron, the pounds, the test and the fat, and where I have made an error I have drawn a line through it and put my letters against the line, and I have never written a figure over a figure. I have made the tests in the same order and at the same hour of every working day of every season. I have heard what the patrons say of me and I have heard what the officers say of me. Neither of them has come up that road on a working morning and stood at my elbow and watched the work done. I am asked to account for figures, and the figures are what I read and what I wrote and nothing besides. I am an old man and I have no other trade. I have tested as I was taught and as the state man saw me do it, thirty-one times a week for twenty-nine seasons, and I cannot tell you why the figures are what they are. I have never taken a pound of cream out of that station and there is not a man in this room who thinks I have when he is by himself.

**First leaf of the test book** (r03, r07 Item 3):
> Ostrey Hollow station. Begun this 4th day of May, 1896. Glass borrowed of Larrow Green until our own comes. A. Keddie.

**Averages leaf, made up 1922** (r03, r04, r07):
> Average test of all patrons, this station: 1896, 3.80. 1897, part year. 1898, 3.61. And 3.61 or within three hundredths of it every year since, save the nine weeks of 1911 that are noted in their place, when it stood at 3.80.

**Test book entries, 1911** (r03):
> May 8. Pipette broke this morning. Using a borrowed measure.

> July 10. Our own measure in use again from today. Tests are back where they were. ? — A.K.

**Ivy Keddie's measurement sheet, 9 March 1958** (r04, r07 Item 14):
> Nine pipettes from the Ostrey Hollow crate, stems blown 17.6 c.c. Delivered against burette, mean of five runs each: 16.70, 16.71, 16.72, 16.72, 16.72, 16.73, 16.73, 16.74, 16.71. Mean 16.72 c.c. Short 0.88 c.c. That is 95 hundredths of the mark, and the mark is what the neck of the bottle was ruled for.

**State Agricultural College Bulletin No. 214 (1911)** (r04, r07 Item 12):
> Bog hay fed as the whole of the roughage depressed the butter fat test by not more than five hundredths of one per cent in winter, and by nothing that could be measured on summer pasture.

**Bulletin No. 471 (October 1958), closing paragraph** (r04, r07 Item 15):
> No milk was watered at Ostrey Hollow and no cream was stolen there. From the first of May, 1897, to the twelfth of June, 1923, the station's tests were low by one part in twenty because its pipettes were short by one part in twenty, and the difference — seventy-six thousand pounds of butter fat — stands credited in the Association's own ledgers, year by year, as station gain.

**Ledger station-account entry** (r05 "the entry for 1898"; r07 Item 6 "typical"):
> Ostrey Hollow: butter fat credited to patrons on station test, 57,760 lb. Butter fat received at Larrow Green in station cream, 60,800 lb. Station gain, 3,040 lb, five and a quarter per cent, as usual. Carried to the general account.

**Strawn's circuit book, 12 June 1923** (r06, r07 Item 9):
> Ostrey Hollow. Scales true. Lactometer, no added water, as always here. Ran their milk pipettes against the burette. These measures will not deliver their mark. Condemned the lot, the whole crate of them, and told the manager to order new. Glass is glass and I did not weigh what it has cost them.

**Ammon & Sons invoice, 14 April 1897** (r07 Item 2):
> Sold to the Larrow Valley Co-op. Creamery Assn., for the Ostrey Hollow station:
> 6 doz. milk test pipettes, 17.6 c.c., at .35 — 25.20
> 12 doz. test bottles, 8 per cent neck, at .22 — 31.68
> 1 acid measure — .90
> 1 hand tester, 4 bottle — 18.00
> Freight — 2.15
> Total — 77.93

**Brant weigh book, leaf for 1910** (r07 Item 4):
> Year 1910. Delivered to the station, two hundred thousand pounds even, by my own scale and theirs agreeing. Paid on a test of 3.42.

**Report of the investigating committee, 1908** (r07 Item 11):
> We have sampled the herds of Hessel Bottom through January, February and March. The milk is whole and the scales are honest. It is our opinion that the wet hay of that bottom is what thins the cream, and that the remedy lies with the land and not with the station.

**Documents mentioned but not transcribed:** the 1919 circular (Vira: "it said there was water in the cans coming down from Ostrey Hollow"; Vose: it "warned ... that added water would be looked for and would be dealt with ... this Association pays for butterfat and cannot pay for creek water"; "It named nobody. It accused nobody."); Ammon & Sons letter of 2 July 1923 (acknowledges order of 28 June for replacement pipettes and bottles, promises shipment that month, declines to say anything of the earlier lot); Keddie's statement leaf (Nym Item 8, "one leaf, ruled, both sides, in the hand of Item 3, signed"); Alder Corners figures for 1919 (Nym Item 13); the minutes of the three sessions ("in the safe").

## 7. Per-retelling detail

### r01 — Vira Toland (b. 1912), Hessel Bottom patron's daughter, taped at Ostrey Hollow, 1979
- Tape at her kitchen table; interviewer asked three questions in an hour; transcribed as spoken with doublings-back.
- Born 1912 at the top end of the Hollow in her grandfather's house; two years at Ashlin when first married, "would not count those two years."
- Nine families hauled to the station; "in and out of each other's kitchens."
- Station description (see Places). Keddie went up in '95, the year before they opened it, "and he tested that first summer too."
- The book: everybody knew about it; ruled with his own straightedge; every line the same height; figures like print; wrote standing at a slope desk by the window; could turn to any day and read it off; "tested a thousand cans a week and never a blot"; father's joke about lawyers in Ashlin ("There are still lawyers in Ashlin").
- 1919: "the year everything went sour"; she was seven; a printed letter over the manager's signature: water in the cans coming down from Ostrey Hollow; went to nine families; father read it twice with his hat still on; mother stood at the stove with her back turned; letter into the desk drawer in the front room, still there when he died, brother has it yet; one woman had hers pinned inside her trunk lid; "keep the paper forty years to prove what was said of them."
- Signer: Selby Vose, down at Larrow Green, old Gideon Vose's nephew; Gideon started the Association; "the family always thought the valley belonged to them a little."
- Orra Keddie and old Mrs. Vose first cousins — "it was somebody's cousin saying it."
- Orra = Ansel's wife = Emmet Brant's sister; Emmet biggest man on the Bottom; "the man that tested the milk had married the sister of the man that got the worst of it"; Sunday dinners together before 1919, two houses in one kitchen, men at the fence after; after 1919 not one word, not at the store, not at a funeral; Orra walked to Larrow Green rather than pass her brother's lane; lived within sight of each other's chimney smoke.
- Then the tests went up, all at once one summer, and stayed up; nobody said why; father had done nothing different — "the queerest part" to her.
- Hearing at Ashlin "the winter and spring after," in an office, lawyers, Association officers, the men going down in wagons, "went on and on"; at the end they put Keddie out; paid patrons $3,500 "and called it settled"; split nine ways; father's share "did not buy a hay rake."
- Her verdict: "He was taking the cream. They caught him at it and they put him out, and that is why the numbers went right the summer after"; man alone with the separator running, door shut, "nobody in the valley can add"; "nobody ever came up that road and looked"; not wicked. Father never said it aloud: "an honest man who had got into something."
- Keddie died in the thirties "still under it"; valley looked at him a certain way in church; Orra outlived him and had it the whole time.
- 1958: young woman from the college away east; poked about the shut station (fence wire and salt); sat where the interviewer sits; careful, wrote everything down; Hazel Brant's first cousin once removed, "of the family both sides"; Vira told her about the letter; "here it comes round again ... nobody left alive that it can do any good to."
- It rained the day of the letter; wet on father's shoulders; sixty years she can't think of one without the other.

### r02 — Effie Loomis, secretary 1901–1940, "Notes toward a history of the Association," 1941
- Numbered sections for the Association's files, written the year after she laid down the minute book; minutes vs recollection kept apart.
- §1 organizing: 1889 at Larrow Green; Gideon Vose president 1889–1911; she was 14 in 1889, secretary from 1901; 1889–1892 minute book lost in the 1938 office move — "governs everything I can and cannot write."
- §2 creamery: Larrow Green, 1891, on Larrow Creek; before it, no common test or price, "every man's arithmetic was his own"; "a plain remedy for a plain grievance."
- §3 by-laws: Gideon drafted them, not a lawyer; Article VII adopted at the 1892 annual meeting; "misquoted at me across this desk for forty years"; the unquoted last clause — the station's test governs, no appeal — "is the hinge of the whole Ostrey Hollow business," written by a man who died in 1917.
- §4 officers: herself; Teague bookkeeper from 1905; Hobart Sill buttermaker with churn book and cream receiving book; Selby Vose, son of the late president, manager 1913–1938; none of the four ever stood in the station on a working morning.
- §5 station: seven miles up; separated milk, cream down, skim back; Hessel Bottom wet meadow, seven farms with rights, Emmet Brant largest patron every recorded year.
- §6 petition January 1925: milk "had never tested as their neighbors' milk tested," asked to be made whole; board under Alonzo Frick, attorney, at Ashlin 17 March–4 May 1925; three explanations argued, none accepted, "in the minutes and the minutes are in the safe"; award recorded by her on the last day; "how both halves of a sentence could be true at once."
- §7 quantities: 25 years, 40 million lb, 1.6 million/yr, 25¢/lb; Teague's figures from the annual sheets; she added the columns twice; "I do not vouch for the cows, only for the copying."
- §8 record stops: (1) lost minute book — carried out, never arrived; anyone claiming to know the first four annual meetings "is telling you a story he had from his father"; (2) the crate — never mentioned in any of the three sessions by patrons, officers, or board; crate of measures condemned by the state's inspector June 1923, set aside, new ones ordered; "books and appliances" was a form of words, not a list; "the interesting part is generally what none of them thought worth saying."

### r03 — Ansel Keddie: statement 17 March 1925 and unsent letter, spring 1925
- Statement: nine sentences, standing, taken down as given (quoted in §6 above).
- Letter (never sent, found among his papers): "a man is allowed to answer and not allowed to explain."
- Routine: day began half past five; wagons up in the dark most of the year; intake room open, lamps lit, scale swinging free; every can weighed and the weight called and written before the milk went anywhere; scale tested by the state's man, true; never a patron disputed a weight and was right.
- Sampling: from each patron's milk as weighed into his own numbered jar; composite over ten days; testing morning: jar in warm water until blood-warm through, poured vessel to vessel until the risen cream was carried through; "a boy could tell you so."
- Charge: milk drawn up in the measure to the mark on the stem, tip wiped, charge blown into the patron-numbered bottle; acid down the side, turned until dark; four bottles in the hand tester weighted against each other, cover down, crank at the taught speed, "no man ever stood beside me and made me hurry it"; fat in the neck read in per cent against the rulings with dividers. "It is not a hard trade. It is only a trade that must be done the same way every time."
- Glass: "The glass I began on in ninety-six was borrowed off the Ashlin creamery, and we used it until our own outfit came up the valley in a crate the spring following." [vs first leaf "Larrow Green"]
- Book: five columns; crossed errors initialed; never wrote over a figure ("a figure no man can swear to afterward"); 29 seasons 1896–1924; "I did not test a pound in the season of 1925."
- 1911: measure broke 8 May, borrowed one, tests up to 3.80; 10 July own measure again, tests down the same day; the "?" stood fourteen years; "I will not put anything in its place that I do not know."
- The gap did not come and go with season — same in July and February, flush and dry hay, wet and dry years. 1924 average 3.80 "with the same pen and the same measure of care."
- History: complaints 1902; 1908 committee sampled herds through the winter, blamed wet hay, "remedy lay with the land"; 1919 circular to nine families; "I carry no charge against any man who put his name to that paper"; nine kitchens keep the letter.
- Orra is Emmet Brant's sister; Emmet brought the most milk; every pound weighed and tested by Keddie himself; not spoken since the circular — "the plainest fact in this letter and it is the one I find I have the least to say about."
- Put out 30 April; "found no cause established and they put me out on the last day of the month, and both of those things are true at once."

### r04 — Ivy Keddie, "What the Crate Held," Ordell County Historical Society, Ashlin, 1959
- Read from typescript; crate on a table; borrowed books open beside it; papers read from originals.
- Ansel her grandfather; father Duncan Keddie, schoolteacher at Ashlin; "nothing I am going to show you tonight depends in the smallest degree on my opinion of my grandfather. It depends on nine pieces of glass and a burette."
- Five record sets never before in one room: station test book (Association office since 1925, to the Rooms 1959); Brant weigh book from 1897 (lent by Roy Brant); Alder Corners Cheese Company figures (Redlow Ridge, Vessey County; copied by Rosalie Cudd, Jerome Cudd's niece; book never left Vessey County, never seen by Ivy); Merle Strawn's circuit notebooks ("state's dairy inspector for this county from 1908"; in her college's library at Farland since 1947 — eleven years unquestioned); the Association's annual ledgers.
- The crate: February 1958, station building (storage since it closed); loft over the intake room by ladder; under a tarpaulin at the back; stenciled AMMON & SONS DAIRY SUPPLY TARNET; carried up June 1923; condemned pipettes in straw; six dozen bought 1897 on a $77.93 invoice for the whole outfit; nine unbroken.
- 9 March: measured at Farland against a calibrated burette (sheet quoted).
- Arithmetic: short charge → short reading in proportion; 16.72/17.6 = 0.95 exactly; every test 95% of truth; 3.61/0.95 = 3.80; gap 0.19 is not fixed, it is 5% — "It lost a twentieth of whatever was there."
- 25 full delivery years 1898–1922: 40 million lb; 1,444,000 credited vs 1,520,000 true; 76,000 lb; $19,000; award $3,800 "a fifth of it."
- Two percentages: 5% of delivered, 5¼% of credited; "the Association's ledger line uses the second"; "five percent is the one that is also the glass."
- Fell hardest on best milk: 4.56 short 0.24; 3.42 short 0.18; "his good cows cost him more than his poor ones."
- 1911 "experiment nobody knew was one": own measure broke 8 May, substitute to 10 July; borrowed measure came up from Larrow Green with the cream wagon; everything else the same; average 3.80 then back to 3.61 the day the station's own measure returned; "twenty-six-year anomaly."
- Wet hay: Bulletin No. 214 (1911) — four times too small and the wrong shape; Alder Corners: four Hessel Bottom men in summer flush, fourteen overlapping seasons, ~0.19 higher over the ridge than at home.
- Bulletin No. 471 issued "last October," last paragraph Vaught's wording and hers.
- 35 / 33 / 24 years; "I have no remedy to propose. I have only the crate."

### r05 — Wilbur Teague, "The Books of the Association," Chapter Nine, 1948
- Bookkeeper 1905–1945; "never carried a column that would not prove"; written at Ashlin, winter 1948, three years after leaving the desk.
- Office at Ashlin: one room over a harness shop; ledgers on a taller predecessor's shelf; sloped desk with brass rail; station sheets in March, stiffer paper than the creamery's.
- Association = creamery at Larrow Green + office at Ashlin + station at Ostrey Hollow; Selby Vose manager, Effie Loomis secretary, Teague ledger; "the station was the smallest of the three and it made the most noise."
- Station-gain column explained: credited on station test vs received at Larrow Green in the station's cream, "the second was always the larger"; difference carried to the general account "since the Association had received it."
- "The station-gain line was my own device; I began it when I came in 1905" — not "some old dodge of the founders"; a difference not entered "will come back and bite you at audit."
- Entry quoted; "That is the entry for 1898, and there is one like it for every year after"; "The gain ran at five and a quarter percent of the fat that was actually in the cream"; "as usual" got in and stayed; evenness "stops him looking"; "A figure that comes in at five and a quarter every year is furniture."
- Big numbers: 44 million lb; average test of all Ostrey Hollow patrons 3.16 "over the whole period"; Larrow Green did better; 25 years of soreness.
- 1911: measure broke in the spring; borrowed for six weeks "before their own came up from town"; entered as a small replacement charge; "as though a bookkeeper kept the weather."
- To the board 1925, repeated: "Cream is portable, and that station was in one pair of hands for twenty-nine seasons"; one man weighed, sampled, tested, wrote the pay-slip figure, ran the separator; no second man spring to fall; "The remedy is two men or a lock, and the Association had neither"; does not say what any person did.
- Petition in the winter; Frick fair; Teague called; board "found nothing established against anybody"; $3,800 without admission charged to station account; "it balanced."
- Vose knew: signed the annual sheet every spring; Teague raised it in 1916, told to let it alone; "I was the Association's bookkeeper and not its conscience."
- Figures in the ledgers at Ashlin, in ink, dated.

### r06 — Merle Strawn, "Extracts from twenty-eight circuit books," with covering note, 1936

- 1919 verbatim sense: manager sent a circular about water in the cans; "I have tested for water at this station on every visit I have made to it and I have never found any, and I told him so."
- 1915: "A field man who lets a floor go will be shown a floor at every plant on his circuit inside of two years."
- 1917: "two testers doing the work of three men, which is where mistakes live."

- 1928: loft over the intake room full; "a loft over a milk room is a place for nothing but air"; told it had been full "since before the war."
- Closing: 28 books, oilcloth, pocket size, to the state dairy office; "I do not keep an estimation, I keep a book"; "A theory is a thing a man carries out to the platform with him and it gets in the way of the weights."

### r08 — Selby Vose, manager's annual address, 1926
- Printed in the annual report; "thirteen years this spring, since 1913"; worst year to report and gladdest to have behind.
- Ordinary business: wet season into June, heavy hauling; Larrow Green creamery no day lost through the flush; butter held its place; churn room clean; ice in early; Ostrey Hollow station ran its season, cream down daily and sweet.
- The petition of January 1925; board under Frick; "found no cause established against the station, against the office, or against any officer or servant"; "the only sentence in this business that was arrived at by men under oath with the books in front of them."
- $3,800 without admission charged to station account; he supported and supports it; "a generous settlement of an unproved claim"; "we are not a business house; we are the farmers of this valley in company together."
- "I will not have peace bought a second time": "Water was going into cans at the head of that valley and it stopped when the Association began to look. The rise of 1924 is the measure of it." Excuses over a generation: wet hay, tester, office, weather. Attention → petition → hearing → "in the very next season the tests at that station rose by twenty-one hundredths and the average for 1924 stood at 3.90." Nothing done to cows, land, feed, pasture.
- The state man: "every year or two"; scales true; "condemned the station's testing apparatus on the twelfth of July, 1923, and told us to order new. We ordered new inside the month and had it in use before the flush was over." "Apparatus wears"; station in daily use since 1896; "I decline to build a mystery on a piece of housekeeping that appears in our own order book with a date and a price against it."
- Names no one; "A little water in a can is not a felony in a man's own mind"; nobody prosecuted or will be.
- Defends the 1919 circular: duty; temperate warning that added water would be looked for; named nobody; "pays for butterfat and cannot pay for creek water"; "would write every word of it again tomorrow"; silence "is being idle at their expense."
- Father = first president, wrote the by-law; Jerseys at the home place; Selby kept them after him; 1910 herd tested 4.56; "no better milk in the state" when it "comes to the platform as it left the cow."
- Close: "Bring it as it left the cow. Wash the cans, cool the milk, keep the dog out of the milk house"; tests will stay where they went in 1924; "I thank you for thirteen years of your patience and I ask for one more."

## 8. Conflicts and oddities — between and within sources

1. **Award amount**: $3,500 split nine ways (r01) vs $3,800.00 (r02 award text, r04, r05, r07, r08).
2. **Selby's relation to Gideon**: nephew (r01) vs son (r02 "son of the late president"; r08 "My father, who was your first president").
3. **Total milk**: 40,000,000 lb over 25 years (r02, r04, r07) vs 44,000,000 lb (r05).
4. **Station average**: 3.61 (r03, r04, r06, r07) vs 3.16 "over the whole period" (r05) — looks like a digit transposition.
5. **1924 average / size of rise**: 3.80, up 0.19 (r03, r04, r06 "Their book gives the year at 3.80") vs 3.90, up "twenty-one hundredths" (r08) — and 3.61 + 0.21 = 3.82, so r08 is internally inconsistent too.
6. **Condemnation date**: 12 June 1923 (r06 entry, r07 Items 5 & 9, r04 Bulletin 471 "twelfth of June") vs "the twelfth of July, 1923" (r08). The replacement order was 28 June and the supplier's letter 2 July (r07 Item 10), new glass in use July (r06) — impossible under Vose's date; consistent with June.
7. **1911 borrowed-measure episode**: nine weeks, 8 May–10 July (r03 entries, averages leaf, r04) vs "six weeks" (r05); station's own measure "in use again" (r03) / "returned" (r04) vs "before their own came up from town" i.e. a replacement (r05); r05 books it as "a small charge for replacement." r04 says the borrowed measure came up from Larrow Green with the cream wagon; r03 does not say where it came from.
8. **Source of the 1896 borrowed glass**: "borrowed off the Ashlin creamery" (r03 letter) vs "Glass borrowed of Larrow Green" (r03 first leaf, r07 Item 3, r07 Item 2 office file "Larrow Green having lent until then") — Keddie contradicts his own first leaf.
9. **Where the crate was found**: the station loft over the intake room, under a tarpaulin, February 1958 (r04; consistent with r06 1928 "loft ... full of odds and ends" and Effie's "set aside") vs "found in the Association office at Ashlin, in February 1958" (r07 Item 5).
10. **Strawn's start year**: 1907 (r06 covering note and first entry) vs "from 1908" (r04).
11. **Alder Corners overlap**: "fourteen seasons" of four Hessel Bottom men (r04) vs figures copied for "the season of 1919" only (r07 Item 13). Also Rosalie Cudd "runs that factory and is Jerome Cudd's niece" (r04) vs book passed "from its founder to his daughter in 1941" (r07).
12. **Origin of the station-gain line**: Teague says it was "my own device; I began it when I came in 1905," yet he quotes "the entry for 1898" in that form, and Nym says the station column runs from 1897 with each year "in one form."
13. **Denominator of "five and a quarter per cent"**: Teague says the gain ran at 5¼% "of the fat that was actually in the cream" (received) — but 3,040/60,800 = 5.0%; 5¼% is of the credited 57,760 (Ivy, Nym both say so).
14. **Test-book columns**: five — date, patron, pounds, test, fat (r03 statement and letter) vs four — date, patron, pounds and test (r07 Item 3).
15. **Nym's swapped labels**: Item 3 "Station test book, kept by E. Brant" (but first leaf signed A. Keddie, one hand 29 seasons) and Item 4 "Farm weigh book, kept by A. Keddie" (but begun at Hessel Bottom, one household, to Roy Brant) — the two keepers are reversed.
16. **Dismissal vs award dates**: Keddie put out 30 April 1925 (r03) while the board sat until 4 May and the award was recorded on the last day (r02, r07) — dismissed before the finding of "no cause established."
17. **Water in the cans**: Vira and Keddie read the circular as saying there WAS water; Vose (r08) says it only warned water would be looked for and "named nobody" — though it went to nine specific families; Strawn (r06) never found added water on any visit 1912–1934 and told the manager so in 1919; Bulletin 471: "No milk was watered."
18. **Vira's internal chronology**: she says the tests went up one summer, then the hearing "the winter and spring after," then Keddie was put out — yet concludes "they put him out, and that is why the numbers went right the summer after." The rise (1924) preceded the dismissal (1925).
19. **Vose's chronology**: he presents petition → hearing → "in the very next season" the rise of 1924; but the petition was January 1925 and the hearing spring 1925 — the rise came before the "looking," not after.
20. **Article VII's date**: Effie says adopted at the 1892 annual meeting but the 1889–1892 minute book is lost; the printed pamphlet carries no year, only "adopted at the annual meeting" (r07 Item 1).
21. **Number of patron families/farms**: nine families (r01, r03, circular) vs seven farms with rights on Hessel Bottom (r02) plus "three places on the hill" (r06) = ten.
22. **The 4.56 herd**: Ivy's example "a herd whose milk was recorded at 4.56 was short 0.24" implies a station herd; the only 4.56 in the sources is Gideon/Selby Vose's Jersey herd "at the home place" in 1910 (r08) — which would deliver to Larrow Green, not the station. Ivy's other example (3.42) matches Brant's 1910 weigh-book leaf (r07 Item 4).
23. **Strawn's books**: "twenty-eight of them, one to the year" over a 1907–1936 circuit (30 calendar years); the extracts list only 20 years (no 1908, 1910, 1914, 1918, 1927, 1929, 1930, 1932, 1933, 1935).
24. **Strawn's June 1911 visit** falls inside the 8 May–10 July borrowed-measure window; he watched a test through and wrote "Nothing here to note" — no remark on the different measure or the higher figures.
25. **Volume of work**: "a thousand cans a week" (r01) vs "thirty-one times a week" (r03 — tests, not cans).
26. **Office location/move**: Teague's office "at Ashlin ... over a harness shop" throughout 1905–1945; Vira places the manager "down at Larrow Green"; Effie says "the office was moved in 1938" without saying from/to; Nym says the test book was "in the Association's office at Ashlin from 1925."
27. **Loft "full since before the war"** (r06, 1928) vs crate carried up June 1923 (after the war) — not contradictory (other odds and ends), but the crate sat there five years by then. Vose (1926) treats the condemned glass as ordinary housekeeping in the order book.
28. **Period of the error**: Bulletin 471 says 1 May 1897 to 12 June 1923 (26 years, Ivy's "twenty-six-year anomaly") but the 76,000 lb / $19,000 figure covers only the 25 "full delivery years" 1898–1922; 1897 (part year) and 1923 (part year) are excluded; 1896 intake exists nowhere.
29. **Keddie "tested that first summer too"** (r01, 1895?) vs book begun 4 May 1896 — Vira may mean 1896; ambiguous.
30. **Effie's "three explanations argued"** are never named in r02; from other sources they were presumably wet hay (1908 committee), the tester skimming cream (Teague's "cream is portable"), and watered cans (Vose) — none was the glass, which "was never mentioned in the hearing at all."
31. **Vose's "every year or two"** for the state man vs Strawn's near-annual visits (twice in 1920).
32. **Ivy's father's name** Duncan Keddie appears only in r04; Vira never names the 1958 visitor, calling her Hazel Brant's first cousin once removed and "of the family both sides."
33. **Old Mrs. Vose = Orra's first cousin** appears only in r01 ("nobody wrote down anywhere").
34. **Teague's 1916 warning to Vose** (r05) vs Vose's 1926 claim that he "sees his figures going one way for twenty years" and wrote the 1919 circular from duty — Vose had been told by his own bookkeeper in 1916 that the station-gain line was anomalous and told him to let it alone; Vose signed the sheet every year.
