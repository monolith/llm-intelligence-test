# Retention notes after retellings r01–r08 (noisy mode, segment 1 of 3)

Status: this is the FIRST notes file for this run. No earlier notes existed to carry forward (segment 1 began at r01). Everything below comes from the eight retellings read in segment 1. Unrelated distractor documents (road commission, playhouse, drugstore, buoy, court column, lacings paper, water-cure ledger, ferry API) are deliberately excluded.

The story: the Ostrey Hollow skimming station of the Larrow Valley Cooperative Creamery Association tested low for ~26 years (1897–1923) because its milk-test pipettes were blown short by one part in twenty. Everyone blamed the wet hay, the tester (Ansel Keddie), or the patrons (watering). The truth came out in 1958 when Ansel's granddaughter Ivy Keddie measured the condemned pipettes.

## 2. People and relationships

**Keddie family**
- **Ansel Keddie** — tester at Ostrey Hollow skimming station. Went up in '95 (1895), the year before it opened, to get it ready (Vira). Kept the test book from 4 May 1896. Tested 29 seasons, 1896 through 1924; "did not test a pound in the season of 1925." Put out 30 April 1925 (his own words: "the last day of the month"). Died 1934 (Ivy); "died in the thirties" (Vira). "I am an old man and I have no other trade." Worked alone; nobody ever watched him except the state man.
- **Orra Keddie** — Ansel's wife. **Emmet Brant's sister.** **First cousin of old Mrs. Vose** (Vira: "everybody up here knew and nobody wrote down anywhere"). After the 1919 letter she would walk down to Larrow Green rather than pass her brother's lane. Outlived Ansel "a good while" and bore the valley's suspicion the whole time.
- **Duncan Keddie** — Ansel's son; taught school at Ashlin; Ivy's father.
- **Ivy Keddie** — Ansel's granddaughter; "Miss Ivy Keddie of the State Agricultural College at Farland" (Nym). Found the crate February 1958; measured pipettes 9 March 1958; gave talk 1959. She is the "young woman from the college away east" of 1958 in Vira's account, described there as "Hazel Brant's first cousin once removed" — consistent, since Ivy's grandmother Orra was Emmet Brant's sister (Ivy is Emmet's grand-niece, so first cousin once removed to Emmet's children).

**Brant family**
- **Emmet Brant** — biggest man on Hessel Bottom: most cows, most hay ground, hauled the most milk; the station's largest patron in every recorded year (Effie); "the one that letter cost the most" (Vira). Orra's brother; Ansel's brother-in-law. Before 1919 the Keddie and Brant households ate Sunday dinners together (Vira saw it: "the two houses in one kitchen and the men out at the fence after"). After the 1919 circular, Emmet and Ansel never spoke again ("not at the store, not at a funeral"). Kept the Hessel Bottom farm weigh book from 1 January 1897. Weigh book passed "to Roy Brant in 1929" (Nym) — implies Emmet died 1929 (Ivy: Roy "has kept it up himself since his father died").
- **Roy Brant** — Emmet's son; kept the weigh book after his father died; lent it to Ivy 1958 (returned).
- **Hazel Brant** — "Miss Hazel Brant"; received the weigh book 1962; gave it to the Rooms 1963. Presumably Emmet's daughter (Vira names her as Ivy's first cousin once removed).

**Vose family**
- **Gideon Vose** — "old Gideon"; started the Association; first president 1889–1911 (22 years, longest ever). Drafted the by-laws himself (not a lawyer; "they read as a farmer wrote them"). Wrote Article VII in 1892. **Died 1917** (Effie: "a man who died in 1917 and could not be asked about it afterward"). Kept a herd of Jerseys at the home place; in 1910 that herd tested 4.56 (Selby). "The family always thought the valley belonged to them a little" (Vira).
- **Old Mrs. Vose** — Gideon's wife (implied); Orra Keddie's first cousin.
- **Selby Vose** — manager 1913–1938 (Effie). Relationship to Gideon CONFLICTS: Vira says "old Gideon Vose's nephew"; Effie says "son of the late president"; Selby himself says "My father, who was your first president and who wrote the by-law." Signed the 1919 circular. Kept the Jerseys after Gideon. Signed the annual ledger sheet every year (Teague). Told Teague in 1916 to "let it alone." Located "down at Larrow Green" (Vira). Strawn 1913: "New manager at the Association this year and he came out with me to the platform, which the last one never did."

**Association officers**
- **Effie Loomis** — secretary 1901–1940; kept the minute book in her own hand throughout; 14 years old in 1889 (born c. 1875); recorded the 1925 award "on the last day" (4 May 1925). Office moved 1938.

## 3. Places

- **Larrow Valley** — the valley; **Larrow Valley Cooperative Creamery Association** organized there 1889.
- **Larrow Green** — hamlet where the Association was organized (1889) and the main creamery built (1891) on **Larrow Creek**; "the same building yet" (1941). Manager Selby Vose "down at Larrow Green" (Vira). Lent the first testing glass to the station (1896–97).
- **Ostrey Hollow** — side hollow seven miles up the valley from Larrow Green. The **skimming station** at its head/top, "where the road bends and then goes to nothing but the ridge." Low whitewashed building, cold as a spring house even in August, smelling of milk, steam, hot iron; engine audible before the last turn. Intake room with a loft over it reached by a ladder. Shut for years by 1958; used for storing fence wire and salt.
- **Hessel Bottom** — the wet meadow below the hamlet of Ostrey Hollow; seven farms had rights on it (hay and pasture); the best milk came off it (Effie); Emmet Brant's farm the largest. Strawn 1909: the station "takes from the farms of the wet bottom below it and from three places on the hill."
- **Ashlin** — town; site of the 1925 arbitration; Association office "one room over a harness shop" (Teague); lawyers there; Duncan Keddie taught school there; Ordell County Historical Society/Rooms there; "Ashlin creamery" (Ansel's letter says the first glass was borrowed from it).
- **Ordell County** — the county; Ordell circuit (Strawn); Ordell County Historical Society (1959 talk) / Historical Rooms (1972 accession).
- **Vessey County** — over **Redlow Ridge**; **Alder Corners Cheese Company** there; Vessey County Historical Society (holds Alder Corners test book since 1970).
- **Farland** — State Agricultural College; its library holds Strawn's notebooks since 1947; Ivy measured pipettes there.
- **Tarnet** — location of Ammon & Sons Dairy Supply.
- "The college away east" (Vira's description of Ivy's college).

## 5. Quantities and figures

- Station: seven miles up the valley from Larrow Green (Effie, Teague, Strawn, Vose all agree).
- Nine families hauling to the station (Vira); the 1919 letter went to nine families/kitchens (Vira, Ansel). Seven farms with rights on Hessel Bottom (Effie). Strawn: wet-bottom farms plus three places on the hill.
- Ansel tested "thirty-one times a week for twenty-nine seasons"; Vira: "a thousand cans a week."
- Day began half past five.
- Composite sample jars made up over ten days.
- Four-bottle hand tester.
- Pipettes blown at 17.6 c.c.; delivered mean 16.72 c.c.; short 0.88 c.c.; 16.72/17.6 = 0.95 exactly. Individual deliveries (mean of five runs each): 16.70, 16.71, 16.72, 16.72, 16.72, 16.73, 16.73, 16.74, 16.71.
- Six dozen pipettes bought 1897; nine survived unbroken in the crate (Nym: "two chipped at the tip").
- 1897 invoice: 6 doz. pipettes 17.6 c.c. at $.35 = $25.20; 12 doz. test bottles, 8 per cent neck, at $.22 = $31.68; 1 acid measure $.90; 1 hand tester, 4 bottle, $18.00; freight $2.15; total $77.93. (Arithmetic checks.)
- Averages: 1896 = 3.80; 1898 onward = 3.61 or within three hundredths; nine weeks of 1911 = 3.80; 1924 = 3.80 (Vose: 3.90).
- Gap: 0.19 = 5% of 3.80. Proportional, not fixed: herd recorded 4.56 short 0.24 (true 4.80); herd recorded 3.42 short 0.18 (true 3.60).
- 25 years 1898–1922: 40,000,000 lb milk; 1,600,000 lb/yr average. Credited fat at 3.61 = 1,444,000 lb; delivered fat at 3.80 = 1,520,000 lb; difference 76,000 lb; at 25 cents/lb = $19,000. Award $3,800 = one fifth.
- 76,000 lb = 5% of 1,520,000 (delivered) = 5¼% (5.26%) of 1,444,000 (credited). Ledger uses the credited base.
- 1898 ledger entry: credited 57,760 lb; received 60,800 lb; gain 3,040 lb; "five and a quarter per cent." (57,760 = 1,600,000 × 3.61%; 60,800 = 1,600,000 × 3.80%; 3,040/57,760 = 5.26%; 3,040/60,800 = 5.00%.)
- Teague's figures: 44,000,000 lb milk; average test 3.16 (both conflict with everyone else).
- Vira: settlement $3,500 split nine ways. All others: $3,800.
- Brant weigh book 1910: 200,000 lb even, paid on 3.42.
- Vose Jerseys 1910: 4.56.
- Larrow Green average: "a little under two tenths of a point better" than 3.61 (Nym Item 16).
- Alder Corners: four Hessel Bottom men's summer-flush milk ran ~0.19 higher there than at home over 14 overlapping seasons (Ivy); Nym has copies for the 1919 season only.
- Bulletin 214: bog hay depressed fat by not more than 0.05 in winter, nothing measurable in summer. Ivy: "four times too small and the wrong shape."
- Strawn: 28 pocket books, "one to the year," oilcloth covers; circuit 1907–1936 (30 calendar years); fifty-odd plants in four counties; burette used twice after 1926.
- Ivy's intervals: glass condemned 1923 → cause found 1958 = 35 years; dismissed 1925 → 1958 = 33 years; died 1934 → 1958 = 24 years dead.
- Ivy: "this valley's twenty-six-year anomaly" (1897–1923).
- Effie: Gideon Vose president 22 years; she was 14 in 1889; secretary 39 years (1901–1940).
- Teague: 40 years (1905–1945). Vose: 13 years (1913–1926) at the time of the address.
- Vira: born 1912; seven in 1919; "sixty years" since the letter.
- Test book: five columns (date, patron, pounds, test, fat) per Ansel; Nym lists four (date, patron, pounds, test).

## 7. Documents quoted verbatim

**Article VII of the by-laws** (Effie §3; Nym Item 1 — identical):
> *Article VII. Every patron shall be paid for the milk he delivers according to the number of pounds of butter fat contained in it, ascertained by test, and not according to the weight of the milk; and the test of the station where he delivers shall govern.*

**Award of the board of arbitration, 4 May 1925** (Effie §6; Nym Item 7 — identical):
> *The board having heard the patrons and the officers, and having had before it the books and appliances of the station, finds no cause established. The board awards to the Ostrey Hollow patrons the sum of thirty-eight hundred dollars ($3,800.00), without admission, to be charged to the station account.*

**Ansel Keddie's statement to the board, 17 March 1925** (r03, nine sentences):
> I have kept the test book of this station in my own hand since the station opened, and there is not a figure in it that I did not put there. I have set down the date, the patron, the pounds, the test and the fat, and where I have made an error I have drawn a line through it and put my letters against the line, and I have never written a figure over a figure. I have made the tests in the same order and at the same hour of every working day of every season. I have heard what the patrons say of me and I have heard what the officers say of me. Neither of them has come up that road on a working morning and stood at my elbow and watched the work done. I am asked to account for figures, and the figures are what I read and what I wrote and nothing besides. I am an old man and I have no other trade. I have tested as I was taught and as the state man saw me do it, thirty-one times a week for twenty-nine seasons, and I cannot tell you why the figures are what they are. I have never taken a pound of cream out of that station and there is not a man in this room who thinks I have when he is by himself.

**First leaf of the test book** (Ansel; Nym Item 3 — identical):
> *Ostrey Hollow station. Begun this 4th day of May, 1896. Glass borrowed of Larrow Green until our own comes. A. Keddie.*

**Summary leaf, made up 1922** (Ansel; Ivy; Nym — identical):
> *Average test of all patrons, this station: 1896, 3.80. 1897, part year. 1898, 3.61. And 3.61 or within three hundredths of it every year since, save the nine weeks of 1911 that are noted in their place, when it stood at 3.80.*

**Test book entries, 1911** (Ansel):
> *May 8. Pipette broke this morning. Using a borrowed measure.*

> *July 10. Our own measure in use again from today. Tests are back where they were. ? — A.K.*

**Ammon & Sons invoice, 14 April 1897** (Nym Item 2):
> *Sold to the Larrow Valley Co-op. Creamery Assn., for the Ostrey Hollow station:
> 6 doz. milk test pipettes, 17.6 c.c., at .35 — 25.20
> 12 doz. test bottles, 8 per cent neck, at .22 — 31.68
> 1 acid measure — .90
> 1 hand tester, 4 bottle — 18.00
> Freight — 2.15
> Total — 77.93*

**Brant weigh book, leaf for 1910** (Nym Item 4):
> *Year 1910. Delivered to the station, two hundred thousand pounds even, by my own scale and theirs agreeing. Paid on a test of 3.42.*

**Ledger entry for 1898 / typical station-gain entry** (Teague; Nym Item 6 — identical):
> *Ostrey Hollow: butter fat credited to patrons on station test, 57,760 lb. Butter fat received at Larrow Green in station cream, 60,800 lb. Station gain, 3,040 lb, five and a quarter per cent, as usual. Carried to the general account.*

**Strawn's circuit notebook, 12 June 1923** (Strawn; Nym Item 9 — identical):
> *Ostrey Hollow. Scales true. Lactometer, no added water, as always here. Ran their milk pipettes against the burette. These measures will not deliver their mark. Condemned the lot, the whole crate of them, and told the manager to order new. Glass is glass and I did not weigh what it has cost them.*

**1908 investigating committee report** (Nym Item 11):
> *We have sampled the herds of Hessel Bottom through January, February and March. The milk is whole and the scales are honest. It is our opinion that the wet hay of that bottom is what thins the cream, and that the remedy lies with the land and not with the station.*

**Bulletin No. 214 (1911), summary paragraph** (Ivy; Nym Item 12 — identical):
> *Bog hay fed as the whole of the roughage depressed the butter fat test by not more than five hundredths of one per cent in winter, and by nothing that could be measured on summer pasture.*

**Ivy Keddie's measurement sheet, 9 March 1958** (Ivy; Nym Item 14 — identical):
> *Nine pipettes from the Ostrey Hollow crate, stems blown 17.6 c.c. Delivered against burette, mean of five runs each: 16.70, 16.71, 16.72, 16.72, 16.72, 16.73, 16.73, 16.74, 16.71. Mean 16.72 c.c. Short 0.88 c.c. That is 95 hundredths of the mark, and the mark is what the neck of the bottle was ruled for.*

**Bulletin No. 471 (October 1958), closing paragraph** (Ivy; Nym Item 15 — identical; "Professor Vaught's wording and mine together"):
> *No milk was watered at Ostrey Hollow and no cream was stolen there. From the first of May, 1897, to the twelfth of June, 1923, the station's tests were low by one part in twenty because its pipettes were short by one part in twenty, and the difference — seventy-six thousand pounds of butter fat — stands credited in the Association's own ledgers, year by year, as station gain.*

## 8. Per-source detail (everything not already above)

**r01 Vira Toland**
- Nine families "in and out of each other's kitchens the whole year round."
- Ansel "went up there in ninety-five, the year before they opened it, to get it ready, and he tested that first summer too."
- The book: "every line in it was the same height as every other line, and his figures were like print"; "never a blot"; he read a man's figure off without hunting.
- 1919 letter: "it said there was water in the cans coming down from Ostrey Hollow." Read twice. Rained that day; wet on her father's shoulders.
- The Vose family "always thought the valley belonged to them a little."
- "So when the letter came it was not only the Association saying it... it was somebody's cousin saying it."
- Tests went up "all at once, one summer... and nobody ever said why." Father: milk testing better, he'd done nothing different; none of them had. "the thing corrected itself and no man stood up and gave a reason for it."
- Hearing "the winter and spring after," in an office at Ashlin, lawyers, Association officers, "our men going down in the wagons," "went on and on." "they put Ansel Keddie out. An old man."
- Her theory: alone with the separator running and the door shut, "nobody in the valley can add... it is not that he is a wicked man, it is that nobody ever came up that road and looked."
- The valley "looking at him a certain way in church."
- 1958 visitor: "Nice girl, careful, wrote everything down"; "of the family both sides in a manner of speaking"; Vira told her about the letter; "I don't know what she made of it."

**r02 Effie Loomis**
- Effie is "a reader of its papers like anybody else" for 1889–1901.
- Before the creamery: "every man's arithmetic was his own."
- Article VII "has been misquoted at me across this desk for forty years." The last clause "is the hinge of the whole Ostrey Hollow business."
- Patrons' complaint (1925): milk "had never tested as their neighbors' milk tested and that they had been paid accordingly for a very long time, and they asked to be made whole."
- Three explanations argued before the board; none accepted; "they are in the minutes and the minutes are in the safe." (Not named; from other sources presumably wet hay, tester skimming, watering.)
- Quantities: "for the twenty-five years over which the patrons laid their claim"; figures are Teague's from the annual sheets; "I do not vouch for the cows, only for the copying."
- Lost minute book "was not thrown out; it was carried out with the rest and it did not arrive."
- The crate "was never mentioned in the hearing at all... Not a word of it was spoken in any of the three sessions."

**r03 Ansel Keddie**
- Full procedure: weigh every can first; sample into patron's own numbered jar; ten-day composite; warm to blood-warm; pour vessel to vessel; draw milk in the measure to the mark on the stem; wipe the tip; blow charge into numbered bottle; acid down the side; turn until dark; four bottles weighted against each other; cover down; crank at the taught speed; fat column read in per cent against the rulings with dividers.
- "The glass I began on in ninety-six was borrowed off the Ashlin creamery, and we used it until our own outfit came up the valley in a crate the spring following." (Conflicts with his own first leaf: "borrowed of Larrow Green.")
- Gap non-seasonal: "there in July as it was there in February, in the flush of the grass and in the dead of the winter on dry hay... the same in a wet year as in a dry one."
- 1908 committee "came up and sampled the herds through the winter and reported that the wet hay of the Bottom thinned the cream, and that the remedy lay with the land."
- "I carry no charge against any man who put his name to that paper" (the 1919 circular).
- "Emmet brought more milk to my door than any other man on the Bottom, and every pound of it I weighed myself and every test of it I made myself."

**r04 Ivy Keddie**
- "nothing I am going to show you tonight depends in the smallest degree on my opinion of my grandfather."
- Five record sets "no two of them had ever been in one room before last year."
- Alder Corners book: "has never left Vessey County and I have never seen it."
- Strawn's notebooks: "eleven years in which nobody put a question to them."
- Crate "had been carried up there in June of 1923 and it was there still."
- "Put in a short charge and you get a short reading, in the same proportion."
- "everyone who looked at this before treated it as a fixed quantity, and it is not one."
- "Because the error was a proportion, it fell hardest on the best milk... no man had any way of knowing that his good cows cost him more than his poor ones."
- 1911: "Same patrons, same herds, same pasture, same season, same acid, same bottles, same man at the crank — one object changed."
- "I have no remedy to propose. I have only the crate, and it is on the table, and you may look into it on your way out."

**r05 Wilbur Teague**
- "a bookkeeper who wants admiring has generally got something behind him."
- Office description; station sheets "a stiffer paper than the creamery used."
- "The station was the smallest of the three and it made the most noise."
- Station-gain line: "I have heard it said in the valley that the line was some old dodge of the founders. It was not."
- "a difference that will not be entered somewhere will come back and bite you at audit."
- 1911: "The station's own measure broke in the spring of 1911, and they made shift with a borrowed one for six weeks before their own came up from town. It went into my accounts as a small charge for replacement." "as though a bookkeeper kept the weather."
- "The patrons petitioned in the winter, when a farmer has time to be angry."
- "I do not say what any particular person did on any particular day, because I was not there."
- Vose "signed the sheet every year... a man who signs a sheet has had the sheet in front of him. What he made of it he never told me."
- "a bookkeeper who takes his employer's business up a second time after being told once has generally begun to look for another employer."

**r06 Merle Strawn**
- Travelled by train, livery, or neither in April.
- "learns the plants by their smells before he learns them by their names."
- Four things in fixed order: scales (both intake and cream, light and heavy, against sealed weights); lactometer patron by patron for added water; boiler/water supply/cold room; watch a test through, "and I did not let him hurry it."
- 1907: "his hand is better than most... He works slow and clean and reads his own figure without being coaxed to it."
- 1917: "two testers doing the work of three men, which is where mistakes live."
- 1921 burette: "It is a slow business to run a plant's glass against it and most plants have never had it done."
- 1922: "I did not run their glass against the burette this year, the day being short and the road home being what it was, and I have put that down because it is what happened."
- 1923 June 12 heading: "I had carried the burette two seasons before this check."
- 1925: "tests improved. New man at the tester's bench this year."
- Closing: books are "dull reading and they were meant to be."

**r07 Halbert Nym**
- Item 3 provenance: "Kept at the station from 4 May 1896; impounded by the board of arbitration in March 1925; in the Association's office at Ashlin from 1925 to 1959; received here in 1959."
- Item 4 provenance: "Begun at Hessel Bottom on 1 January 1897 and kept there by one household throughout; to Roy Brant in 1929; lent by him to Miss Ivy Keddie in 1958 and returned; to Miss Hazel Brant in 1962; received here in 1963."
- Item 5: "Condemned by the state dairy inspector on 12 June 1923. The crate was found in the Association office at Ashlin, in February 1958, by Miss Ivy Keddie... taken by her to Farland, measured there, and given to these Rooms in 1959."
- Item 8: Ansel's statement, "in the hand of Item 3, signed. Not transcribed."
- Item 9 provenance: notebooks to state dairy office 1936; to Farland library 1947; 1923 volume found and copied 1958; "The notebooks are not ours."
- Item 10: Ammon & Sons letter 2 July 1923, "acknowledging the Association's order of 28 June for replacement pipettes and bottles, promising shipment that month, and declining to say anything of the earlier lot. Not transcribed."
- Item 13: Alder Corners figures "for the season of 1919. Not transcribed. The test book itself has never been in this county; it passed from its founder to his daughter in 1941 and to the Vessey County Historical Society in 1970."
- Item 16: Larrow Green receiving and churn books; "Over the same years for which the station's 3.61 is recorded, the Larrow Green average runs a little under two tenths of a point better."
- Notes and queries: no 1896 intake figure; Item 5 wants a locked case; accession numbers still to be cut for Items 13 and 16.

**r08 Selby Vose**
- Ordinary business 1925: wet season into June; creamery ran through the flush without a day lost; butter held its place; ice went in early and lasted; station "ran its season out and its cream came down to us daily and sweet."
- The board's finding "is the only sentence in this business that was arrived at by men under oath with the books in front of them."
- Settlement: "a generous settlement of an unproved claim... There is not a business house in Ordell County that would have done it."
- "But I will not have peace bought a second time."
- Claims: for a generation the station tested below Larrow Green; blamed on wet hay, tester, office, weather; "in the very next season the tests at that station rose by twenty-one hundredths and the average for 1924 stood at 3.90."
- On the state man: "he condemned the station's testing apparatus on the twelfth of July, 1923, and told us to order new. We ordered new inside the month and had it in use before the flush was over. Apparatus wears."
- "A little water in a can is not a felony in a man's own mind... I have no wish to see anybody prosecuted, and nobody has been or will be."
- On the 1919 circular: "thrown at me a good deal in the last two years"; "It warned the patrons of Ostrey Hollow, in temperate language, that added water would be looked for and would be dealt with. It named nobody. It accused nobody."
- Gideon's Jerseys 4.56 in 1910; "they are gone now and the ones I have are no better than yours."
- "I thank you for thirteen years of your patience and I ask for one more."

## 9. Conflicts and oddities (between and within sources)

1. **Settlement amount**: Vira says $3,500 ("thirty-five hundred dollars"); Effie, Teague, Ivy, Nym, Vose all say $3,800.
2. **Selby Vose's relation to Gideon**: Vira "nephew"; Effie "son of the late president"; Selby "my father, who was your first president."
3. **Condemnation date**: Strawn, Nym, Ivy, Bulletin 471 = 12 June 1923; Vose = 12 July 1923. The Ammon & Sons order of 28 June and letter of 2 July support June.
4. **1924 average**: Ansel and Strawn = 3.80; Vose = 3.90, with a rise of "twenty-one hundredths" — internally inconsistent (3.61 + 0.21 = 3.82, not 3.90); everyone else's gap is 0.19.
5. **Milk total**: Effie, Ivy, Nym = 40,000,000 lb (1898–1922); Teague = 44,000,000 lb ("over the years the account ran," possibly a longer span since the station column runs from 1897 and he served to 1945).
6. **Average test**: Teague = 3.16; all others = 3.61 (looks like a digit transposition).
7. **Station-gain percentage base**: Teague says 5¼% "of the fat that was actually in the cream" — but 3,040/60,800 = 5.0%; 3,040/57,760 = 5.26%. Ivy and Nym correctly say the ledger's 5¼% is of the credited fat and 5% is of the delivered fat.
8. **Who began the station-gain line**: Teague says it was his own device begun 1905, yet quotes "the entry for 1898... in the same hand for as long as the hand was mine"; Nym says the station column runs from 1897 with each year carrying the entry in one form. 1898 predates Teague's arrival.
9. **1911 borrowed-measure duration**: Ansel, Ivy, summary leaf = nine weeks (8 May–10 July); Teague = six weeks.
10. **1911 replacement source**: Ivy says the borrowed measure "came up from Larrow Green with the cream wagon"; Teague says they used a borrowed one "before their own came up from town" and entered "a small charge for replacement"; Ansel says "Our own measure in use again from today." Also odd: six dozen pipettes were bought in 1897, so spares should have been in hand — why borrow at all?
11. **Crate location when found**: Ivy — loft over the intake room at the station, under a tarpaulin, February 1958; Nym Item 5 — "found in the Association office at Ashlin, in February 1958."
12. **Loft contents**: Strawn 1928 was told the loft "had been full since before the war"; Ivy says the crate was carried up in June 1923 (after WWI). Not strictly contradictory (other odds and ends), but odd.
13. **Nym's transposed keepers**: Item 3 "Station test book, kept by E. Brant" (but first leaf signed A. Keddie, hand of Item 8 = Keddie); Item 4 "Farm weigh book, kept by A. Keddie" (but kept at Hessel Bottom by one household → Roy Brant). Names swapped.
14. **Test book columns**: Ansel says five (date, patron, pounds, test, fat); Nym Item 3 lists four (date, patron, pounds, test).
15. **Pipette condition**: Ivy "Nine of them were unbroken"; Nym "two chipped at the tip."
16. **Where the first glass was borrowed from**: Ansel's letter "borrowed off the Ashlin creamery"; his own first leaf "Glass borrowed of Larrow Green"; Nym "Larrow Green having lent until then."
17. **Strawn's start year**: Strawn "took the Ordell circuit in nineteen hundred and seven"; Ivy says notebooks "kept... from 1908." Also 28 books "one to the year" vs 30 calendar years 1907–1936.
18. **Alder Corners overlap**: Ivy — four Hessel Bottom men, "fourteen seasons" of overlap; Nym Item 13 — figures copied 1958 "for the season of 1919" only. Ivy: Rosalie Cudd (Jerome Cudd's niece) runs the factory and copied the figures; Nym: book passed "from its founder to his daughter in 1941."
19. **Ansel's dismissal vs board's end**: Ansel put out 30 April 1925; the board sat until 4 May 1925 and recorded the award on the last day. He was dismissed before the award.
20. **Association office location**: Teague — office at Ashlin over a harness shop; Nym — Association office at Ashlin; Vira — manager Selby Vose "down at Larrow Green"; Effie — office "moved" in 1938 (from/to unstated).
21. **Content of the 1919 circular**: Vira — "it said there was water in the cans coming down from Ostrey Hollow"; Ansel — "saying there was water in their cans"; Vose — "warned... that added water would be looked for and would be dealt with. It named nobody. It accused nobody." Strawn told the manager he had never found water at that station.
22. **Vose's chronology inverted**: he says tests rose "in the very next season" after the petition/hearing and "when the Association began to look" — but the petition was January 1925 and the hearing March–May 1925, while the rise was in 1924 (after new glass, July 1923).
23. **Vira's internal contradiction**: she says tests went up one summer, THEN the hearing came the winter and spring after; but also that "the numbers went right the summer after" they put Ansel out.
24. **Vira's dating of Ansel's start**: "went up there in ninety-five... and he tested that first summer too" vs book begun 4 May 1896 and 29 seasons 1896–1924.
25. **Emmet Brant's low test**: Effie says the best milk came off Hessel Bottom and Emmet was the largest patron, yet his 1910 weigh book shows he was paid on 3.42 — below the station's 3.61 average. Ivy's illustrative herds (4.56 short 0.24; 3.42 short 0.18) correspond exactly to Vose's Jerseys (4.56, 1910) and Brant's herd (3.42, 1910) — but Vose's herd was at the home place (Larrow Green), not at the station.
26. **Household count**: nine families hauling / nine letters (Vira, Ansel); seven farms on Hessel Bottom (Effie); wet-bottom farms plus three hill places (Strawn).
27. **Strawn's June 1911 visit** fell inside the borrowed-measure window (8 May–10 July) when tests were at 3.80: "Watched a test through. Nothing here to note."
28. **"Apparatus wears"** (Vose) vs glass pipettes blown short from manufacture; the 1896 average of 3.80 on borrowed glass, 3.80 again in 1911 on a borrowed measure, and 3.80 in 1924 on new glass — three independent confirmations that the station's own 1897 glass was short from the start.
29. **Ivy's "twenty-six-year anomaly"** (1897–1923) vs "twenty-five full delivery years" (1898–1922) — both used.
30. **Ansel's "thirty-one times a week"** vs Vira's "a thousand cans a week" — cans weighed vs tests made; not contradictory but different units.
31. **Bulletin 471 "no cream was stolen"** vs Vira's belief ("He was taking the cream"), Teague's insinuation ("Cream is portable"), and Vose's watering theory. Strawn's lactometer never found water; Strawn watched Ansel test and found him slow and clean.
32. **1908 committee sampling**: Ansel "through the winter"; Nym "January, February and March 1908 only, as the report states."
33. **Vose says the state man came "every year or two"**; Strawn's extracts show near-annual visits, twice in 1920.
34. **Vose on the 1919 circular**: "thrown at me a good deal in the last two years" (1924–26) — but Vira and Ansel treat it as the wound of 1919 onward.
35. **Ivy calls Strawn "the state's dairy inspector for this county"**; Strawn describes a four-county circuit.
36. **Nym's Item 6 curatorial note and Ivy** agree exactly (40,000,000 lb; 76,000 lb; $19,000); Effie gives 40,000,000 lb and 25 cents but not the 76,000 lb figure.
37. **The $3,800 was "charged to the station account"** — the very account that had accumulated 76,000 lb of "station gain" ($19,000) carried to the general account; the award was a fifth of the gain.
38. **Effie's three explanations** at the hearing are never named by her; the crate was never mentioned in any of the three sessions despite the award's "books and appliances" wording.
39. **Hazel Brant**: named by Vira only as the reference point for Ivy's kinship; by Nym only as a later holder of the weigh book (1962–63); nowhere explicitly identified as Emmet's daughter.
40. **Vose's "thirteen times"**: he says he has stood up thirteen times (1913–1926 inclusive = 14 annual meetings if counting 1913; consistent if first address was spring 1914).
