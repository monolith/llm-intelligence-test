# Retention notes after retellings r01–r16 (noisy mode, segment 2 of 3)

Status: SECOND notes file for this run. Segment 1 (r01–r08) notes are carried forward in full below — nothing dropped — and everything from segment 2 (r09–r16) is added and marked where it changes or contradicts the earlier picture. Unrelated distractor documents (segment 1: road commission, playhouse, drugstore, buoy, court column, lacings paper, water-cure ledger, ferry API; segment 2: Pemberton casebook, bus shelter, Antiphon travel piece, Corbin Junction telegraph book, floor-safe story, summability paper, Purlwright plugin API, laundromat transcript) are deliberately excluded.

The story: the Ostrey Hollow skimming station of the Larrow Valley Cooperative Creamery Association tested low for ~26 years (1897–1923) because its milk-test pipettes were blown short by one part in twenty. Everyone blamed the wet hay of Hessel Bottom (1908 Grigg committee; Kepp 1926), the tester Ansel Keddie (valley opinion, Teague's insinuation, Rundle's "settled opinion"), or the patrons watering the milk (Vose's 1919 circular; Sill). The truth came out in 1958 when Ansel's granddaughter Ivy Keddie measured the condemned pipettes (Bulletin No. 471, October 1958, with Professor Leland Vaught). The Association's own ledgers had carried the missing fat every year as "station gain" (5¼%), and the Larrow Green buttermaker Hobart Sill had weighed the Hollow's cream in heavy every year since 1893 without anyone asking him about it. The four Hessel Bottom men who hauled to Alder Corners (Vessey County) in summer 1915–1930 tested ~3.80 there while being paid on 3.61 at home.

## 2. People and relationships

**Keddie family**
- **Ansel Keddie** — tester at Ostrey Hollow skimming station. Went up in '95 (1895), the year before it opened, to get it ready (Vira). Kept the test book from 4 May 1896. Tested 29 seasons, 1896 through 1924; "did not test a pound in the season of 1925." Put out 30 April 1925 (his own words: "the last day of the month"; Frick: "four days before we made our award"). Died 1934 (Ivy); "died in the thirties" (Vira). "I am an old man and I have no other trade." Worked alone; nobody ever watched him except the state man. Frick (r13) believed him "then and now," but adds that in the corridor at Ashlin Keddie told him privately "that he had always thought the patrons were at fault and would not say so in the room" — "he went to his grave accusing nobody, which is true, and that he suspected nobody, which is not." Roy Brant (r09): Emmet's grievance against Ansel from 1919 was "not from any quarrel over the water, but because my father held that the man who kept the test book ought to have stood up in front of that circular and did not."
- **Orra Keddie** — Ansel's wife. **Emmet Brant's sister** — Roy: "They were full brother and sister, same mother and same father, whatever you may hear from people who like a story with a stepmother in it." **First cousin of old Mrs. Vose** (Vira: "everybody up here knew and nobody wrote down anywhere"). After the 1919 letter she would walk down to Larrow Green rather than pass her brother's lane. Outlived Ansel "a good while" and bore the valley's suspicion the whole time.
- **Duncan Keddie** — Ansel and Orra's son ("boy"); taught school at Ashlin; Ivy's father; Roy Brant's first cousin (Roy).
- **Ivy Keddie** — Ansel's granddaughter; "Miss Ivy Keddie of the State Agricultural College at Farland" (Nym); "a graduate student at Farland" (r10 headnote); "Duncan's girl Ivy" (Roy). Found the crate February 1958; measured pipettes 9 March 1958; wrote to Rosalie Cudd 1958 ("asks one in four lines and apologizes for troubling me"); borrowed the weigh book from Roy 1958 ("She is careful with paper"); gave talk 1959. Kinship to Hazel Brant: Vira — "Hazel Brant's first cousin once removed"; Roy and Rundle's 1959 footnote — **second cousins** (correct: Ivy is Orra's granddaughter, Hazel is Emmet's granddaughter). Roy: "both of them have had to have it explained to them by me more than once."

**Brant family**
- **Emmet Brant** — **born 1854, died 1929** (Roy). Biggest man on Hessel Bottom: most cows, most hay ground, hauled the most milk; the station's largest patron in every recorded year (Effie); "the one that letter cost the most" (Vira). Roy: "sixty-odd acres of the meadow, and thirty-eight cows in a good year, which for this valley was a herd"; theirs the largest of the six farms. Orra's brother; Ansel's brother-in-law. Before 1919 the Keddie and Brant households ate Sunday dinners together (Vira). After the 1919 circular, Emmet and Ansel never spoke again. Kept the Hessel Bottom farm weigh book from 1 January 1897 (Nym; Frick's exhibit list) — Roy says **1 January 1898** — "until the week before he died." One of the first men to complain out loud in 1902 (Roy). Wrote at the kitchen table after the second milking, in the lamp, hat still on; Roy's mother would not have the table cleared till he was done. Wrote "even" and underlined it on the 1910 leaf, "which for him was carrying on." Read the 1908 report twice, put it in the drawer, "said that the land had been wet since the flood and the tests had not." Noticed the 1911 rise and fall and entered both, "and he could make nothing of it." Weigh book passed to Roy 1929.
- **Roy Brant** — Emmet's son; born c. 1888 (67 in winter 1955); "still milking"; kept the weigh book after his father died, "in the same book until it was filled and then in another, in the same way"; did NOT haul to Alder Corners ("the biggest herd is the one that cannot afford to be talked about... a poor reason and cost us money"); lent the book to Ivy 1958 (returned); first cousin of Duncan Keddie; father of Hazel. "The book has outlived two of the three men who are in it." Has cut the meadow "with a scythe and with a mower behind two horses."
- **Roy's mother** (Emmet's wife) — unnamed; kept the table uncleared while Emmet wrote.
- **Hazel Brant** — "Miss Hazel Brant"; **Roy's daughter** (Roy: "My daughter Hazel"), hence Emmet's granddaughter — resolves the earlier open question; NOT Emmet's daughter. Received the weigh book 1962; gave it to the Rooms 1963. Roy 1955: "It goes to Hazel when I am done with it... after me it is Hazel's."

**Vose family**
- **Gideon Vose** — "old Gideon"; started the Association; first president 1889–1911 (22 years, longest ever). Drafted the by-laws himself (not a lawyer; "they read as a farmer wrote them"; Rundle: "had once thought of the law and thought better of it," wrote them "at his own kitchen table, in a hand that the Association's clerks were still complaining about thirty years later"; "a heavy, quiet, obstinate man"). Wrote Article VII in 1892 (Effie) / 1891 (Rundle). **Died 1917** (Effie). Kept a herd of Jerseys at the home place; in 1910 that herd tested 4.56 (Selby). "The family always thought the valley belonged to them a little" (Vira).
- **Old Mrs. Vose** — Gideon's wife (implied); Orra Keddie's first cousin.
- **Selby Vose** — manager 1913–1938 (Effie). Relationship to Gideon CONFLICTS: Vira "old Gideon Vose's nephew"; **Rundle "His nephew Selby Vose"** (Selby used to say "his uncle wrote by-laws the way other men build stone wall — slowly, and to last"); Effie "son of the late president"; Selby himself "My father, who was your first president and who wrote the by-law." Signed the 1919 circular (Sill: "the only plain thing that office ever did"). Kept the Jerseys after Gideon. Signed the annual ledger sheet every year (Teague). Told Teague in 1916 to "let it alone." Located "down at Larrow Green" (Vira). Strawn 1913: "New manager at the Association this year and he came out with me to the platform, which the last one never did." Before the 1925 board put the WATER explanation (Frick): "he stood by it before us... He put it without heat and without proof."

**Association officers**
- **Effie Loomis** — secretary 1901–1940; kept the minute book in her own hand throughout; 14 years old in 1889 (born c. 1875); recorded the 1925 award "on the last day" (4 May 1925). Office moved 1938.
- **Wilbur Teague** — bookkeeper 1905–1945 ("forty years by the calendar"); kept the ledgers; invented the "station gain" line (he says) in 1905; put the matter to Selby Vose in 1916 and was told to let it alone; testified to the board 1925 ("Cream is portable"). ## 3. Places

- **Larrow Valley** — the valley; **Larrow Valley Cooperative Creamery Association** organized there 1889 (met in the schoolhouse, cold weather, "went on very late," somebody's horse got loose — Rundle). Farmers "tired of hauling butter to Ashlin and taking whatever the merchant chose to allow them."
- **Larrow Green** — hamlet where the Association was organized (1889) and the main creamery built (1891) on **Larrow Creek** ("on the creek below the mill" — Rundle); "the same building yet" (1941). Manager Selby Vose "down at Larrow Green" (Vira). Lent the first testing glass to the station (1896–97) — Sill says it was his own outfit off his own bench. Churn room: plank floor in 1893, cooled with creek ice "out of the pond." Platform, cold room. Larrow Green patrons "the men on this creek" ran 3.78 (Sill). Nyle Grigg hauls here. Rundle teaches the school here.
- **Ostrey Hollow** — side hollow seven miles up the valley from Larrow Green ("in the nineties the pull down was three hours in mud and could not be done at all in March" — Rundle). The **skimming station** at its head/top, "where the road bends and then goes to nothing but the ridge." Low whitewashed building, cold as a spring house even in August, smelling of milk, steam, hot iron; engine audible before the last turn. Rundle: "plain... boarded and battened, with the intake room at the road end and a loft over it that I believe has never been cleared out since it was put up; and behind it the creek, which in a wet April comes up to the sill and has twice come over it." Intake room with a loft over it reached by a ladder. Weigh room (Frick held sessions two and three there). Shut for years by 1958; used for storing fence wire and salt. Cream wagon down "every day of the week but Sunday" (Sill).
- **Hessel Bottom** — the wet meadow below the hamlet of Ostrey Hollow; "a hundred and sixty acres of wet meadow lying under the hill at Ostrey Hollow" (Roy); **six** farms had rights (Roy, Grigg report, Kepp) / **seven** (Effie); the best milk came off it (Effie); Emmet Brant's farm the largest (sixty-odd acres, 38 cows). Strawn 1909: the station "takes from the farms of the wet bottom below it and from three places on the hill." Roy: water stands on the low end into May; "a soft place by the alders that has taken a horse to the hock in my time and my father's"; hay coarse, cuts late, "never failed to grow something." Grigg report: "lying along the creek under Ostrey Hollow... springy the year round and is under water in part every spring... sedge, rush, and the coarse sour grasses... hay is cut late and heavy and is fed as the main dependence of those farms from November to April." Kepp: "the completest example of wet meadow in this county... the springs come out at the foot of the slope and run in it the year round."
- **Ashlin** — town; site of the first 1925 arbitration session (Association office); Association office "one room over a harness shop" (Teague); lawyers there; Duncan Keddie taught school there; Dr. Kepp practices there; Ordell County Historical Society/Rooms there; "Ashlin creamery" (Ansel's letter says the first glass was borrowed from it). The corridor at Ashlin where Keddie spoke privately to Frick.
- **Ordell County** — the county; Ordell circuit (Strawn); Ordell County Historical Society (1959 talk) / Historical Rooms (1972 accession); Ordell County Bar Association (Frick 1946); Ordell County Farm Club (Kepp 1926); county farm bureau local-history collection (Roy 1955).
- **Vessey County** — over **Redlow Ridge** ("four miles of switchback, cut for a lumber road and never much improved since. It shuts in November and it does not open before April, some years May... the drifts sit in the cut below the top" — Rosalie); **Alder Corners Cheese Company** there, on the flat below the church; Vessey County Historical Society (holds Alder Corners test book since 1970). "the cloud that sits on the top of the ridge till the sun is well up."
- **Farland** — State Agricultural College; its library holds Strawn's notebooks since 1947; Ivy measured pipettes there; Vaught's bulletin issued there.
- **Tarnet** — location of Ammon & Sons Dairy Supply.
- "The college away east" (Vira's description of Ivy's college).

## 4. Timeline (all dates)

- **1854** — Emmet Brant born (Roy).
- **1889** — Association organized at Larrow Green (schoolhouse meeting); Gideon Vose first president.
- **1889–1892** — minute book of these years LOST in the 1938 office move.

**Ansel Keddie's statement to the board, 17 March 1925** (r03, nine sentences; Frick paraphrases the last three):
> I have kept the test book of this station in my own hand since the station opened, and there is not a figure in it that I did not put there. I have made the tests in the same order and at the same hour of every working day of every season. I have heard what the patrons say of me and I have heard what the officers say of me. I have tested as I was taught and as the state man saw me do it, thirty-one times a week for twenty-nine seasons, and I cannot tell you why the figures are what they are. **1908 investigating committee report — full text as reprinted 1919** (Grigg r15):
> REPORT OF THE COMMITTEE APPOINTED TO INQUIRE INTO THE COMPLAINTS OF THE PATRONS OF THE OSTREY HOLLOW STATION.
>
> To the President and Directors of the Larrow Valley Cooperative Creamery Association.
>
> Your committee, appointed in January last, respectfully reports as follows.
>
> The complaint before us is of some years' standing and is in substance this: that the milk delivered at the Ostrey Hollow skimming station is returned by the test at a figure below that of milk delivered elsewhere in this valley, and that the patrons of that station are in consequence paid less for the same labor and the same cows than their neighbors are paid. Since payment by test came in among us and the pound of butter fat took the place of the pound of milk as the measure of a man's month, a difference of this kind is no longer a matter of opinion. It is a matter of money, and it comes out of the same pocket every month, and your committee has approached it in that light and not as a quarrel.
>
> Hessel Bottom, where six farms cut hay and turn out stock, is a wet meadow lying along the creek under Ostrey Hollow. Its ground is springy the year round and is under water in part every spring. Its herbage is sedge, rush, and the coarse sour grasses that come in on such land, and its hay is cut late and heavy and is fed as the main dependence of those farms from November to April. There is no better winter keep to be had there. We say this without reproach to any man on that bottom; the land is what it is, and they took it as their fathers left it to them.
>
> Your committee visited each of the six farms, some of them three and four times. We drew our samples ourselves and we sealed them ourselves. We saw the herds milked, we saw the cans filled, and we saw them hauled to the station and weighed in at the intake, and we stood by while the test was made and saw nothing whatever in the doing of it to remark upon. We tried the station scales against a known weight and we tried the patrons' own scales against the station's. We inquired into the water supply at each barn and satisfied ourselves upon it.
>
> Your committee further observed the general condition of the stock upon that bottom, as men who have kept cattle all their lives are entitled to observe it. The herds are thin in the flank at the end of the winter feeding and slow to come to their flesh in the spring. Their coats are rough later in the season than the coats of hill herds. They do well enough in flow, for the wet land is generous of milk, but it is our opinion, formed on the ground and not out of a book, that what such land gives in quantity it withholds in quality, and that a cow cannot put into her pail what her keep has never put into her.
>
> We report that we found no dishonesty of any kind, in any quarter, and we wish that finding to be printed in the same type as the rest.
>
> We have sampled the herds of Hessel Bottom through January, February and March. The milk is whole and the scales are honest. It is our opinion that the wet hay of that bottom is what thins the cream, and that the remedy lies with the land and not with the station.
>
> NYLE GRIGG, chairman, and the committee.

## 9. Conflicts and oddities (between and within sources)

Carried forward from segment 1 (1–40), with additions where segment 2 bears on them:

1. **Settlement amount**: Vira says $3,500 ("thirty-five hundred dollars"); Effie, Teague, Ivy, Nym, Vose, Frick all say $3,800.
2. **Selby Vose's relation to Gideon**: Vira "nephew"; **Rundle "His nephew Selby Vose"** (and quotes Selby saying "his uncle"); Effie "son of the late president"; Selby "my father, who was your first president." Now 2 vs 2.
3. **Condemnation date**: Strawn, Nym, Ivy, Bulletin 471 conclusion, Vaught ("June of 1923") = 12 June 1923; Vose and **Rundle** = 12 July 1923. The Ammon & Sons order of 28 June and letter of 2 July support June.
4. **1924 average**: Ansel, Strawn, Vaught = 3.80; Vose = 3.90, with a rise of "twenty-one hundredths" — internally inconsistent (3.61 + 0.21 = 3.82, not 3.90); everyone else's gap is 0.19. Kepp also uses 0.21.
5. **Milk total**: Effie, Ivy, Nym, Vaught = 40,000,000 lb (1898–1922); Teague and **Rundle** = 44,000,000 lb.
6. **Average test**: Teague = 3.16; **Frick = 3.16** ("Three and sixteen is not a scandalous figure"); all others = 3.61 (digit transposition, now in two sources).
7. **Station-gain percentage base**: Teague says 5¼% "of the fat that was actually in the cream"; **Vaught says "five and a quarter percent of the butterfat actually delivered"** — but 3,040/60,800 = 5.0%; 3,040/57,760 = 5.26%. Ivy and Nym correctly say the ledger's 5¼% is of the credited fat and 5% is of the delivered fat.
8. **Who began the station-gain line**: Teague says it was his own device begun 1905, yet quotes "the entry for 1898... in the same hand for as long as the hand was mine"; Nym says the station column runs from 1897 with each year carrying the entry in one form. 1898 predates Teague's arrival. Rundle (1939) saw the entry "every year, in the bookkeeper's hand."
9. **1911 borrowed-measure duration**: Ansel, Ivy, summary leaf, Roy, Vaught = nine weeks (8 May–10 July); Teague and **Kepp** = six weeks (Kepp: "Six weeks of June and July").
10. **1911 replacement source**: Ivy says the borrowed measure "came up from Larrow Green with the cream wagon"; Teague says "before their own came up from town" and entered "a small charge for replacement"; Ansel says "Our own measure in use again from today"; **Roy says "The state man left his own measure that June and that is what they used"** — but the break was 8 May, Strawn's June 1911 note says only "Watched a test through. Nothing here to note," and Strawn had no burette until 1921. Also odd: six dozen pipettes were bought in 1897, so spares should have been in hand — why borrow at all?
11. **Crate location when found**: Ivy — loft over the intake room at the station, under a tarpaulin, February 1958; Nym Item 5 — "found in the Association office at Ashlin, in February 1958." Vaught says only "the station's condemned crate."
12. **Loft contents**: Strawn 1928 was told the loft "had been full since before the war"; Ivy says the crate was carried up in June 1923 (after WWI). Rundle: the loft "has never been cleared out since it was put up." Not strictly contradictory, but odd.
13. **Nym's transposed keepers**: Item 3 "Station test book, kept by E. Brant" (but first leaf signed A. Keddie; Frick's exhibit 1 "in the hand of Ansel Keddie"); Item 4 "Farm weigh book, kept by A. Keddie" (but kept at Hessel Bottom by one household → Roy Brant; Frick's exhibit 3 "The private weigh book of Emmet Brant"). Names swapped.
