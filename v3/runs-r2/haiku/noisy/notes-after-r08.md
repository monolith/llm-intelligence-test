# Notes after Retelling 8

## People and Relationships

**Vira Toland** — Born 1912 in Ostrey Hollow, never left except 2 years at Ashlin; records the scandal 1979 at age 67. Narrator of retelling 1.

**Ansel Keddie** — Station tester at Ostrey Hollow, began work 1895, kept test book 1896–1924 in own hand over 29 seasons. Wife Orra Keddie. Eventually caught taking cream, dismissed April 30 (year unspecified but after 1925 arbitration). Died in 1930s (Vira) or 1934 specifically (Ivy). Made unsent letter spring 1925. Gave statement to arbitration board March 17, 1925 (9 sentences). Grandfather of Ivy Keddie.

**Orra Keddie** — Ansel's wife. First cousin of old Mrs. Vose (Mrs. Gideon Vose). Sister of Emmet Brant. After 1919 circular accusing water in cans, stopped speaking to her brother Emmet; had no communication for years. Outlived Ansel by many years.

**Emmet Brant** — Largest producer on Hessel Bottom, brought more milk to station than any other patron every year. Brother of Orra Keddie. Had most cows, most hay ground. Paid highest for water-accusation in 1919. Never spoke to Brant after 1919 circular (actually after Ansel/Orra). Son is Roy Brant (had weigh book, lent to Ivy Keddie 1958). Father of Roy.

**Selby Vose** — Became manager 1913, served until 1938 (Loomis notes 1901–1940 context). Son of Gideon Vose. Signed annual station sheets every year. Sent 1919 circular warning Ostrey Hollow patrons of water detection. Supported $3,800 payment in 1925. Gave annual address 1926 presenting his theory: water was added to cans, stopped when Association looked; attributes 1924 test rise to scrutiny not equipment. In retelling 8 he speaks as though not naming names: "without naming a name."

**Gideon Vose** — Founder/first president of Association 1889, held office until 1911 (22 years). Drafted by-laws himself. Father of Selby Vose. Died by 1917 (per Loomis: "died in 1917 and could not be asked about it afterward" re: Article VII). Old Gideon Vose is Mrs. Gideon Vose's husband.

**Old Mrs. Vose** — Gideon Vose's wife. First cousin of Orra Keddie. Vira notes the family connection made the 1919 accusation particularly damaging ("if you follow me, it was somebody's cousin saying it").

**Effie Loomis** — Secretary 1901–1940, kept minute book in own hand. Writes "Notes toward a history" 1941 (one year after leaving desk). Records Association history and gaps in documentation. Notes crate never mentioned in 1925 hearing. Notes 1889–1892 minute book lost in 1938 office move.

**Wilbur Teague** — Bookkeeper 1905–1945 (40 years). Invented "station gain" line 1905 to record difference between credited and received fat. Carried the consistent 5.25% station gain to general account every year. Testified to board 1925: cream portable, one man for 29 seasons allows systematic underpayment. Confronted Vose 1916 about the account, was told to let alone. Remarks he was "Association's bookkeeper and not its conscience." Writing retrospective 1948.

**Hobart Sill** — Buttermaker at Larrow Green, had churn book and cream receiving book.

**Merle Strawn** — State dairy inspector, Ordell circuit 1907–1936. Visited Ostrey Hollow station repeatedly: found scales true, no water added (consistently), condemned glassware June 12, 1923. Ran milk pipettes against burette June 12, 1923: "These measures will not deliver their mark. Condemned the lot, the whole crate of them." Verified new glass October 1923. Notes in circuit book confirm "tests improved" by 1924 with new glass. Retired 1936, notebooks went to state dairy office; found at Farland library 1947, photostatted 1958. Kept 28 pocket oilcloth-cover circuit books.

**Ivy Keddie** — Granddaughter of Ansel Keddie, daughter of Duncan Keddie (who taught school at Ashlin). Student/researcher at State Agricultural College at Farland. Found condemned crate in Ostrey Hollow station loft February 1958. Measured 9 surviving pipettes against burette March 9, 1958: all blown 17.6 cc, delivered mean 16.72 cc (short 0.88 cc = 95 hundredths of mark). Proved in 1958 (33 years after Ansel's dismissal, 24 years after his death) that glassware was the cause. Gave talk to Ordell County Historical Society 1959 presenting mathematics of error and exoneration. Collaborated with Professor Vaught on Bulletin 471 (October 1958).

**Duncan Keddie** — Son of Ansel Keddie, father of Ivy. Taught school at Ashlin.

**Roy Brant** — Son of Emmet Brant. Kept weigh book after his father died. Lent it to Ivy Keddie 1958.

**Hazel Brant** — Kept weigh book 1962. First cousin once removed of young researcher from college (1958).

**Halbert Nym** — Curator, Ordell County Historical Rooms. Catalogues 16 items 1972, writes accession notes.

**Alonzo Frick** — Attorney at Ashlin, chairman of board of arbitration March 17–May 4, 1925. Described as able and impartial.

## Places

**Larrow Valley** — Geographic region containing the Association and stations.

**Larrow Green** — Main creamery built 1891 on Larrow Creek. Office at Ashlin. Larrow Green also the location of the Association's founding 1889. Did better test averages than Ostrey Hollow (by little under 2 tenths point per Nym retelling).

**Ostrey Hollow** — Skimming station 7 miles up valley from Larrow Green. At very top of Hollow where road bends and goes to nothing but ridge (per Vira). Low whitewashed building, cold inside, smelled of milk steam hot iron. Engine audible before last turn. Opened before Merle Strawn's circuit began (1907). Station also called "skimming station." Contains loft over intake room where crate was stored.

**Hessel Bottom** — Wet meadow below hamlet in Ostrey Hollow. 7 farms had rights on it. Largest was Emmet Brant's. Milk from this region tested lower on average.

**Ashlin** — County seat. Office of Association. Location of arbitration board March 17–May 4, 1925. Where Duncan Keddie taught school. Where Vira lived 2 years after marriage.

**Ordell County** — County containing Larrow Valley, Ashlin, Ostrey Hollow.

**Vessey County** — Across ridge from Ostrey Hollow. Contains Alder Corners Cheese Company (test figures 1919 season). Test book never left Vessey County. Moved to Vessey County Historical Society 1970.

**Farland** — Location of State Agricultural College. Merle Strawn's circuit books housed at library since 1947. Ivy Keddie studied there. Bulletin 471 issued October 1958.

## Organizations and Entities

**Larrow Valley Cooperative Creamery Association** — Founded 1889 at Larrow Green. Three components: creamery at Larrow Green (built 1891), office at Ashlin, skimming station at Ostrey Hollow (7 miles up valley). Article VII adopted 1892: patrons paid by butterfat test at station where delivered (no appeal from station test). By-laws drafted by Gideon Vose. Officers in period 1901–1940: Gideon Vose (president 1889–1911), Selby Vose (manager 1913–1938), Effie Loomis (secretary 1901–1940), Wilbur Teague (bookkeeper 1905–1945), Hobart Sill (buttermaker).

**Board of Arbitration** — Constituted 1925. Sat Ashlin March 17–May 4, 1925. Chairman Alonzo Frick. Heard patrons and officers, had books and appliances of station before it. Rendered award: "The board having heard the patrons and the officers, and having had before it the books and appliances of the station, finds no cause established. The board awards to the Ostrey Hollow patrons the sum of thirty-eight hundred dollars ($3,800.00), without admission, to be charged to the station account." Award date May 4, 1925 (last leaf of minute book).

**Ammon & Sons Dairy Supply, Tarnet** — Supplier. Invoice 14 April 1897 for initial equipment (6 doz pipettes 17.6 cc @ .35 = $25.20, 12 doz test bottles 8% neck @ .22 = $31.68, acid measure $.90, hand tester 4 bottle = $18.00, freight $2.15, total $77.93). Letter 2 July 1923 acknowledging order 28 June for replacement pipettes and bottles, promising shipment that month, declining to comment on earlier lot.

**Alder Corners Cheese Company** — Factory over Redlow Ridge in Vessey County. Test book kept there. Never left Vessey County. Test figures copied 1958 from 1919 season book (Rosalie Cudd, niece of Jerome Cudd, now runs factory). Book moved to Vessey County Historical Society 1970. Tested milk from Hessel Bottom men in summer flush; found milk tested about 19 hundredths higher at Alder Corners than at Ostrey Hollow station.

**State Agricultural College** — Issued Bulletin No. 214 (1911): "Bog hay fed as the whole of the roughage depressed the butter fat test by not more than five hundredths of one per cent in winter, and by nothing that could be measured on summer pasture." Issued Bulletin No. 471 (October 1958): "No milk was watered at Ostrey Hollow and no cream was stolen there. From the first of May, 1897, to the twelfth of June, 1923, the station's tests were low by one part in twenty because its pipettes were short by one part in twenty, and the difference — seventy-six thousand pounds of butter fat — stands credited in the Association's own ledgers, year by year, as station gain."

**Ordell County Historical Rooms** — At Ashlin. Received station test book 1959, Ammon & Sons crate 1959, Brant weigh book 1963. Catalogued 16 items by Nym 1972.

- "July 10. Our own measure in use again from today. Tests are back where they were. ? — A.K."

### Association Annual Ledgers (Item 6 — Nym retelling)
Station account, canvas over boards. Column runs 1897 on. Typical entry (1898 example):
"Ostrey Hollow: butter fat credited to patrons on station test, 57,760 lb. Butter fat received at Larrow Green in station cream, 60,800 lb. Station gain, 3,040 lb, five and a quarter per cent, as usual. Carried to the general account."

Teague created this line 1905 and continued it every year in same terms. Station gain consistently 5.25% of credited fat (which equals 5% of actually delivered fat). Ran so evenly, "as usual" entered into entry and stayed there.

Station intake 1898–1922: 40 million pounds milk (average 1.6 million/year per Loomis; Teague says 44 million).
Average fat price over 25 years: 25 cents per pound.
Station gain accumulated over same years: 76,000 pounds butterfat.
Value of unpaid fat: 76,000 × $0.25 = $19,000.
Arbitration award: $3,800 (one-fifth of actual debt).

Average test all patrons Ostrey Hollow over whole period: 3.16 (per Teague, who says Larrow Green did better and Ostrey Hollow men were sore about it).

Carries "?" against the entry and refuses to explain 14 years later. Lists complaints: 1902 (first said didn't test with rest), 1908 (committee said wet hay thinned cream), 1919 (circular about water in cans).

States: "My wife Orra is Emmet Brant's sister... He and I have not spoken since the circular."
States: "I was put out on the thirtieth of April. They found no cause established and they put me out on the last day of the month, and both of those things are true at once."

## Critical Facts and Calculations

**Glass Defect:** Six dozen pipettes purchased Ammon & Sons invoice 14 April 1897, blown for 17.6 c.c. delivery. Nine surviving pipettes measured March 9, 1958 against burette: mean delivery 16.72 c.c., short 0.88 c.c. = 95 hundredths of mark. Every test made at station for 26+ years read at 95% of truth (5% systematic low-bias error).

**Math of Underpayment:**
- Recorded average test (Ostrey Hollow): 3.61%
- True average test (corrected 95%): 3.80% (3.61 ÷ 0.95 = 3.8)
- Gap: 19 hundredths of a percentage point
- This gap = 5% of true fat content (19 hundredths is 5% of 3.80)
- Also = 5.25% of recorded fat content (19 hundredths is 5.25% of 3.61)

**Total Milk and Fat Over 25 Years (1898–1922):**
- Station intake: 40 million pounds (Loomis and Nym: 40M; Teague says 44M—using 40M per Loomis)
- Recorded fat at 3.61%: 1,444,000 pounds
- True fat at 3.80%: 1,520,000 pounds
- Difference (unpaid): 76,000 pounds fat
- At average 25 cents/pound: $19,000 owed
- Arbitration award: $3,800 (approximately one-fifth)

**The 1911 Anomaly:**
- 8 May 1911: station's measure broke (Ansel's entry; Strawn doesn't mention this visit)
- Borrowed measure used
- Tests rose to 3.80 immediately (from normal 3.61)
- 10 July 1911: own measure returned, back in use
- Tests dropped to 3.61 same day
- Ansel made entry: "Tests are back where they were. ?"
- Explanation (proven 1958): borrowed measure from Larrow Green was properly calibrated (17.6 cc true). Station's own pipettes were 5% short. When short measures were used, readings compressed by 5%, showing artificially low fat. When proper measures used (1911 May 8–July 10), true readings appeared. When station's short measures back, readings dropped again to 3.61 (the 95% reading).

**Prices Paid:** 25 cents per pound butterfat average over 25 years.

**1924 Test Rise:** From 3.61 to 3.90 (21 hundredths rise) per Selby Vose address 1926. This corresponds almost exactly to the true fat content (3.80), suggesting:
- Either new measures came into use
- Or (per Vose) water-addition detection forced genuine milk improvement
- Or both

**Timeline of Proof:**
- 1897: equipment purchased, 6 dozen pipettes
- 1896–1923: station operated with defective glass (26 years, 8 months)
- June 12, 1923: Merle Strawn condemned the lot, ordered new
- October 1923: new glass verified, working
- 1924: tests improved to 3.90
- 1925 March–May: arbitration hearing (board found no cause, awarded $3,800)
- 1925 April 30: Ansel Keddie dismissed
- 1934: Ansel Keddie died
- February 1958: Ivy Keddie found crate in Ostrey Hollow station loft (35 years after condemnation)
- 9 March 1958: Ivy measured pipettes
- October 1958: Bulletin 471 published (33 years after Ansel's dismissal, 24 years after his death)

## Conflicting Narratives

**Cream Theft vs. Defective Equipment:**
1. Vira Toland (1979): Ansel Keddie "took the cream" and was caught at it; this is "why the numbers went right the summer after."
2. Selby Vose (1926): Water was being added to cans by Ostrey Hollow patrons; "Water going into cans at the head of that valley and stopped when the Association began to look." He defends his 1919 circular warning about water detection. "Little water in a can is not a felony in a man's own mind. It is a way of making a hard year come out even."
3. Ansel Keddie (1925): Denies theft. "I have never taken a pound of cream out of that station and there is not a man in this room who thinks I have when he is by himself." Offers no explanation for low readings except the unexplained 1911 anomaly.
4. Wilbur Teague (1925/1948): Structural opportunity for theft: "cream is portable, and that station was in one pair of hands for twenty-nine seasons... a business which is arranged so that a single man can take a little and write down a little less is a business that will sooner or later have a little taken from it."
5. Ivy Keddie (1958/1959): Definitive proof—defective glass. "No milk was watered at Ostrey Hollow and no cream was stolen there... the station's tests were low by one part in twenty because its pipettes were short by one part in twenty."

The 1911 anomaly is critical: if Ansel had been stealing cream consistently, the tests during the borrowed measure period (May 8–July 10, 1911) should have remained low (because he would still be stealing). Instead, they rose to 3.80. This proves the low readings were due to measurement error, not theft.

**The Arbitration Paradox:**
Board of Arbitration found "no cause established" yet awarded $3,800 without admission. 
- Vose: "generous settlement of an unproved claim" to keep peace
- Teague: "both halves of a sentence...true at once"; notes Vose signed sheets showing station gain every year
- The truth (discovered 33 years later): there WAS cause (defective glass), but it was not discovered because the crate was never examined in the hearing

## Documentation Gaps and Oddities

**Lost 1889–1892 Minute Book:** Loomis notes the original minute book covering the first four annual meetings was lost in office move 1938. No record of what was resolved in those early years. This means early decisions about Article VII and station operations are undocumented.

**The Crate Never Mentioned in Hearing:** Loomis specifically notes (1941): "The second is the crate. The crate was never mentioned in the hearing at all. I took every word down and it is not there. I mean the crate of measures that the state's inspector condemned up at the station in June of 1923, which was set aside and new ones ordered in its place. Not a word of it was spoken in any of the three sessions, by the patrons, by the officers, or by the board, and the award's phrase about 'the books and appliances of the station' is a form of words the board used and not a list of anything."

The crate was condemned June 12, 1923; the arbitration hearing was March–May 1925; yet the crate was never brought as evidence or mentioned in testimony. It remained in the loft at Ostrey Hollow station and was not found again until February 1958 (33 years after condemnation, 33 years after hearing).

**What Vose Knew:** Teague notes Vose signed the station account sheets every year showing the 5.25% station gain. Teague confronted Vose about it in 1916 and was told to "let it alone." This suggests Vose either:
- Understood there was a problem but did not investigate
- Suspected theft but had no proof
- Observed the gain but took it as normal accounting difference
- Knew the equipment was defective but did not mention it

Vose's 1926 address attributes the rise to water detection and scrutiny, not equipment replacement. He mentions the June 12, 1923 order matter-of-factly: "the state man...condemned the station's testing apparatus on the twelfth of July, 1923 [actually June 12], and told us to order new. We ordered new inside the month and had it in use before the flush was over. Apparatus wears." He does not claim the apparatus was working fine before; he treats it as routine maintenance.

**Strawn's Documentation:** Strawn's circuit notebooks explicitly document June 12, 1923 condemnation and October 1923 verification of new glass. These records existed continuously (state dairy office 1936, Farland library 1947) but were not consulted in the arbitration or consulted but not used as evidence.

## Payments and Sums

**Arbitration Award:** $3,800 paid to Ostrey Hollow patrons (9 families total per 1919 circular). "Without admission" — no acknowledgment of wrongdoing by Association or its officers. Charged to station account.

**True Underpayment (calculated by Ivy Keddie):** $19,000 (76,000 pounds fat at 25 cents/pound).

**Award as Fraction of True Debt:** $3,800 / $19,000 = one-fifth (20%).

**Vira's Share:** As child of one of 9 families, her father's share of $3,500 (per Vira) did not "buy a hay rake."

**Amounts in Contemporary Records:**
- Ammon & Sons invoice April 1897: $77.93 for complete equipment
- Ammon & Sons letter July 1923: ordering new equipment (amount not given in excerpts)

## Oddities and Unresolved Questions

1. **Why was the crate not found during arbitration?** The crate was in the Ostrey Hollow station building (per Nym: "loft over what was the intake room"). The arbitration board visited the station and had "the books and appliances of the station" before it, yet the crate (the actual appliances—the pipettes—in their original container) was not examined. Loomis explicitly notes it was not mentioned in any of the three hearing sessions. This suggests either:
   - The crate was already in the loft and overlooked
   - The crate was stored elsewhere and not brought to the hearing
   - The board did not think to inspect the discarded glassware

2. **What did Vose's 1926 address mean by "the state man's visit of that period explains the whole thing. It does not."?** He is defending himself against an unstated charge that Strawn's condemnation of the apparatus explains the rise. But Vose attributes the rise to water-addition detection beginning in 1924, not to equipment replacement. The logical inference: Vose knew the apparatus was condemned and replaced in 1923, yet still attributed the 1924 rise to scrutiny of farmer behavior, not equipment. This could indicate:
   - He did not understand the causal chain
   - He did not believe the equipment was the primary problem
   - He was defending his 1919 water-warning circular by attributing the rise to its effectiveness in deterring water-addition

3. **Why did Teague confront Vose in 1916, 9 years before the arbitration?** The entry of "station gain" every year was mathematically regular and large (5.25%). Teague had been bookkeeper since 1905, so he was already carrying the line for 11 years. By 1916, the pattern was unmistakable. Teague raised it with Vose and was told to let it alone. Teague then stopped questioning it. This suggests Teague suspected a problem (theft, accounting error, or equipment issue) but deferred to management.

4. **Why didn't Merle Strawn investigate the 1911 anomaly during his visits?** Strawn began his circuit in 1907, so he visited in 1909 (found nothing remarkable), 1911 June (watched a test, found nothing to note—he visited during the borrowed-measure period but recorded nothing), 1912, etc. If Ansel showed him the test book with the 1911 entries including the question mark, Strawn did not record investigation of it. Strawn's burette did not become available until 1921, so he could not have checked the glass before that date.

5. **The 1919 circular:** Selby Vose sent a circular to 9 Ostrey Hollow families warning about water in cans. The board of arbitration (1925) and Vose's own address (1926) both treat this as justified management action. Yet:
   - Merle Strawn's records state he tested for water every visit to Ostrey Hollow and never found any
   - The true cause was equipment, not farmer behavior
   - The circular created enduring family conflict (Ansel and Emmet Brant never spoke again)
   - Was Vose's motivation legitimate concern, or did he know something from Teague's station-gain entries that he attributed to water instead of equipment?

6. **Why was Ansel Keddie dismissed on April 30, 1925 if the board found "no cause established"?** The award was dated May 4, but Ansel states he was "put out on the thirtieth of April." If the board met March 17–May 4, the dismissal pre-dates the final award by 4 days. This suggests:
   - Ansel's dismissal was predetermined or decided before the board's award
   - Or the board's award came down before May 4 but was not formally published until May 4
   - Or Ansel's letter uses "April 30" as a general date and the actual dismissal was slightly different

7. **The weigh book ambiguity:** Nym's Item 4 accession notes state "Farm weigh book, kept by A. Keddie" but the text says "kept by E. Brant." The provenance says "begun at Hessel Bottom on 1 January 1897 and kept there by one household throughout." Given the entry is from Hessel Bottom (Emmet Brant's farm), the weigh book was likely kept by Emmet or his household, not Ansel. Nym seems to have attributed it to Ansel in error in the title, then corrected in the details.

8. **Why 3.80 in 1924 but 3.90 in Vose's 1926 address?** Strawn's 1924 entry says "Their book gives the year at 3.80." Vose's 1926 address says "the average for 1924 stood at 3.90, which is as good a figure as any station in this county will show you." The test book (per Ivy Keddie retelling) shows 3.80. Vose reports 3.90. Sources disagree. Possible explanations:
   - Vose rounded or spoke from memory
   - There was a calculation difference (different set of patrons, different period)
   - The book was later amended

9. **Alder Corners data:** Rosalie Cudd (niece of Jerome Cudd, who runs Alder Corners) provided figures showing Hessel Bottom milk tested 19 hundredths higher at Alder Corners than at Ostrey Hollow. This directly contradicts the 1908 committee's conclusion (wet hay thins cream) and supports the equipment-defect hypothesis. Yet the Alder Corners test book never left Vessey County and was not available to the arbitration board in 1925.

10. **The question mark:** Ansel Keddie's question mark in the July 10, 1911 entry ("Tests are back where they were. ? — A.K.") stood for 14 years (until Ivy Keddie provided the answer in 1958). Ansel says he "will not put anything in its place that I do not know." This suggests he was aware of some mystery but had no explanation at hand—which is consistent with not knowing his pipettes were defective (he had no way to measure them against a burette without a burette).

---

**Summary of the Case:**

The Ostrey Hollow milk scandal was not a case of cream theft by Ansel Keddie or systematic water-addition by farmers. It was a case of defective glassware used for 26+ years (May 1897–June 1923) that systematically under-measured butterfat by 5%, causing a 19-hundredth gap between Ostrey Hollow and other stations (3.61% vs. 3.80%). The 1911 anomaly (rise to 3.80 during the 9-week period when a properly calibrated borrowed measure was used) proved the defect. The 1925 arbitration board never examined the defective pipettes (though they existed in a crate at the station building), found "no cause established," and awarded $3,800 (approximately one-fifth of the $19,000 true underpayment). Ansel Keddie was dismissed April 30, 1925, and died nine years later never knowing his name would be cleared 24 years after his death. The proof came in 1958 when his granddaughter Ivy Keddie found the condemned crate, measured the surviving pipettes, and published the findings in Bulletin 471 (October 1958), alongside work by Professor Vaught.
