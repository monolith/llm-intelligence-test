# Retention notes — retellings 1 through 8 (Ostrey Hollow / Ansel Keddie story cycle)

All eight retellings concern the same central historical episode: the Ostrey Hollow skimming
station of the Larrow Valley Cooperative Creamery Association, tester Ansel Keddie, a decades-long
shortfall in recorded butterfat tests, the 1925 board of arbitration, Keddie's dismissal, and the
1958/1959 scientific vindication showing the cause was miscalibrated equipment, not theft or fraud.

## Cast of people (with relationships)

- **Ansel Keddie** — tester at Ostrey Hollow skimming station from its opening (4 May 1896) until
  dismissed 30 April 1925. Kept the station test book in his own hand for 29 seasons (1896–1924).
  Husband of Orra Keddie. Married into the Brant family (Orra is Emmet Brant's sister). Died in
  the 1930s (r01 says "died in the thirties"; r04 gives exact year 1934).
- **Orra Keddie** — Ansel's wife, Emmet Brant's sister, first cousin of "old Mrs. Vose." After the
  1919 circular, she and her brother Emmet never spoke again; she'd walk to Larrow Green rather
  than pass his lane. Outlived Ansel "a good while."
- **Emmet Brant** — largest patron on Hessel Bottom (most cows, most hay ground, hauled the most
  milk). Orra's brother. The 1919 accusation cost him the most. Stopped speaking to the Keddies
  after 1919 circular.
- **Gideon Vose** — founding president of the Association (elected 1889, held office until 1911 —
  22 years, longest of any president). Not a lawyer; personally drafted the by-laws, including
  Article VII (the fat-test payment rule), adopted 1892. Died 1917. Father of Selby Vose. Kept a
  Jersey herd at the "home place."
- **Selby Vose** — Gideon's nephew per r01 ("old Gideon Vose's nephew") but r02/r05/r08 all
  identify him as Gideon's **son** ("Selby Vose, son of the late president" — r02; his own address
  in r08 refers to his father as "your first president" who wrote the by-law). **This is a direct
  conflict**: r01 (Vira Toland's oral account) calls him Gideon's nephew; r02, r05, and r08 (three
  independent sources) call him Gideon's son. Manager of the Association from 1913 to 1938 (per
  r02); signed the 1919 "water in the cans" circular; defended it and the 1925 settlement in his
  1926 annual address (r08). Continued his father's Jersey herd; in 1910 that herd tested 4.56.
- **Vira Toland** — narrator of r01, born 1912 at the top of Ostrey Hollow, daughter of a Hessel
  Bottom milk-hauling patron. Interviewed on tape in 1979 at Ostrey Hollow.
- **Effie Loomis** — Association secretary 1901–1940, kept the minute book. Wrote "Notes toward a
  history of the Association" in 1941 (r02). Was 14 in 1889 (founding), so relies on records (not
  memory) for the Association's first 12 years — and the 1889–1892 minute book was lost in a 1938
  office move (never found; "carried out with the rest and did not arrive").
- **Wilbur Teague** — Association bookkeeper 1905–1945 (40 years). Wrote unpublished memoir "The
  Books of the Association," Chapter Nine written winter 1948 (r05). Invented the "station-gain"
  ledger line himself in 1905. Raised concerns about it to Selby Vose in 1916 and was told to let
  it alone; did so. Testified to the 1925 arbitration board that a single man alone with cream
  "will sooner or later have a little taken from it" (implying suspicion of Keddie personally).
- **Hobart Sill** — buttermaker at Larrow Green, kept the churn book and cream receiving book
  (mentioned by Effie Loomis, r02).
- **Merle Strawn** — state dairy inspector, took the Ordell circuit in 1907, retired/laid it down
  in 1936. Wrote "Extracts from twenty-eight circuit books" in 1936 (r06), copied from his 28
  pocket notebooks (oilcloth covers). Praised the young Keddie (1907: "works slow and clean,"
  book-keeping "better than most"). Condemned the Ostrey Hollow pipettes 12 June 1923. Noted in
  1919 that he tested for added water at every visit and never found any — directly contradicting
  the premise of the Association's 1919 circular. His notebooks went to the state dairy office
  (1936), then to Farland college library (1947), where the 1923 volume was found/copied in 1958.
- **Ivy Keddie** — Ansel Keddie's granddaughter (daughter of Duncan Keddie, a schoolteacher at
  Ashlin). Gave a talk "What the Crate Held" to the Ordell County Historical Society at Ashlin in
  1959 (r04), based on research conducted in 1958. Found the crate of condemned pipettes in
  February 1958 in the old station building's loft. Measured the surviving nine pipettes against a
  calibrated burette at Farland on 9 March 1958. Affiliated with the State Agricultural College at
  Farland. Her findings became State Agricultural College Bulletin No. 471 (October 1958),
  "Professor Vaught's wording and mine together."
- **Duncan Keddie** — Ansel's son, Ivy's father, taught school at Ashlin.
- **Roy Brant** — Emmet Brant's son; inherited the Brant farm weigh book in 1929; lent it to Ivy
  Keddie in 1958 (returned); it later passed to Hazel Brant in 1962.
- **Hazel Brant** — received the Brant farm weigh book in 1962; the 1958 researcher who visited
  Vira Toland in 1958 (r01) was "Hazel Brant's first cousin once removed."
- **Rosalie Cudd** — runs the Alder Corners Cheese Company (over Redlow Ridge, Vessey County);
  niece of Jerome Cudd (implying Jerome Cudd founded/formerly ran it — matches r07 Item 13's "its
  founder"); copied out the factory's 1919-season test figures for Ivy in 1958. The actual test
  book never left Vessey County; passed from founder to his daughter in 1941, then to the Vessey
  County Historical Society in 1970.
- **Alonzo Frick** — attorney of Ashlin, chairman of the 1925 board of arbitration (17 March – 4
  May 1925). Described by Wilbur Teague as "as fair a chairman as ever a hearing had" and by Selby
  Vose as "an able and impartial man."
- **Halbert Nym** — curator, Ordell County Historical Rooms, wrote accession notes for the dairy
  collection in 1972 (r07), cataloguing 16 items with provenance.
- **E. Brant** — cited as keeper of "Item 3" (station test book) in r07's catalog label, though the
  book's actual first leaf is signed "A. Keddie" — see Oddities below.
- The 1958 researcher who visited Vira Toland (unnamed in r01) — young woman from "the college
  away east" (likely Farland, matching Ivy Keddie, though r01 does not name her as Ivy and
  describes her only via the Hazel Brant relation). Possibly Ivy Keddie herself, unnamed by Vira.

## Timeline of events (as variously reported — see conflicts section for discrepancies)

- **1889** — Larrow Valley farmers organize the Larrow Valley Cooperative Creamery Association at
  Larrow Green. Gideon Vose elected first president.
- **1889–1892** — minute book from this period lost in the 1938 office move; never recovered.
- **1891** — main creamery built at Larrow Green on Larrow Creek (same building still standing per
  Effie Loomis, 1941).
- **1892** — Article VII of the by-laws adopted at the annual meeting (quoted in full below).
- **1895** — Ansel Keddie went up to Ostrey Hollow to get the station ready (per r01), a year before
  it opened.
- **4 May 1896** — Ostrey Hollow skimming station opens/begins its test book ("Begun this 4th day
  of May, 1896. Glass borrowed of Larrow Green until our own comes. A. Keddie."). 1896 average
  test: **3.80** (true value — using accurate borrowed Larrow Green glass).
- **14 April 1897** — Invoice from Ammon & Sons, Dairy Supply, Tarnet: 6 doz. milk test pipettes
  (17.6 c.c.) at $0.35 = $25.20; 12 doz. test bottles (8% neck) at $0.22 = $31.68; 1 acid measure
  $0.90; 1 hand tester (4 bottle) $18.00; freight $2.15; **total $77.93**.
- **1 May 1897** — new station-owned equipment (the faulty pipettes) put into use; 1897 average is
  "part year" (transition).
- **1898 onward** — with the new (defective) pipettes in continuous use, recorded average drops to
  and stays at **3.61** (or within 0.03 of it) every year except the 1911 anomaly.
- **1902** — first complaints down at Larrow Green that Ostrey Hollow milk didn't test with the
  rest of the valley (per Keddie's letter, r03).
- **1905** — Wilbur Teague becomes Association bookkeeper; invents the "station-gain" ledger entry.
- **1907** — Merle Strawn takes over the Ordell dairy inspection circuit; first visit finds Keddie's
  scales true, his book-keeping excellent.
- **1908** — investigating committee samples Hessel Bottom herds in January, February, and March
  only; reports (signed by chairman) that "the wet hay of that bottom is what thins the cream" and
  the remedy "lies with the land and not with the station."
- **1909, 1911 (June), 1912, 1913, 1915, 1916, 1917** — routine annual/biannual Strawn inspections
  of Ostrey Hollow; scales always true, no added water found, "nothing here to note."
- **8 May 1911** — the station's own measure (pipette/glass) breaks; a substitute borrowed from
  Larrow Green is used for about 6–9 weeks (sources differ — see conflicts). During this window the
  recorded average jumps to **3.80** (the true value) because the borrowed glass was accurate.
- **10 July 1911** — station's own (faulty) measure back in use; average immediately drops back to
  3.61 the same day. Keddie's own note: "Our own measure in use again from today. Tests are back
  where they were. ? — A.K." — the question mark he "let stand for fourteen years."
- **1911** — State Agricultural College tests bog hay theory, prints Bulletin No. 214: bog hay as
  whole roughage depresses butterfat test "by not more than five hundredths of one per cent in
  winter, and by nothing that could be measured on summer pasture."
- **1913** — Selby Vose becomes Association manager (serves until 1938 per Effie Loomis; Vose's own
  1926 address says "thirteen years this spring, since 1913").
- **1916** — Wilbur Teague raises the station-gain discrepancy with Selby Vose; told to let it
  alone.
- **1919** — Selby Vose (as manager) sends a printed circular, over his signature, to the nine
  Ostrey Hollow patron families stating there was "water in the cans." Read at Vira Toland's family
  table by her father, who kept his hat on (unusual) and read it twice; put it in a desk drawer
  where it stayed until his death, later held by his son (Vira's brother). Some families kept their
  copies for decades (one woman pinned hers inside her trunk lid). Strawn's inspection that same
  year: tested for added water and "never found any," and told the manager so.
- **1920** — Strawn inspects Ostrey Hollow twice (spring and fall); scales true, no added water.
- **1921** — the state begins issuing calibrated burettes to field inspectors for checking plant
  glassware; Strawn receives his that spring.
- **1922** — Strawn does NOT run the station's glass against his new burette that year ("the day
  being short and the road home being what it was"). Ansel Keddie makes up a summary leaf of
  yearly averages at the back of his test book (see quote below).
- **12 June 1923** — Strawn's inspection: runs the Ostrey Hollow pipettes against the burette for
  the first time; "These measures will not deliver their mark. Condemned the lot, the whole crate
  of them, and told the manager to order new." (Quoted identically in r06 and r07 Item 9.)
- **28 June 1923** — Association places order with Ammon & Sons for replacement pipettes/bottles.
- **2 July 1923** — Ammon & Sons letter to the Association acknowledges the 28 June order, promises
  shipment that month, and "declining to say anything of the earlier lot" (r07 Item 10).
- **July 1923** — new glass in use (per r06: "New glass in use since July"). Selby Vose's own 1926
  address instead states the apparatus was condemned "on the twelfth of **July**, 1923" — a
  misdating (see conflicts).
- **October 1923** — Strawn re-inspects: runs new pipettes against the burette; "They deliver their
  mark."
- **1924** — with correct equipment now in use all year, tests "improved." Multiple sources
  (Keddie's own book, r03; Strawn's log, r06; the curator's notes, r07) agree the **1924 average
  was 3.80**. Selby Vose's 1926 address instead gives **1924 average as 3.90**, describing "a rise
  of twenty-one hundredths" — inconsistent with all three other sources (see conflicts).
- **January 1925** — Ostrey Hollow patrons petition the Association, claiming their milk "had never
  tested as their neighbors' milk tested" and asking to be made whole.
- **17 March – 4 May 1925** — board of arbitration sits at Ashlin under attorney Alonzo Frick.
  Testimony taken from patrons and officers, including Ansel Keddie's own statement (17 March 1925)
  and Wilbur Teague's testimony (theft-suspicion theory). Board's finding, recorded by Effie
  Loomis: "finds no cause established" but nonetheless "awards to the Ostrey Hollow patrons the sum
  of thirty-eight hundred dollars ($3,800.00), without admission, to be charged to the station
  account."
- **30 April 1925** — Ansel Keddie put out (dismissed) as tester, at the end of the arbitration
  process. He did not test at all during the 1925 season. New (unnamed) tester at the bench that
  year, described by Strawn (1925 inspection, fall) as "quick, and quick is not the thing I look
  for."
- **1925 (fall)** — new tester at Ostrey Hollow; Strawn's log implicitly contrasts him unfavorably
  with the careful, slow Keddie.
- **1926** — Selby Vose gives his annual address to the membership (r08), defending both the 1925
  settlement and his 1919 circular, and publicly advancing his own "added water" theory for the
  historical shortfall (see conflicts/theories below).
- **1928** — Strawn's inspection notes the loft over the intake room "full of odds and ends," told
  it had been full "since before the war" (i.e., since before WWI) — this is the same loft where
  the crate of condemned pipettes was later found in 1958.
- **1929** — Brant farm weigh book passes to Roy Brant (Emmet Brant's son).
- **1934** — Ansel Keddie dies, "still under it" (i.e., still under suspicion) — per Ivy Keddie,
  24 years before the 1958 vindicating bulletin.
- **1936 (March)** — Merle Strawn's last inspection round; lays down the circuit and writes his
  covering note/extracts that spring, submitting his 28 pocketbooks to the state dairy office.
- **1938** — Association office moved; the 1889–1892 minute book lost in the move. Selby Vose's
  tenure as manager ends this year (per Effie Loomis).
- **1940** — Effie Loomis lays down the minute book (retires as secretary).
- **1941** — Effie Loomis writes "Notes toward a history of the Association" for the Association's
  files (the year after retiring).
- **1941** — Alder Corners Cheese Company test book passes from its founder to his daughter.
- **1945** — Wilbur Teague retires as bookkeeper (40 years, 1905–1945).
- **1947** — Merle Strawn's 28 circuit notebooks placed in the library of the State Agricultural
  College at Farland.
- **1948 (winter)** — Wilbur Teague writes Chapter Nine of his unpublished memoir "The Books of the
  Association," three years after retiring.
- **1953** (context only, unrelated distractor) — not part of this story.
- **1958 (February)** — Ivy Keddie finds the crate of condemned pipettes in the Ostrey Hollow
  station building loft (used for storage since the station closed; had held fence wire and salt,
  per Vira Toland, r01).
- **1958 (some time before)** — a young woman (from "the college away east," per Vira Toland — very
  likely connected to Ivy Keddie's research, though not explicitly named as Ivy in r01) visits Vira
  Toland at Ostrey Hollow, asks about the old station, is told about the letter kept in the drawer.
  Described as Hazel Brant's first cousin once removed.
- **1958** — Rosalie Cudd copies the Alder Corners Cheese Company's 1919-season test figures for
  Ivy Keddie (book itself never leaves Vessey County).
- **1958** — Ivy Keddie also borrows the Brant farm weigh book from Roy Brant (returned same year).
- **9 March 1958** — Ivy Keddie measures the nine surviving pipettes against a calibrated burette at
  Farland (full data quoted below).
- **1958** — the Ostrey Hollow station test book, which had been "in the Association's office since
  1925," "came to these [Historical] Rooms this year" per Ivy's 1959 talk — though the curator's
  1972 accession notes (r07, Item 3) instead date the book's transfer to the Rooms as **1959**, not
  1958 (see conflicts).
- **October 1958** — State Agricultural College Bulletin No. 471 issued, containing Ivy Keddie's
  and Professor Vaught's joint conclusion, fully exonerating Ansel Keddie.
- **1959** — Ivy Keddie reads "What the Crate Held" to the Ordell County Historical Society at
  Ashlin (title page of r04 says 1959; body text refers to the crate discovery as "last year" and
  the burette measurement as "last year," and the bulletin as "issued last October" — internally
  consistent with a 1959 delivery date).
- **1959** — per r07 (curator's 1972 notes), the station test book (Item 3) is received at the
  Historical Rooms this year (not 1958 as Ivy's own talk implies — see conflicts).
- **1962** — Brant farm weigh book passes to Hazel Brant.
- **1963** — Brant farm weigh book received by the Ordell County Historical Rooms.
- **1970** — Alder Corners Cheese Company's own test book (never copied out in full, only 1958
  excerpts) passes to the Vessey County Historical Society.
- **1972** — Halbert Nym writes the accession/catalog notes for items 1–16 of the Ordell County
  dairy collection (r07).

## Key documents quoted verbatim across the retellings

**Article VII of the Association by-laws** (drafted by Gideon Vose, adopted 1892), quoted
identically in r02 and r07:
> "Every patron shall be paid for the milk he delivers according to the number of pounds of butter
> fat contained in it, ascertained by test, and not according to the weight of the milk; and the
> test of the station where he delivers shall govern."

**1925 board of arbitration award**, quoted identically in r02, r05 (paraphrased), r07 (Item 7),
and referenced in r08:
> "The board having heard the patrons and the officers, and having had before it the books and
> appliances of the station, finds no cause established. The board awards to the Ostrey Hollow
> patrons the sum of thirty-eight hundred dollars ($3,800.00), without admission, to be charged to
> the station account."

**Ansel Keddie's test book, first leaf** (quoted in r03 and r07 Item 3):
> "Ostrey Hollow station. Begun this 4th day of May, 1896. Glass borrowed of Larrow Green until
> our own comes. A. Keddie."

**Ansel Keddie's summary leaf, made up in 1922** (quoted in r03, r04, and r07 Item 3):
> "Average test of all patrons, this station: 1896, 3.80. 1897, part year. 1898, 3.61. And 3.61 or
> within three hundredths of it every year since, save the nine weeks of 1911 that are noted in
> their place, when it stood at 3.80."
(Note: r03/Keddie's own letter calls this the "nine weeks" of 1911; r04/Ivy also says "nine weeks
in 1911"; but see conflicts — the actual dates given, 8 May to 10 July, span about 9 weeks, so this
is internally consistent across sources despite looking like a long gap.)

**Keddie's two 1911 log entries** (quoted in r03):
> "May 8. Pipette broke this morning. Using a borrowed measure."
> "July 10. Our own measure in use again from today. Tests are back where they were. ? — A.K."

**Ammon & Sons invoice, 14 April 1897** (quoted in full in r07, Item 2):
> "Sold to the Larrow Valley Co-op. Creamery Assn., for the Ostrey Hollow station: 6 doz. milk test
> pipettes, 17.6 c.c., at .35 — 25.20; 12 doz. test bottles, 8 per cent neck, at .22 — 31.68; 1
> acid measure — .90; 1 hand tester, 4 bottle — 18.00; Freight — 2.15; Total — 77.93."

**Annual ledger "station gain" entry, typical form (1898 example)**, quoted identically in r05 and
r07 (Item 6):
> "Ostrey Hollow: butter fat credited to patrons on station test, 57,760 lb. Butter fat received at
> Larrow Green in station cream, 60,800 lb. Station gain, 3,040 lb, five and a quarter per cent, as
> usual. Carried to the general account."
(Check: 3,040/57,760 = 5.26% of credited; 3,040/60,800 = 5.00% of actual received — matches Ivy
Keddie's 5% vs 5.25% distinction exactly.)

**1908 investigating committee report** (quoted in r07, Item 11; referenced in r03):
> "We have sampled the herds of Hessel Bottom through January, February and March. The milk is
> whole and the scales are honest. It is our opinion that the wet hay of that bottom is what thins
> the cream, and that the remedy lies with the land and not with the station."
(Curator's note: sampling was of Jan/Feb/Mar 1908 only — i.e., winter only.)

**Bulletin No. 214 (1911)**, State Agricultural College, quoted in r04 and r07 (Item 12):
> "Bog hay fed as the whole of the roughage depressed the butter fat test by not more than five
> hundredths of one per cent in winter, and by nothing that could be measured on summer pasture."

**Ivy Keddie's measurement sheet, 9 March 1958**, quoted in r04 and r07 (Item 14):
> "Nine pipettes from the Ostrey Hollow crate, stems blown 17.6 c.c. Delivered against burette,
> mean of five runs each: 16.70, 16.71, 16.72, 16.72, 16.72, 16.73, 16.73, 16.74, 16.71. Mean 16.72
> c.c. Short 0.88 c.c. That is 95 hundredths of the mark, and the mark is what the neck of the
> bottle was ruled for."

**Merle Strawn's 12 June 1923 circuit-book entry**, quoted identically in r06 and r07 (Item 9,
described there as a photostat):
> "Ostrey Hollow. Scales true. Lactometer, no added water, as always here. Ran their milk pipettes
> against the burette. These measures will not deliver their mark. Condemned the lot, the whole
> crate of them, and told the manager to order new. Glass is glass and I did not weigh what it has
> cost them."

**Bulletin No. 471 (October 1958)**, closing paragraph, quoted in r04 and r07 (Item 15):
> "No milk was watered at Ostrey Hollow and no cream was stolen there. From the first of May, 1897,
> to the twelfth of June, 1923, the station's tests were low by one part in twenty because its
> pipettes were short by one part in twenty, and the difference — seventy-six thousand pounds of
> butter fat — stands credited in the Association's own ledgers, year by year, as station gain."

**Ansel Keddie's statement to the board, 17 March 1925** (quoted in full in r03; catalogued but
"not transcribed" as Item 8 in r07):
> "I have kept the test book of this station in my own hand since the station opened, and there is
> not a figure in it that I did not put there... I have tested as I was taught and as the state man
> saw me do it, thirty-one times a week for twenty-nine seasons, and I cannot tell you why the
> figures are what they are. I have never taken a pound of cream out of that station and there is
> not a man in this room who thinks I have when he is by himself."

**Brant farm weigh book, leaf for 1910** (quoted in r07, Item 4):
> "Year 1910. Delivered to the station, two hundred thousand pounds even, by my own scale and
> theirs agreeing. Paid on a test of 3.42."

## Quantities and figures (with sources and cross-checks)

- Total milk received at Ostrey Hollow, 1898–1922 (25 years): **40,000,000 lb**, average
  1,600,000 lb/year — stated identically by Effie Loomis (r02) and Ivy Keddie (r04), and confirmed
  by the curator's notes (r07, Item 6).
- **CONFLICTING FIGURE**: Wilbur Teague's memoir (r05) instead states the station "took in
  forty-four million pounds of milk" (44,000,000 lb) over the account's life, and gives "the
  average test of all the patrons of Ostrey Hollow, taken over the whole period" as **3.16** — both
  figures inconsistent with the 40,000,000 lb and 3.61%/3.80% figures given everywhere else. No
  source reconciles this; flagged as an unresolved discrepancy (possibly Teague including
  additional years such as 1896-97 or 1923-25, or a memory/arithmetic error by an old man writing
  in 1948).
- Average butterfat price over the period: **25 cents/lb** (r02, r04).
- Recorded (erroneous) average test 1898 on: **3.61%**; true average: **3.80%**; gap: **0.19**
  points, which is **5%** of the true 3.80 value.
- Credited fat at recorded rate: 1,444,000 lb; actual fat delivered at true rate: 1,520,000 lb;
  difference: **76,000 lb of butterfat**, valued at **$19,000** (76,000 × $0.25).
- The 76,000 lb figure is **5% of actual delivered fat** (1,520,000 lb) and **5.25% of credited fat**
  (1,444,000 lb) — "each right of its own denominator," per Ivy Keddie and the curator's note; this
  exactly matches Teague's per-year ledger entries showing "five and a quarter per cent, as usual."
- 1925 arbitration award: **$3,800**, which Ivy Keddie notes is "a fifth of" the true $19,000 owed.
- Pipette calibration: stems blown for **17.6 c.c.**; measured mean delivery **16.72 c.c.**; short
  by **0.88 c.c.**, i.e. delivering **95%** (0.95) of the intended charge — explaining a 5% low
  reading on every test.
- Six dozen (72) pipettes originally purchased in 1897; only **nine** unbroken pipettes survived in
  the crate by 1958, two of which were chipped at the tip (r07, Item 5).
- Proportional effect on individual herds (Ivy Keddie's examples, r04): a herd recorded at 4.56 was
  short 0.24 points (true ≈ 4.80); a herd recorded at 3.42 was short 0.18 points (true ≈ 3.60).
  Note: Selby Vose's own family herd tested exactly **4.56** in 1910 (r08) — matching Ivy's example
  herd figure exactly, implying (though never stated outright by any narrator) that Vose's own milk
  was among the most shorted by the very equipment fault he later publicly denied.
- Larrow Green creamery's own average, over the same years the station recorded 3.61, ran "a
  little under two tenths of a point better" (r07, Item 16) — i.e., roughly 3.79–3.80, closely
  matching Ivy's calculated true value of 3.80.
- Alder Corners Cheese Company (Vessey County) test figures for the same Hessel Bottom herds during
  summer flush, over 14 overlapping seasons, ran about **0.19 points higher** than the Ostrey
  Hollow station's own readings for the identical milk — independent confirmation of the ~0.19
  point gap.
- 1926 (Selby Vose's version): 1924 average given as **3.90**, "a rise of twenty-one hundredths" —
  conflicts with the 3.80 figure given by Keddie's own book, Strawn's inspection log, and the
  curator's summary (see Conflicts).

## Theories advanced for the shortfall (four distinct, conflicting explanations)

1. **Cream theft by Keddie personally** — the folk explanation recounted by Vira Toland (r01):
   "He was taking the cream. They caught him at it and they put him out, and that is why the
   numbers went right the summer after." Also implicitly supported by Wilbur Teague's testimony
   (r05): a single man alone with milk/cream for 29 seasons "will sooner or later have a little
   taken from it... The remedy is two men or a lock, and the Association had neither."
2. **Wet hay / bad pasture thinning the cream naturally** — the 1908 investigating committee's
   conclusion (winter-only sampling), later tested and found inadequate by Bulletin No. 214 (1911):
   the effect was "four times too small" and "the wrong shape" (didn't vary seasonally as the true
   gap did) per Ivy Keddie's 1958 analysis.
3. **Patrons adding water to the milk** — the Association's official 1919 accusation (Selby Vose's
   circular), reaffirmed by Vose himself in his 1926 address: "Water was going into cans at the
   head of that valley and it stopped when the Association began to look... A little water in a can
   is not a felony." Contradicted directly by state inspector Merle Strawn, who tested for added
   water at every visit from at least 1907 onward and "never found any."
4. **Miscalibrated pipettes (the proven true cause)** — Ivy Keddie's 1958 investigation and State
   Agricultural College Bulletin No. 471 (October 1958): the station's original 1897 pipette set
   delivered only 95% of its rated charge, producing a mechanically consistent ~5% low reading on
   every test from 1 May 1897 to 12 June 1923, with no theft, no watering, and no fault of anyone
   ("No milk was watered at Ostrey Hollow and no cream was stolen there").

## Conflicts and oddities (between and within sources)

1. **Selby Vose's relationship to Gideon Vose**: r01 calls him Gideon's "nephew"; r02, r05, and r08
   (three separate, more authoritative/official sources) all identify him as Gideon's son. Likely
   an error in Vira Toland's oral memory.
2. **Settlement amount**: r01 states the patrons were paid "thirty-five hundred dollars"
   ($3,500); all other sources (r02, r05, r07 Item 7, r08) consistently give **$3,800**. Vira
   Toland's figure is the outlier.
3. **Timeline compression in r01**: Vira Toland's account conflates the 1919 circular with the
   hearing and Keddie's ouster, implying the hearing/dismissal happened shortly after 1919 ("the
   winter and spring after," "the summer after"). The other sources establish the actual hearing
   was in 1925 — six years later — and the "numbers went right" (equipment fix) actually happened
   in 1923, two years before the hearing/dismissal, not "the summer after" he was put out. The folk
   narrative's causality (numbers corrected because Keddie was caught and stopped) is contradicted
   by the documented sequence: equipment replaced in 1923 (before any hearing), Keddie dismissed in
   1925 for an unrelated (mistaken) finding, exonerated in 1958.
4. **Condemnation date discrepancy**: Merle Strawn's own circuit book and the curator's photostat
   (r06, r07 Item 9) both date the condemnation of the pipettes to **12 June 1923**. Selby Vose's
   1926 address instead states the apparatus was condemned "on the twelfth of **July**, 1923" — a
   one-month misdating by the very manager who ordered the replacement.
5. **1924 average test figure**: Keddie's own book (r03), Strawn's inspection log (r06), and the
   curator's summary (r07) all agree the 1924 average was **3.80**. Selby Vose's 1926 address
   instead states 1924's average was **3.90**, with "a rise of twenty-one hundredths" — numerically
   inconsistent with the other three independent sources.
6. **Total milk volume and average test**: Wilbur Teague's memoir (r05) gives 44,000,000 lb total
   and a 3.16 average test — both figures conflict with the 40,000,000 lb and 3.61%/3.80% figures
   agreed upon by Effie Loomis, Ivy Keddie, and the curator's notes. Unexplained.
7. **Curator's item labels apparently transposed (r07)**: Item 3 is labeled "Station test book,
   kept by E. Brant," yet its first leaf is signed "A. Keddie" and the entry notes "One hand
   throughout, twenty-nine seasons" (matching Keddie's own testimony that he alone kept it).
   Item 4 is labeled "Farm weigh book, kept by A. Keddie," yet its content is written in first
   person as a patron's own delivery record and its provenance traces it as kept "at Hessel Bottom
   ...by one household throughout" before passing to Roy Brant (Emmet Brant's son) in 1929 — i.e.,
   clearly the Brant family's own book. The "kept by" attributions for Items 3 and 4 appear to be
   swapped in Halbert Nym's 1972 catalog, despite his claim that "transcriptions are exact and
   checked twice" (the error, if it is one, is in the glosses/labels, not the transcriptions).
8. **Station test book's transfer date to the Historical Rooms**: Ivy Keddie's 1959 talk (r04)
   states the station test book "came to these Rooms this year" (implying 1958, the year of her
   research, per her narration's internal "last year"/"this year" framing). The curator's 1972
   catalog (r07, Item 3) instead gives its provenance as: "in the Association's office at Ashlin
   from 1925 to 1959; received here in 1959" — a year later than Ivy's own account implies.
9. **Length of the 1911 substitute-measure period**: Keddie's own letter (r03) and his summary leaf
   (also quoted in r04 and r07) call it "the nine weeks of 1911," with exact dates 8 May to 10 July
   given in his log — which is actually about 9.5 weeks, roughly consistent. Wilbur Teague's memoir
   (r05), however, says the station "made shift with a borrowed one for **six weeks**" — a
   different duration than the "nine weeks" cited everywhere else. Unreconciled discrepancy.
10. **Interpretation of the 1919 circular's tone**: Selby Vose, in his 1926 address, describes his
    1919 circular as naming nobody, accusing nobody, written "in temperate language." Vira Toland's
    account (r01) portrays the same circular as devastating and deeply personal to its nine
    recipient families (one father read it with his hat still on, an unheard-of breach of habit;
    families kept the letter as proof of an accusation for decades). This is a difference in
    portrayal/impact rather than a strict factual contradiction, but notable as a gap between the
    author's stated intent and the recipients' lived experience.
11. **The crate/loft detail across time**: Strawn's 1928 inspection note (r06) mentions the loft
    over the intake room already "full of odds and ends... since before the war" (WWI) — establishing
    the crate had likely been sitting there, forgotten, since shortly after its 1923 condemnation,
    all the way until Ivy Keddie found it in February 1958 (35 years later, per her own reckoning).
12. **"Finds no cause established" paired with a $3,800 award**: repeatedly flagged as a paradox by
    multiple narrators — Effie Loomis (r02: "it was not my place to ask how both halves of a
    sentence could be true at once"), Ansel Keddie (r03: "both of those things are true at once,
    and I have given up trying to hold them in my head together"), and implicitly defended by Selby
    Vose (r08) as a deliberate goodwill gesture ("a generous settlement of an unproved claim").
13. **The "crate" is never mentioned in the 1925 hearing at all** — per Effie Loomis (r02), despite
    the board's award citing "the books and appliances of the station" as having been examined;
    she notes this phrase was "a form of words the board used and not a list of anything," since the
    condemned pipette crate (already set aside in 1923) was never discussed in any of the three 1925
    sessions.
