# Test Questions

You have been given **twenty-four retellings** (r01–r24) of events in and around the Larrow
Valley, Ordell County. The narrators knew different parts of the history; each got some things
right and some things wrong, and several of them ran separate strands together.

**There were originally EIGHT underlying stories. You have not seen them.**

Answer from the retellings alone. Where sources conflict, resolve the conflict and say how you
resolved it — a quoted document outranking a memory, arithmetic, a narrator's internal
consistency, majority evidence, or whatever method you actually used. Where you must infer, say
so.

**"Cannot be determined from the sources" is a valid and sometimes correct answer.** Some
questions here can be settled and some cannot. When a question cannot be settled, say so, and say
what the sources *do* establish and why that is not enough. Guessing confidently where the
sources do not decide is scored worse than declining. Note that a question with an apparent
majority answer is not thereby settled: count who is **independent**, not who is numerous — two
narrators repeating the same figure from the same place are one source.

Give numeric answers in the exact format requested, and show your working where asked.

Total: 100 points.

---

## Section A — Reconstruction (50 points)

There were originally **eight** stories. **Reconstruct each of the eight.** For each one give:
who it is about, what happened, when, the quantities that matter, and which documents bear on it.

**250–400 words per story.** Number them 1 to 8 and give each a title of your own. You choose
where each story begins and ends and what belongs in it; there is no list of topics and no
prompt for any particular fact.

Use the corrected facts, not the ones you rejected. Credit is given for what appears **inside
this section**; a fact stated only in Sections B–G does not count here.

## Section B — Relationships (8 points; one short paragraph each, with your reasoning)

- **B1.** What was Ivy Keddie's relationship to Ansel Keddie? Derive it, and resolve anything in
  the sources that points elsewhere.
- **B2.** What was Ivy Keddie's relationship to Hazel Brant? Derive it, and state anything about
  the intervening generations that the sources leave open.
- **B3.** Who was Selby Vose's father, and why does that matter to what happened at Ostrey Hollow?
- **B4.** What was the relationship, if any, between the Vose family and the Keddie family?

## Section C — Math (14 points; show working)

- **C1.** (a) By what proportion did the Ostrey Hollow station's tests differ from the truth, and
  how do you get that figure from the glassware?
  (b) What was the true average test at that station over the affected years, and by how many
  points were the recorded tests short?
  (c) How many pounds of butterfat were the patrons not credited with, over the full delivery
  years affected?
  (d) What was that worth in money, and what fraction of it did the patrons actually receive?

- **C2.** For the largest patron's year of 1910: (a) how many pounds of butterfat was he credited
  with? (b) how many pounds was he owed and not credited? (c) what was the difference worth in
  money that year?

- **C3.** Over the same full delivery years: (a) how many pounds of butterfat were credited to the
  station's patrons in total? (b) how many pounds actually reached the main creamery in the
  station's cream? (c) express the difference as a percentage — and be exact about what you are
  taking a percentage **of**.

- **C4.** (a) What was the total of the invoice for the station's own testing outfit? (b) How many
  pipettes did that invoice buy, and by how many cubic centimeters was each one short?

- **C5.** (a) For exactly how long was the faulty glassware in use, and over how many full
  delivery years does the money calculation run? State both, and say why they are different
  numbers.
  (b) How many pounds of milk did the station receive in its first season?

## Section D — Logic (10 points; a short paragraph each)

- **D1.** At one point the station's own measure broke and a borrowed one was used for a time.
  Treat that interval as an experiment. When was it and how long did it last? What was the
  variable, what was held constant, what was the result, and what does it establish that a
  calculation alone would not? Say what the interval does **not** establish.

- **D2.** Give at least three independent reasons, drawn from different narrators, why the
  station's tester cannot have been stealing cream. Say why your reasons are independent of one
  another, and name at least one that would still hold if the glassware had never been measured
  in 1958. Then say whether the Association's bookkeeper ever questioned the ledger entries that
  refute the accusation he made.

- **D3.** The manager signed the ledger sheets carrying the "station gain" line every year from
  the time he took office. Did he know, before the glassware was condemned, that the station's
  tests ran low? Answer, and justify your answer from the sources.

## Section E — Contradictions (8 points)

- **E1.** List every direct factual contradiction you can find between the retellings, or within a
  single retelling. For each: the conflicting claims; which narrators made them; your resolution
  — **or a statement that the sources cannot settle it**; and the reasoning method you used.

  Call out separately every case in which **two narrators carry the same wrong value**, and say
  exactly what breaks the tie.

  Call out separately every case in which a narrator **contradicts itself** on the page.

## Section F — Theory (6 points)

- **F1.** State, as a single coherent causal theory, what actually produced the shortfall at
  Ostrey Hollow. Your theory must explain the size of the error, why that particular size, why it
  began when it did, why it ended when it did, and why nothing at the station or on the farms had
  to change for either.

  The sources offer **three** competing explanations. Name all three, say who advanced each, and
  give for each the specific evidence that defeats it.

  Then answer two subsidiary questions: whether the 1925 board of arbitration ever examined the
  condemned glassware; and what the tester himself believed the cause was.

## Section G — Summary (4 points)

- **G1.** Summarize the whole eight-story history in **at most 150 words**, so that a reader who
  knows nothing of it would grasp what happened and why it took eight separate records to
  understand it.
