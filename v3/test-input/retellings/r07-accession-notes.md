# Retelling 07 — Halbert Nym, curator, accession notes for the Ordell County dairy collection, Ordell County Historical Rooms, 1972

*Working notes for the published catalogue, items 1–16, from the deposit slips, the office file, and the material. Transcriptions are exact and checked twice. The glosses are mine and are worth what an afternoon is worth.*

**Item 1. By-laws of the Larrow Valley Cooperative Creamery Association**, printed pamphlet, gray wrappers. Article VII, transcribed entire:

> *Article VII. Every patron shall be paid for the milk he delivers according to the number of
> pounds of butter fat contained in it, ascertained by test, and not according to the weight of
> the milk; and the test of the station where he delivers shall govern.*

The printed copies carry no year on their face, only the words "adopted at the annual meeting."

**Item 2. Invoice, Ammon & Sons, Dairy Supply, Tarnet, 14 April 1897.** One leaf, folded in three:

> *Sold to the Larrow Valley Co-op. Creamery Assn., for the Ostrey Hollow station:
> 6 doz. milk test pipettes, 17.6 c.c., at .35 — 25.20
> 12 doz. test bottles, 8 per cent neck, at .22 — 31.68
> 1 acid measure — .90
> 1 hand tester, 4 bottle — 18.00
> Freight — 2.15
> Total — 77.93*

Six dozen pipettes; $77.93 for the outfit, which the office file has in use at the station from 1 May 1897, Larrow Green having lent until then.

**Item 3. Station test book, kept by E. Brant.** Half-bound, marbled boards, back mended with cloth; columns for date, patron, pounds and test. One hand throughout, twenty-nine seasons of it. First leaf:

> *Ostrey Hollow station. Begun this 4th day of May, 1896. Glass borrowed of Larrow Green until
> our own comes. A. Keddie.*

Summary leaf at the back, made up in 1922:

> *Average test of all patrons, this station: 1896, 3.80. 1897, part year. 1898, 3.61. And 3.61
> or within three hundredths of it every year since, save the nine weeks of 1911 that are noted
> in their place, when it stood at 3.80.*

*Curatorial note.* The summary leaf gathers the yearly averages: 3.80 for the first season, 3.61 from 1898 on.

*Provenance.* Kept at the station from 4 May 1896; impounded by the board of arbitration in March 1925; in the Association's office at Ashlin from 1925 to 1959; received here in 1959.

**Item 4. Farm weigh book, kept by A. Keddie.** Small quarto, sheepskin, covers greasy at the corners. Kept daily from 1 January 1897: weights delivered, with the test paid on set against them. Leaf for 1910:

> *Year 1910. Delivered to the station, two hundred thousand pounds even, by my own scale and
> theirs agreeing. Paid on a test of 3.42.*

*Provenance.* Begun at Hessel Bottom on 1 January 1897 and kept there by one household throughout; to Roy Brant in 1929; lent by him to Miss Ivy Keddie in 1958 and returned; to Miss Hazel Brant in 1962; received here in 1963.

**Item 5. Crate, deal, with nine milk test pipettes.** Stenciled with the supplier's name, much stained. Nine pipettes survive, each blown on the stem at 17.6 c.c.; two chipped at the tip. Condemned by the state dairy inspector on 12 June 1923. The crate was found in the Association office at Ashlin, in February 1958, by Miss Ivy Keddie of the State Agricultural College at Farland, taken by her to Farland, measured there, and given to these Rooms in 1959. Measured mean delivery of the nine, 16.72 c.c.

**Item 6. Annual ledgers of the Association, station account**, canvas over boards. The station column runs from 1897; each year carries an entry in one form, of which this is typical:

> *Ostrey Hollow: butter fat credited to patrons on station test, 57,760 lb. Butter fat received
> at Larrow Green in station cream, 60,800 lb. Station gain, 3,040 lb, five and a quarter per
> cent, as usual. Carried to the general account.*

*Curatorial note.* The station's intake for the years 1898 to 1922 is given in these ledgers as 40,000,000 lb of milk, and the station gain accumulated across those years as 76,000 lb of butter fat, worth $19,000 at the average price paid for fat over the period. The two percentages in this file are not interchangeable: five and a quarter percent of the credited fat is five percent of the fat delivered, and each is right of its own denominator and of no other.

**Item 7. Minute book of the board of arbitration, Ashlin, 17 March to 4 May 1925**, in the hand of the Association's secretary. The award of $3,800, at the last leaf:

> *The board having heard the patrons and the officers, and having had before it the books and
> appliances of the station, finds no cause established. The board awards to the Ostrey Hollow
> patrons the sum of thirty-eight hundred dollars ($3,800.00), without admission, to be charged
> to the station account.*

**Item 8. Statement of A. Keddie to the board, 17 March 1925.** One leaf, ruled, both sides, in the hand of Item 3, signed. Not transcribed.

**Item 9. Photostat of the circuit notebook of M. Strawn, state dairy inspector, for 1923.** The entry of 12 June:

> *Ostrey Hollow. Scales true. Lactometer, no added water, as always here. Ran their milk
> pipettes against the burette. These measures will not deliver their mark. Condemned the lot,
> the whole crate of them, and told the manager to order new. Glass is glass and I did not
> weigh what it has cost them.*

*Provenance of the original.* To the state dairy office on the inspector's retirement in 1936; to the library of the State Agricultural College at Farland in 1947, where the 1923 volume was found and copied in 1958. The notebooks are not ours.

**Item 10. Letter, Ammon & Sons to the Association, 2 July 1923.** One leaf, the firm's head, acknowledging the Association's order of 28 June for replacement pipettes and bottles, promising shipment that month, and declining to say anything of the earlier lot. Not transcribed.

**Item 11. Report of the investigating committee, 1908**, fair copy, signed by the chairman:

> *We have sampled the herds of Hessel Bottom through January, February and March. The milk is
> whole and the scales are honest. It is our opinion that the wet hay of that bottom is what
> thins the cream, and that the remedy lies with the land and not with the station.*

The sampling was of January, February and March 1908 only, as the report states.

**Item 12. State Agricultural College, Bulletin No. 214 (1911)**, printed, in wrappers. The summary paragraph:

> *Bog hay fed as the whole of the roughage depressed the butter fat test by not more than five
> hundredths of one per cent in winter, and by nothing that could be measured on summer pasture.*

**Item 13. Correspondence file, Vessey County**, holding figures copied in 1958 from the test book of the Alder Corners Cheese Company for the season of 1919. Not transcribed. The test book itself has never been in this county; it passed from its founder to his daughter in 1941 and to the Vessey County Historical Society in 1970, where it is.

**Item 14. Measurement sheet, 9 March 1958**, one leaf, typed, figures entered by hand, signed by Miss Ivy Keddie:

> *Nine pipettes from the Ostrey Hollow crate, stems blown 17.6 c.c. Delivered against burette,
> mean of five runs each: 16.70, 16.71, 16.72, 16.72, 16.72, 16.73, 16.73, 16.74, 16.71. Mean
> 16.72 c.c. Short 0.88 c.c. That is 95 hundredths of the mark, and the mark is what the neck of
> the bottle was ruled for.*

**Item 15. Bulletin No. 471 (October 1958)**, printed, in wrappers. The closing paragraph:

> *No milk was watered at Ostrey Hollow and no cream was stolen there. From the first of May,
> 1897, to the twelfth of June, 1923, the station's tests were low by one part in twenty because
> its pipettes were short by one part in twenty, and the difference — seventy-six thousand pounds
> of butter fat — stands credited in the Association's own ledgers, year by year, as station
> gain.*

**Item 16. Receiving and churn books of the Larrow Green creamery**, water-damaged at the foot. Cream in, butter out, and the creamery's own tests. Over the same years for which the station's 3.61 is recorded, the Larrow Green average runs about three-quarters of a point better.

*Notes and queries.* — The station's intake for its first season, 4 May to the end of 1896, is not in the ledgers and is nowhere else; the station column begins with 1897, and inquirers wanting a figure for 1896 must be told there is none. — Item 5 wants a case with a lock. — Accession numbers still to be cut for Items 13 and 16.
