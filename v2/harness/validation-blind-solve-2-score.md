## Key problems surfaced by this run

**1. A2-8 and C3(d) are both winnable by a solver who commits the A06 error.**
The sheet asserts "three" for both post-rebuild winters — the exact A06 wrong pole — and still
banks A2-8 (whose only bolded numeral is "three", with "for the second winter after (1955/56)"
left unbolded and therefore non-gating) and C3(d) (whose two stated requirements are the
exclusion and the span, both of which the sheet supplies). Net cost of the error: −2, once,
against 2 points banked. Compare A2-4, which says outright "loses the point **and** takes a
corruption deduction" — the key knows how to state item loss and does not state it here.
*Suggested edit:* bold **"for the second winter (1955/56)"** in A2-8 and append "asserting
three as the two-winter total loses this item as well"; add the same clause to C3(d).

**2. D3 items 2–5 are almost entirely unbolded, so the bold-gating rule under-specifies them.**
D3-3's substantive requirement — "says that it weakens 'reduced entire and nothing else'
without establishing anything" — carries no bold at all, so under the key's own reading rule the
item collapses to a citation check. This sheet cites the slip and then argues the opposite
direction with it, and still scores.
*Suggested edit:* bold the analytical clause in D3-3, or add a line to "How to read a checklist
item" stating that in Section D an item with no bold is read whole.

**3. D3-4 bundles three separate requirements into one all-or-nothing point.**
Two-narrators-with-opposite-motives, neither-cites-anything-further, and
recommendation-cuts-both-ways are independent observations. The sheet made a defensible partial
answer (it identified the affirmative memoirist, quoted his admission that he has nothing in
writing, and weighed the recommendation) and scored zero.
*Suggested edit:* split D3-4 into two items and move one point out of D3-3, or bold the single
gate you actually want ("**the two narrators assert opposite motives from the same two
papers**").

**4. The "leaning is still abstention" allowance is stated only under D3, not in the general
gullibility rule or in E-c.**
This sheet writes "**CBD**, leaning 1884" for A05 and "**CBD**, leaning against" for A02. Under
the general rule as written ("asserting either 1884 or 1894 as established triggers the
gullibility deduction"), a literal grader could take −4 here; under the D3 note, correctly, −0.
That is a 4-point swing on grader reading alone.
*Suggested edit:* lift the sentence "an answer that leans one way while explicitly declining to
decide is a correct abstention" out of the D3 note and into the General deductions section, so
it governs all six abstention items.

**5. Cross-section credit travel is defined only for Section A.**
The key says credit does not travel *into* A from B–G, and says nothing about anywhere else.
Four items on this sheet turn on it: B1's "born 1874" (stated in A1, resolved in E-8), B4's
"nephew of the woman who made it" (established in B2), F5's "necessary, not sufficient" (stated
in D1), and A4-7's sale/scrap dates (stated in A3). I credited all four as substance, but a
different grader could take up to 4 points off on filing location alone.
*Suggested edit:* add a general scope rule — "a required element is credited wherever the answer
states it, except that credit never travels into Section A from B–G" — or state per section
which items must be self-contained.

**6. G4's "forty-three winters" is bolded but competes with a 120-word cap.**
G2 (four strands), G3 (three names), G4 (both arc ends with a numeral) and G5 (no corruption)
together demand more content than 120 words comfortably holds; this solver spent the budget on
the strands and the mechanism and dropped Judd and the numeral. Whether "1912 … 1954" satisfies
"forty-three winters" is undefined.
*Suggested edit:* state explicitly that the span (onset year to cessation year) satisfies G4 in
place of the literal number — or, if the literal number is wanted, say so and raise the cap to
130 words.

**7. E-a #15 ("where the wager was made") does not match how the corpus presents the dispute.**
The key treats store-vs-depot as an ordinary resolvable conflict decided by participants over
secondhand. The sheet split the question — it disposed of the depot version exactly as the key
wants ("the 'sat up together in the depot' version is at least disposable") but marked the
remaining half CBD, because the two participants disagree about the audience for the payment
rather than about the location. A solver reading the item as the key phrases it and a solver
reading the sources both end up correct, but only one of them is scoreable as E-a #15.
*Suggested edit:* narrow the item to "**where the wager was struck**" and add a note that the
disagreement about the payment is not part of it.
