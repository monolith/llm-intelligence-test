## Key problems surfaced by this run

**1. D3 / A01 — the abstention rule collides with the key's own evidence.** *(highest value)*
The key tells the grader that "reduced entire" is "genuine evidence against the punitive
reading" but still requires the solver to decline. The sheet splits the question: the
**causal** claim (did the objection cost her the job) it decides No on the letter of 30 April
1901 — reduced entire, pay to the last day, Tice's recommendation entered on her card — and
the **mental-state** claim (did Tice resent it) it declares unsettleable at E1.12 and in its
own abstention list. That split is analytically sound and is arguably what the question
("did the objection have anything to do with her leaving?") asks. As keyed it costs 3 points
(1 item + 2 deduction), which is the difference between 94 and 97.
*Suggested edit:* split D3.1 into two graded outcomes — (a) asserts a retaliatory motive as
fact → 0 and −2; (b) concludes non-retaliation **on the strength of the letter** while
explicitly leaving Tice's private view open → 1 and no deduction; (c) declines both → 1.
And reword the question to name which of the two it is asking about.

**2. Section A gives no scope rule for checklist credit.** A1.7 (the 1898 runaway plus the
1901 sale) is keyed under the *viaduct* story, but any solver will file the runaway and the
sale under A3, where this one did — and A3.3 keys the same sale again, so the fact is
double-counted across sub-questions. Nothing in the key says whether credit travels between
sub-answers or between sections.
*Suggested edit:* state explicitly that Section A items are credited if satisfied anywhere in
Section A; and either move the runaway to A3 or drop the duplicated sale clause from A1.7,
replacing it with a viaduct-specific fact (the line opening of 14 November 1897).

**3. A2.4 penalizes exactly the behavior the question demands.** The item requires the A2
narrative to carry the false lore ("the 1898 runaway inflated into a drowned engine and a
dead crew") while `questions.md` instructs solvers to "use the corrected facts, not the ones
you rejected." A solver who keeps refuted lore out of a factual reconstruction — and refutes
it thoroughly in D2 — loses a point for obedience.
*Suggested edit:* credit the lore wherever it is stated in the answers (D2/E qualify), or
reword A2 to ask what the valley believed the sound was and why.

**4. A4.7 bundles the survey's finding with a post-survey epilogue.** The question asks what
the **1962 survey** established; the item requires, in the same yes/no, the later deposit of
material with the Pell County Historical Society and the 1963/1967/1969 dates. Inside a
450-word cap that is a predictable omission, and it is not part of what the survey
established.
*Suggested edit:* split into two items, or drop the deposit clause and score it in B5 where
the chain of custody actually lives.

**5. Compound yes/no items with unmarked load-bearing parts.** A4.1 carries "(b. 1934)",
A4.6 carries "(kept from 1 January 1894)", A2.8 carries "14 January 1936" — each embedded in
an item worth a whole point with no partial credit, and none of them tested anywhere else in
the key. Two graders will disagree on these, which is fatal for the κ validation the key
calls for.
*Suggested edit:* mark the load-bearing sub-facts of every compound item in bold and state
that unbolded parentheticals do not gate the point.

**6. E row 11 (Kettle Bench start year) is the weakest "resolvable" call in the key.** The
key resolves 1894 by majority, but both 1894 sources (r04, r07) are demonstrably wrong on
other Wexler particulars in the same passage — r04 on the record low, r07 on "two brothers"
— and r06, the keeper, checks her 1884 twice against her own text. The sheet declined, which
the key neither credits nor penalizes. Meanwhile 1884 sits on the corruption list, so a
solver who follows the keeper loses a point *and* a deduction while one who abstains loses
only the point.
*Suggested edit:* either state a stronger method for row 11 (e.g. two independent physical
handlings of the boxes outrank a keeper's round recollection) or list it as a permitted
abstention alongside A02–A04.

**7. The Section E cap makes the section insensitive.** Resolvable conflicts cap at 6 out of
20 listed. This sheet found and resolved 18 and scored the same 6 as a sheet that found 6.
Two of the key's near-ties — its stated discriminators — carry no more weight than a birth
year.
*Suggested edit:* raise the resolvable cap, or weight the two near-ties (Sheet 11, sale
price) and the two internal-inconsistency rows (r07, r08) at 2 points each within the same
cap.

**8. G2 packs two requirements into one point under a 120-word ceiling.** Naming four
strands *and* the kinship chain inside 120 words, alongside G3's arc start, G4's arc end and
G5's cleanliness, leaves almost no slack. The sheet spent its words on the four strands and
lost the item on a missing "nephew."
*Suggested edit:* split G2 into "names the four strands" and "names the family link," or drop
the link requirement to "names Adela, Judd and Ruth."
