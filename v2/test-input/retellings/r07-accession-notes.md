# Retelling 07 — Merrit Sable, curator, accession notes, Pell County Historical Society, Ninestone, 1970

*Draft catalogue entries for four collections and one object, worked up in a single week with the whole of it on one table and two other accessions waiting. Provenance from the depositors' slips where slips exist and from inquiry where they do not. My guesses are marked as guesses where I remembered to mark them. Queries for a return visit at the end.*

**Item 1. Field book, black oilcloth covers**, pocket size, roughly 4½ by 7 inches, sewn, edges rubbed through at the corners. Attributed: **J. Rennick.** Perhaps a hundred and twenty pages used, in pencil, upright, small, very regular; level notes, offsets, and temperature readings, with the pages of the years after 1898 much less full than those before. Front matter carries, on the inside cover, a single ruled line evidently kept there for reference and transcribed here exactly:

> *Rule for the long span: one inch in three hundred feet for forty degrees.*

Page 62, the most substantial entry in the book, and the one the survey of 1962 (Item 5) turns on:

> *Sept. 12, 1897. Span closed 3:40 p.m., thermometer 66°. Rollers set at the two-inch mark. Sheet 11 calls for three. Reported to W.T., who answers that the pedestal cannot be recast before the fall haul and that two inches is more than the valley will ever ask for. Entered here so that it is somewhere. — A.R.*

Page 71, in the same hand:

> *March 6, 1898. No. 9 off the iron at the north approach, 7:20 a.m., running away from the quarry. Nobody killed. Lidell's arm broke, Sherrod scalded at the deck. Nine days to rerail her.*

The page 71 entry agrees in every particular with a newspaper account of 8 March 1898 which I have seen in the Society's own file of the *Sentinel* — the same hour, the same north end, the same two men hurt and none killed. Worth a footnote when this is published.

Loose in the back of Item 1, unpinned, folded in three: one letter on railroad paper, Cadder Valley Railroad to A. Rennick, dated 30 April 1901.

> *The engineering party is reduced entire on the 15th May next, and your time is paid to that day. Mr. Tice asks that his recommendation be entered upon your card.*

*Provenance, Item 1.* Given to the Society in 1963 by Mr. Wendell Frayne of Cadder Falls. It was his mother's book, and by his account she had it in her own keeping from 1901 until her death in 1959, after which it was his. Depositor's slip is in his hand and is not signed.

**Item 2. Station ledger, half-bound, "the night book"** (so called on the wrapper and by everyone who has asked after it). Attributed: **A. Rennick.** Five ruled columns: the date; the day's high; the night's low; whether the sound came; the hour it came. Forty-three winters, unbroken. A heavier, blunter pencil than Item 1 and a hand that slopes. Front matter carries a retrospective note, undated in itself but written up from a station slate:

> *Bridge gang here three weeks, August 1909. New 85-lb. steel, new ties, both shoes jacked and set again to the mark. Thermometer on the north pier read 86 at four o'clock when they seated the south nest. Mr. Coyle says the deck is forty ton heavier than it was.*

At the back, on a page ruled off from the rest and headed only with a date, a summary:

> *Forty-three winters. Sixty-one nights she spoke. Thirty-four winters she spoke at least once and nine she never spoke at all. Five nights answered both the conditions and gave nothing, which makes sixty-six nights that answered them in all.*

And, two leaves further on, the last thing written in the book:

> *Second winter since the rockers went in. Three nights under six degrees, three falls of better than thirty, and not a sound out of her either winter. I have kept this book forty-three winters and I do not know now what it was I was listening to.*

Dated 2 March 1956. Nothing after it but ruled blanks.

A few particulars from Item 2 worth recording in the entry, as I am asked for them regularly. The fullest winter in the book is 1917–18, with five nights. The last night entered as heard is 9 February 1954. The new bearings went into the bridge in March 1954.

*Provenance, Item 2.* Received in 1969 from the estate of Mr. Judd Rennick, station agent at Cadder Falls 1915–1954, who died that year. The book had been lent by him personally to the author of Item 5 in 1962 and returned to him afterward; it was never the Society's before 1969, and any earlier citation of it is a citation of the loan.

**Item 3. Weather record, four cardboard boxes**, farm daybooks and later ruled sheets, kept at the Wexler farm at Kettle Bench, three miles above Cadder Falls, from 1 January 1894. Daily high, daily low, precipitation. Two hands, the second taking over partway through.

*Provenance, Item 3.* Received 1967 from the farm. The record was kept by two brothers named Wexler, the younger of whom carried it on after the elder gave it up. The boxes came to us in the same condition in which they went down to the 1962 survey and came back.

Extremes of the series, from the depositor's own index sheet: record low **eleven below zero, January 1918**; record high **one hundred and one degrees, July 1936**.

**Item 4. Pedestal, cast iron**, broken at one corner, two men to move it, on the floor of the north store room where it will have to stay. Card with the object, in the survey author's hand:

> *Recovered from the creek bed forty feet below the south abutment. Chisel mark on the roller seat at two inches.*

Recovered September 1962; given to the Society in 1963 with Item 5.

**Item 5. Survey report**, county highway department, September 1962, typescript with a linen tracing folded in. The author is a county highway engineer. Its closing paragraph, which has been quoted at us by three separate correspondents and which I therefore transcribe entire:

> *The south pedestal was recovered from the creek bed forty feet below the abutment, where the 1954 gang threw it. The chisel mark on the roller seat is at two inches. Sheet 11 calls for three. There is no locomotive in this creek and there never was one. What the valley heard for forty-three winters was a bridge letting go of itself, one inch late.*

**Item 6. Photostats, two pages**, supplied to the 1962 survey by another institution and left with the report. The first, from a railroad stock book, 19 June 1901:

> *Locomotive No. 9, 4-4-0, Rowan Works 1889, works no. 1142, sold this day to the Tolliver Lumber Company, Bly County, for two thousand one hundred and fifty dollars ($2,150.00), delivered on her own wheels.*

The second, from a lumber company shop book, May 1929, the engine having been set aside the previous year:

> *No. 4 (ex-Cadder Valley 9) cut up at Sixmile this month. Scrap to Ravel Brothers, $470.*

These are copies only. The Tolliver Lumber Company's stock book and shop book are **not** in this collection and have never been; they are at the Bly County Historical Rooms, two hundred miles north, deposited there in 1938, and the correspondence file shows they were catalogued on arrival. We hold the two pages and nothing else of that company.

**Note on the depositors and their relations**, taken off the slips and from one conversation. The author of Item 5 is the granddaughter of the writer of the field book. Her father, Mr. Wendell Frayne, who deposited Item 1, and Judd Rennick, who wrote the night book, were first cousins. Judd Rennick's father, Emil, and Adela, who wrote the field book, were brother and sister. So Items 1 and 2 come out of one family two generations apart, and Item 5 out of the third generation of it, which is unusual enough in a county collection to be worth saying in the published note.

Of the depositors named above: Judd Rennick died in 1969; Mr. Wendell Frayne is living; Lettie Wexler died in 1967, the same year her boxes came in.

*Queries for the return visit.* — The pencilled attributions on the wrappers of Items 1 and 2 sit oddly with the initials inside them; both wrappers were marked before the material reached me and I have carried them over as they stood, having had no time to compare hands. Resolve before the accession is published. — Whether Item 6 should be described here at all, or simply referred to Bly County. — Accession numbers for Item 4, which is too heavy to bring up to the desk and which nobody has yet measured. — The date and circumstances of the newspaper file's own deposit, which is not on any slip I can find.
