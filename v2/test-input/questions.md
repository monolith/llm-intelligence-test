# Test Questions

You have been given **twelve retellings** (r01–r12) of events in and around Cadder Falls,
Pell County. The narrators knew different parts of the history; each got some things right
and some things wrong, and several of them ran separate strands together. There were
originally **four** underlying stories. You have not seen them.

Answer from the retellings alone. Where sources conflict, resolve the conflict and say how
you resolved it — majority evidence, arithmetic, a document outranking a memory, internal
consistency, or whatever method you actually used. Where you must infer, say so.

**"Cannot be determined from the sources" is a valid and sometimes correct answer.** Some
questions here can be settled and some cannot. When a question cannot be settled, say so,
and say what the sources *do* establish and why that is not enough. Guessing confidently
where the sources do not decide is scored worse than declining.

Give numeric answers in the exact format requested, and show your working where asked.

---

## Section A — Reconstruction (30 points)

Reconstruct each underlying story as a factual narrative of **250–450 words**: who, when,
the key numbers, the key decisions. Use the corrected facts, not the ones you rejected.

- **A1.** The building of the Sallow Creek Viaduct.
- **A2.** The night book and what was heard from the bridge.
- **A3.** The locomotive's second life.
- **A4.** The 1962 survey and what it established.

## Section B — Relationships (10 points; one line each, with your reasoning)

- **B1.** What was the relationship between Adela Rennick and Emil Rennick?
- **B2.** What was Judd Rennick's relationship to Adela Rennick? Cite what establishes it, and resolve
  anything in the sources that points elsewhere.
- **B3.** What was Ruth Frayne's relationship to Judd Rennick? Derive it.
- **B4.** Who was Dorsey Tice's father, and why does that matter to the events of January
  1936?
- **B5.** Trace the chain of custody by which the field book, the night book, the weather
  record, and the Bly County ledgers all came into one person's hands in 1962.

## Section C — Math (20 points; show working)

- **C1.** Using the movement rule for the center span and the temperature at which the span
  was closed in 1897:
  (a) at what temperature would the span have run out of cold travel had the roller nest
  been set as the contract drawing specified?
  (b) at what temperature did it run out of cold travel as the nest was actually set in
  1897?
  (c) at what temperature did it run out of cold travel after the August work of 1909?
  (d) how many degrees of margin did that 1909 work cost?

- **C2.** On the afternoon of the wager the station thermometer read 38 degrees at four
  o'clock and was falling four degrees an hour.
  (a) At that rate, at about what hour would it have crossed the threshold?
  (b) What was the actual fall from that day's high to that night's low, in degrees, and
  did it satisfy the second condition?

- **C3.** From the night book:
  (a) the average number of nights per logged winter on which the bridge was heard, to one
  decimal place;
  (b) the total number of nights that met both conditions, whether or not anything was
  heard;
  (c) the average per winter counting only winters in which it was heard at least once, to
  one decimal place.
  State any nights you excluded from these counts, and why.

- **C4.** The locomotive:
  (a) how old was she when she was cut up?
  (b) what percentage of her original cost did her sale to her second owner realize?
  (c) how many years did she work for her second owner?

- **C5.** (a) The total length of the viaduct.
  (b) The fall of the line, in feet, from the quarry to the viaduct.
  (c) The distance, in inches, that the center span moves between the highest and the
  lowest temperature in the valley's weather record.

## Section D — Logic (15 points; a short paragraph each)

- **D1.** Were "a night low below the threshold" and "a fall of at least thirty degrees
  from that day's high" **necessary** conditions for the bridge to be heard, **sufficient**
  conditions, both, or neither? Justify from the logged numbers.

- **D2.** Give at least three independent reasons, drawn from different narrators, why what
  the valley heard cannot have been Locomotive No. 9.

- **D3.** Adela Rennick's engagement with the railroad ended in 1901, four years after she
  entered her objection about the roller nest. Did the objection have anything to do with
  her leaving? Answer, and justify your answer from the sources.

## Section E — Contradictions (10 points)

- **E1.** List every direct factual contradiction you can find between the retellings, or
  within a single retelling. For each one give: the conflicting claims; which narrators
  made them; your resolution — **or a statement that the sources cannot settle it**; and
  the reasoning method you used.

## Section F — Theory (10 points)

- **F1.** State, as a single coherent causal theory, what actually produced the sound. Your
  theory must explain: the character of the sound; the temperature threshold and why that
  particular number; the second condition; why some qualifying nights were silent; why the
  sound began when it did and not earlier; and why it stopped permanently when it did — and
  it must name the physical evidence recovered in 1962 that confirms it.

## Section G — Summary (5 points)

- **G1.** Summarize the whole four-story history in **at most 120 words**, so that a reader
  who knows nothing of it would grasp what happened and why it took four separate records
  to understand it.
