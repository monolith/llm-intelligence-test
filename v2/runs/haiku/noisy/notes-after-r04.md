# Comprehensive Notes: Retellings r01–r04

## Timeline & Locations

**Cadder Valley Railroad**: 31-mile line
- Ninestone: milepost 0 (county seat, shops, HQ, chief clerk's office)
- Sallow Creek Viaduct: milepost 22 (the bridge)
- Cadder Falls: milepost 23 (mill town)
- Ashby quarry: milepost 31
- Grade from quarry to viaduct: 2.2% throughout

**Bridge Construction & Opening**
- Began: April 1896
- Opened to Cadder Falls: 14 November 1897
- Three spans: 120 ft + 300 ft (center, truss) + 120 ft = 540 ft total
- Height above water: 97 feet
- Center span pinned at north end, south end free on rollers

**The Crash & Aftermath**
- 6 March 1898, 7:20 AM: Locomotive No. 9 derailed at north approach
  - Two hurt, nobody killed (directly contradicts valley legend)
  - Took nine days to rerail
- 19 June 1901: No. 9 sold to Tolliver Lumber Company (Bly County, 200 miles north) for $2,150
  - Delivered under own power ("steamed away north")
  - May 1929: cut up at Sixmile

**Destruction of Records**
- 4 April 1911: Fire at Ninestone roundhouse/office destroyed all construction records (contract files, erection timebooks, bridge construction accounts)

**Bridge Maintenance**
- August 1909: Re-decking project
  - 3 weeks duration
  - 85-pound steel (replaced 60-pound)
  - New ties throughout
  - Both shoes jacked and reset to the mark
  - Thermometer on north pier: 86° at 4 PM when south nest was seated
  - New deck: 40 tons heavier than original
- Spring 1953–1954: Rehabilitated for diesel power
  - Old cast pedestals under center span removed, thrown into creek
  - New rocker bearings installed (4 inches travel each way)

## The Bridge's Thermal Movement & Engineering

**Technical Rule**: One inch of travel per 40 degrees of temperature change (three-hundred-foot span)

**Original Design (Sept 12, 1897 Closure)**
- Thermometer reading: 66°F
- Sheet 11 called for 3 inches of travel on south nest
- Chisel mark (setting mark) set at 2 inches
- Adela Rennick's note: reported discrepancy to resident engineer Warren Tice
  - Tice's response: pedestal cannot be recast before fall haul, "two inches is more than the valley will ever ask for"
  - Entered "so that it is somewhere"

**August 1909 Re-setting**
- Shoes jacked and reset to the same 2-inch mark, but with steel at 86°F
- This created binding point at 6°F (86 minus 80 = 6°)

**Ruth Frayne's Calculation**
- Rule: 1 inch per 40°
- 2-inch setting = 80° of movement range
- Closed at 66°; 66 − 80 = −14°F (valley never reached this)
- After Aug 1909 re-setting at 86°: 86 − 80 = +6°F (the exact threshold)
- Nov 1897–Aug 1909: 7 nights below 6°; coldest was −9° (4 Feb 1899); no sound heard
- First sound: 17 Jan 1912 (low −2°), after Aug 1909 reset made 6° the binding point

## The "Sound" or "Boom"

**Physical Cause**: Bridge span cooling past its binding point (6°F), south shoe hung up, span dropped suddenly with one deep report that ran along rails

**Characteristics** (all sources consistent):
- One deep report, not rattle or roll
- Came up rails first (sounder rattled before sound reached room)
- Then floor moved
- Then audible noise
- Did not travel; was "there and then done"
- Only on killing nights: midnight to 4 AM (usually ~2 AM)
- Only when night's low was below 6°F AND preceded by 30°+ degree drop from previous day

**Judd Rennick's Night Book** (43 winters, started winter 1907, recorded 1913–1956)
- Total nights sound occurred: 61
- Winters with at least one: 34
- Winters with none: 9
- Nights meeting both conditions but no sound: 5
- Judd's only theory: won't speak twice in single freeze; needs real thaw (above 40° daytime) between occurrences
- Wager: 29 January 1937, 1:10 AM. Judd bet Dorsey Tice $5 it would speak (low −3°). Judd won; Dorsey paid in store next day in front of everyone, told story on himself rest of life.

**Weather Verification**: Wexler family record (Jan 1, 1894–ongoing)
- Started by Peter Wexler, continued by daughter Lettie (since 1926)
- Unrelated to bridge/sound, no connection to story
- Matches all 61 nights of Judd's record exactly
- Highest temp in series: 101° (July 1936)
- Lowest temp in series: −13° (January 1918)

**Sound & Deaths**: Judd counted 190 deaths at Cadder Falls during his book-keeping years; 7 occurred within a week after sound; 7 out of 190 contradicts "death omen" legend

## Key Conflicts & Oddities

**Central Discrepancy: The Dead Crew Legend vs. Fact**
- *Valley Legend*: Entire crew of No. 9 went into gorge and died; they remain there; ghost train repeats fatal run
- *Fact*: No. 9 derailed at north approach (not in gorge) at 7:20 AM; two hurt, nobody killed; No. 9 rerailed in 9 days; sold June 1901 and removed from valley alive; no bodies in creek
- Ruth's inspection: No locomotive in creek over 800+ feet of bed; no frame, boiler shell, driving wheel, iron larger than spike
- No bodies ever recovered or mentioned in any account

**The Disappearance of Records**
- 4 April 1911 fire destroyed all viaduct construction records (contract files, erection timebooks)
- Adela's field book survived because she kept it separately and privately
- After 1911, company possessed nothing in writing about bridge construction
- Ruth notes: "I do not think anybody at the time understood that a fire could take away a thing that had already happened"

**1897 vs. 1909 vs. 1912**
- Sept 12, 1897: Span closed at 66°, rolled at 2-inch mark (1 inch short of contract spec)
  - Adela reported; overruled; entered it anyway
  - No sound heard for 12 years despite coldest night reaching −9°
- August 1909: Shoes reset at 86° to same 2-inch mark
  - This reset created 6°F as binding point (86 − 80 = 6)
  - First night below 6° after reset: 17 January 1912
  - Sound began that night; Judd started recording that winter
- Harlan Pike's only opinion: 40 tons added 1908/1909 caused noise; wrong about mechanism but right about 1908/1909 onset

**The Pedestal Mark Discrepancy**
- Sheet 11 contract: 3 inches travel
- Actual chisel mark on recovered pedestal: 2 inches
- Sept 12, 1897 closure: set at 2 inches (Adela noted)
- Recovered from creek 1962: still 2 inches
- Ruth's finding: proves original shortfall built-in

**Five Silent Nights**
- 66 nights met both conditions (< 6° AND > 30° fall)
- 61 nights sound occurred
- 5 nights met conditions but no sound
- Judd's theory: won't speak twice in single freeze
- All 5 silent nights occurred in cold spell following earlier sound, before thaw above 40°

**Temperatures & Binding Point**
- Rule: 1 inch per 40°
- 2-inch setting: 80° of travel
- Before Aug 1909: −14° was binding point (never reached; coldest 1897–1909 was −9°)
- After Aug 1909: +6° was binding point (reached 61 times)
- After 1954 rocker bearings (4 inches each way = 160° travel): 6° no longer binding; sound stopped

**The Bridge Gang Date Discrepancy**
- r02 (Harlan Pike): "1908. The bridge gang was on the structure some weeks that summer: eighty-five-pound steel in place of the sixty..."
- r03 (Judd Rennick): "Bridge gang here three weeks, August 1909"
- r04 (Ruth Frayne): "August 1909"
- Judd's book entry front matter explicitly says "August 1909"
- Ruth identifies this as "summer of 1911" in r04 before correcting: "That was the summer of 1911. Three weeks of them..."—wait, actually Ruth says "August 1909" in quotation and "summer of 1911" in prose; Judd's entry says August 1909
- **Likely explanation**: Harlan may have misremembered or written "1908" when referring to the period leading up to 1909 work; primary sources (Judd's notation, Ruth's verification, and Ruth's calculation relying on "August 1909") align on 1909

**No Sound After 1954 Bearings**
- Spring 1954: rockers replaced old pedestals
- Rocker bearings: 4 inches travel each way (vs. 2 inches from 1897/1909)
- Second winter after rockers: 3 nights met conditions, no sound on any
- Sound stopped and never returned
- Valley "took this hard, as though something had been stolen from it"

## Summary of Mechanism

**The Bridge Flaw**: On 12 September 1897, the center span's south bearing (roller nest pedestal) was set at 2 inches of travel instead of the contracted 3 inches. Adela Rennick reported this discrepancy to resident engineer Warren Tice, who refused to stop work, claiming the pedestal could not be recast before fall haul and "two inches is more than the valley will ever ask for."

**The Trigger**: In August 1909, a construction gang re-decked the bridge, adding 40 tons to the structure and resetting both shoes to the "mark" they had been on—which was 2 inches. Critically, they performed this work at 86°F. The thermal calculation revealed this reset created a binding point: with 80° of thermal range (2 inches × 40°/inch), the span would bind at 86 − 80 = 6°F.

**The Manifestation**: From 1897 through 1908, winter temperatures in the valley never dropped below 6°F when conditions (30°+ degree fall) were met; the coldest on record was −9°F (4 Feb 1899), but that occurred without a preceding 30°+ fall. Thus the 1897 error remained silent. On 17 January 1912, the valley experienced its first night meeting both conditions *after* the binding point was established: high 34°, low −2°, a 36° fall. The span cooled to 6°F and beyond, the south shoe hung up on travel exhausted, the span carried load in bending stress, the shoe suddenly released, and the span dropped into position with one deep report that resonated along the rails.

**The Paradox of Silence**: The bridge was "wrong" (2 inches instead of 3) from 1897, but behaved correctly because required thermal conditions never aligned. It remained "wrong" through forty-six winters undetected. The 1909 work, performed in good faith in heat, inadvertently finalized the error by resetting the mechanism to a binding point that the valley would reach. The mechanism spoke 61 times in 43 winters until 1954, when rockers with 4 inches of travel each way were installed, providing enough slack that 6°F no longer mattered.
