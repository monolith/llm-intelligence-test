## The tolling — mechanism (the buried theory)

- Gate No. 3's culvert **invert (lip) sits at 14 ft on the spillway gauge** (stated in the ledger; Ilsa painted the gauge herself).
- When the gauge falls **below 14 ft**, air enters the culvert. The undersized, worn trunnion pin lets Gate 3 swing loose in its frame; a sustained **east wind** (off the moor) rocks it, and the knocking resonates in the empty culvert as a slow, bell-like toll.
- Hence: tolling requires gauge < 14 ft AND east wind (necessary conditions; not sufficient — the wind must also hold through the night).
- Tolling era: 1918–1946. Started when draw-down first regularly crossed 14 ft; **stopped permanently after the March 1946 pin replacement**.
- The church bell **cannot** be the cause: sold 11 Mar 1906, chamber found empty (headstock and wheel only) in 1968.
- In 1968 Vera finds the **original pin in the Gate 3 culvert mud, worn oval**; the 1946 replacement pins are visibly newer.

## Aron's log — the numbers

- Log spans **17 winters** (1929/30 – 1945/46): **43 tolling nights** total.
- Every tolling night: east wind + gauge below 14 ft. **6 additional nights** met both conditions with **no** tolling → 49 qualifying nights total; conditions necessary, not sufficient.
- "Rings for the dead" test: **9 village deaths** in the period; only **2** fell within a day of a tolling night → lore debunked.
- Wager math: 1 Nov 1930 gauge = **16 ft 6 in**; winter draw-down **3 in/week**; 30 in ÷ 3 = 10 weeks → gauge below 14 ft on **10 Jan 1931**. Aron named "before January is out"; first east wind came 21 Jan; he heard it and won.

## Ilsa's arithmetic

- Corven's promise: lake fills in **120 days**. Ilsa: inflow 12 M gal/day (wet winter) minus 2 M gal/day compensation flow owed to the downstream mills = 10 M net; 1,440 ÷ 10 = **144 days**. Actual: 147 (rain variance).
- Pin undersize: 1/8 in on 4 in = **3.125%**.
- Ledger front matter: Selde flow abstracts **1876–1904** copied from the millers' books; dry years **1893 and 1902**.

## Vera's dendrochronology

- **9 stumps** sectioned ("the glass orchard" — anaerobically preserved, grey and hard).
- Oldest: **88 rings**, outermost ring **1906** → innermost ring **1819** (1906 − 88 + 1); planted ~1818.
- Narrow rings at **1893 and 1902** match the ledger's dry years → trees and ledger cross-date ("two witnesses who never met").
