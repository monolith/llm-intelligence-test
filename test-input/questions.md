# Test Questions

You have been given six retellings (r1–r6) of events surrounding the Selde Weir. The
narrators knew different parts of the history; each got some things right and some
wrong, and some mixed separate strands together. There were originally **three**
underlying stories. You have not seen them.

Answer from the retellings alone. Where sources conflict, resolve the conflict and say
how (majority evidence, arithmetic, document vs. memory, internal consistency). Where
you must infer, say so. Give numeric answers in the exact format requested.

## Section A — Reconstruction

- **A1.** Reconstruct the first underlying story (the building of the weir) as a factual
  narrative of 300–600 words: who, when, the key numbers, the key decisions.
- **A2.** Reconstruct the second underlying story (the tolling and the log), same form.
- **A3.** Reconstruct the third underlying story (the drought survey), same form.

## Section B — Relationships (one line each)

- **B1.** What was the relationship between Ilsa and Tomas?
- **B2.** What was Aron Keel's relationship to Ilsa? Cite the evidence that resolves the
  conflicting claims.
- **B3.** What was Vera Brandt's relationship to Aron Keel? Derive it.
- **B4.** Whose family orchard was drowned at Aldercote? Resolve the conflict.
- **B5.** Trace the chain of custody by which the green ledger and the tolling log came
  to be found together in 1968.

## Section C — Math (show working)

- **C1.** The company promised the lake would fill in 120 days. Reproduce the corrected
  calculation, state the corrected estimate, and state the actual figure.
- **C2.** By what fraction of an inch, and what percentage, was the Gate No. 3 trunnion
  pin undersized? (Resolve the conflicting undersize claims first.)
- **C3.** From the gauge reading of 1 November 1930 and the stated draw-down rate, on
  about what date did the gauge first fall below 14 feet? Answer as a date.
- **C4.** What calendar year does the innermost ring of the oldest orchard stump
  correspond to? Show the subtraction.
- **C5.** (a) What was the average number of tolling nights per logged winter, to one
  decimal place? (b) How many nights in total met both conditions (east wind and gauge
  below 14 ft), whether or not tolling occurred?

## Section D — Logic (short paragraph each)

- **D1.** Were "east wind" and "gauge below 14 ft" necessary conditions for the tolling,
  sufficient conditions, both, or neither? Justify from the logged numbers.
- **D2.** Give at least three independent reasons, drawn from different narrators, why
  the tolling cannot have been the Aldercote church bell.
- **D3.** Explain (a) why the tolling was first heard only in winter 1918 and not before,
  and (b) why it ceased permanently after March 1946.

## Section E — Contradictions

- **E1.** List every direct factual contradiction you can find between (or within) the
  retellings. For each: the conflicting claims, which narrators, your resolution, and
  the reasoning method used.

## Section F — Theory

- **F1.** State, as a single coherent causal theory, what actually produced the tolling.
  Your theory must explain: the sound's character, the 14-foot threshold, the wind
  condition, why some qualifying nights were silent, the 1918 onset, and the permanent
  cessation after March 1946 — and it must name the physical evidence recovered in the
  drought year that confirms it.

## Section G — Summary

- **G1.** Summarize the whole three-story history in **at most 120 words**, such that a
  reader who knows nothing of it would grasp what happened and why it took three
  people to understand it.
