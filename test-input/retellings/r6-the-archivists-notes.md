# Retelling 6 — Working notes of the county archivist (fragmentary)

*Catalogue notes toward an accession from the Harden museum, drafted from a hurried single day with the material. Not checked. My interleavings and guesses are marked as such where I remembered to.*

**Item 1. Green canvas ledger** ("the weir book"). Attributed: **A. Keel**. Dense gauge figures and flow tables in an upright hand. Front matter: flow abstracts for the R. Selde, **1876–1904**, evidently copied from millers' records; conspicuous low-flow years **1893 and 1902**. Noteworthy entry, p. 47, dated **14 April 1905**, transcribed exactly:

> *No. 3 trunnion pin gauged 3⅞ in. against 4 in. Blowhole at collar. Flagged to E.C. Overruled. — I.V.*

Later entry, spring 1906: sale of the Aldercote church bell, **11 March 1906, £64**, to the foundry at Harden, proceeds to the grave-relocation fund. Loose in the back: a letter of dismissal from the Selde Weir Company dated **September 1906**.

**Item 2. Exercise book** ("the night book"). Attributed: **I. Voss**. A log of nocturnal tolling occurrences over seventeen winters, tabulating date, wind direction, and spillway gauge reading. (I confess the two attributions were pencilled on the wrappers when the material reached Harden and I have simply carried them over; I had no time to compare hands.)

**Item 3. Nine transverse stump sections**, ex the drowned orchard at Aldercote — the Keel family orchard, as I understand it, the schoolmaster's people. Remarkable preservation. The oldest section: **eighty-eight rings, outermost year 1906**. Depositor's slip notes pinched rings in agreement with the ledger's low-flow years — the depositor's phrase, rather fine for a survey report: *"two witnesses who never met."*

**Item 4. One iron trunnion pin**, heavily worn, markedly oval in section, recovered from culvert mud at the Selde Weir, gate not recorded on the slip — but cf. the ledger entry above.

**Item 5. Survey report**, county waterworks, dated **September 1968**, signed V. Brandt: conditions of record drought, spillway gauge at **9 ft 4 in**, lakebed traversable. Includes the bell-chamber observation (headstock and wheel in situ, **no bell present**) and closes with the sentence the newspapers took up: *"The lake never had a ghost. It had a hinge."*

*Query for the return visit: the pencilled attributions on Items 1 and 2 sit oddly with the initials inside them. Also: relation of V. Brandt (Item 5) to the Voss and Keel names above — depositor's slip calls the schoolmaster "my mother's cousin," which I have not yet squared with the orchard being Keel property. Resolve before the accession is published.*
